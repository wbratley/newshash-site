← Teenage boy on trial accused of murdering 14-year-old girl in south Wales park

Schoolgirl stabbed 55 times after being 'lured' to park by 14-year-old boy after she found out he was 'two-timing' her, jury hears

Article text

Schoolgirl stabbed 55 times after being 'lured' to park by 14-year-old boy after she found out he was 'two-timing' her, jury hears A boy of 14 stabbed a schoolgirl 55 times after she discovered he was secretly dating another girl behind her back, a murder trial heard today. Music-loving Lilly Jones, 14, 'joined forces' with the other girl and the 'mature' pair planned to confront the cheating teenager, it is alleged. But Lilly arranged to meet the boy, who cannot be named for legal reasons, first and vanished soon afterwards. Both were captured on CCTV together, in the town of Blaina, South Wales, around 6.50pm, on Saturday June 20 but Lilly, who had celebrated her 14th birthday the day before, never returned home. Newport Crown Court heard her body was discovered two days later in woods at the edge of a local park, just 500 yards from where she lived. Prosecutor Anna Pope, KC, said relatives reported Lilly missing and police quickly established that the boy was the last person to see her alive. 'When he was first approached by police, he told them that he had been due to meet up with Lilly, but she didn't turn up,' Ms Pope said. 'We say Lilly did not meet up with anyone else that night. 'We say that the boy was the last person to see her alive. We say that he was the person who murdered Lilly and then left her in the woods, either dying or already dead.' Lilly Jones, 14, was found with 55 stab wounds to her neck, chest and back Lilly's body was found in a patch of woodland in Duffryn Park, a nine-acre expanse of sports pitches and nature trails along the banks of the Ebbw Fach river Floral tributes were left near Duffryn Park in Blaina after Lilly's body was discovered following a two-day search Lilly's body was found in undergrowth in Duffryn Park having suffered more than 50 stab wounds to her face, neck, back and chest. The court heard that the boy's DNA was found under Lilly's nails during a post-mortem examination. Ms Pope said: 'When police had spoken to (the defendant) on the Sunday they noted that he had scratches inside his arm. 'You will see evidence which we say shows that those scratches were not on his arm on the Saturday during the daytime. 'We say that you can consider whether, during the incident, Lilly has used her nails to scratch out at the person who was killing her.' Jurors were also told that Lilly's DNA was also found on a blood-stained camping knife recovered from the boy's bedroom during a search of his home. Ms Pope said: 'We say that (the defendant) has not yet given any reason why it would be that Lilly's DNA was found on a knife in his drawer, after a time when we know that she had been stabbed repeatedly by someone using a knife. 'The prosecution says that this was the weapon which (the defendant) used to kill her and that whilst he had made some attempts to clean the knife, we say he was not able to remove traces of her DNA which had ended up down inside the handle of the knife.' Ms Pope said Lilly and the boy started to 'take an interest' in each other in March, when she was still 13. They messaged each other for several months before she found out the boy was also 'in some sort of relationship' with another girl, who also cannot be named for legal reasons. Jurors were told that the two girls contacted each other and formed a friendship and planned to confront the boy over his deception. Lilly had been messaging the boy for four months, the court heard 'Rather than have any bad feelings towards each other, the two girls got in contact, and, it would appear, showed an understanding of the other girl's situation,' Ms Pope said. 'Something you may consider to be quite mature for their ages. And so, they joined forces, becoming friends together. 'We say that (the boy) did not know that they each knew about the other. We say that he did not know that the two girls he was seeing were by now friends with each other.' The court heard the boy initially told police he had planned to meet Lilly on June 20 but she had not turned up. He later changed his story and told officers they had been together that night but that Lilly went on to meet someone else. But, Ms Pope said, police seized his mobile phone and found it was used to search for 'Gwent Police' around 30 times the morning after Lilly disappeared, between 5.49am and 10.02am. The barrister said photographs were also discovered on the handset with one in particular 'looking back towards the scene where we say Lilly's body was later found.' Ms Pope added: 'We will ask you to consider why it is that (the defendant) would take a photograph of some distance, of the area where we know Lilly to have died that night – particularly if, as he suggests, he left her alive, when she was going to meet a friend?' Earlier, High Court Judge, Mrs Justice Stacey, told the jury panel there was no dispute that Lilly was stabbed to death. 'The boy says it was not him who did it but it was someone else she met after he left her in the park,' the judge said. 'The death of a teenage girl, especially in these circumstances is very distressing, and the fact a 14-year-old boy has been accused of her murder is also very distressing and of course we all understand that. 'But it is your task to judge the case on the evidence. 'To do that you will need to put emotion aside so that you're concentrating on the evidence and not on your emotions. 'Emotion does not help in assessing evidence.' The boy, who was supported by an intermediary in the dock, denies murder. The trial, expected to last four weeks, continues.
