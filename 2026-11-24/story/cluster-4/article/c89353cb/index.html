Teenage girl stabbed multiple times by 14-year-old boy, murder trial told

Article text

Teenage girl stabbed multiple times by 14-year-old boy, murder trial told - Published Warning: This article contains details readers may find distressing A teenage girl was stabbed "around 55 times" by a 14-year-old boy, a murder trial has been told. Lilly Jones, from Blaina, in Blaenau Gwent, left home at about 18:30 on 20 June, telling her father she was "popping out for a bit". Her body was discovered face down in woodland close to Duffryn Park two days later. A 14-year-old boy, who cannot be named because he is under 18, denies her murder. He and Lilly shared an interest in Pokémon and were "in some kind of relationship", the court heard. The boy was dressed in a white short-sleeved shirt and tie as he sat in the dock at Newport Crown Court on Monday. Anna Pope KC, prosecuting, told the jury a camping knife containing a complete DNA profile had been found in the boy's bedroom drawer. "It was calculated to be a billion times more likely if this DNA had come from Lilly Jones than if it had come from someone else," she said. Pope said the boy had not given an explanation as to why Lilly's DNA was on the knife. She added scratch marks were discovered on the inside of his arm and forensic swabs taken from Lilly's fingernails identified his DNA. She had been stabbed in the heart, both lungs and an artery in her neck. There were 17 stab wounds to her neck, and knife wounds were found on her face, jaw, chest and bottom. Lilly's phone has never been found. Newport Crown Court was told Lilly and the boy became friends in March this year after a chance meeting in the street. They were captured on CCTV cameras in Blaina and were last seen together at 18:50 as they walked towards a footbridge leading to the park, near to where her body was later found. At 22:19 that evening, Lilly's family contacted the police to report her missing. It was the day after her 14th birthday. Police searching for Lilly spoke to the teenager the following day. He told officers he was supposed to meet up with Lilly at the Red Lion pub in Blaina, but she never arrived. He later told police they had been together for part of the previous night, before they separated. He said he came home after she told him she was going to meet up with someone else. In a further conversation with police on the day Lilly's body was found, he said she had told him she wanted to show him a disused park, before leaving. When he was arrested he told police he did not murder Lilly and did not know where her phone was. The trial heard that, when a phone belonging to the defendant was examined, he was found to have searched for Gwent Police, and similar terms, about 30 times the morning after Lilly disappeared, between 05:49 and 10:02. Pope added: "The phone also showed that photographs had been taken by someone using [the defendant's] phone, we say that's [the defendant]." She added it was "of note" that one of the photographs "is looking back towards the scene where we say Lilly's body was later found". Addressing the jury, Pope said: "We will ask you to consider why it is that [the defendant] would take a photograph of some distance, of the area where we know Lilly to have died that night? Particularly if, as he suggests, he left her alive, when she was going to meet a friend." The trial continues. More top stories - Published15 hours ago - Published15 hours ago
