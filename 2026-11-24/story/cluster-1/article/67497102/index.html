Victims being failed 'at every step' of criminal justice system, according to damning report - just as thousands of thugs are freed early under Labour's release scheme

Article text

Victims being failed 'at every step' of criminal justice system, according to damning report - just as thousands of thugs are freed early under Labour's release scheme Victims are being failed 'at every step' of the criminal justice system, according to a damning report – just as thousands of thugs are freed early under Labour's release scheme. The study, based on feedback from thousands of victims, found nearly a third (28 per cent) don't report offences amid dwindling faith in the system. This included more than a third (39 per cent) of rape and sexual assault victims. Meanwhile, nearly half (47 per cent) who suffered serious crimes tried in crown courts reported hearings being delayed. Nearly a third (28 per cent) said trial dates were postponed by a year or more after they were first scheduled. It comes after reports that some victims targeted by criminals on early release are not being told that the offender was let out of prison under Labour's controversial scheme. Under the initiative, aimed at tackling prison overcrowding, thousands of prisoners are being freed from jails across England and Wales. The latest batch of 650 convicts was released this month, with thousands more, including killers, paedophiles and other violent thugs, set to be freed early in the coming months. Today's report, by Victims' Commissioner for England and Wales, Claire Waxman, also found more than half (51 per cent) of victims had to phone police for updates about their case. Victims are being failed 'at every step' of the criminal justice system, according to a damning report (stock photo) Victims' Commissioner for England and Wales, Claire Waxman, said 'victims experienced the justice process as a siloed system rather than a coordinated journey' Of those who said they were dissatisfied (46 per cent) with the level of communication from officers, more than eight in ten (85 per cent) said they wouldn't report a crime again. Less than six in ten (58 per cent) of victims said they had received some form of support within a month of being referred to agencies. And only around one in seven (14 per cent) were aware of their rights through the Victims' Code. The study, which highlighted 'poor police communication', 'limited access' to support services and 'unprecedented' court delays amid a record backlog of crown court cases, lays bare a criminal justice system at breaking point and which the report said often leaves victims as an 'after thought'. While there were some examples of 'excellent practice', on the whole victims 'remain largely invisible', it concluded. It criticised a system based on 'agencies and processes rather than people', calling for an 'end-to-end review' and more of a 'joined-up and victim-centred approach'. One rape victim told researchers how she was only told at 5.15pm on a Friday that her trial would be starting on the Monday morning and that she only met the prosecuting barrister two minutes before giving evidence. Another female rape victim told how her trial suffered delays, telling researchers: 'It feels like my life was put on hold for another year, my mental health certainly suffered and while my abuser has been charged, he's free until we go to trial.' A record 23,706 Crown Court cases were outstanding for more than a year, researchers found at the time of compiling the report, which was around a third of outstanding cases. Ms Waxman said: 'Too many are left chasing updates, fighting for support, and navigating a process that compounds, rather than alleviates, the trauma of crime. 'Victims should not have to battle the very system that is meant to protect them. After more than a decade of decline, the cracks in the system are too deep to paper over.' In her foreword to the report, which analysed more than 6,000 victim responses, she added: 'Too often, victims experienced the justice process as a siloed system rather than a coordinated journey. 'As victims moved between agencies, communication broke down, accountability became blurred and support diminished, leaving victims to navigate an increasingly complex system alone…Victims told us that uncertainty, repeated adjournments and a lack of information left them unable to move forward, delaying recovery and extending the impact of the crime on their lives.' Chief Executive of Victim Support, Katie Kempen, said the report was a 'damning indictment of the criminal justice system's failings', adding: 'Victims should not be paying the price for a system in crisis.' Victims minister Alex Davies-Jones said: 'When we came into office, we were faced with a growing court backlog, prisons almost full to the brim and victims waiting unacceptably long for justice. 'We have already taken important steps to fix this, investing an unprecedented £550 million in victims' services over three years and taking action to tackle the crown court backlog to make sure victims get the fast justice they deserve.'
