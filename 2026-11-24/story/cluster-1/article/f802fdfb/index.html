Tens of thousands of criminals to stay in prison for longer under Tory plans to scrap Labour's early release scheme

Article text

Tens of thousands of criminals to stay in prison for longer under Tory plans to scrap Labour's early release scheme Tens of thousands of criminals will stay in prison for longer under Kemi Badenoch's plans to scrap Labour's early release scheme, the party will announce. The presumption against short prison sentences will be ended and prisoners will have to serve half their sentence in custody once again if the Tories win power. Up to 80,000 criminals will spend more time behind bars under the plans, with shoplifters, burglars and drug dealers among those locked up for longer. Judges will also be able to hand down prison sentences of a year or less without having to start from a suspended sentence. The Tories have previously vowed to build 50,000 new prison places, increasing the size of the estate by more than 50 per cent, to meet the extra demand. More than 70,000 criminals have been let out of jail under Labour's early release scheme, under which prisoners could be freed after serving just 40 per cent of their sentence. And since October, Labour's new rules have allowed some offenders to be released after serving just one third of the sentence handed down by a court if they have shown good behaviour behind bars. Mrs Badenoch and Nick Timothy, the shadow justice secretary, will announce the measures in a speech on Tuesday and also launch an expert-led review on how to expand the prison estate. More than 70,000 criminals have been freed from jail under Labour's early release scheme, which the Tories say they will scrap (Pictured: People leaving HMP Durham earlier this month) The review will be chaired by former Tory MP Sir Simon Clarke and based at the Onward think tank, with advisers including figures from law, construction, real estate finance and criminology. Conservative leader Mrs Badenoch said that prison should mean 'real punishment'. 'Victims of horrific crimes live with the consequences every day, but somehow criminals seem to be getting a better deal. 'Families left terrified by burglaries and violent attacks suffer while those responsible get chance after chance. 'When criminals believe they can get away with breaking the law, there is a collapse in consequences. Prison should mean real punishment, not a slap on the wrist or revolving door where sentences are cut short because Labour can't find enough cells. 'Labour seems more concerned about getting criminals out of prison than making them face up to the crimes they choose to commit. Sentences must actually mean something in order to keep law-abiding people safe.' Mr Timothy added: 'Prison works, but we need the capacity to make sure more criminals are locked up. 'Labour have failed to build prison places, instead cutting corners and letting criminals out early. The message to every criminal in the country is that the odds are in their favour. 'That is why the next Conservative Government will deliver the largest prison building programme in modern history. It will let us end Labour's early release schemes once and for all and bring back shorter sentences to lock up criminals like shoplifters.' The Tories announced plans to slash foreign aid to fund 50,000 new jail cells at their party conference last month. The prison-building programme is designed to revive the Tories' traditional reputation as the party of law and order and restore public confidence in the creaking criminal justice system. The £2billion-a-year building programme will be funded by steep cuts to foreign aid and the Whitehall spin machine. Reform UK were sceptical of the Tory plans. The party's home affairs spokesman Zia Yusuf said: 'The Tories managed to build just 500 net new prison places in 14 years to cope with the 5 million people they imported. At that rate, it would take them 1,400 years to build the 50,000 new places they now claim they'll create. 'Their destruction of the criminal justice system saw over 10,000 prisoners released early and even cost lives. Reform will build emergency Nightingale prisons and deport all foreign criminals so that we can end the early prison releases the Tories started.' A Labour spokesman said: 'The Tories left the prison system in complete disarray and now they want you to forget it. Labour won't let them. We are determined to do everything possible to keep the most serious offenders behind bars, while preventing the prison system we inherited from collapsing. 'Across 14 years in government, the Tories added just 500 prison places. Now they're expecting the British people to believe the laughable claim that they'll build 50,000. 'Labour is fixing the broken system the Tories left behind, with the largest prison building programme since the Victorian era, so victims of crime get the justice they deserve.'
