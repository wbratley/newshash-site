Ian Huntley killer felt ‘sick of him’ and that he had nothing to lose, court told

Article text

A prisoner thought he had nothing to lose when he killed the Soham murderer, Ian Huntley, by repeatedly striking him over the head with a 4kg iron bar, a court has heard. Anthony Russell, 44, who is serving a whole-life term at HMP Frankland in Durham, told prison staff Huntley had been bullying him and that he had had enough. He denied murder at a trial that opened at Newcastle crown court on Monday. The court will hear that he claims the crime was committed as a result of diminished responsibility. Jurors heard that Russell said he had killed Huntley because he was sick of him “getting in my face”. He told the prison governor: “I ain’t putting up with it any more, I’m not getting out and I’m a natural lifer, so I thought ‘fuck it’.” The prosecutor, Matthew Bean KC, told jurors that Huntley’s name “will be known to many, if not all of you. He had been convicted of the murder of two girls, Holly Wells and Jessica Chapman, that took place in the village of Soham in Cambridgeshire in August 2002.” He said Huntley’s crimes aroused feelings of disgust and anger, and jurors might not have sympathy for what happened to him. “More than that, the prosecution do not ask for sympathy,” Bean said. “Ultimately, this case is not about the appalling actions of Ian Huntley during his life, but the manner of his death.” He said it was the crown’s case that Russell was responsible for a “cold-blooded murder that had been planned weeks in advance of its execution”. The court heard that he was in jail for the murder of three people in 2020. He and Huntley were serving their sentences on Frankland’s A wing. On 26 February, the two men were working in the prison’s recycling workshop, a large room which had a conveyor belt in its centre where the prisoners would sort waste. Bean said they had made threats against each other over a number of months. In January, Russell had a black eye he said had been the result of falling over. The jury would hear that Huntley may have caused the black eye, Bean said. On the day of the incident, there were no raised voices or any suggestion of tension, he said. There was then a commotion “caused by the defendant, Anthony Russell, repeatedly striking Ian Huntley to the head with a long orange metal bar”. The jury heard that there were four orange metal bars kept in the workshop, each weighing 4kg and 1.2 metres (4ft) in length, that were used as stillage posts for stacking items on pallets. One witness would give evidence that he had seen Russell hit Huntley on the head six times, describing the blows as brutal, Bean said. Russell later told a prison officer that Huntley had been “pushing my buttons for months” and was “winding us up”. Huntley was bleeding heavily and unconscious when he was taken to the Royal Victoria Infirmary in Newcastle where treatment included removing a section of his skull to reduce pressure on his brain. He did not regain consciousness and medical staff concluded he could not survive the serious brain injury he had suffered, the court heard. After a week a decision was taken to withdraw treatment and he died on 7 March. Bean said jurors would see footage of Russell telling the prison governor: “I’m sick of Ian Huntley ... He put it on me. I’ve been in this jail nearly five years … He put it on me about six months ago but I walked away, but … “When he sees weakness in people he tries to bully them and he’s been getting in my face the last few weeks and my head just thought ‘fuck it’ … I ain’t putting up with it any more. I’m not getting out and I’m a natural lifer, so I thought ‘fuck it’. Bean said the “natural lifer” phrase was because Russell “would never be able to seek parole and would never be released from prison”. The court heard that when Russell was asked what his intention had been in the workshop that morning, he replied: “To kill him … that’s why I caved his head in about six, seven times with a metal bar.” In a police interview Russell described an incident in March in which Huntley had threatened him, he said, and “got in my face”. “Since that time I walked away from him, cos I wasn’t going to lose it all for him, he’s been getting really arrogant, cocky, in my face, winding me up. Just little smiles as if to say ‘yeah’ sort of thing, and I’d had enough of it. “I thought ‘fuck you, you’re getting away with it’, ‘I’ve got nothing to lose, I’m never getting out’.” Bean said Russell did not deny causing Huntley’s death with an iron bar but “in this trial, the defendant maintains that he is not guilty of the murder charge on the basis of diminished responsibility”. The trial continues.
