Prisoner plotted ‘cold-blooded murder’ of Soham killer Ian Huntley for weeks, court hears

Log in Child killer Ian Huntley was subjected to a “cold-blooded murder” in a prison attack which had been planned for weeks, a court has heard. Anthony Russell, 44, is on trial at Newcastle Crown Court, charged with murdering Huntley at HMP Frankland earlier this year. He denies the charge. Russell, who had already been convicted of murdering three people, repeatedly hit Huntley, 52, over the head with a heavy metal bar, the court heard. The prosecution said that jurors may feel no sympathy for Huntley, who murdered schoolgirls Holly Wells and Jessica Chapman in Soham in 2002. However, the case was not about his “appalling actions” but the manner in which he died. Matthew Bean KC, prosecuting, told jurors: “At shortly after 9am on 26th February 2026, the prosecution say that the defendant in this trial, Anthony Russell, murdered Ian Huntley, by striking him repeatedly to the head with a heavy metal bar. “The prosecution case is that this was a cold-blooded murder that had been planned weeks in advance of its execution.” Huntley’s offences “aroused feelings of disgust and anger”, Mr Bean said. “Due to the nature of his offences, you may feel little or no sympathy for what happened to him. “More than that, the prosecution do not ask for sympathy. “Ultimately, this case is not about the appalling actions of Ian Huntley during his life, but the manner of his death. “The prosecution well understands the emotions that may be stirred during this trial. “It is essential, however, to be true to your oath or affirmation, to put emotion to one side, to try this case on the evidence and the evidence alone, and to come to verdicts based on that evidence.” Mr Bean told jurors the two murderers were held on A wing at the high security prison and there had been “tension” between them in the months leading up to the killing. About a year earlier, in March 2025, prison staff witnessed an argument between them in which they threatened one another and had to be separated. They later agreed with staff that nothing further would happen, the court heard. About 10 months later, Russell was seen with a black eye, and told staff he had fallen. Mr Bean said: “Some members of staff believed, however, that someone had assaulted him and you will hear some evidence during the trial that indicates that it may have been caused by Ian Huntley.” Those incidents were the only two issues between the men in the prison records, the court heard. The two killers were allowed to work in the recycling workshop, which had a conveyor belt in the middle where inmates would sift through refuse, the court heard. The room had previously been a kitchen area and so there were no CCTV cameras installed, Mr Bean said, but prison officers could see prisoners at work from a small office. About 15 prisoners were working in the recycling workshop that morning, and they appeared to be pleased to be there, as it had been closed for some months. But within moments of stepping into the office, staff became aware of a commotion, Mr Bean said, although there had been no tension in the lead-up. The trial continues.
