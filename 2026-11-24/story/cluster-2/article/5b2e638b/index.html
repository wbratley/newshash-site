UK to host 2035 Women's World Cup, Fifa confirms

Article text

UK to host 2035 Women's World Cup, Fifa confirms - Published England, Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales will jointly host the 2035 Women's World Cup, Fifa has confirmed. It will be the first time a football World Cup has been held in the United Kingdom since the men's tournament in 1966. The four countries were the only bidders for the tournament, and were ratified as co-hosts at a specially arranged virtual meeting from Fifa's Zurich headquarters. The tournament will consist of 104 matches contested by 48 teams over 39 days. The 'All Together' bid included a proposal to use 19 venues, with 14 in England, one in Northern Ireland, two in Scotland and two in Wales. Monday's special meeting of Fifa's Congress also approved the joint bid by Mexico, the United States, Costa Rica and Jamaica to host the 2031 finals, which will be the first to feature 48 teams. The news comes on the same day the UK government announced a vision for one million more girls to play, watch or be in leadership roles in sport by the time the tournament takes place. - Hosting World Cup means decade of excitement - Weir - Published8 hours ago Where will matches be held in 2035? England failed to win the right to host the men's World Cups in 2006 and 2018, and a proposed UK and Ireland bid for the 2030 tournament was abandoned. But England and Scotland hosted matches at the men's Euros in 2021, and England staged the women's European Championship in 2022, which the Lionesses won. England, Scotland, Wales, and the Republic of Ireland will jointly host the men's European Championship in 2028. Mark Bullingham, chief executive of the Football Association, said: "Hosting Euro 2022 allowed us to change the game in our country, by creating a generation of aspirational role models and growing demand from women and girls to play. "Hosting a World Cup would give us the chance to build on that across all four nations." The English stadiums included in the 2035 bid are: Villa Park, The Amex, Ashton Gate, Elland Road, Hill Dickinson Stadium, Emirates Stadium, Selhurst Park, Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, Wembley, Etihad Stadium, St James' Park, the City Ground, the Stadium of Light and Old Trafford. If Manchester United's proposed new Old Trafford stadium does not go ahead, it would still be considered in its current form. Cardiff City Stadium and Principality Stadium are the two Welsh venues, with Easter Road in Edinburgh and Hampden Park in Glasgow in Scotland. Northern Ireland missed out on co-hosting the men's Euros in 2028 after the UK government said in 2024 it would not provide funding to redevelop Casement Park in time - but a redeveloped Windsor Park is set to stage matches in 2035. A further three stadiums could be added - Birmingham's new Powerhouse Stadium, Wrexham's redeveloped Racecourse Ground, and a potential new Chelsea ground - which would take the tournament to 16 cities and 22 venues. Fifa is yet to decide whether all four co-hosts will qualify automatically. Shaping the future of the game The participation of women and girls in football has risen by 2.3m since the Women's European Championship was held in England in 2022. However, women remain significantly underrepresented in leadership positions. Only a quarter of chief executive, chair or performance director roles in major UK sports are held by women and only 12% of sports federations are led by a female. Noel Mooney, the chief executive of the Football Association of Wales, said: "This is a proud day for Welsh football. "We've put forward Wrexham and Cardiff as host cities, and it's exciting to think what a World Cup could bring to both cities and the communities around them. "In Llunio Cymru - our 10-year strategy - we've set ourselves the ambition of reaching the knockout stages in 2035. Some of the players who could take us there are learning the game at clubs across Wales today. Our job is to make sure that wherever a girl grows up in Wales, she can find a team close to home, have a coach who helps her develop and see a route to the Cymru shirt." Irish Football Association chief executive Patrick Nelson said the event "would be felt in every club and every age group, in every part of the country". "Our young players would grow up having watched the best in the world play at home, and there is no substitute for that," he added. Scottish Football Association chief executive Ian Maxwell said hosting the tournament would be "the most significant thing to happen to the women's and girls' game in Scotland". He added: "The impact would go far beyond just the matches themselves. The level of investment into coaching, facilities and player pathways in the years beforehand will shape the future of the game for the generations to come." Fifa's evaluation, published earlier this month, rated the bid 4.3 out of five. Its contractual framework was rated 'medium risk', and the document claimed "certain government guarantees contain material qualifications, particularly regarding tax and safety and security". That is thought to refer to a lack of agreement over a request by Fifa for tax-free status for all football-related activities. The bid evaluation said: "The bidder has submitted an extensive set of government support documents and contractual hosting agreements, demonstrating the commitment of the relevant authorities and stakeholders. "However, certain government guarantees, particularly those relating to safety and security and tax, contain certain qualifications which have been duly noted in the legal evaluation. "While certain contractual matters remain subject to further discussion, the bidder's proactive engagement with Fifa and the substantial lead-in time provide a sound basis for resolving these outstanding issues." - Published13 hours ago - Published8 June - Published28 August
