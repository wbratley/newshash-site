Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor received £750,000 from Turkish millionaire, court told

Article text

Andrew received £750,000 from Turkish millionaire, court told - Published A Turkish millionaire has told the High Court she was misled over a £750,000 payment she made to Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor in 2019. Nebahat Isbilen is alleging her former business adviser, Selman Turk, misappropriated £38m of her assets, including a payment to the then-Duke of York which she believed was for help with her passport. Turk has rejected the allegations, saying the 81-year-old Isbilen knew the money was actually being paid in the hope that Mountbatten-Windsor could help her husband, a politician who is in prison in Turkey. Mountbatten-Windsor repaid the £750,000 and is expected to give evidence to the High Court in December. He is not accused of any wrongdoing. Isbilen's lawyers also told the court on Monday that Turk had instructed a separate £15,000 payment to Mountbatten-Windsor's ex-wife, Sarah Ferguson, purportedly for the former Duchess of York's 60th birthday party in 2019, and an unquantified "wedding gift" payment for their daughter Princess Beatrice. Isbilen's family founded a major soap and cosmetics firm in Turkey. But when her husband, Ilhan, a former member of the Turkish parliament, was arrested in 2015, she wanted help moving her assets out of Turkey. Her lawyers said the charges were "politically motivated" and that in 2017 she was facing "political persecution". She left Turkey for Greece in 2017 and moved to London in 2018. Simon Colton KC, representing Isbilen, said she met Turk in January 2017 and gave him control of most of her financial assets. But Turk "deliberately and systematically abused" her trust and transferred the money to companies he owned or controlled, Colton added. Colton added that Isbilen approved the £750,000 payment to Andrew, but that Turk "lied to her" about its purpose, which was "a further example of his dishonesty". The court heard that Isbilen had a private dinner with the then Duke and Duchess of York at Turk's flat in December 2019 where she discussed her husband's case. Turk's lawyers told the court that Isbilen understood and directed the management of her own affairs. Joshua Hitchens, representing Turk, said in written submissions: "In circumstances where the claimant dined with the duke and duchess and discussed her husband with them, she cannot have believed that the £750,000 was a disguised fee for help with a passport, or with a visa."
