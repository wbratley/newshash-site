AJ Odudu's mother hits back at Mariella Frostrup for making her daughter cry during the race row on I'm A Celebrity as she says 'It's been a living hell'

Article text

AJ Odudu's mother Florence has hit back at Mariella Frostrup for making her daughter cry during the race row on I'm A Celebrity on Sunday night. Florence, 58, emotionally said it was a 'living hell' watching her daughter, 38, being accused of being 'aggressive and dramatic' in a dispute over camp cooking duties. Mariella's comments have seen her accused of perpetuating racial stereotypes with her choice of language toward AJ, with some saying 'calling black women "aggressive" is a harmful stereotype.' After watching the simmering row reach boiling point, Florence told the Daily Mail: 'I know she was upset - that word is triggering. As a mother watching it, it was a living hell. 'But I know she is going to be alright. She is strong and is a survivor. We are survivors - we are Africans.' Speaking from her terraced home in Blackburn, Lancashire, she added: 'I have been watching the show. I am very, very proud of her. She has done well.' Mariella's Loose Women co-star Judi Love has also waded into the I'm A Celebrity race row. Judi shared her thoughts in an Instagram video, admitting 'for us [black women] this is our lived experiences in most spaces'. The 46-year-old added: 'If I’m honest, part of me wasn’t even going to post and talk about this, simply because it’s exhausting and, although some people will be seeing this kind of scenario play out for the first time, for us this is our lived experiences in most spaces. 'You all know what the issues are when it comes to these words, like "aggressive" and "dramatic" when it is suggested that that's how a black woman has acted. 'You know what's the most hurtful thing? AJ has been so patient and was so calm. Trust me, that is not aggressive.' Judi added that 'what hurt me' is when AJ apologised to Mariella, pointing out 'she didn't need to because we know, who feels it knows, that she did that to try to pacify the situation'. She added: 'Why? Because she knows that any form of retaliation would cause the stereotypes, the labels to be placed on her standing up for herself. 'What really made me emotional as well was when she then, in a calmly manner, said "please don't call me aggressive" and it was doubled down. 'For most of us black women who have been through this in all scenarios, workplace, the shop, schools, wherever... we know what AJ's feeling in that moment. And then when she said "not feeling welcomed in a space".' Judi explained that she did not want to discuss the issue any more because she has been 'triggered', explaining: 'I'm protecting my mental health today and my peace.
