Nigel Farage leads supporters defending I'm a Celebrity's Mariella Frostrup against racism claims after calling AJ Odudu 'aggressive'

Article text

Nigel Farage today claimed Mariella Frostrup is being unfairly smeared as a racist because she was 'sharp' with AJ Odudu in camp. The Reform UK leader said he was the target of similar accusations during his appearance on I'm a Celebrity... Get Me Out of Here! in 2023 and that Ms Frostrup 'should not be hung out to dry'. In last night's episode, Ms Frostrup, 64, branded Ms Odudu 'dramatic and aggressive' as tensions rose about cooking for the camp taking too long. Ms Odudu, 38, cried in the aftermath and later said: 'Being called aggressive, especially someone like me, is just a triggering word.' Her campmate Leomie Anderson, 33, explained: 'Black women are always painted to be aggressive when we're just expressing ourselves. I don't think that's the right term to use in this instance at all.' The Daily Mail revealed today that ITV bosses reacted by speaking to Ms Frostrup in an off-camera chat where producers told her about the use of 'offensive, outdated and inappropriate terminology'. Mr Farage said today that the broadcaster must not be 'hung out to dry' over comments deemed offensive by ITV bosses, arguing that a 'sharp comment' should not be conflated with racism. He told the Daily Mail: 'The racism card was aimed at me in the camp. 'Tensions do rise in there. Making a sharp comment is not the same thing as racism.' On Sunday night's episode journalist Mariella Frostrup, 64, accused presenter AJ Odudu, 38, of being 'aggressive and dramatic' as their row over camp cooking duties intensified Ms Odudu broke down in tears and said that 'being called aggressive, especially someone like me, is just a triggering word' Camp leader Wayne Rooney tried to diffuse the situation Ms Frostrup took charge of cooking on the very first day after no other campmate volunteered for the role. However, her efforts have since sparked a series of clashes with Gavin & Stacey star Joanna Page, 49, and TV presenter Ms Odudu. Hours earlier Wayne Rooney found himself at the centre of a different race row after singing the reggae song, Rat In Mi Kitchen, in a Jamaican accent on Friday night's episode. The former Manchester United striker, 41, mindlessly sang the tune which was written by the Birmingham band, UB40, but his mimicking of the accent has led some viewers to brand it 'cultural appropriation'. But on Saturday, the band's frontman, Ali Campbell, labelled the backlash against Rooney as 'ridiculous controversy'. Mr Farage became embroiled in a row with influencer Nella Rose over cultural appropriation when he was on the show. The argument began when another campmate, Fred Sirieix, used the Jamaican pronunciation of the word 'water'. Mr Farage remarked: 'If you sang that they'd call it cultural appropriation, Fred. No, they would.' Later, after noticing Ms Rose seemed quiet, the MP asked whether he had upset her. She replied: 'I just feel like you were very dismissive about cultural appropriation yesterday.' He insisted: 'No, I was concerned about it,' but Ms Rose responded: 'You made a joke about it.' His supporters claimed ITV had deliberately focused on the row in an effort to stop him winning the show. Left-wing media outlet Byline Times later published allegations that he had used a racial slur in a Cameo video. His team dismissed the claims as a 'stitch-up', arguing that he was unable to respond because he was in Australia filming the programme. Mr Farage went on to reach the final alongside Tony Bellew and eventual winner Sam Thompson. The row between Ms Frostrup and Ms Odudu sparked a debate on social media. Ms Frostrup has previously clashed with Gavin & Stacey star Ms Page over cooking.
