Storm Boelo live: Two men killed by falling trees as 84mph gusts batter UK

Article text

Storm Boelo live: Two men killed by falling trees as 84mph gusts batter UK Storm Boelo continues to batter parts of the UK with gusts reaching 84mph and two people have tragically been killed after being hit by falling trees Recovery is under way after two storms hit the UK in three days, leaving two people dead after being hit by falling trees during ferocious gales. The final weather warning ended at 9pm on Sunday after Storm Boelo pummelled the north of the UK. More than 82,000 properties were without power as the second named storm continued to batter northern parts of the UK with gusts having reached 84mph. A 60-year-old man was pronounced dead at the scene on a road in Mid Kelton near Castle Douglas, Dumfries and Galloway, soon after 10.30am Sunday, after he was hit by a falling tree. Additionally, two women were taken to hospital after they were struck by another falling tree in Aberfeldy, Perth and Kinross. On Sunday evening the Metropolitan Police confirmed that a man, aged in his 20s died in Mill Hill, north London, after a tree fell on on his car. No weather warnings are in place today but further disruption is likely as many services recover. A yellow Met Office warning for strong winds and potentially damaging gusts is in place from 3am Tuesday to midnight for the far north of England, Scotland, and the far north east of Northern Ireland. Key Events Weather warning in place again on Tuesday A yellow Met Office warning for strong winds and potentially damaging gusts is in place from 3am Tuesday to midnight for the far north of England, Scotland, and the far north east of Northern Ireland. Thousands still without power Workers are still trying to restore power to homes cut off during Storm Boelo. The storm, which saw yellow and amber weather warnings for wind brought into force across Scotland, led to power outages across the nation. In their final updates on Sunday evening, the Scottish and Southern Electricity Networks (SSEN) said at around 7pm that 38,000 customers were still without power, having restored electricity to around 54,000, and SP Energy Networks (SPEN) said at 7.45pm that around 4,500 were still without power. Maps show rain and wind over past 24 hours Met Office weather maps show the wind and rain that has covered the UK over the last 24 hours. Flights continue to face disruption A Ryanair flight from Spain declared an emergency near Manchester at over 12,000 foot. The flight was travelling from Seville to Manchester when the emergency code was transmitted. The plane has now landed at Manchester airport. Travel disruption expected to ease on Monday Travel services across the UK have been impacted due to the storm, but are expected to return to normal on Monday. Gary Hopkirk, route director at Network Rail Scotland, said: “It’s been a really challenging weekend for Scotland’s Railway and particularly for the teams who’ve been out in some very difficult conditions responding to whatever the weather has thrown at us. Our focus throughout has been on keeping people safe and trains moving wherever possible. “Now the worst of the weather has passed, our attention is on getting the railway back to normal. We’ll be working as quickly as we can, but we won’t compromise on safety. We know disruption is frustrating and we really appreciate the patience people have shown this weekend while our teams do everything they can to get everyone moving again.” Man dies in London after tree falls on car A man, aged in his 20s, has died in north London after a tree fell on his car. Police were called to Dollis Road, in Mill Hill, at around 12.40pm following reports a tree had fallen onto a moving car and a man was declared dead at the scene. Chief Inspector Guy Ellwood, from the North West command unit, said: "This is a deeply tragic incident and our thoughts are with the loved ones of the young man who lost his life. "We will now work with the local authority and coroner to establish the circumstances surrounding the incident." Map shows more wind and rain on Monday A Met Office map shows a cold start to Monday while winds will start to pick up again during the day along with heavy rain. The national weather agency states: "A chilly start to Monday in the north with much lighter winds. "Turning unsettled again from the southwest with outbreaks of rain, heavy at times with winds strengthening." Clear up operation to remove debris from train tracks 'to continue through the night' A clear up operation will continue through the night to remove debris from tracks, says Network Rail Scotland. It stated on X: "We’ll work through the night to repair and reopen routes affected by #StormBoelo. Flooding, fallen trees/debris, damage to overhead wires, and power failures are among the problems the railway has faced." An earlier post showed a team clearing a fallen tree. "Our maintenance team on site at Bishopton on the Glasgow Central - Gourock/Wemyss Bay route. They're clearing away a fallen tree, ready for our overhead line engineers to move in to repair the damage it's caused to our equipment," it said. 'Significant recovery work' for transport network needed Stuart Wilson, of Transport Scotland, said that there will be "significant recovery work" ahead after the damage caused by Storm Boelo. “The weekend of storms has brought challenges across the transport network and, in some cases, there will be significant recovery work required before we have the network back to normal," he said. “Our operating companies are working around the clock to restore parts of the trunk road network that have been impacted. The Traffic Scotland website and X account have regular updates on trunk road conditions, so I’d continue to urge drivers to plan their journey before setting off, particularly ahead of the return to work on Monday morning. “The conditions have also impacted rail, ferry and air networks, so it’s vital travellers check with operators to see if their services have been affected.” Brits warned of more 'severe gales' within days Brits have been warned to brace for more "severe gales" as forecasters predict powerful winds could batter parts of the UK again within days. Met Office forecaster Stav Danaos said north-west Scotland and Northern Ireland could bear the brunt of the next bout of unsettled weather, with a new area of low pressure moving in from the Atlantic. Winds could reach gale or severe gale force in western Scotland and potentially Northern Ireland on Tuesday, accompanied by heavy rain, he warned. The system could become the third named storm to hit the UK and Ireland in quick succession , following Storm Austen and the more powerful Storm Boelo. If it meets the criteria to be named, it would be called Storm Chloe. However, forecasters have stressed that the latest system does not currently look as disruptive as Storm Boelo. Mr Danaos said: "Wet and windy weather is set to continue as we head from Monday into Tuesday with this area of low pressure just moving to the north west of Scotland. "So into Tuesday we could see stronger winds for the north western areas touching gale or severe gales for the west of Scotland and perhaps Northern Ireland along with heavy rain as well. The further south you are it will tend to stay drier, still blustery mind due but with sunshine and 17C the high, a bit cooler further north." More than 50,000 homes without power in Scotland Around 53,000 homes across Scotland are without power after Storm Boelo has battered the country. Scottish and Southern Electricity Networks said more than 32,500 customers have had their power restored, but 47,000 remain without. SP Energy Networks (SPEN) says that around 6,000 homes, mainly in Ayrshire and Dumfries and Galloway, were also without power, meaning the overall number of homes between both providers who remain cut off is likely to be around 53,000. Met office map shows Storm Boelo's progress eastwards tonight A Met Office map shows Storm Boelo's expected progress eastwards tonight as winds are expected to ease. The national weather agency states: "Winds easing this evening as Storm Boelo pushes eastwards. A mixture of clear spells and showers in the north. Cloud thickening in the far southwest into the night." An amber wind warning came into force at 9pm on Saturday night, and will remain in place until 9pm tonight, with only Orkney remaining under a yellow warning. The storm brought 80mph wind gusts to Islay, just off the West Coast in Argyll and Bute. Man, 60, tragically dies after being hit by fallen tree in Storm Boelo A man has died after being hit by a fallen tree in Dumfries and Galloway. Police said the 60-year-old was pronounced dead at the scene on a road in Mid Kelton near Castle Douglas soon after 10.30am on Sunday, reported the Daily Record. The tragedy came as Storm Boelo raged around the country bringing gusts of 84mph. A Police Scotland spokesperson said: “We were made aware of a 60-year-old man having been struck by a falling tree on a minor road at Mid Kelton near Castle Douglas around 10.30am on Sunday, 25 October, 2026. An airport spokesperson thanked customers for their patience, adding: “We appreciate that this is a challenging situation. Our teams are working in very difficult conditions to support passengers as best they can.” Train journeys were also disrupted. Network Rail Scotland shared an image of flooding from the tracks to the platform in one Glasgow station and fallen trees above the lines near another. It came after Network Rail posted photographs of a trampoline beneath a train and a shed by the tracks, in a call for those living near rail lines to firmly secure their garden furniture. Collapsed trees, cables on carriageways, strong winds and damaged surfaces prompted closures to major roads in Scotland. Bridges closed in Scotland due to ferocious winds from Storm Boelo Bridges across Scotland are facing closures and warnings over high wind speeds and severe weather from Storm Boelo. The Queensferry Crossing, M90 and the Clackmannanshire Bridge A876 have been closed to high sided vehicles on Sunday, and a 30mph speed limit has been imposed on other traffic. The Dornoch Bridge, near Tain on the A9, has been closed to all vehicles because of strong winds. Kessock Bridge, near Inverness, has also been closed to high vehicles. Tay Bridge, near Dundee on the A92, was open to cars only on Sunday afternoon. Storm Boelo brought 80mph gusts and travel disruption to much of Scotland this weekend, shortly after Storm Austen delivered heavy rain to many areas on Friday. The Met Office has warned of potential injuries and danger to life from flying debris as well as a chance of damage to buildings, power cuts and travel disruption. The amber wind warning came into force at 9pm on Saturday and will remain in place until 9pm on Sunday, with only Orkney remaining under a yellow warning. Storm Boelo brought gusts in excess of 80mph across Argyll, with wind speeds of 85mph recorded in Machrahanish. Could another storm be on the way? A third storm appears to be on its way towards the UK and could develop into a "significant" weather phenomenon over the next 48 hours. Storm Chloe could batter the country a lot sooner than some may have hoped or expected, following a weekend of torrential rain, flooding and "damaging gusts" brough by Storm Austen and its much stronger successor, Storm Boelo. The next storm, as per the alphabetical names released by the Met Office, Met Éireann in Ireland and KNMI in the Netherlands, will be called Storm Chloe.
