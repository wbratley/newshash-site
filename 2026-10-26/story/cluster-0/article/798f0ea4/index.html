Michael Carrick future LIVE: Board hold crisis talks as Man Utd boss on verge of sack

Michael Carrick future LIVE: Board hold crisis talks as Man Utd boss on verge of sack Manchester United head coach Michael Carrick could be facing the sack following Sunday's 3-0 defeat to Bournemouth at Old Trafford Michael Carrick's future at Manchester United is hanging by a thread after the Reds suffered a humiliating 3-0 defeat at home to Bournemouth on Sunday. Carrick, 45, had come into the game against the Cherries under huge pressure following a dismal start to the season, with the Reds winning just TWO in 11 fixtures across all competitions. But United were humiliated on home soil with Bournemouth running out comfortable winners. Rayan put the Cherries ahead in the 38th minute before Marcus Tavernier capitalised on a defensive error from Leny Yoro to double the visitors' lead. Despite an improved start to the second half, Bournemouth made it three in the 71st minute with Evanilson rounding Senne Lammens before curling the ball home from a tight angle. That is how it remained with Carrick now facing the sack after United extended their winless run to seven matches. They have dropped to 16th in the Premier League table on just seven points from a possible 24. Stay tuned for the latest and the fallout from United after the defeat to Bournemouth... Key Events Bruno has eyes on Ballon d'Or amid Carrick row Speculation over Carrick's future comes days after captain Bruno Fernandes appeared to criticise the manager's tactics in the 4-1 Champions League loss to Como. Carrick responded by not bringing the midfielder on from the bench against Bournemouth in a telling move. Bruno enjoyed his best season at United last term, winning the PFA player of the year award. On Monday, there is the small matter of the Ballon d'Or, which he is also nominated for. He could be crowned the world's best at the ceremony, set to be held in London, but Harry Kane, Kylian Mbappe, and Lamine Yamal are favourites for the crown. 'The fans are probably sick of me saying it all the time and the amount of times I’ve sat here and said it' Michael Carrick was quizzed by reporters after the 3-0 loss yesterday and caught the eye with a comment about how he was surprised to hear so much support from fans. He said: "I have to say I have really mixed emotions, I wasn’t really expecting that of the supporters, if I’m honest. I’ve always felt their support, and always felt that connection with them anyway. [They’re] probably sick of me saying it all the time and the amount of times I’ve sat here and said it, but the respect I’ve got for them, and for them to show that back meant an awful lot. "To be in such a difficult situation and go through a day like we have done, and a spell like we have done is difficult to take, so there’s real mixed emotions in there, for sure." United's next five matches It doesn't matter who is United manager - the club's next five matches are a tricky set of fixtures. Next Saturday, the Red Devils go to Chelsea in the Premier League, and have a quick turnaround to host Roma in the Champions League the following Tuesday. There's a longer break until their next match, but it's against high-flying Aston Villa on Sunday, November 8. Then, after a two-week hiatus, it's one of English football's most important fixtures - Liverpool vs Manchester United, at Anfield. Three days later, they fly to Portugal to play Sporting. That could be a baptism of fire for a new manager - or the making of Carrick's tenure. Reported frontrunners named Former Newcastle manager Eddie Howe and ex-Germany boss Julian Nagelsmann, who both left their positions in the summer, are the reported frontrunners to replace Carrick at Old Trafford, according to Sky Sports. Earlier this week, Antonio Conte - the only manager to win Serie A with three different clubs - distanced himself from the position, appearing to rule out another return to the Premier League following a title-winning spell with Chelsea and securing Champions League football for Tottenham Hotspur. Bournemouth loss was Carrick's third home league defeat in this spell as manager Sunday's 3-0 loss to Bournemouth was Carrick's third home Premier League loss since January, when he was initially brought in as caretaker manager. The Old Trafford league losses this year include: - Manchester United 1 - 2 Leeds United, April 14 - Manchester United 0 - 1 Manchester City, September 14 - Manchester United 0 - 3 AFC Bournemouth, October 25 United have also lost to Brighton at home in this season's EFL Cup. 7 managers that Man Utd can appoint It now seems inevitable that Michael Carrick will imminently be relieved of his duties at Manchester United. So, who are some big free-agent coaches who could step in to replace him? Although the fans inside Old Trafford applauded Carrick down the tunnel, the consensus seems to be amongst pundits and supporters alike that we've seen the last of the 45-year-old in the United dugout. So what next? Giggs' outburst at Neville Rhodri Giggs, the brother of Manchester United legend Ryan, has launched a X-Rated attack on Gary Neville amid the club's growing crisis. After the Reds were beaten against Bournemouth, Rhodri, 49, turned on Neville and United's ownership, accusing the former England defender of failing to hold Sir Jim Ratcliffe accountable. In an explosive post on X, he wrote: "Gary Neville you’re an absolute w****r. Prime Pep couldn't get a tune out of this lot. Call your mate Jim [Ratcliffe] out who got us into this mess further. "The owners are parasites and don't have a clue." Carrick is a scapegoat - the Old Trafford faithful know who is to blame at Man Utd Michael Carrick was the man standing in the eye of the storm on the Old Trafford touchline, but the chant from the stands made clear the feelings of the Manchester United supporters. After being taunted by the jubilant Bournemouth fans with shouts of "you're getting sacked in the morning", the United faithful issued their response. It was unequivocal support for their beleaguered boss. The Sky Sports cameras then honed in on two of the other architects of the current mess at United: Omar Berrada and Jason Wilcox. The United chiefs are responsible for the decision to hire Carrick as the full-time manager in the summer and they were also behind the lopsided transfer window which saw £155million spent on midfielders and nothing elsewhere. Carrick on silence from Berrada and Wilcox When asked in his post-match press conference if it should be Omar Berrada and Jason Wilcox speaking to the media and the supporters about the club's current situation, Michael Carrick said: "Again, like I said to you the other day, it's not for me to say who speaks, who doesn't speak, when they speak and how they speak, I think I'm in my role here to speak to you guys and represent the team and the football club as best as I possibly can from this position. "Yeah, as I said about the atmosphere in the stadium and the feelings of supporters, the frustration, the disappointment, it gave me really mixed feelings, standing there and being in that position, it was a difficult day for sure." Maguire on Carrick and Bournemouth Harry Maguire insisted the Manchester United players are behind under pressure manager Michael Carrick. As individuals, we're not playing well enough." How much it would cost to sack Carrick Manchester United will have to pay more than £7million to sack Michael Carrick. Carrick's position at Old Trafford is hanging by a threat following Sunday's 3-0 defeat at home to Bournemouth. Goals from Rayan, Marcus Tavernier and Evanilson condemned the Reds to a third defeat in four and extended their winless run to eight matches across all competitions. United have since dropped down to 16th in the Premier League table, having picked up just seven points from a possible 24. It has seen the pressure increase on Carrick's future with some calling for him to be sacked now. But if United were to dismiss the 45-year-old, it would cost them between £7.2m and £8.8m, according to Football Insider. Carrick: Man Utd players are hurting Michael Carrick has opened up on Manchester United's defeat to Bournemouth as pressure continues to rise on his future. Carrick told Sky Sports: "It was a strange, mixed of emotions at the end of the game. It wasn't a nice way to end the game. "It was a tough day for the players. We went into the game having been rocked. You can feel that. "They're really hurting and disappointed in there. It's not a nice feeling. You have to accept it, we're in that situation." He added: "It's really difficult to have a day like this at this football club and feel the emotion and dynamics of what it was like today. Not for me personally but for Manchester United. "It was really mixed. I feel responsibility with the position I'm in. I've been at the club a long time. It hurts a lot going through this." Although Fernandes warmed up and was an option in the second half, Carrick opted to leave him as an unused substitute, with United fans appearing to boo him as he walked off the pitch. Those two [Omar Berrada and Jason Wilcox] tomorrow are probably going to make a decision that will disgust Manchester United fans, but it's probably necessary. It's just an absolute mess." Not just for the good of Manchester United but also for the good of Michael Carrick." Man Utd 0-3 Bournemouth Manchester United's season reached new lows on Saturday as Bournemouth secured a comfortable victory on an afternoon where Old Trafford turned toxic. A Bruno Fernandes-less Red Devils side lacked any spark with Michael Carrick now surely on the brink of losing his job as the Cherries won 3-0 at United for the third time in four years. The hosts were two goals down by half-time with Rayan and Marcus Tavernier putting the Cherries on course for another success in Manchester. Evanilson capped the afternoon with a sensational third 20 minutes from time. The two opening goals coming inside five minutes of the first-half - Rayan finishing from a free-kick before Tavernier benefited from a defensive slip. United showed nothing by way of a fightback in the second-half and Evanilson was able to round Senne Lammens before finishing brilliant from out wide in front of the travelling fans.
