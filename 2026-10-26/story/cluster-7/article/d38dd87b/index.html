Thomas Markle went to the 'Far East because 12-year-olds are legal there', ex-wife 'claimed in letter to Met' Thomas Markle told his first wife that he visited the Far East because '12-year-olds are legal there', according to a letter she is reported to have sent to the Metropolitan Police. Authorities are currently trawling through a trove of child abuse images that allegedly belong to Meghan Markle's 82-year-old father. Philippines interior minister Jonvic Remulla said the forensics data is expected to be completed in around a week, but 'right now all signs point to a possible sex offender of the grossest kind'. Mr Markle denies the allegations. But a newly uncovered letter allegedly sent by Roslyn Loveless - his ex-wife of nine years - claims that Mr Markle admitted that he travelled to the Far East to have sex with young girls. The seven-page letter, seen by The Sun and sent to the Met Police in 2019, details a conversation which Loveless, who died two years ago, claims to have had with the Duchess of Sussex's father. She reportedly wrote: 'I questioned Thomas Sr. He denied accusation but stated that he went to the Philippines and Thailand because 12-year-olds are legal there.' Ms Loveless's letter was addressed to Scotland Yard's Fixated Threat Assessment Centre, which analyses intelligence about threats to the Royal Family. It is said to have accused Mr Markle of being a threat to his daughter and her husband, Prince Harry. The information was reportedly passed on to the Sussexes, though the Met Police did not investigate the allegations made by Mr Markle's ex-wife because they occurred outside of Britain. Markle Jr, 60, says he discovered the material when he went looking for a phone for his fiancée Rose in storage boxes in the house he and his father shared. When he opened one phone he saw 'vile' child sex images and then turned on a computer and found a screensaver with 'hundreds' of sexual pictures of children. The 'sickening' collection is said to include videos of girls no older than ten or 11 involved in sex acts. Mr Remulla, the Filipino interior minister, said on the weekend that data on Mr Markle's laptop is being analysed to 'determine the contents and come up with a definitive picture'. He added: 'The Philippine National Police, upon the reports of the son, inspected the former home of a certain Mr Thomas Markle. Found were a trove of child pornography materials as well as a laptop.' He said the country planned to take 'appropriate legal actions' if sufficient evidence was found. Mr Remulla told The Guardian: 'In these cases where family disputes are mixed up, we have to consider all the angles. Right now all signs point to a possible sex offender of the grossest kind. We want to be sure before we release the findings. The forensics data will be complete in a week.' When asked for a response to the interior minister's claim that he may be a 'sex offender of the grossest kind', Mr Markle said: 'I am not. I am innocent. I never did anything in the Philippines.' It comes as exclusive images taken by the Daily Mail show the moment Mr Markle, 82, was given a letter from Meghan cutting him out of his grandchildren's lives. It was delivered while he was in hospital in December, hooked up to a drip after having a leg amputated. The explosive message revealed Meghan 'knew her father had abused young girls', and that he would never meet his grandchildren, Archie, seven, and Lilibet, five. Mr Markle has told the Mail his computers had not been in his possession since he left Mexico with his son and moved to the Philippines.
