All the financial help available this winter as energy price cap to rise by £443

Log in Ofgem has said the energy price cap will rise by 26 per cent from 1 January for a typical household in England, Scotland and Wales. According to the regulator, the price cap will rise by £443 per year – or £36.91 per month – to £2,166 for the average household using both electricity and gas, if this level was sustained for a year. The hike, which will leave the cap at its highest level in four years, comes on top of already high energy costs, with the price cap having increased by 4 per cent at the start of last month. Meanwhile, a recent poll conducted by Uswitch found that more than nine million homes are heading into winter in debt to their energy supplier or with no credit. It also revealed that 43 per cent of bill payers intend to wear extra layers rather than turn the heating on, and one in 12 (8 per cent) plans to spend less on food to pay for their energy. However, there are a number of financial support options available to those struggling to pay their energy bills. Here, The Independent takes a look at whether you could be eligible for the different schemes in place. Household Energy Payment Millions of households across the UK will begin receiving a £100 cash payment from January 4 to help with energy bills, ministers have said. The measure, unveiled at the autumn Budget, gives an automatic £100 rebate to properties in council tax bands A to D. 13 million households who pay by direct debit will automatically receive the payment directly into their bank accounts, with no action required. However, those with alternative payment methods will be contacted and given the choice of receiving the £100 into their bank account, or as credit against their council tax bill. Students and other residents exempt from council tax will still be eligible for the payment if their property falls within tax bands A to D. Households in England can check their council tax category via the government website. It will also be printed on any paper bills. The scheme will remain open until the end of May in a bid to ensure none of those eligible miss out on the support. According to the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero, households who do not meet the requirements for the Household Energy Payment may be able to access discretionary support from their local authority. It adds: “Local councils will be responsible for setting their eligibility criteria for the discretionary fund and making sure it benefits households who most need support. Your local council will make information on eligibility and how you can apply available on their website, once local arrangements are in place.” Winter Fuel Payment Nearly ten million people are set to receive support with heating costs this year through the government’s winter fuel payment scheme, which is designed to help pensioners through the colder months. The scheme is currently available to those born before or on 27 June 1960 and live in England, Wales or Northern Ireland. Those who apply could get between £100 and £300 to help with heating bills for winter 2026- 2027. It’s important to note that theres’s no single payment date. Instead, payments will be made in stages, and those eligible will be automatically paid in November or December this year. Pensioners should have already received a letter informing them of how much they will get and which bank account it will be paid into. It’s usually the same account as State Pension or other benefits. A person qualifies for the benefit if their total income is less than £35,000. For those with an income over this amount who receive the benefit, the government has said HMRC will take back the payment. There are four other groups of people who are ineligible for the payment: Warm Home Discount The Warm Home Discount scheme gives those eligible a “one-off £150 discount” on their electricity bill, according to the government. The discount is applied to your bill by your energy provider and those eligible will receive it automatically, it adds. For those being provided with both gas and electricity by the same provider, it may be that the discount can be applied to your gas bill instead. In England and Wales, you could be eligible for the discount if all of the following conditions were met on 23 August 2026: - Your energy provider is included in the scheme - Your or your partner receive Universal Credit, Housing Benefit, income-related Employment and Support Allowance or Pension Credit - Your name or your partner’s name is listed on the electricity bill Those eligible for the scheme, or those who may have been eligible, should receive a letter by early January 2027 about the discount. Cold Weather Payment Cold Weather Payments provide “£25 for each 7-day period of very cold weather between 1 November 2026 and 31 March 2027,” according to the government. It adds: “You’ll get a payment if the average temperature in your area is recorded as, or forecast to be, zero degrees celsius or below over 7 consecutive days.” Those receiving certain benefits or Support for Mortgage Interest could be eligible for the financial support, which is given automatically. However, those living in Scotland are not able to receive Cold Weather Payments, but could “get an annual Winter Heating Payment instead”, according to GOV UK. Other support available In England, customers can access the Crisis and Resilience Fund for emergency cash and fuel vouchers. In Wales, emergency grants are available from the Welsh Government Discretionary Assistance Fund, and the Scottish Welfare Fund in Scotland. The Winter Heating Payment is also available for those living in Scotland and receiving “certain benefits”, according to GOV UK.
