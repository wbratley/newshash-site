Octopus Energy boss says price cap surge ‘heavy blow’ for struggling families

Start your Independent Membership today. Join todayAlready a member? Log in An energy boss has called the price cap rise of 26 per cent for a typical household a “heavy blow”. Regulator Ofgem said the price cap will rise by £443 per year from 1 January, or £36.91 per month, to £2,166 for the average household in England, Scotland and Wales using both electricity and gas if this level was sustained for a year. Greg Jackson, founder and chief executive officer of Octopus Energy, said: “Today’s eye-watering price cap rise is a heavy blow for families. Whilst Hormuz is the short-term reason, we are so vulnerable because Britain’s energy market is broken. “We pay billions to switch off cheap British wind power on windy days while firing up expensive gas instead. We should give this cheap homegrown energy to people for free instead of wasting it. And this is worsened because we let gas set the electricity price, so consumers don’t get the benefit of cheap British wind. “The government’s support is helping, but it may not be enough for some. If you are struggling, please do call your supplier. More support is available.” The bleak outlook for energy bills comes after escalating violence in the Middle East sparked a fresh jump in oil and gas prices through October, amid deepening fears of gas supply levels in Europe. Wholesale prices have since eased back, helping to support a reduction in petrol prices – but it was too late to affect the calculation for the new price cap. The hike, which will leave the cap at its highest level in four years, comes on top of already high energy costs, with the price cap having increased by 4 per cent at the start of last month. ADVERTISEMENT The rise in energy bills in the new year is largely due to disruptions to the supply of gas brought about by the escalating conflict in the Middle East. Wholesale gas prices surged in October due to mounting tensions between the US and Iran, with European natural gas prices reaching their highest level since the 2022 invasion of Ukraine. This also meant suppliers in Europe and the UK buying gas ahead of the winter were forced to spend more, and will now charge customers more for those expensive supplies. Gas prices have eased a little since October, but it was too late to stop the January increase. When will prices rise and who is affected? The energy price cap will rise by 26 per cent on 1 January 2027, and will impact households who do not pay for their gas and electricity on a fixed-rate tariff. Around 22 million households are not on fixed-rate deals and are governed by the price cap. They have seen prices soar since the start of the conflict in Iran in February, with the price cap rising by 13 per cent in July and 4 per cent in October. Around one third of households are on fixed rate tariffs, and are therefore not impacted by the energy price cap. What is the government doing to help households? The government has announced a series of measures to help households struggling with the cost of living this winter. Chancellor John Healey said up to 20 million households in England on council tax bands A to D will receive £100 towards their energy costs from January as a one-time contribution to energy bills. The payment for up to 20 million households also comes alongside a temporary 5 per cent VAT cut from electricity bills until June next year. Mr Healey also announced in his autumn budget that gas standing charges will be scrapped in England, Scotland and Wales. Energy secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh said there is “more to do” to take the pressure off families this winter. “I know people are deeply concerned by another rise in energy bills,” she said. “For too long, British families have been paying the price for conflicts overseas and volatile fossil fuel markets.” She added the UK must invest in “cheap, clean, homegrown power” so the country is less reliant on expensive fossil fuels. But campaigners have said that current support will still leave millions of people facing a surge in their bills. Emily Seymour, Which? Energy Editor, said the price cap is a “devastating blow” for millions of households struggling with the cost of living. Downing Street said the government’s position on its commitment to reduce household energy bills by £300 has not changed, despite soaring costs. The prime minister’s official spokesperson said the government would reform the energy system so its “fairer for the British people, whilst creating more cheap, clean home growth”. Charles McAllister, director of the Green Gas Taskforce, urged the government to confirm it’s support for the Green Gas Support Scheme and said: “Today’s Price Cap rise shows how exposed households remain to imported energy - the answer is to generate more at home.” Neil Kenward, Ofgem’s director general for markets, said: “Conflict in the Middle East continues to drive energy costs higher, and we know this price increase will be a worry for many households as winter approaches. “We urge anyone who is struggling to afford their energy bills to reach out to their supplier, who must offer appropriate support and advice – we are writing to suppliers today to remind them of their obligations and ensure they are ready to provide that support.” Equalities minister Bridget Phillipson said she recognises the 26 per cent rise in the energy price cap will cause “real worry”, adding the UK must invest in “cheaper, homegrown energy”. Ms Phillipson told Times Radio: “I think it underlines how important it is that we move away from the exposure that we have when things happen across the Middle East and in other parts of the world that then have a knock-on effect for families here. “We’ve got to be less exposed in the future. We need to invest more in cheaper, homegrown energy, so that when those events do occur, families here are less exposed to those price fluctuations.” Join our commenting forum Join thought-provoking conversations, follow other Independent readers and see their replies Comments
