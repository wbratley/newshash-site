The cheapest fixed energy deal this week: An 18-month tariff from Fuse Energy, priced at £1,754 annually for the average household that pays by direct debit. This is £31 above October's price cap and £412 below the predicted one for January. Exit fees are £50 per fuel. Energy bills are set to surge once again. The energy price cap, which determines the price paid by the majority of households, increased by 4 per cent on October 1, leaving millions of households facing higher costs as we head into the colder months. And it's now set to rise by another 26 per cent in January next year. The energy regulator Ofgem has announced that the price cap will rise to £2,166 annually for the average household that pays by direct debit. However, you may be able to cut your bills by comparing energy deals and fixing if you find a better deal. There are still deals priced below the January rate, according to the price comparison website Uswitch. If you haven't fixed your tariff in a year or more, it's likely you're on one that follows the price cap – and you could save money by choosing a new deal. Signing up to a fixed deal protects against any further rises and give you certainty over your bills. Fixed deals do not follow the price cap and so do not see the cost of energy change every three months when the cap is revised. Instead, the cost of your energy is fixed for the full term of the deal. The price cap or fixed tariff doesn't limit your total bill. Instead it sets the maximum that energy suppliers can charge for each unit of energy. If your energy usage isn't the same as the average household, your annual bill will look different to the cap. It's therefore important to check the unit rate – how much you're charged per kWh – on a new fixed tariff against that of your existing deal. Prices are predicted to remain elevated for some time, although there is no guarantee as they are determined by wholesale energy prices that are influenced by global factors such as ongoing conflict in the Middle East. An early prediction for April 2027 from energy market forecaster, Cornwall Insight, has the cap falling just 1 per cent to £2,151 so bills could end up marginally lower across the year. Your actual bill will depend on how much energy you use, so check the unit cost of energy when comparing tariffs. Under October's price cap the unit costs are 26.32p/kWh for electricity and 7.97p/kWh for gas, with standing charges of 54.83p and 29.68p per day respectively. And under January 2027's cap, the unit costs are 32.69p/kWh for electricity and 10.96p/kWh for gas, with standing charges of 54.77p and 29.65p per day respectively. Should I fix my energy and switch providers? Switching to a fixed tariff from a variable one could put more money in your pocket. It's also worth thinking about fixing your energy if you like to know how much your bills will be each month. Energy unit costs and standing charges stay the same over the length of a fixed contract, helping you budget. Energy suppliers themselves also predict movements in the price cap and EDF Energy is forecasting the cap will increase slightly to £2,254 in April 2027, as of November 23. But the market is easily influenced by international events and political tensions that can't be forecast, so predictions should be taken with a pinch of salt. EDF Energy says its confidence in its April prediction is very low. Fixing could at least help to provide some stability for your bills over the longer term. Your energy provider can't raise your unit cost of energy or standing charge while you're on a fixed tariff. Your bill could still move up and down depending on how much energy you use. If you use more, you'll pay more. It's the standing charge and cost per unit of energy that's fixed, not your overall bill. Is now a good time to fix energy prices? The main benefit that fixing provides at the moment is stability and protection against unpredictable future pricing. You need to consider how prices will move over the entire length of the fix rather than just the next few months. And be sure to consider exit fees, because you'll need to pay these if cheaper fixes do return to the market and you want to switch to a better deal. With the energy price cap increasing significantly in January 2027, it's strongly worth considering fixing if you can find a year-long fix priced around the current cap but below January's rise. This would lock in savings for the coldest months. You can compare the best energy deals* for you based on your home and gas and electricity costs through This is Money's recommended partner Uswitch. What else do I need to know? Keep in mind that Ofgem updated its average energy consumption figures from July to reflect the fact that people are using less energy. This means that average annual costs listed under the price cap and fixed deals will look lower than they did before. The July cap for a typical household that pays by direct debit was £1,663 under the new figures, but was previously announced at £1,862 under the old ones. It's worth highlighting that the Government has cut VAT to 0 per cent for six months from October, equivalent to a saving of £45 a year, and certain levies were also removed in April, bringing energy bills down. The Government also confirmed at Autumn Budget 2026 that it's paying a £100 Household Energy Payment to households in Council Tax bands A to D in England.
