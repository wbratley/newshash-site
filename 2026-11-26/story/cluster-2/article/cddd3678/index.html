Ex Top Gear host reveals reason he turned down return as Gordon Ramsay is announced

Article text

Ex Top Gear host reveals reason he turned down return as Gordon Ramsay is announced A former favourite on Top Gear has revealed bosses tried to get him to return to his presenting role before announcing Gordon Ramsay as the show's new host A former Top Gear presenter has revealed he was approached to return to the show but rejected the BBC's efforts. It comes after the shock announcement that Gordon Ramsay will be taking on the presenting duties on the iconic car show. But the ex star admits he said no simply because he didn't want to "drag it back to what it was". Richard Hammond, who starred alongside Jeremy Clarkson and James May in what many labelled the best trio, told Radio 4’s PM programme: "I’ll be honest, they did talk to me about it. They came to me about maybe leading the new Top Gear and I don’t want to drag it back to what it was." Richard was asked by PM host Evan Davis about the dangerous nature of the show; both he and fellow former host Freddie Flintoff suffered near fatal crashes while filming for the show. Richard said: "Freddie had his moment, it’s bad, I had my moment, yes we got a bit carried away, but maybe it’ll go in a different direction." And rather than Richard take on the reins again, celebrity chef Gordon Ramsay, known for his no-nonsense approach, has been named as the face of the new series. It's an appointment Richard can get behind and he admits he can see why the BBC chose him. "He’s a TV chef. They love three things- cooking- well in his case four- cooking, swearing, big watches and cars.” Gordon revealed his excitement and getting behind the wheel of the show in the announcement. He said: "My love affair with cars began as a child, growing up a field away from Silverstone, where I would climb over the fences and sneak in for a glimpse of the racing. "That early excitement sparked a lifelong passion for cars, motor sport, beautiful design and extraordinary engineering. "At its very best Top Gear has always been about the unexpected – surprising, entertaining and just the right amount of chaos. That’s the spirit we want to build on, bringing a fresh energy and ambition to create something truly thrilling for a whole new generation of audiences. It’s an incredibly exciting opportunity, and I genuinely can’t wait to get started!" The BBC's Director of Entertainment, Ed Harvey, hailed the appointment of the chef as the beginning of an "exciting new era". He said: "It’s the coming together of two iconic global powerhouses: the world’s most famous, high octane motor show and a car obsessed megastar. Gordon’s fierce and fearless approach makes him a natural fit, and we're thrilled that audiences will see him in the driving seat as the show speeds into its next exciting era." Like this story?
