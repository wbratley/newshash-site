Lammens errors cost Manchester United victory in frantic finale at Sporting

Article text

“We are a strong team,” Mauricio Pochettino exhorted, then watched Manchester United’s unerring ability to cause drama every time they lace up their boots. Cruising, it seemed, at 2-1 and with 10 minutes left, they unravelled, as Senne Lammens dropped a high ball in front of his goal and a grateful Luis Suárez equalised with a bicycle kick. Cut almost instantly to Sporting’s Kaique Pereira who bundled the ball in for United’s apparent winner only for Szymon Marciniak to be ordered to the screen by the video assistant referee, where the official ruled the goal out for a foul on the goalkeeper by Patrick Dorgu. There was more – of course: Youri Tielemans, in stoppage time, was sent off – this for a second yellow card and United’s fifth red in six weeks. Marciniak issued the first yellow to the Belgian for what looked a non-existent foul that also resulted in Amad Diallo’s perfectly legal strike being chalked off. The bedlam had also featured United conceding two penalties in two first-half minutes, Lammens saving the first from Suárez, then conceding the second, and being unable to stop the No 9’s second effort. Marciniak’s questionable display followed Leny Yoro’s red card in Sunday’s 4-0 debacle at Liverpool being rescinded on appeal. Pochettino greeted questions about the official’s first caution of Tielemans with an initial silence but a gesture of his face that showed agreement. Then he said: “It’s difficult to understand why that happened on the field. Impossible.” Before this United had come from 1-0 behind and were heading for a first victory under Pochettino and only a fourth of this troubled campaign that would have rewarded the 2,597 travelling fans. But, after the humiliation at Anfield, victory eluded them. Pochettino will have to build from this, his players remain a jumble of disjointedness. Brentford are up next in Saturday’s first outing in front of the Old Trafford crowd for Pochettino. Keith Andrews is sure to instruct his men to bombard Lammens, whose previous form this term features howlers for two goals in the 4-4 draw with Aston Villa earlier this month, plus a mix-up with Lisandro Martínez that allowed Fulham’s goal in their draw in September. Pochettino preferred to look at the collective regarding Lammens’ drop: “In this type of action also our defensive line needs to cover better the keeper because it was very difficult.” Pochettino reacted to the Anfield horror-show by dropping Matthijs de Ligt for Harry Maguire, one of four changes that included Joshua Zirkzee starting at centre-forward for the suspended Matheus Cunha. The Dutchman blasted one chance over – a sign of his less than elite quality. When Pochettino also witnessed Diallo fail to go at his man down the right, Yoro dawdle in the centre circle, and Kobbie Mainoo pass straight to Issa Doumbia, he saw the scale of his task. Now came serious farce from those in blue. Martínez lost Geny Catamo, scrambled across to challenge, the No 10 went down, and Marciniak awarded the penalty. Suárez hit the spot-kick straight at Lammens: a turning point in United’s horrendous campaign? No. Moments later Maximiliano Araújo twisted past United’s No 1 and was barged over for the second spot-kick. Lammens was lucky to escape a red card and this time Suárez scored. Bad luck was next for United. Because when Diallo scored Marciniak blew for the “foul” by Tielemans on Gonçalo Inácio just before the finish, so no video assistant referee enquiry was allowed. The wider picture, though, concerned Pochettino’s insistence of a lack of fragility. Understandable, as he could not tell reporters otherwise. But, after the PR, came the evidence of another 45 minutes in which his new charges were certainly not robust. One more mountain to climb, then, in the second half against the 21-time Portuguese champions, who were led by Paulo Fonseca in only a third game in charge since being prised from Lyon for roughly £4.5m compensation. Five minutes after the break, United pulled ahead. First, a marauding Martínez was fouled by Eduardo Quaresma and Marciniak awarded a third penalty, which Bruno Fernandes converted for the first goal of the Pochettino era. Then, the captain scored a remarkable second. A speculative left-foot shot from outside the area looked harmless before hitting a divot in front of Pereira, bouncing over the goalkeeper, and in. Fortune, at last, smiled on United and in replays it transpired Fernandes himself had accidentally created the divot just moments earlier. On 65 minutes Pochettino made a move, as Dorgu and Diogo Dalot came on, Bryan Mbeumo becoming the centre-forward. United were a yard quicker, more streetwise: confidence and belief surging. On 77 minutes Diallo was forced off, Shea Lacey replacing him. But then came the latest chaos to engulf Pochettino’s nascent tenure.
