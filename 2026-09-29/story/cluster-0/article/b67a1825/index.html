Labour conference live - Mahmood says 'more to come' on RAF Fairford 'terror attack' as Burnham chairs COBRA meeting

Article text

Labour conference live - Mahmood says 'more to come' on RAF Fairford 'terror attack' as Burnham chairs COBRA meeting The Prime Minister told LBC this afternoon that he wanted to get the 'full picture' of what happened at the base, and added he would share more details about the upcoming meeting The Prime Minister announced on the sidelines of the Labour Party Conference today that he would be holding a COBRA meeting to address the RAF Fairford incident, with Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood confirming after that she expects there to be "more to say in the days to come". The Home Secretary told a fringe event at the Labour Party Conference that the decision to bail the suspects was a police operational decision. The Home Secretary said: "You will have heard some news from counter-terrorism policing about the release under bail. These are investigations, these are matters for the police, they have got to take the lead on how they use all of the powers at their disposal in cases such as these, but the investigations are continuing. "So there'll be more to say in the days to come, I'm sure, as how those investigations are progressing. I've just come from a Cobra meeting with other colleagues in government chaired by the Prime Minister, and everyone should know that we are focused on this. You shouldn't read anything into the fact that we're limited in what we can say publicly at this stage except for the fact that nobody wants to compromise any investigation or any future court cases either." Speaking with LBC's Shelagh Fogarty from Liverpool this afternoon, Andy Burnham said the security situation will be on the minds of many Brits following the arrests of five men in the nearby village of Whelford on on suspicion of explosives and terrorism offences on Sunday. He told LBC that he wanted to get the “full picture” on what had happened at the base used by the British and US air forces in Gloucestershire. Earlier, Wes Streeting gave an update on Britain's national security after five men were arrested over an alleged terror plot at an RAF base. The Foreign Secretary, Ed Miliband, said our response to any attempt to destroy a two-state solution in the Middle East would be: "we will not let you succeed" And Chancellor John Healey set out plans to reindustrialise Britain with investment in industry and support for apprenticeships - while pledging that getting young people back into work was not just an "economic must" but a "moral duty". The Chancellor used his speech to the Labour Party conference to seek to reassure financial markets with a commitment to being responsible with the nation’s finances Follow along live below.... Key Events What will Burnham say? It's Andy Burnham's first keynote speech as PM to Labour conference on Tuesday. He is expected to use his address to set out a new course for a “Britain where people feel the country has their back again”. He will flesh out his plans for free social care in England, promise measures on youth unemployment and public control of utilities – with a pledge to hold an “honest conversation” about tough issues. Echoing the Liverpool conference’s slogan of “Hope again”, Mr Burnham will tell party members that “hope comes from honesty and from saying the things politicians usually avoid”. Burnham to lay out 'more serious prospectus for Britain' in keynote speech The Prime Minister is set to promise a "more serious prospectus for Britain than people have been offered in a generation" during his keynote speech at the Labour Party conference tomorrow. Sky News reports that he will also call for a more honest conversation with the British public, saying: "We can't carry on as we are. "Politics-as-usual has taken Britain backwards. More of the same isn't good enough and isn’t going to get us where we need to be." Healey warned Budget is 'real test' after conference address Shevaun Havilland, the director of the British Chambers of Commerce, told Sky News following the Chancellor's speech today that Healey mentioned a number of "good things". She said: "He referenced the real cost of doing business at the moment that we're all struggling under. He talked about building confidence, investment, profits, you know, all those good things." But she warned that, for struggling businesses, the real test for the Government would be the upcoming Budget, adding: "I think the test is the budget. That is what we are waiting for... "It's a challenging environment for everybody. But what they need to remember and understand, and I think that the chancellor and the prime minister does, is that only by investing in business will we get the return we need to spend on our public services." Andy Burnham to set up publicly-owned 'Great British Grid' on mission to fix 'broken' energy market Andy Burnham will pledge to drive down energy costs with a new publicly-owned Great British Grid to overhaul the "broken energy market". In his big speech to Labour conference, the Prime Minister will set out plans for greater public control of energy as he warned firms and businesses are left shouldering the cost of energy prices dictated by markets miles away. GB Grid, a new publicly-owned company, will aim to speed up grid connections for businesses and drive down costs by increasing competition in the market. It will also make it easier for firms waiting to connect to build infrastructure themselves so they don't have to wait. It marks the first major step in Mr Burnham's drive to bring essential services back into public control. He told the Mirror last week that Britain took a "series of wrong turns" in the 1980s when the Tories sold off a string of core utilities. Voters 'recoil in horror' at Reform UK deportation policies, Mahmood says The Home Secretary said voters "suddenly recoil in horror" at some of Reform UK's deportation policies. Shabana Mahmood said: "I worry a lot about the sort of Britain that my two nephews and two nieces are going to grow up in. "I wonder if they will have a place in their own land. I do worry as well about that. And then at the same time, I love this country and I believe in our people. "So I also shut that down by thinking because that's actually not what Britain is, that's not who we are. "And I take heart when actually, people who might be, for example, minded to consider voting for Reform UK suddenly recoil in horror when they come forward with policies that are about kicking out people who are residents here, who are citizens here, or start questioning whether the validity of having a passport depends on who it belongs to." 'Left wing must seriously talk about migration or cede ground to racists' Mahmood warns Shabana Mahmood has said left-wing politicians must grasp the debate on migration - warning racists will fill the space if they do not. She said that the number of people coming to live in the UK has been "unprecedented" in recent years, and ceding ground is dangerous. The Home Secretary told a fringe event at the Labour conference: "You do have to accept and understand that the level and the pace of change people have seen in the last few years, it is unprecedented. "It has never happened. It didn't happen when my granddad and my dad came to this country. And in fact, in the last four years, we've seen a level of change that we saw in totality over the previous 40 years. "So when people are saying, 'Hang on a minute, I'm not sure about this', or 'I'm worried' and it's a top issue for them to consider and how they feel about politics and political parties. "I'm not going to sort of turn around and say 'Oh yeah, but we're not going to do anything about that, and we don't want to talk about it,' because that cedes the territory to the racists. "The racists get to talk to more people if mainstream politicians basically vacate that space. And I think vacating the space, not shaping the debate, is a dangerous thing to do." Immigration reforms to straddle 'fairness to those who have come, but also fairness for Brits' Mahmood says Is Shabana Mahmood planning to row back on changes to immigration policy? Both she and Andy Burnham have sent out signals that a carve-out for care workers could be on the cards. Proposals that could see migrant care workers wait 15 years until they qualify for indefinite leave to remain (ILR) have sparked a furious backlash. Pressed at a fringe event about what happens next, Ms Mahmood insisted no decisions have yet been reached. She said: "When I announced the earned settlement reforms, I announced at that time the consultation that we have been undertaking. "I was very clear that we would be looking at transitional arrangements as part of that consultation. What I said today is a sort of articulation of the principles that will underpin the choices that the government will be making in the public interest, in the national interest, when we respond to that consultation. "I've been very clear that the test will be one of fairness, fairness to those who have come, but also fairness for British citizens that in the end pay for the systems that we all rely on. "So it's a question of finding the right balance for that fairness to all sides. That has always been the sort of principal basis of the reforms, and we will be setting policy later this year, so no final decisions have been made. "We've been very carefully analysing the responses to the consultation, engaging more broadly as well with stakeholders, other civil society groups, to make sure that we've heard from everybody that we should. "We will publish all those findings and align with setting up government policy later." Mahmood says there will be 'more to say' about Fairford after PM holds COBRA meeting Speaking after attending a Cobra meeting about the RAF Fairford incident, Shabana Mahmood said she expects there to be "more to say in the days to come". The Home Secretary told a fringe event at the Labour Party Conference that the decision to bail the suspects was a police operational decision. Mahmood said: "I want to be able to share as much information as possible, but I hope people will understand that while investigations are taking place, there is actually a limit to what government ministers can and should say in matters like these. "I want to place on record my thanks to our colleagues in policing and in our security services for the swiftness of their response and the speed at which the investigations are progressing. "So big thank you to them, and also to the people of the local community for their forbearance as investigations are taking place in such a scene. "I don't want to say any more than that at this stage." The Home Secretary continued: "You will have heard some news from counter-terrorism policing about the release under bail. These are investigations, these are matters for the police, they have got to take the lead on how they use all of the powers at their disposal in cases such as these, but the investigations are continuing. "So there'll be more to say in the days to come, I'm sure, as how those investigations are progressing. I've just come from a Cobra meeting with other colleagues in government chaired by the Prime Minister, and everyone should know that we are focused on this. "You shouldn't read anything into the fact that we're limited in what we can say publicly at this stage except for the fact that nobody wants to compromise any investigation or any future court cases either." Mahmood condemns 'masked racists' as she says communities left 'in fear' The Home Secretary has said “masked racists” whose actions threaten communities like hers give her a “shiver of revulsion”. Shabana Mahmood said: “I know the fear that some communities have felt in recent times. “The sense that the forces of division are growing stronger while unifying voices grow weaker. “I have felt the same shiver of revulsion and fear at the sight of racists masked up and dressed all in black whose actions are designed to threaten communities like mine. “I know other communities have felt the same fear, like British Jews facing a rising tide of antisemitism, and targeted by the hostile acts of foreign powers, who plot violence against our fellow countrymen and women. “This country is only truly fair when we are all safe within it.” Home Secretary promises to expand safe asylum routes after Channel deaths Shabana Mahmood has pledged to expand legal routes to claim asylum in the UK - saying three deaths in the Channel on Monday show the urgent need to act. The Home Secretary said cruel smuggling gangs must not get to decide who comes to the UK - pledging to work with the United Nations (UN) to give refugees a chance to rebuild their lives. A quarter of those who come will be women from Afghanistan escaping the Taliban and people fleeing the horrors of Gaza, Ms Mahmood said. Her pledge came hours after a 10-year-old child and two women died attempting to cross the Channel when their dinghy capsized. Migration reforms to be balanced with 'fairness' Mahmood promises Mahmood has promised her migration reforms will “come to a position that balances fairness”. She said: “I know that, throughout our consultation process, some have said elements of these reforms are not fair. “To that, let me say this: We will come to a position that balances fairness for those who came here legally, and fairness to British citizens across this country. “Fairness cannot end at our borders. It must extend to every street in this country.” 'We must restore control' Shabana Mahmood says during conference address To restore fairness in the UK, “we must restore control”, Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood has said. Speaking at the Labour party conference in Liverpool, Mahmood said fairness is “what unites us in this country” and it was her job “to create that fairness”. “There is nothing fair about a lack of control at our borders,” she said. “It is not fair that criminal gangs determine who comes to this country. “It is not fair that they put dangerous boats into the water, with no regard for human life – shown once again today, with three more tragic deaths in the Channel. “It is not fair that those with a genuine need of protection cannot find a safe and legal way to claim asylum. “It is also not fair that communities across this country have been asked to bear so great a burden, with around 90,000 people living in asylum accommodation. For that reason, to restore fairness, we must restore control.” She said the work was beginning to bear fruit, with small boat arrivals down significantly, with “this summer recording the lowest number since 2021”. Burnham says visa reforms should 'strike the right balance' after Rayner's comments Andy Burnham rejected suggestions Shabana Mahmood’s planned visa changes may not happen because Angela Rayner views them as “un-British”, but said reforms would need to “strike the right balance”. It was put to the Prime Minister that the proposals may not go forward after the Housing Secretary called them “un-British”, to which he replied: “Well, no, I wouldn’t put it that way. “I think what we’ve got to do is strike the right balance and that’s what the end result of the consultation will be.” Asked whether he was “Team Shabana” or “Team Angela,” he told LBC: “I’m both of them, I’m Team Angela and Shabana. “But it just reflects that there’s a consultation, there’s different not just opinions from politicians, but obviously there’s a range of views that have gone into that consultation, so we’re in the process now of looking at that.” 'I don't like the scenes I've seen' PM says as he mulls balaclava ban Asked whether he would enact a balaclava ban on the UK after masked anti-immigrant protesters took to the street across the country, the PM said he did not want to go quite that far. He said: "I don't like the scenes I've seen in recent weeks, people wearing black balaclavas, black clothing on British streets. "You don't want to necessarily reach for extreme measures but when people are causing fear and intimidation in communities you have to prevent them doing that." The PM said there could be "localised provisions", adding that he wouldn't go as far as a blanket ban. 'Ways we can work together' with EU and other non-members, says Ed Miliband Ed Miliband said he was not 'pitching for associate membership' when he told the Labour conference the UK’s future lay in a deeper relationship with the EU. Speaking at a fringe event at the conference, the Foreign Secretary said: “I was at the EU informal foreign ministers’ meeting in Ireland at the beginning of September and round the table you had the foreign ministers meeting of Ukraine, Canada, Iceland that had just voted narrowly not to begin accession talks, and the UK and indeed Norway. “And I think we remarked around the table that there was a sort of wider grouping. “I think it does go to the (Mark) Carney agenda, by the way, which is countries working together, united by values, by alliances, on a whole range of issues. “So, I’m certainly not pitching for associate membership, but I think there are ways that we can work together.” Chancellor vows not to write off a million young people - 'moral duty' John Healey attempted to make the moral case for welfare reform today as he vowed not to write off a million young people who are out of work. He described the cash as "down-payment" on an imminent review by Alan Milburn into the thorny issue of youngsters not in education, employment or training. The Chancellor said the scheme - led by mayors with local teams - would help thousands and would be "a bit like football scouts for apprenticeships". An estimated 981,000 youngsters between the ages of 16 and 24 were out of work between April and June - 30,000 higher than a year ago.
