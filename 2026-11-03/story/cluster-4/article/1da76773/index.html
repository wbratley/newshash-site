Nine men who abused wife who was drugged and raped were snared after one million images found on her husband's phone, court hears Nine men accused of sexually abusing an unconscious wife were snared after over a million photographs and videos were found on her husband's phone, a court heard today. The group 'derived sexual gratification and enjoyment' from the 'use and abuse' of the woman, who was given a 'cocktail of stupefying drugs', jurors were told. The husband's black Samsung S25 'acted like a confessional' which 'provided police with almost a script' cataloguing the abuse. Prosecutor Louise Oakley, KC, highlighted a message from one of the accused to the husband after he visited, urging him to delete all pictures, and warning: 'Remember what happened to the guy in France'. 'The reference in the message related to the man in France who had been raping his wife, a lady by the name of Giselle Pelicot,' Ms Oakley said. Opening the case this afternoon, Ms Oakley told jurors at Manchester Minshull Street Crown Court that footage of the wife was 'taken without her consent', where she was 'obviously unconscious'. On trial are Jonathan Kirk, 43, a senior paramedic; Robert Stewart, 70, a retired DJ; Mohammed Sabir, 28, a businessman; youth football coach Alan Keelan, 42; dog walker Jordan Wallace, 31; taxi driver Philip Wild, 59; metal worker Richard Townsend, 38; David Graves, 60, and Daniel Rayner, 42. All deny a variety of sexual offences. In September, the woman's husband of 40 years admitted 62 charges including multiple rapes, jurors were told. Ms Oakley said the husband 'lived a double life masked in respectability' but 'hid dark and depraved secrets' which involved drugging and raping his wife for over 20 years until his arrest last October. Of the other men, Ms Oakley added: 'Their depravity, also, we'd suggest, had no bounds. They, too, were living double lives'. Ms Oakley said the group 'conspired'… 'to administer substances to her so that her body could be used and abused'. The men would arrive at the four-bedroom house in Stockport, Greater Manchester, 'quietly and carefully after dark', jurors heard. Footage would be posted 'within minutes' or sometimes even while abuse was ongoing, the court was told. Jurors were shown two of the videos. Ms Oakley said the wife 'had absolutely no idea what was going on' – but the men 'knew that she was not a willing participant'. The court heard the 'life (the woman) knew ended' when police knocked on the door of the family home.
