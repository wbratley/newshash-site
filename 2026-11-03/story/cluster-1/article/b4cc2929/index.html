Doomed Learjet appeared to start breaking up before it crashed, NTSB says, as 20-year-old female co-pilot's final text to boyfriend before fireball that killed her and eight others is revealed

Article text

A doomed Learjet aircraft that crashed and killed nine people in Palm Beach, Florida, started breaking apart in the air after being battered by a severe thunderstorm, officials believe. The tragedy unfolded Sunday just miles from President Donald Trump's Mar-a-Lago estate, where footage showed the Learjet 60 plane nose-diving into an intersection and bursting into flames. Audio also captured the co-pilot of the aircraft, Nathalia Filgueiras, 20, screaming in a final call to air traffic control, while the pilot Jeff Gregory, 55, next to her could be heard reciting the Lord's Prayer as they fell to the ground. At a press conference on Monday, officials with the National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) said they believe severe weather was a 'significant factor' to the disaster. Officials said the pilots requested to gain 2,000ft of elevation when they encountered a thunderstorm, moments before the aircraft was sent spiraling to the ground. The first passenger victims onboard the doomed flight were named as tech tycoon Adam Robinson, 45, and his wife Helen Sharp, 40, their nanny, two friends, and their two children, the Daily Mail revealed. Officials said another man also sustained a leg injury by being struck by debris near the crash scene. Ms Filgueiras's heartbroken family said the 20-year-old was a passionate aviator, who had texted her fellow pilot boyfriend about experiencing difficult conditions before the crash. Her stepfather Thiago told the New York Post that she texted her boyfriend ten minutes before the crash, warning him: 'I've got turbulence, I've got ice.' She told CBS12 that she 'bragged' about the achievement to her friends, because they would say to her, 'Oh, you can’t drive,' and she would respond, 'Yeah, but I can fly.' Her mother Camila often shared images of Ms Filgueiras and her siblings traveling on private jets, with her father Allan also having his pilot's license. In air traffic control audio released in the wake of Sunday's crash, Mr Gregory could be heard saying, 'who art in heaven, hallowed be thy name.' Moments later, the plane crashed in a fireball by the side of the road, killing all nine people who were on board. Officials said they are investigating stormy weather and ice buildup on the aircraft as possible causes of the crash, which happened as the $2 million business jet was flying from Austin, Texas, to the Bahamas. Coffin corner is a phenomenon whereby the jet's minimum safe speed and maximum safe speed converge into a very narrow range at high altitude, making it extremely difficult to fly safely. If the plane goes too slow or fast it will stall and quickly become uncontrollable. Former pilot Martin Varsavsky, who now runs his own AI firm, posted to X saying that he believed that bad weather in the area could have caused the fatal error. He said: 'The radars clearly showed those cells; in light jets, the rule is to deviate with a good margin, not to fly through them – this could be an error by the woman who was piloting. 'What storms do by changing the outside temperature is that airplanes can no longer stay aloft at the same speed. It's called the coffin corner. Or they become very difficult to keep under control.'
