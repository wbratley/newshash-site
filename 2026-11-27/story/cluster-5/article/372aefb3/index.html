Andy Burnham faces rebellion on Rosebank as nearly 100 Labour MPs warn against approving

Log in Nearly 100 Labour MPs, including ministerial aides, have reportedly written to Andy Burnham to oppose drilling in the Rosebank oil field, saying it would “permanently damage” the UK’s climate credentials and harm the party’s chances of re-election. The decision has been the subject of fierce debate around the future of the UK’s energy industry, with its backers stressing the impact of jobs, particularly in the north east of Scotland, while its opponents point to its impact on climate change. The Guardian reports that 92 backbenchers and at least four frontbenchers have signed the letter to the prime minister urging against ministerial approval for the scheme in the North Sea site, found 80 miles north west of Shetland. According to the newspaper, energy secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh is believed to be leaning towards rejecting it but chancellor John Healey is keen for it to be approved. In their letter, the group said they were “increasingly concerned that the approval of the Rosebank oil field would permanently damage our climate credentials as a party and as a country”. They added: “Polling is clear – in order to stand a chance of winning parliamentary majorities, maintaining our ‘progressive electoral base’ is imperative… approving Rosebank risks becoming an irreversible ‘point of no return’ for the party’s climate credibility, and risks alienating members of the electorate who have put their trust in our message of hope and positivity.” It comes afterThe Independent reported last week that Labour MPs are evenly split on whether or not to allow drilling after permission was granted for the Jackdaw oil and gas field last month. Following the collapse of the Cop31 climate talks in Turkey last week, the backbenchers said the UK “must be wary about what signals we are sending our counterparts about our commitment to transition away from fossil fuels”. And they cast doubt on the argument that Rosebank would be a significant boost for the Treasury, describing it as “not a serious revenue raising measure” and claiming it would provide “less than 0.1 per cent of our annual UK tax revenue”. The Independent revealed last week that overwhelming public support for the government to allow drilling at Rosebank would evaporate if it fails to lower bills. Overall, 52 per cent support approving Rosebank and 19 per cent oppose it, a lead of 33 points. But over a third (35 per cent) believe approving Rosebank would lower their energy bills, and among this group support is overwhelming: 73 per cent back approval and just 10 per cent are opposed. Among everyone else, support falls to 41 per cent and opposition rises to 23 per cent. The government is yet to say when Ms Fahnbulleh will decide whether to approve Rosebank, although one of the companies with a stake in the project has said it expects a decision by the end of the year. There were reports last week that eight cabinet ministers are against drilling at Rosebank and there is a belief that the current energy secretary Ms Fahnbulleh is one of them but cannot express a view because she has a quasi-judicial role over whether to allow it to go ahead. Another opponent is believed to be her predecessor Ed Miliband, who is now foreign secretary. The Independent was told that the divisions in Mr Burnham’s ministerial team “run much deeper”, with even junior ministers letting the government know their position on what is becoming a totemic issue, said to define the government’s climate credibility. Writing in The Guardian, Ms Fookes said: “Our continued dependence on fossil fuels is driving up bills, fuelling deadly extreme weather and causing widespread harm to people in this country right now. “Rosebank will do nothing to reduce energy bills for our constituents. The oil belongs to private companies who will ship it away to the highest bidder. “If Rosebank is approved, champagne corks will pop in the offices of oil giants. But it will not make an iota of difference to our constituents’ energy bills. “And it will accelerate the climate crisis, which is already pushing up food bills, dealing devastating floods in winter and in summer turning our green and pleasant land into a parched, burnt-out expanse.” A spokesperson for the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero said: “Any decision on Rosebank will be made through the quasi-judicial process, taking into account all relevant evidence, including environmental assessments and public representations received as part of the regulatory process.” Join our commenting forum Join thought-provoking conversations, follow other Independent readers and see their replies Comments
