Knifeman found guilty of attempted murder against 14 people in mass train attack

Article text

Anthony Williams has been found guilty of the attempted murder of 14 people on a London-bound train. The 33-year-old launched a rampage on an LNER train between Peterborough and Huntingdon on November 1 last year. He attacked 14 people in total during the incident, including 10 on a train. Williams had previously pleaded not guilty to all 14 charges of attempted murder at Cambridge Crown Court. His trial was told that Williams stabbed a boy and attempted to stab two men in Peterborough on the evening of Halloween last year, before travelling to London by train. Once in the capital, he stabbed a boy on a DLR train in the early hours of November 1, before returning to Peterborough and spending much of the day at home. On the evening of November 1, he walked from his home to Peterborough station, boarded a London-bound LNER service and stabbed nine passengers and a member of on-board staff after the high-speed train had departed. LATEST DEVELOPMENTS: He was first found guilty of the attempted murder of train staff member Mr Zitouni, 48, and six passengers: Jonathan Gjoshe, 22; Sachin Balakrishnan, 29; David Presland, 73; Scott Bletcher, 50; Scott Green, 52; and 61-year-old Stephen Crean. He was then found guilty of the remaining counts shortly after, including the attempted murder of three more people on the LNER train: Kevin Deely, 58, Raza Aslam, 44, and 34-year-old Michael Paffett. Williams was also found guilty of the attempted murders of three people in Peterborough on October 31 last year, at a footbridge near Henry Penn Walk and in the car park of the Railworld attraction. They were a 14-year-old boy whom he stabbed, who cannot be identified for legal reasons, and two men whom he attempted to stab: Dawid Taborski, 26, and 22-year-old William Ogelby. He was then found guilty of the attempted murder of Isaiah Idris, then aged 17 and now aged 18, whom he stabbed on a DLR train that was approaching Pontoon Dock station in London in the early hours of November. Defending Williams at Cambridge Crown Court, Gillian Jones KC described him as a “man with a chaotic and disordered mind”. She said that psychiatrists for the court had determined that he was “in the clutches of an acute psychotic episode” during the attack. “At the time of these events, Mr Williams was seriously mentally unwell,” Ms Jones said. Prosecutor Christopher Paxton KC said that Williams was “rocking and smiling to himself, and said to the officer, 'Do you reckon it was worth it?' while in custody after the attack. The trial heard how on-board staff member Samir Zitouni tried to fend off Williams with a frying pan, and one passenger used a toaster, while others tried to block his way with trolleys and luggage. Some sheltered in toilets, or in whatever safe areas they could find. The driver stopped the train at Huntingdon with the help of a signaller, ahead of its next scheduled stop at Stevenage, and Williams was tasered and arrested in a car park at the station. Jurors were told that Mr Zitouni was taken to intensive care in a life-threatening condition, having sustained stab wounds, including three to the back. He suffered “catastrophic blood loss” and required four blood transfusions. Ben Samples from the Crown Prosecution Service said: "Anthony Williams committed a terrifying series of attacks, culminating in a brutal series of stabbings in a very public, enclosed space. “This trial had a single purpose, to prove to a jury that when Anthony Williams attacked these people, he intended to kill them. He equipped himself with a knife and deliberately went out to end people’s lives. The fact that nobody was killed in his rampage is down to the courage, quick thinking and good fortune of the people he attacked. “We have presented evidence of Williams’s movements, the nature of his attacks and his behaviour as he closed in on his victims. This has proven to the jury that he attacked with lethal intent. “The victims of these attacks showed immense courage in defending themselves and others. Their support has enabled us as prosecutors to build a compelling case that showed the court the full horrific nature of Williams’s actions." Follow related topics:
