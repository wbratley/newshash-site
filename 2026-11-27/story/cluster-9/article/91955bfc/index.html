Monthly mortgage payments to soar for homeowners in 2027, says Bank of England

Log in Around one million homeowners are set to see their monthly mortgage payments soar by £200 next year while a further 400,000 face a £500 hike, a stark new report from the Bank of England warns. The Bank’s latest Financial Stability Report, published on Thursday, shows far more households than expected are set to suffer huge hikes in mortgage repayments over the next three years as the war in Iran pushes up interest rates. The grim prediction comes just days after it was announced that energy bills would rise by 26 per cent in January. The Bank’s experts have warned that homeowners may need to cut down on spending as they also grapple with soaring household costs. The report shows around 6.5 million are expected to see payments rise by an average of about £100 a month – around double the amount predicted by the Bank in its last report in July. But many households are facing a substantially larger jump, with more than 1 million currently on mortgage rates below 3 per cent and due to refinance by the end of next year. They are facing an average increase of nearly £200 a month when they remortgage on to an average 5.5 per cent rate. About 430,000 households can also expect to see their payments go up by more than £500 a month over the next three years, the new projections show. Those who secured five-year terms on their mortgages in the first half of 2022 – when the base rate was still between 0.5 and 1 per cent – will see the biggest shock on their monthly repayments. Interest rates were lifted to 4 per cent earlier in November and there remains a strong likelihood of another hike, either in December or February, according to most analysts and economists. The BoE has consistently pointed to there being no pre-determined rate path, but ongoing inflation fears, a massive incoming hike to energy bills and the ongoing war in Iran all point to further potential rises – with some predictions even suggesting a return to 5 per cent in 2027 is possible. The report said households facing higher payments “may need to reduce discretionary spending or draw down savings as higher energy and other essential costs feed through”. Most have savings buffers but lower-income households, including renters, “have less room to adjust because they spend a larger share of their income on energy, food and other essentials”. The report nonetheless found that there were no signs of an increase in borrowers defaulting on their loans, while UK households remain resilient. Meanwhile, the Bank warned that cyber attacks had become a more prominent threat to the UK’s financial stability and financial firms are being urged to invest in their defences against the heightened cyber threat. It said the recent outages affecting Microsoft and Google Cloud, which affected millions who use the services, “illustrated how operational dependencies mean disruption at a single provider can affect many firms at once”. Financial firms need to “keep pace with the growing scale and sophistication of cyber threats” and deploy defences against them, especially as AI technology advances and quantum computing develops. “A failure to take action will compromise firms’ operational resilience with potential system-wide implications,” it warned.
