Thousands raised for Jason Arday’s family, and vigil planned as public support pours in

Article text

Thousands raised for Jason Arday’s family, and vigil planned as public support pours in Andy Burnham describes the professor’s death as a ‘tragedy’ and calls for a moment of reflection - Bookmark - CommentsGo to comments Thousands of pounds have been raised for the family of former Cambridge academic Jason Arday as members of the public rally to offer support and prepare to hold a vigil in his memory. The professor, who stepped down following a controversy over plagiarism, was declared dead after emergency services were called to an address in Battersea, south London, on Friday. A fundraising page set up on Saturday afternoon by Windrush activist Sir Patrick Vernon has collected more than £5,000 to assist Arday’s family with funeral costs. Writing on GoFundMe, Sir Patrick paid tribute to the “pioneering” academic. He said: “I was one of the people who publicly supported Jason during the recent controversy because I was deeply concerned about the nature and intensity of the attacks directed towards him. “I was also subject to media scrutiny for signing the letter of support and challenged in supporting Jason. “Following his death, my immediate concern is now for Jason’s family. “I am therefore establishing this fund to help contribute towards funeral, memorial and associated family costs and to provide practical support during an unimaginably difficult period.” Vigil for Jason Arday planned Stand Up To Racism has also confirmed plans to hold a memorial vigil for Arday. The event will take place on the steps of the National Gallery in Trafalgar Square, central London, at 6pm on Monday. Weyman Bennett, co-convenor of the group, said in a statement: “Jason Arday’s death is a tragedy – but make no mistake, it was the result of a racist witch hunt. “For more than a week, the right-wing press, politicians and the far right have hounded Jason with the most vicious racism. “They are spreading the far-right lie that Black people are only appointed because of the colour of our skin, rather than our ability. “They have blood on their hands. Our thoughts are with Jason’s family, but we are furious with those who are to blame. Enough is enough.” Burnham calls Arday’s death a ‘tragedy’ This follows comments from Prime Minister Andy Burnham, who described the professor’s death as a “tragedy” and called for a “moment of reflection”. Speaking to reporters on a trip to Cornwall, Mr Burnham said: “It’s a tragedy on so many levels, but particularly just for everybody who knew Jason, his family obviously, his friends. We think of them today. “It’s not a moment for any rushing to judgement. It’s a moment for reflection, I would say, reflecting on how things came to this. “A really sad, sad and sorry state of affairs, and I think we all today think of Jason, his contribution, and his family and friends.” In the hours leading up to his death, Arday told University of Cambridge professor and director of the Autism Research Centre Sir Simon Baron-Cohen that he “couldn’t go on”. Sir Simon, who spoke with Prof Arday on Friday morning, told the BBC: “He was being subjected to relentless scrutiny, including ridicule, discrediting absolutely every detail of his life, and he wasn’t coping with the loss of his career, the loss of his reputation, and he felt he couldn’t go on.” Arday, a professor of sociology of education who became Cambridge’s youngest black professor in 2023, resigned after press reports alleged plagiarism in his PhD thesis – completed at Liverpool John Moores University – as well as other research work. Media reports raised further questions about other claims, including assertions that he ran 600 miles within six days and raised £5.5m for charity. An investigation carried out by Liverpool John Moores University cleared Arday of any wrongdoing.
