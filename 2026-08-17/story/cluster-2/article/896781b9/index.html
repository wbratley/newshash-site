University of Cambridge urged to continue Jason Arday investigation by professor

Article text

University of Cambridge urged to continue Jason Arday investigation by professor A fundraiser for Prof Arday’s family, set up by Windrush campaigner Sir Patrick Vernon, has raised more than £60,000 - Bookmark The University of Cambridge ought to proceed with its probe into how Professor Jason Arday was recruited and his period teaching at the institution, according to a friend and former colleague. Prof Arday, who was found dead on Friday, stepped down from his post as professor of sociology of education last week following allegations of plagiarism made against him. Prior to his death, the university stated that it would look into the circumstances surrounding his "appointment and tenure", with the conclusions feeding into a wider review of senior recruitment processes. It is understood that the university currently feels it would not be right to decide whether to continue the inquiry while Prof Arday’s family is grieving. Sir Simon Baron-Cohen, a friend and former colleague of Prof Arday, told Times Radio that the university should "always be reflecting on how it’s operating and whether it can do better". The Cambridge professor and director of the Autism Research Centre said: “They have announced an investigation, and we should let that proceed. “We should we should see what we learn from it. This is the whole purpose of investigations. “When you’re talking about what’s in the public interest, when you’ve got public bodies like universities or, arguably, the media, political institutions, whatever, when we have investigations, it’s all about how can we improve them.” Sir Simon said the professor’s mental health had been left “in tatters”, adding there were questions about whether the media failed in its duty of care towards him as a vulnerable person with disabilities. He said the professor faced racist abuse and was used as a “lightning rod for a culture war on DEI (diversity, equity and inclusion)”. “(Prof Arday) started reporting racism within weeks of him being at Cambridge,” Sir Simon said. “It included things like threats of physical assault to himself and his family, racist hate speech, and racist actions, hate actions.” Lord Simon Woolley, principal of Homerton College, Cambridge, and a former mentor of Prof Arday, told BBC Radio 4’s Broadcasting House programme that his friend felt “hounded to death”. Lord Woolley said: “In a conversation I had with Jason on the Monday – we met on Monday, with a few dear friends – he said he felt hounded to death, his words, that he couldn’t see a way out.” He added: “On that day, we saw a man half his body weight and desperate beyond belief.” The crossbench peer said Prof Arday had been subjected to “the most horrendous, toxic, vicious witch hunt”, and called for an investigation into the “vicious media onslaught”. “The whole of the black community and black academics have been brought into this debate as though we don’t belong in these positions, because we are collectively the diversity, equity and inclusion hires,” Lord Woolley added. A fundraiser for Prof Arday’s family, set up by Windrush campaigner Sir Patrick Vernon, has raised more than £60,000. The organisation Stand Up To Racism has announced plans for a vigil for Prof Arday to be held on the steps of the National Gallery in Trafalgar Square, central London, at 6pm on Monday. On Saturday, Prime Minister Andy Burnham described the professor’s death as a “tragedy on so many levels”, adding: “It’s not a moment for any rushing to judgment. It’s a moment for reflection, I would say, reflecting on how things came to this.” In a statement issued by his publisher Simon & Schuster UK on Friday, Prof Arday’s family said he had been subjected to a “campaign of sustained abuse” since he accepted the role of professor at Cambridge. They said: “The campaign of misinformation was too much for Jason, who was a gentle man and who always wanted to see the best in everyone.”
