Dozens remain missing as desperate search enters its second day after 7.1 magnitude earthquake rocked Japan - flattening mall and splitting roads in two

Article text

- See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source Dozens of people remain missing after a 7.1 magnitude earthquake flattened a shopping centre in Japan. At least 13 people were confirmed dead on Tuesday after the quake, Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said. A number were understood to have been killed following an explosion at the Aeon mall in Kashima Town, thought to have been triggered by a gas leak. A large chimney at a paper factory also collapsed amid reports of injuries and damage to roads, bridges and buildings, as well as blackouts and fire. An unknown number of people were trapped when the second floor of the shopping centre collapsed, according to the Fire and Disaster Management Agency. Four people were pulled out with injuries and taken to a hospital while 10 others were unaccounted for. The extent of the damage is still under investigation as search-and-rescue efforts continue. A 35-year-old shopkeeper told Japan's Asahi newspaper he fled the building after a quake alert and heard an explosion sounding like a bomb blast around an hour later. When he turned around he reported seeing plumes of white smoke billowing from the mall. An aerial view shows the damaged Aeon Mall shopping centre after an earthquake with a preliminary magnitude of 7.1 struck Japan's southern Kumamoto prefecture Footage shows clouds of smoke billowing into the air as flames engulf a building This aerial image shows a section of the Kyushu Expressway damaged by the earthquake is seen in Yatsushiro, Kumamoto Prefecture, on Monday, July 28, 2026 An employee at the mall told Japanese broadcaster TBS television she and her colleagues smelled gas when they returned to their shops after helping customers to evacuate out of the building, then heard the explosion. And a man interviewed by Yomiuri newspaper said his friend who works at the mall was missing, and he was praying for those who are unaccounted for to be safely rescued. Footage on social media shows members of the public running for cover and crouching down on the floor as the ground shook at the shopping centre. Other videos showed clouds of smoke billowing into the air as flames engulfed a building, while firefighters scrambled to tackle the blaze. A separate clip showed the section of a collapsed bridge lying in a river in Yatsushiro City, while another showed how multiple freight wagons were derailed at Yatsushiro Station. Sections of the Kyushu Expressway, a major highway linking Fukuoka and Kagoshima, suffered severe damage. Footage appeared to show how sections were separated from the main structure following the quake, while separate images showed how parts of the road appeared to have split in two. Around 30 people were injured across the Kagoshima prefecture, the Fire and Disaster Management Agency said on Wednesday. Footage shows toppled-over boxed food items, as people climb over knocked-over shelves. Large cracks appeared on major roads, including an elevated highway, and a goods train derailed, according to NHK, while rail services were halted and flights grounded. At the Nippon Paper Industries Co.'s Yatsushiro factory, where a chimney collapsed and the building was damaged, 11 people were trapped underneath the debris, the Kumamoto emergency team said. Two of them were later found without vital signs, while the conditions of nine others were unknown. The Fire and Disaster Management Agency said more than 260,000 people were advised to evacuate, most of them in Kumamoto prefecture, but also in the neighbouring Nagasaki prefecture. The affected area is around 540 miles south-west of Tokyo, the country's capital. A tsunami advisory for the Ariake and Yatsushiro Seas on the western coasts of Kumamoto prefecture and three neighbouring prefectures was lifted within two hours, the Japan Meteorological Agency said. Emergency workers gather outside the Aeon Mall after scores of people were trapped inside following an explosion A damaged motorcycle and bicycles lie in a debris-covered car park following an explosion at the Aeon Mall An unknown number of people were trapped when the second floor of the shopping centre collapsed, according to the Fire and Disaster Management Agency View of the heavily damaged Aeon Mall Kumamoto building in Kashima, Kumamoto Prefecture, southwestern Japan Japan's Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi told journalists there were reports of damage to roads, bridges and buildings as well as blackouts and fires, though details were unclear. The Fire and Disaster Management Agency said major public facilities or infrastructure were not damaged. Japan's Nuclear Regulation Authority said no abnormalities were found at three nearby nuclear power plants. It comes as Kumamoto police are believed to have received more than 100 emergency calls following the quake. Kyodo News said a hospital in the city of Yatsushiro took in around 40 people with injuries and about 50 others were taken to a hospital in Kumamoto city. The quake also affected a number of major manufacturers in Kyushu. Shinkansen bullet trains and local trains in Kyushu were suspended for safety checks, and the runway at Aso Kumamoto Airport was closed, with 'no prospect of resuming operations' in the near future, according to a notice on the airport's website. The Japanese Defense Ministry said it dispatched military aircraft to the area to assess the situation. A train derailed and fell on its side at Yatsushiro station, and stone walls were damaged at Kumamoto Castle, a main tourist destination that was badly damaged in the 2016 quake and is still being repaired, Kyodo said. 'The shaking reminded me of the Kumamoto quake (10 years ago) and I was frightened,' Hiroki Shimoda said, an official at Mifune town hall, who saw roof tiles of nearby homes crash to the ground. Kumamoto was hit with a deadly quake in 2016 that killed at least 50 people. Shinji Kiyomoto, from the Japan Meteorological Agency, has urged residents to be cautious over the next two to three days. UN Secretary-General António Guterres' deputy spokesman extended sympathy to those in danger and the people of Japan. 'Our hearts go out to the people affected by this earthquake,' Farhan Haq, the deputy spokesman, said. 'The national authorities are leading the response, but we stand ready to support if needed.'
