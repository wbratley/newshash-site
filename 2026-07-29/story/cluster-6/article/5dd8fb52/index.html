Japan earthquake live: Death toll climbs to 13 as rescue efforts continue

Article text

Japan earthquake live: Death toll climbs to 13 as rescue efforts continue Multiple people have died while more are missing after a shopping centre collapsed following a huge 7.1 magnitude earthquake on the Japanese island of Kyushu A major magnitude-7.1 earthquake struck the Japanese island of Kyushu on Tuesday, according to Japan's meteorological survey. The quaketoppled buildings, sparked fires, brought down part of an ancient castle and even triggered an explosion at a popular shopping centre. 13 people have been confirmed dead, including two women who were at an Aeon Mall shopping centre in Kumamoto prefecture, where the explosion occurred following the massive earthquake. Multiple people remain missing at the nearby Nippon Paper Industries Yatsushiro Plant, where the tip of a chimney fell down, national broadcaster NHK has reported. The massive quake measured at the highest possible level seven on Japan's own Shindo "scale of shaking". This is an ongoing incident. Check back as the day progresses for further updates. Key Events A slew of photographs have documented the scale of damage at an Aeon Mall in the wake of an explosion, thought to have been triggered by gas, after the earthquake on Tuesday. Specialist teams from around Japan are now heading to Kumamoto to join in on rescue operations. The Tokyo Metropolitan Police Department took to X to confirm its Disaster Preparedness Division's Special Rescue Team and Aviation Unit travelled to the prefecture today "to support rescue operations". "The team members will do their upmost," the team said on X, formerly Twitter. The Japanese government has confirmed that more than 9,000 people were reported as staying at evacuation centres. "As of 7am today, 9,186 people have sought refuge in 506 evacuation centres across five prefectures, including Kumamoto and Fukuoka," government spokesman Minoru Kihara said. Kumamoto Castle, a top tourist site in the prefecture, was among one of many locations to suffer damage during yesterday's earthquake. Parts of the 400-year-old castle's exterior walls collapsed, with the aftermath caught on camera by local eyewitness Ryu. The iconic Kumamoto castle that we see today was completed in 1607 by Kato Kiyomasa, the first daimyo - or feudal lord - of the castle. It is now without parts of its exterior wall following Tuesday's quake. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi has issued her condolences to those who had died and has praised emergency services for their continued efforts to rescue those trapped. She says there are still many people waiting to be rescued, adding that it is a "race against time". Officials in Kumamoto prefecture report that 9,872 people have been evacuated from their homes. A further 36,700 residences are without electricity after an earthquake struck south-west Japan. Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi says the death toll from the quake has risen to 13. A third person is now dead, as per an official from Kumamoto Prefecture's Disaster Management Division. The person died after going into cardiac arrest. One person described feeling like the "building beneath you turns into liquid", while another told the BBC: "My TV fell on me." Others have reported "constant aftershocks" in the hours since the quake. Eight people have been pulled from the rubble of Kumamoto's Aeon Mall, Kumamato Prefecture Public Relations official Akino Sonoda has said. Of those eight, two women in their 20s were confirmed dead and another was in cardio arrest, local officials have reported. The remaining five extricated from the collapsed building were injured, but their conditions were deemed not to be life-threatening.Japan's public broadcaster NHK said that two hospitals have taken in at least 50 injured patients each, with one hospital staffer quoted as saying: "People are coming in with burns and fractures from being trapped under objects due to the earthquake, and ambulances are constantly bringing in injured people. Inside the hospital, there are water leaks and electrical system failures." Rescue operations have continued through the night, with Japan's fire, police, and Self Defence Force all working to help people from the rubble of razed buildings. Two women have been confirmed dead in the the Aeon Mall collapse, local officials have told Japan's public broadcaster NHK. As per reports, three others were in a state of cardio arrest, NHK said, including two at a factory in Yatsushiro where a smokestack had collapsed. A gas explosion reportedly caused the devastation at Aeon Mall Kumamoto in Kashima Town, Kumamoto Prefecture where up to 30 people are missing, tearing away one side of the mall. A spokesperson for mall operator Aeon 8267.T said the exact cause of the ensuing blast was unclear, however local news repots do indicate the explosion is being treated as gas-related, as per broadcaster NTV has reported. Kazuya Tsurunaga told news channel TBS he had been cleaning up dishes broken by the earthquake in a nearby pub, located about 300 metres from the mall. "It was a huge impact," he said, revealing he had been left stunned by the follow-up blast. "A large cloud of smoke billowed up, and because the wind happened to be blowing and the wind direction was towards the shop, the area around us was in a state like volcanic ash was falling." The latest earthquake is among more than 200 “significant” quakes in Japan since 2000 and it is one of the more powerful. Japan has so many because it lies on the Ring of Fire, an area of intense seismic and volcanic activity on both sides of the Pacific Ocean. “Japan sits on the boundaries of four tectonic plates, which makes it one of the most earthquake-prone areas in the world,” Shoichi Yoshioka, a professor at Japan’s Kobe University, previously told CNN. “About 10% of the world’s earthquakes of magnitude 6 or higher occur in or around Japan, so the risk is much higher than in places like Europe or the eastern United States, where earthquakes are rare." The worst was the 9.1 magnitude Tohoku earthquake in 2011 that led to a tsunami with around 20,000 people killed. Officials in Kumamoto Prefecture said that two workers were in a state of cardiac arrest at a factory in Yatsushiro City and that nine others are missing. The earthquake caused a smokestack to collapse and other damage at Nippon Paper Industries. The local government stated that 11 people were trapped at the plant, two of the rescued individuals had already suffered cardiac arrest, while the whereabouts of the remaining nine are still unknown. More than 175 schools and centres have been closed following the earthquake in the city of Kumamoto. Some locations have been set up as evacuation points with 178 sites with closed facilities also including sports centres and public parks Many events have also been cancelled over the coming days with the fear also of follow-up tremors. A hospital in Kumamoto is “at full capacity” with dozens of people injured in the earthquake. Saiseikai Kumamoto Hospital, with 400 beds, said it has had to suspend “pre-scheduled hospitalizations”, NHK reported. “People injured by furniture that fell during the earthquake, such as those with fractures, are being brought in one after another. We have 400 beds, but we are already at full capacity,” said a statement from the hospital. Shinkansen bullet trains and local trains in Kyushu have been suspended for safety checks, and the runway at Aso Kumamoto Airport was closed, with “no prospect of resuming operations,” according to a notice on the airport’s website. The Japanese Defense Ministry said it dispatched aircraft to the area to assess the situation. A train derailed and fell on its side at Yatsushiro station, and stone walls were damaged at Kumamoto castle, Kyodo said. “The shaking reminded me of the Kumamoto quake (10 years ago) and I was frightened,” said Hiroki Shimoda, an official at Mifune town hall, who saw roof tiles of nearby homes crash to the ground. An earthquake registering a magnitude of 7.1 shook Japan’s southern main island of Kyushu and caused part of a shopping center to collapse on Tuesday, leaving dozens of people missing or injured, officials and reports said. A tsunami advisory was issued but quickly lifted. The second floor of the Aeon Mall shopping center in Kashima Town collapsed, trapping an unknown number of people. Around 20 to 30 employees were missing while several other people were dug out and taken to a hospital, Kyodo News agency reported, quoting local police. The affected area is about 540 miles southwest of Tokyo. A tsunami advisory for the Ariake and Yatsushiro Seas on the western coasts of Kumamoto prefecture and three neighboring prefectures was lifted within two hours, the Japan Meteorological Agency said. Japan’s Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi told reporters there were reports of damage to roads, bridges and buildings, as well as blackouts and fires, though details were unclear. And outside vehicles have been badly damaged with debris and rubble in the car park. The Japanese government's disaster response taskforce have held a meeting at the prime minister's office. Prime Minister Takaichi Sanae said: "The governor of Kumamoto has requested the SDF be sent, so 3,600 SDF members (self defence force) will be dispatched for disaster relief work. "This is a very crucial moment, and human lives comes first: we would like to minimize the harm to people, and to ensure the safety of our people. We will work closely with the local government, and the government will make all-out efforts." The Fire and Disaster Management Agency says many people could be trapped inside the Aeon Mall Kumamoto in Kashima. Police say they believe about 20 to 30 people are unaccounted for in the shopping mall, with details still unclear about whether these are mainly shop workers or customers. Authorities say rescue operations continue. Troops have been deployed on “life-saving operations” as dozens of people remain missing and thousands are without power. Defense Minister Koizumi said: "The 42nd Rapid Deployment Regiment and the 8th Reconnaissance Unit of the Ground Self-Defense Force are primarily engaged in life-saving operations. We will continue to work with a sense of urgency and full effort in information gathering and providing support for daily life." About 40,000 households in Kumamoto Prefecture are experiencing power outages, meanwhile about 9,500 households are also without gas in the region. The Kumamoto Prefectural Police said one person who was found in a collapsed house and transported to a hospital in Kumamoto has died. Casualty figures are starting to emerge as hospitals in the area receive people injured in the quake. Meanwhile at the Aeon Mall, there are still 20 to 30 employees currently unaccounted for and feared trapped after the second story of the massive building collapsed. There are also reports that a further 10 people are still missing and their whereabouts are unknown as a result of the earthquake. About 10 people were also injured at a special nursing home for the elderly in Hikawa town, but none of them seriously, according to NHK. Firefighters are currently working to rescue nine people at the Nippon Paper Industries Yatsushiro Plant in Yatsushiro City. Several people were reported missing at the plant which saw a massive smokestack collapse in the quake. About 80 people injured by the earthquake are receiving treatment at the Yatsushiro Northern Regional Medical Center, local media reports. Meanwhile Uki General Hospital in Uki City has admitted 40 injured people, 10 of whom are seriously injured. Kumamoto Red Cross Hospital in Higashi Ward, Kumamoto City, is also reported to have received a large number of injured patients. At Yatsushiro Station, a freight train derailed and overturned due to the strong shaking. There were no passengers on board. The driver is being checked for injuries. Rail services across Kyushu, including the Shinkansen bullet train, were suspended.
