The captain wouldn't stop - even when we told him we were taking on water... then he abandoned ship: Ferry passengers tell of the horror that unfolded off the coast of Cyprus - killing eight with fears the 18 missing are dead

- See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source Survivors of a ferry that sank off the coast of northern Cyprus have told how the captain ignored their warnings that the boat was taking on water and abandoned ship as it capsized, killing eight people. Searches are continuing through the night for around 18 people who are still missing after the ship, carrying 270 passengers, capsized and sank. Shortly after the doomed vessel departed from Kyrenia just before 12pm on Sunday, passengers realised that there was a problem. They had discovered that the boat had begun to take on water and warned the captain and crew that they needed to return to port. Unmoved by their concerns, the ferry continued on towards the Turkish port of Tasucu. Fearing for their safety as the boat continued out to sea amid a storm, the terrified passengers scrambled to find lifejackets, only to realise that they were in short supply. They heard a blast come from the ship's bow and the vessel began to capsize, leading to an exodus of passengers who frantically jumped into the sea to escape, many without any buoyancy aids. Among the first to abandon ship, according to survivors, was the captain himself, who was arrested alongside seven of his crew members later on Sunday. The man, aged in his 50s and not named, told local Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus (TRNC) television that the front of the boat 'exploded' and the 'side windows 'blew out' as the boat took on more and more water. 'Those who could, got out,' he said, adding that he saw the captain among the first to abandon ship 'without organising anything'. Several rescued passengers told local media that a blast had gone off at the bow before it rolled over. Another witness, in his 30s, corroborated reports of an explosion. He said he had reported water leaks to the captain. 'The seats started to float, the captain kept going, and the bow exploded,' he said. As the boat went down, elderly people were reportedly left trapped inside the vessel and were unable to escape in time. Video footage showed chaotic scenes on board, as life jackets were in short supply, leading to some jumping into the water without any buoyancy aids. Other clips showed some of the survivors sat atop the ship's overturned hull, as others desperately awaited rescue in the surrounding waters. By late afternoon, the ship had 'completely sunk', settling on the seabed at a depth of 514 metres (1,686 feet), according to authorities. They said divers from Turkey would be assisting the search. A major rescue operation remains ongoing and will continue into the night, after Turkish Cypriot coastguard boats and rescue helicopters rushed to the scene earlier today. Ambulances were also dispatched to the scene to take survivors to local hospitals. Crowds of relatives have gathered at Kyrenia Hospital on Sunday evening, anxiously awaiting news of the condition of their loved ones. Many of the victims and survivors are believed to be Turkish nationals who took the ferry back to their country. 'The vessel had been moving very badly. It was constantly lifting out of the water and slamming back down. It was shaking violently,' survivor Efe Multici told CNN Turk. He added: 'Three or four minutes later, the ferry leapt high into the air, then came crashing down. The front left side burst open and water started pouring in.' The ferry had been carrying a total of 267 people, according to authorities, of which 161 were rescued and taken to hospital and 76 have returned home. The government of the self-styled TRNC has declared a three-day mourning period over the tragedy. Unal Ustel, prime minister of the self-proclaimed TRNC, confirmed the ship's captain and seven crew members had been arrested. Declaring it the 'biggest maritime disaster' in the country's history, he said rescue operations would continue until all of the missing people are found. He added: 'We are deeply saddened. There has been loss of life and there are injuries. 'We are on the ground with all our resources.' Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan offered his condolences and said his government was coordinating with Northern Cyprus in the search-and-rescue effort. 'We hope we'll be receiving positive news,' Mr Erdogan said on Sunday in Ankara while speaking at the graduation ceremony of the Turkish Military Academy. A Foreign Office spokesman told the Daily Mail: 'We are liaising with local authorities following a ferry incident in northern Cyprus.' Currently, it is unknown whether any British people were on board. The Cyprus Department of Meteorology's forecast for the north coast today described the sea as generally Slight – meaning the water had small, gentle waves. After around two miles offshore, ships become more exposed and can move noticeably with the waves. The mayor of Kyrenia, Murat Senkul, said the situation is 'very serious' and rescue teams are still trying to reach 23 people. He said the ship belonged to the Filo Denizcilik ferry company and sank off the coast of Diana beach on the island's northern coast. The TRNC, which is recognised only by Turkey, controls the northern third of the island of Cyprus, which has been divided since 1974. Ankara occupied that part of the island that year in response to an Athens-engineered Greek Cypriot coup seeking union with Greece. The TRNC today is served with regular air and sea links to Turkey. Cypriot government spokesman Constantinos Letymbiotis said in a post on X Sunday that the government had expressed its readiness to assist in search-and-rescue efforts with all available means. 'In these trying times, our thoughts are with those who lost their lives, those who were injured and all those who are living this tragedy,' Mr Letymbiotis said. 'At such times, the protection of human life is paramount.'
