GCSE results day 2026 - live updates as students find out their grades online, grade boundaries and resit options

Article text

GCSE results day 2026 - live updates as students find out their grades online, grade boundaries and resit options School students will collect their results from schools from around 8am, with results availible online to some from 11am. Thousands of teenagers up and down the country will collect their GCSE results this morning after sitting exams earlier in the summer. For the first time many will be accessing their results online, though most students are still expected to go to their schools to find out their grades. The Education Record app has been rolled out following a successful trial in Manchester in 2025, however results will be handed out by schools from 8am before they are updated online at 11am. The Department of Education has encouraged students to go in person to collect their results so that they are able to speak to teachers and staff about what to do next if their results aren't as good as they had hoped. GCSE grade boundaries will be confirmed today by exam boards including AQA, Pearson Edexcel, OCR, CCEA and WJEC. The grade boundaries - which have followed a numerical 9 to 1 system since 2017 are not fixed from one year to the next as exam boards set these once marking is almost complete. Key Events It's natural to wonder whether being a mark or two below a boundary could still get you over the line. Marks are not usually rounded up simply because a candidate falls just short of the next grade. Pupils should receive marks for individual exam papers alongside their grades, while exam boards publish the official boundaries on results day. This allows them to see exactly how their marks compare with the threshold. There are some cases where marks can be scaled to ensure different parts of the qualification have the correct weighting, with the resulting figures rounded to the nearest whole number. Modern foreign languages are one example, as listening, reading, speaking and writing are equally weighted - even when components have different maximum raw marks. Official grade boundaries will be published by exam boards including AQA, Pearson Edexcel, OCR, CCEA and WJEC on results day. Senior examiners will consider the difficulty of each paper, candidates' performance and the quality of the answer before setting the grade boundaries. This means grade boundaries are determined once exams have been taken and marking is nearing completion. If a 2026 paper proves harder than its equivalent in 2025, fewer marks could be needed to secure the same grade. If it's easier, the boundary could be higher. For students who have recently left school or are going to Sixth Form the best option is the former school or the sixth form centre they are planning to attend. Further Education colleges will offer some GCSE subjects, particularly English and maths. For those who are not in education there are private exam centres that accept external candidates for exams. Maths and English GCSE resit exams take place in November for both AQA and Pearson Edexcel. To resit any other subject students must wait until the the standard annual exam dates in May or June next year. Many schools, colleges and sixth form centres will allow students to resit their exams for free in the following year from their first attempt, if the grade was below a 4. For those who passed with a Grade 4 or above, or those wanting to take resits independently there is likely to be a charge of between £75 to £500 depending on the subject and where the exam is taken. In England, GCSEs are graded from 9 to 1. The highest grade of 9 is above the old A* as a 'super grade'. A U means a qualification is 'ungraded' and is the lowest possible result. Grade 4 is considered a standard pass and the equivalent to a low grade C on the former letter-based system. Grade 5 or above is considered a 'strong pass'. Students who receive a grade 3 or below in GCSE maths or English language will generally have to continue studying the subject. The grading system is different elsewhere in the UK. Wales continues to use grades from A to G*, while Northern Ireland also uses letter grades, including the C* grade introduced in 2019. GCSE grade boundaries are not fixed from one year to the next, with exam boards deciding where they should fall once marking is almost complete. Official grade boundaries will be published by exam boards including AQA, Pearson Edexcel, OCR, CCEA and WJEC on results day. GCSE results can be released to students by schools from 8am on Thursday. Results will update online on the Education Record app at 11am. The wait is finally over for hundreds of thousands of teenagers, with GCSE results day arriving across the country today. August 20 is the big day for pupils to find out how they performed in their exams, which they sat earlier this summer. Most students will collect their results from school, although arrangements can vary between schools and colleges. For the first time, pupils can also access their grades through the Education Record app, following a trial involving 95,000 students in Manchester and the West Midlands last summer. The app gives students a digital record of their results, which can be used when applying for further education or jobs. Those heading to school to collect their results are advised to bring photo ID, a charged phone, water and a pen and paper. Having a trusted adult there for support could also help ease the nerves. If you cannot attend school in person, check with your school beforehand to find out what arrangements are in place. And if you're on holiday, you may not necessarily need to rush home to find out your grades.
