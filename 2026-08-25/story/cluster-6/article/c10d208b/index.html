Ukraine gives Britain ‘goldmine’ of secret battlefield data to help build AI weapons & protect bases from Russian spies

Article text

UKRAINE has given Britain access to a treasure trove of top secret battlefield data to develop new AI weapons and protect bases from Russian attacks. It comes as the UK granted Kyiv the blueprints to Storm Shadow long-range missiles, sparking a furious reproach from Vladimir Putin and his Kremlin warmongers. Andy Burnham has now struck a deal with Volodymyr Zelensky to use Ukraine‘s coveted Avengers AI Labs, which holds millions of pieces of real-life combat data gathered from the war. The UK will become the first foreign country to access thousands of cameras and infrared sensors that have tracked Russian tanks, troops, artillery, air defences and drones, creating a “goldmine” for training AI. The Ukrainian military said its systems are now capable of detecting 70 per cent of all Russian targets after combing through years of data collected throughout the war. British scientists and tech firms will now be able to use the data to develop cutting-edge defence technology. Read more about Ukraine And the first system is already about to be deployed at a UK military site to spot protesters and hostile spies trying to gain intelligence. It turns buried fibre-optic cables into giant AI-powered sensors and could eventually be used to protect airports, prisons, railways and power stations. Britain will in return give Ukraine access to its universities, researchers and tech firms to help Kyiv develop new battlefield technology. Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said: “By combining the UK’s world-leading scientists, engineers and tech companies with Ukraine’s unique battlefield experience and data, we will strengthen both of our nations’ defence and security.” Most read in The Sun The deal could also “revolutionise all parts of public life, from air traffic control to train systems,” the Ministry of Defence said. Until now, British defence companies have relied on Ukrainian drone operations posted online, which offer just a snapshot of drone strikes and surveillance missions. It comes at a crucial time for the UK, with Moscow orchestrating a series of surveillance operations on European military bases and nuclear sites. Britain has also been targeted by drones trying to access sensitive facilities with drones launched from its shadow fleet vessels. The UK and Ukraine are also collaborating on a second project to build low-power AI chips that could create more durable and intelligent drones and robotics. Burnham hailed the “ground-breaking partnership” as he visited Kyiv on his first foreign trip as Prime Minister. He said: “Ukraine has become one of the world’s leading innovators in defence technology, forged through courage, ingenuity and the hard lessons of war.” The PM said the deal will give Britain “the opportunity to develop the technologies of the future, changing lives here in Britain and saving lives on the battlefield in Ukraine.” It comes as Russia has accused the UK of “participating” in the war and warned that there would be “consequences” over supplying weapons to Ukraine. Kremlin mouthpiece Dmitry Peskov blasted Britain for “adding fuel to the fire” for giving Zelensky information on the components on Storm Shadow missiles so that Kyiv can manufacture the weapon itself. But Burnham hit back and said: “Who started the fire? That’s the question, isn’t it?” Foreign minister Sergei Lavrov said that Russia would target and “destroy” facilities producing the missiles. It comes amid mounting pressure on Putin’s war machine. Ukrainian forces have repeatedly pummelled the nation’s equivalent of Amazon and are systemically blasting apart their fuel hubs. For a third night in a row, Ukrainian drones have blitzed Ozon, Russia’s second-biggest online retailer, in an attempt to bleed Putin‘s war chest dry.
