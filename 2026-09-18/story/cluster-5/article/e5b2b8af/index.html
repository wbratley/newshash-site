Tuchel talking to wrong fan-base about scars… England boss must be bold with squad AND tactics, not just Southgate 2.0

Article text

DID Thomas Tuchel make a major tactical balls-up against Argentina? Is water wet? The Pope Catholic? We finally got the answer to one of these imponderables from the man himself this week during an hour-long interview with Daniel Sturridge. Tuchel, that is, not the Pope. During a sit-down chat titled Reflections, the England boss admitted he went too defensive, too early against the Argies in World Cup semi-finals. As we all know, Harry Kane and Co sat back to try to defend a 1-0 lead with the best part of half an hour to go, only to concede twice late on. The German also spoke of his “scars”, and those of his team, after the heartbreaking exit, adding that it was his pain to bear. You’re talking to the wrong fanbase about scars, old boy. Embarrassment in South Africa, via a Frank Lampard goal that never was, and group-stage humiliation in Brazil. We’ve seen it all. That’s without mentioning the number of gut-wrenching defeats at Euros over the years, including in the last two finals. After 60 years of punishment, we’re covered in them. The point is that Tuchel was meant to be the man who came in and changed all that, who didn’t make catastrophic tactical decisions with a World Cup final on the line. Instead, at the critical point in a seismic match, what we got was Southgate 2.0. While generally beloved as England chief, Southgate was always out-matched whenever it came down to the really big games against the likes of Roberto Mancini and Luis de la Fuente. We can’t keep going round and round in circles making the same mistakes, otherwise we risk wasting another excellent crop of English players. To talk about pain and gaps to the “top nations” rather than backing this latest golden generation to close that gap themselves. Southgate’s caution cost England in too many big matches. Tuchel cannot inherit that same flinch. We are all tired of talk about mental scars, of self-flagellation after another disappointment and insisting that lessons will be learned. It’s time to deliver. After all, there were actually some positives to take from a World Cup in which we achieved our best finish since 1966. The night, or rather early morning, at the Azteca will always be one of the great England wins, overcoming a good Norway side and thumping France, albeit in a game nobody wants to play in. We need to look forward and Tuchel has to divert all of his energy into England’s response. We all know Atlanta was a mess — now what is he going to do about it? There can be no better opportunity to get the country back onside than against the world champions Spain at Wembley tomorrow week. He will announce his squad for the upcoming international break today, and it is an opportunity to give some fresh blood, who perhaps aren’t bearing so many scars, a crack. Cole Palmer is a no-brainer, as are Alex Scott and Morgan Gibbs-White. It might be too early for Rio Ngumoha, 18, and Max Dowman 16, but they should get a look-in over the next 18 months. The vast majority of England fans will tell you we’d rather go down fighting than clinging on for dear life with ten men camped on the edge of our own box. We don’t want more of the same.
