- Read the first exclusive extract from Earl Spencer's bombshell book here - See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source Prince William, Kate and King Charles carried out royal engagements today amid a war of words with Princess Diana's brother Earl Spencer over his bombshell new memoir. The public events this afternoon came hours after Buckingham Palace issued an extraordinary statement in response to the explosive book by the earl, the first extract of which can be read here on the Daily Mail. The book, Swan Song: Diana, My Sister, describes a devastating row he had with Charles in the days after Diana died. In a riposte that sent shockwaves through royal circles today, Charles's aides acidly suggested that grief can 'cloud reason, affect judgement and colour memory'. It was seen as a barbed echo of the late Queen Elizabeth II's famous 'recollections may vary' retort to Prince Harry and Meghan after their Oprah Winfrey interview in 2021. The palace statement said: 'While we do not comment on books as a matter of principle, His Majesty is mindful that the pain of fraternal grief can cloud reason, affect judgment and colour memory in ways others do not recognise, even many years after such a loss.' It also emerged today that Harry was 'aware' of the memoir. The Daily Mail understands Harry was given advance warning about the book, but he has not read it. Harry stayed at the earl's Althorp home in Northamptonshire in July, when he made an emotional pilgrimage to Diana's grave with his wife Meghan and children Archie at the estate where his mother grew up. William and Kate travelled to the west of Scotland to highlight members of the community who are bringing residents together, and their first stop was Bute Shinty Club to learn how the sporting centre has become a vital social hub. They took part in a beach litter pick, getting their hands dirty and driving a tractor to help clear refuse in picturesque Ettrick Bay. On the second stop of their visit to the Hebridean island, William and Kate joined members of Beachwatch Bute, an organisation that has helped keep the island's beaches free from litter and marine waste for almost 30 years. They walked along the sand with Beach Ranger Sandra Macmillan and supporters of the charity, which has coordinated clean-up activities on Bute since 1998, to keep the coastline safe and accessible for wildlife and local people. Armed with litter pickers and recycled farm feed bags to hold the rubbish, they stopped to pick up bits of packaging and rope and used their bare hands to pick up finer plastic filaments from fishing gear as they heard more about the charity's work. 'It's frightening how much stuff gets washed up on beaches, because if it's like this here, it's doubly bad out there,' said William, gesturing out to sea. 'I hope it's a nice message,' said the Prince. 'I hope it's not a message for help from someone stuck on an island!' William and Kate were then shown a tractor and trailer used by the charity and maintained by volunteer mechanic Robery Murray from West Coast Motors, which also stores the vehicles for Beachwatch when not in use. After being given a quick tutorial by Sandra, William took the wheel, and with Kate giggling alongside him in the cab, drove it down onto the sand and reversed it towards a large plastic mussel buoy that had washed up. Amid horizontal rain, they climbed out to help roll it onto the back of the tractor before driving it back up off the beach. Before leaving, the Prince and Princess thanked the team for their work, with William paying tribute to Sandra. 'She's dedicated to the cause,' he said. He also shrugged off the blustery and wet weather. 'This is the best, to me. I love it wild, it's the best sort of weather.' Admiring the sweeping views, he added: 'It's a stunning scene, beautiful.' The couple stopped to chat to well-wishers, including a family with a young baby who grinned happily at the royal visitors, and a woman who thanked Kate for her work in early years. 'You've done more over the past five years to highlight it than anyone else in my lifetime,' the woman told her. 'I'm really passionate about it,' replied Kate. 'It's needed now more than ever.' Speaking afterwards, Sandra said she felt 'honoured' by the visit. 'It went really well,' she said, although she thought William's tractor driving was 'a bit slow'. For the past two decades, Sandra, who was described by the group as a 'local legend', has been responsible for keeping around 50 miles of Bute's coastline clean, with the help of volunteers and a monthly community beach clean. As well as collecting litter and other refuse, they also dispose of wildlife that washes up along the coast, such as deer, seals, dolphins, porpoises and, last year, a sperm whale. Also in Scotland, the King hosted an artificial intelligence summit at Dumfries House, while Princess Anne opened a history centre in Staffordshire and the Duchess of Edinburgh continued her visit to Zambia. Meanwhile, Prince Harry visited Imperial College London in west London at a special event with Save The Children in support of youngsters injured in conflict. He participated in a discussion panel and was shown the latest prosthetics designed specifically for youngsters – including a specialised knee joint that can grow with the child. In Earl Spencer's book, he claims that Charles, then the Prince of Wales, subjected him to a 'stream of anger' after he insisted Diana would not have wanted Princes William and Harry, then aged 15 and 12, to walk in her funeral procession. According to an excerpt, one of Queen Elizabeth II's senior aides told him that William and Harry were to walk in the procession, prompting the earl to state, 'that's not going to happen'. Charles called minutes later and told him that 18 years earlier he had walked in the funeral procession of his great-uncle Lord Mountbatten, it is claimed. In the book, the earl claims: 'Prince Charles continued his stream of anger, without me interrupting. Then he stopped. 'I assumed the pause signalled an effort to bring himself under control, but then he unleashed down the phone what I have always taken to be his pent-up contempt for Diana, and his horror that the world was so bowled over by her death. 'Rest assured,' he hissed, 'we'll forget her soon enough!'.' The King's remarkable statement disputing Earl Spencer's claims reveals the depth of the his anger, especially because the palace ignored Harry's book Spare, according to experts. ITV royal editor Chris Ship said he was 'not expecting' the palace to respond, but that 'what became very clear from seeing that statement is they're not going to take this one lying down'. 'I know that Harry's aware of what his uncle has written in this book. And we also know that Harry stayed with Charles Spencer when he was over in the summer and had a big family gathering,' he said. During the King's AI summit today, he warned of the 'existential dangers' of artificial intelligence systems falling into the wrong hands, as he addressed tech leaders at a summit on the future of the technology. Charles told an audience that included bosses from Nvidia, Anthropic, Google DeepMind and OpenAI that decisions taken today on the technology will 'shape the world inherited by future generations', and he called on them to ensure that as they develop the technology, 'our humanity remains sacred'. The event at Dumfries House in Cumnock was also attended by guests including the chief adviser to the Vatican on the ethics of AI and technology, the UK's AI Minister, government advisors and others.
