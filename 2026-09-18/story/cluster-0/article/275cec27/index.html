Harry with the Irish Guards Harry's inaugural awards The Spirit of Invictus Awards will recognise those who have beaten the odds, supported others in their recovery, advanced sport and rehabilitation, or made an outstanding contribution to the wider Invictus Community. Nominees are not required to have competed at a previous Games, but must be wounded, injured or sick service personnel or veterans who have used sport or adventure activities as part of their recovery. Honours will be handed out across five categories: the Invictus Resilience Award, for remarkable personal achievement in the face of adversity; a Community Award, recognising collaborative spirit within the Invictus network; the Community of Nations Award, for those advancing sports-based recovery and inclusion across borders; and the Champion Award, honouring outstanding philanthropic support for the movement - alongside one further overall honour to be revealed during the evening. The duke's personal connection connection to wounded, injured and sick service personnel stretches back over a decade. Harry launched the Invictus Games in 2014, a Paralympic-style international sporting event created to support the rehabilitation of wounded or sick service personnel and veterans. Determined that the inaugural London Games shouldn't be a one-off, he established the Invictus Games Foundation to build a lasting legacy, select future host cities and oversee delivery of the Games. The event has since travelled the world, from Orlando to Toronto, Sydney to Düsseldorf, and most recently Whistler, British Columbia.
