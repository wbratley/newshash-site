It ended up being James recruited, and Amol murdered. As the show got underway on Friday night, Richard E Grant made another mistake - and refused to deny he was a Traitor once more, whilst in the mission, Hannah and Jerry found out they'd be up for the next murder. Huge viewership win for the Traitors The Traitors is a major win for the BBC win and proves that it wasn't just a one-off with last year's series featuring Alan Carr. The second episode of Celebrity Traitors drew in a peak audience of 8.6 million viewers. The second season of the BBC gameshow launched on Thursday, with the premiere becoming the most watched debut episode of any series of the programme in the UK, with an average of 8.9 million viewers. The BBC has now revealed that the second episode of the season had a peak audience of 8.6m viewers with an average of 8.5m and an audience share of 55.5 The first series of the celebrity spin-off debuted last autumn with an overnight audience of 6.1 million, according to the BBC. It was a ratings winner for the broadcaster and its nail-biting finale hit a peak of 15.4 million viewers – the biggest TV audience of 2025. Amol Rajan had 'clear the air' lunch with Richard E Grant after show Amol Rajan has revealed he cleared the air with Richard E Grant after being murdered on Celebrity Traitors. Speaking on Radio 4 today, the Journalist, 43, said that the actor, 69, apologised to him and said that he went "completely mad" on the BBC show. "Absolutely [I played it all wrong]," he said. "There is generally a tactic that is applicable and effective - you've got to try and stay schtum. And the one or two things I might say in my defence are: you know how these things work—it's all in the edit. Also, lots of people were having a go at Richard, we all had a go at him because he said he was a traitor, which has never happened before and is a completely ludicrous thing to do. "So it wasn't just me doing it. I actually had a lunch with him (it was all filmed in Spring) a few weeks ago, and I did ask him and he said, 'I'm so sorry, I went completely mad and thought you were a threat.' So, in my defence, there were other people asking hard questions as well but my strategy as it was got me this close to becoming a traitor which, ah I would have loved!" Headache for BBC bosses as US version 'spoiling' huge twists While BBC bosses might be celebrating the soaring ratings for Celebrity Traitors, it's going to prove to be a bit tricky to prevent spoilers leaking out. It's usually a watertight operation for producers, despite being filmed months ago in May. The series is shot back-to-back with the US version's latest series at Ardross castle in the Scottish Highlands. Filming the shows, which includes using the same props and challenges, in quick succession isn't usually a problem. However, for the first time, the US series is airing two weeks ahead of the UK version. In the first episodes of the US version, called Traitors: New Blood, two traitors had to recruit two faithfuls to battle it out to become the third traitor. The unsuccessful faithful was then killed off. Viewers watched a similar version play out on Celebrity Traitors involving Richard E Grant, Maya Jama, Amol Rajan and James Acaster. His wife Lou even muscled in on the jokes, warning: "I’m using AI to replace you in every photo in the house with this one." Record could be set ahead of first banishment If poor Richard E Grant finds himself banished next, it will set a record for the earliest Traitor catch in the history of the UK show. A Traitor has never been caught in the first banishment. Hugo, from series four, was caught in the second banishment, while Ash, from series two, and Armani, from series three, were both caught in the third banishment. It won't, however, be an international record, as the first series of the New Zealand version caught Traitor Loryn in the first banishment they held. The actor had accidentally revealed to multiple people he was a traitor shortly after he found out his role in Thursday’s episode. Speaking about Grant’s gaffe, Amol added: “What was that about? I mean no one’s ever done that. I mean who knows? I think the one tactic that you should try to abide by is to keep a little bit, you know, fly under the radar a little bit for the first few days. Just shut up for the first few shows.” Amol Rajan admits he played ‘the wrong game’ Journalist Amol Rajan has admitted he played “the wrong game” after he became the first player to be murdered. Speaking on The Celebrity Traitors: Uncloaked on Friday, Rajan said: “It’s hard not to watch it and think I, obviously, with 20/20 hindsight, I played the wrong game. That was a crazy, crazy once-in-a-lifetime (opportunity).” The former Today Show host said that he felt that if he had been recruited, he would have made a good traitor. He said: “Obviously, I wish I’d been chosen, and I wish I’d been a traitor. I can say, hand on heart, that if I’d been chosen as a traitor in the first, when Maya and Richard were, if I got the nod or the tap at the roundtable, I think it would have thrown me a bit. “I’d be like, ‘what am I doing? what’s going on?’, having genuinely spent a day as a faithful, if I’d then been recruited, I think I would have been quite a good traitor because I’d actually known what it was like to be a faithful.
