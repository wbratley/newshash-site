River Wear - latest: Three teens dead after car plunges into water in Sunderland

Article text

River Wear - latest: Three teens dead after car plunges into water in Sunderland Three teenagers have tragically died after a car entered the water in the River Wear in Sunderland - a boy who survived has been arrested Three teenagers - two boys and a girl - have died after a car smashed through a barrier and plunged into a river. The vehicle careered out of control and entered the River Wear in Sunderland shortly before 1.30pm. Four people are understood to have been in the black Audi A3 at the time, with a 17-year-old boy managing to reach safety. He is currently in hospital with what have been described as non-serious injuries and has now been arrested in connection with the incident. Northumbria Police said the three bodies had all been recovered from the water. Chief Superintendent Scott Cowie, Area Commander for Sunderland, said: "This is a truly tragic incident where three young people have lost their lives. Our thoughts are with their families, friends and all those impacted by what has happened. "We will continue to support them in any way that we can and ask that their privacy is respected at this time. I want to thank all of our partners who supported the multi-agency search efforts." A huge search and rescue response included an HM Coastguard search and rescue helicopter, the Great North Air Ambulance Service and the North East Ambulance Service's full Hazardous Area Response Team. Pictures from the scene showed emergency services lining the riverbank, where a section of metal railing has been damaged. Key Events Images of the scene The incident sparked a huge emergency response in Sunderland - as you can see below. How to get in touch with police if you have information Northumbria Police have asked that anyone with information about the incident, particularly CCTV or dashcam footage, get in contact via social media, or using the live chat and report forms on the force website. Incident comes after cops' warning to young drivers Tuesday's tragedy comes a week after police chiefs warned that more than 23,000 people have been killed or seriously injured involving a driver aged 17 to 24 in the past five years. Analysis of figures from the Department of Transport between 2021 and 2025 show that the accidents included 7,500 crashes where the driver was in that age bracket, 4,000 who were passengers being driven by a young driver, and 11,600 other road users including pedestrians and people in other cars. Around a fifth of all road accidents that end with death or serious injury involve young drivers, defined as those aged 17 to 24. Families of dead teenagers have been informed, police say The search for the bodies of the teenagers in the water took multiple hours to complete, with rescue teams from the police, ambulance, the RNLI, the Coastguard and the fire and rescue service all involved. The Tyne and Wear Fire and Rescue Service (TWFRS) dispatched two fire engines and a fire boat to assist with the rescue operation. The North East Ambulance service dispatched three emergency ambulances, a full hazardous area response team and air ambulance. The next of kin of the three teenagers who died have been informed and are being supported by officers, police have said. What happened earlier today? If you're just joining us, here is a summary of what happened in the north east earlier today. - Police are continuing to investigate after three teenagers were killed when their Audi A3 entered the River Wear in Sunderland. - A 17-year-old boy, who was also in the vehicle at the time of the crash on Tuesday afternoon, has been arrested and is still in police custody. - Northumbria Police received reports that a black Audi A3 had come off the road at Panns Bank near Wearmouth Bridge and went into the river at around 1.30pm. - Four people were in the car at the time, the police have said. - Following a search, the bodies of two teenage boys and a teenage girl were recovered from the water on Tuesday evening. Chief Supt Scott Cowie, area commander for Sunderland, said on Tuesday night: “This is a truly tragic incident where three young people have lost their lives. “Our thoughts are with their families, friends and all those impacted by what has happened. “We will continue to support them in any way that we can and ask that their privacy is respected at this time." Police ask public not to speculate Police have reminded the public not to speculate about this incident because they have arrested a suspect, who is in hospital. Chief Supt Cowie said: "Our investigation is at a very early stage in establishing the circumstances surrounding what has happened. "We would ask members of the public not to speculate about the incident both on social media and in the community." The 31-year-old said: "There were loads of ambulances here. We had helicopters here at one point. This is the first time I've ever seen anything like this around here. "I've lived here for three years, this is sad to see." He said: "Tonight I’m afraid that the River Wear is a place of profound sorrow. Three young lives have been lost, and another young person is in hospital. I pray that God will hold those families close in their grief, and that the people of Sunderland will surround them with compassion rather than speculation. "May the three young people rest in the peace of Christ, and may all who loved them be given strength and consolation." Breakdown of emergency services involved in search A spokesperson for North East Ambulance Service said a number of resources were sent to the scene, including three ambulances, a clinical team leader, three officers, a full Hazardous Area Response Team (HART) and an air ambulance. Crews from HM Coastguard, RNLI, the fire service and the police were also deployed. A spokesperson for the ambulance service said: "We received a call to 999 at 1.23pm today (Tuesday 29 September 2026) to reports of a car entering the River Wear near Wearmouth Bridge in Sunderland. "We transported one person to hospital by road for further treatment. "Sadly, despite the best efforts of all emergency services in attendance, three teenagers died at the scene. "We extend our deepest condolences to all those affected by this tragic event and thank everyone whose dedication, compassion and support helped those involved during such difficult circumstances."
