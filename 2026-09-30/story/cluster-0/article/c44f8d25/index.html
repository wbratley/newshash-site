Log in Andy Burnham has said he is willing to “pay a political price” by scrapping the state pensions triple lock in order to help fund his social care plans. The controversial move was unveiled in his leadership speech on Tuesday when he said he would remove the link to average earnings to fund a new National Care Service. He told the conference in Liverpool that he wanted to build a “national consensus” around plans being drawn up following a consultation by Baroness Louise Casey. “I won’t pretend some of this won’t be difficult,” he said. “I accept I may pay a political price. But someone has to go through the pain barrier and rip the plaster off.” The prime minister also said he will back abolishing first-past-the-post, as he unveiled plans for a new national commission on electoral reform. He delivered his speech to a packed conference hall, becoming visibly emotional as he talked about his late father Roy and vowing to govern in a way which would make him proud. In a preview of his speech today, he will say: “Within 10 years, I want our energy costs to be in line with other nations in Europe.” The problem is that switching from gas to renewable energy means building a whole new electricity grid. This is what is so expensive, and explains why wind and solar power, which is cheap to run, costs so much by the time the electricity they produce gets to people’s homes. But the cost of investing in new grid networks and connections cannot be magicked away by announcing a new organisation. Journalists have been briefed that GB Grid, which is being set up “within” GB Energy, will be given access to a pot of £4bn that has already been allocated to GB Energy, “though it is not expected to use all of that money”.
