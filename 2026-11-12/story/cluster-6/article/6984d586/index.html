Lucy Beaumont's fight for girls' safety: How comedian became a patron of Women's Aid - as she reveals herself as David Walliams whistleblower

Article text

Comedian Lucy Beaumont bravely revealed on Tuesday that she was a whistleblower on David Walliams' allegations. Walliams was unmasked as the celebrity who fought for a court order to suppress allegations that he sexually assaulted a 15-year-old girl and preyed on multiple teenage girls while 'abusing his status' to exploit teens. He sought a High Court injunction to prevent publication of the claims, arguing he would take his own life if the allegations became public. Just hours after Walliams was finally exposed, Beaumont revealed that she has privately been 'doing everything she can' to support one of his alleged victims. While she is best known for her deadpan jokes on stage, Beaumont dedicates her time offstage to protecting victims of abuse. The Celebrity Traitors star, who is a patron of Hull Women's Aid, uses her platform to advocate for women and girls' safety. She has also explained how her own experience as the victim of predatory behaviour as a teenager 'makes her massively want to help others'. Beaumont, who began her career as a teaching assistant, has also raised thousands of pounds for countless charities including The Children’s Hospital Charity, Cost-of-Living School Gigs and Mothershare. 'Victims and survivors are most at risk when leaving or having just left an abusive partner and without the incredible first response and lasting care that women's aid provide, the most vulnerable families in our society would not be safe.' Beaumont is a hugely talented stand-up, comedy actress and writer, best known for starring and co-writing Meet The Richardsons alongside her ex Jon Richardson. The show first launched on the channel in 2020 with the couple writing it alongside Peter Kay's Car Share scribe Tim Reid. Beaumont has featured on a number of panel shows including Would I Lie To You?, Taskmaster, The Last Leg and Have I Got News For You. In 2022 her comedy Hullraisers, which she co-wrote, was aired on Channel 4 while in that same year she released her first book Drinking Custard: Diary of a Confused Mum.
