James Bulger's mother Denise says she feels 'overwhelming relief' as killer Jon Venables loses latest bid to be freed from prison

Article text

James Bulger's mother Denise says she feels 'overwhelming relief' as killer Jon Venables loses latest bid to be freed from prison James Bulger's mother today told of her 'overwhelming relief' after his killer Jon Venables lost his latest bid to be freed from prison. Venables tortured and murdered two-year-old James in 1993 after he and Robert Thompson, both then aged ten, snatched him from a shopping centre in Bootle, Merseyside. The Parole Board said a panel was 'not satisfied' that Venables, now 44, was suitable for release. Venables himself told parole staff dealing with his latest review, which took place in private, that he was not seeking release. It means that he will remain behind bars for at least another two years following his latest recall to prison for possessing indecent images of children in 2017. The decision is one James's mother Denise Fergus 'has been desperately waiting for', her spokeswoman told the Daily Mail. 'She is delighted that Jon Venables will not be able to harm another family like hers for at least another two years,' Kym Morris said. 'From a personal perspective she was deeply worried and understandably worried that the parole board would deem him fit to be released from prison as she knows first-hand the danger he poses to the nation. 'We are delighted the parole board has made the correct decision. Her main emotion today is one of overwhelming relief.' A summary of the panel's findings, published on Monday, said: 'At the outset of the hearing, Mr Venables submitted an opening statement to the effect that he accepted the need to undertake further offending work in custody and so was not seeking release.' Jon Venables has been denied his latest bid for freedom after a Parole Board panel was 'not satisfied' he was suitable for release The murder of James Bulger caused shockwaves across the nation The panel considered evidence from probation officers based in the prison and in the community, three psychologists and a psychiatrist, and all bar one found that he should remain in prison. Since his initial release on licence in July 2001, he has returned to prison multiple times. In February 2010, he was recalled to prison after indecent images of children were found on his computer. In August 2013, he was freed again – but then called back in November 2017 for the same offence, with parole judges considering his case again in September 2020. At a parole hearing in 2023 the board rejected his bid for freedom, finding he still posed a danger to children and could go on to offend again. In the latest review, a 'victim personal statement which clearly conveyed the devastating impact of Mr Venables' crime and the impact of his offending' was heard by the panel. Members found that there were concerns about how well Venables would work with probation staff if released and 'how open and honest he would be in supervision'. They said there is 'a need for further work in custody to address his sexual interests'. The summary went on: 'Mr Venables articulated his protracted and extreme remorse for his crime. Professionals assessed this to be genuine and sincere.' James's parents have previously condemned the decision to hold the latest hearings in private, calling it an 'insult'. The parole board had insisted that making the hearing private would increase 'the risk of identifying the prisoner and of sensationalising the case'. James's father Ralph Bulger, 59, described himself as 'absolutely raging' at the decision. His mother previously said a public hearing could improve people's understanding of the parole process and make her 'finally feel listened to'.
