Reform councillor who said Nigerians should be 'melted down and used to fill in potholes' guilty of sending grossly offensive message

Article text

Reform councillor who said Nigerians should be 'melted down and used to fill in potholes' guilty of sending grossly offensive message A suspended Reform UK councillor who posted on social media that Nigerians should be melted down and used to fill potholes has been found guilty of posting a grossly offensive message. Sunderland City councillor Glenn Gibbins, 66, denied breaching the Communications Act by making the post on Facebook two years before he was elected, claiming it was a ‘tongue-in-cheek joke’. But his defence was dismissed as he was found guilty at South Tyneside Magistrates’ Court today, after the prosecution said the post ‘conjures up despicable images’. The post came to light when the campaign group Hope Not Hate combed through Gibbins’ social media output when he was announced as a Reform UK candidate for Sunderland City Council. He was subsequently elected to represent the Hylton Castle ward in May but suspended by Reform in August when the post was uncovered. Police were informed after he was successfully voted in. In the post, Gibbins said: ‘Carnt [sic] believe the amount of nigerians in town… should melt them all down and fill in the pot holes!!’ Emma Dowling, prosecuting, said: ‘The comment about melting them down conjures up despicable images.’ She said it implied that Nigerians were ‘somewhat less than human, stripping people in that group of humanity’. Glenn Gibbins was suspended by Reform in August when the post came to light He was charged under the Communications Act 2003 in relation to a post from 2024 about Nigerians Campaign group Hope not Hate discovered the offending post and Gibbins was reported to police Gibbins, a divorced former salesman who runs a stall in Sunderland city centre printing novelty gifts, told the court: ‘I thought it was a tongue-in-cheek joke. ‘It was an unreal scenario. No one took it literally.’ He claimed he wrote the post after a ‘couple of cans of beer’ and it was 'a bit naughty but I thought it was fun’. He denied that it was grossly offensive. However, he was found guilty following a short trial by District Judge Zoe Passfield and sentenced to a 12-month community order with 150 hours of unpaid work. Earlier, Gibbins told the court how he set up his business in the Bridges shopping centre and that on the day he made the post he had been approached by some Nigerians about buying some of his printing equipment. Gibbins said he had 'banter' with the Nigerians at his stall and that the conversation was good fun. He said he then saw a post on Facebook about Nigerians which he tried to share without success, so typed out a message himself and posted it to his public profile. He said the group of Nigerians had 'done my head in, in a funny way not a bad way'. When asked by Ms Dowling what was funny about the post, Gibbins replied: 'It’s an unreal scenario, you cannot melt people down, anyone that takes that literally… you cannot do that.' He added: 'I wouldn’t dream of joking about killing people.' Judge Passfield said Gibbins now accepted that his post was offensive, but not grossly so, and maintained it was a joke. But she found the post suggested a specific ethnic minority could be 'eradicated'. 'The post was nothing more and nothing less than a grossly offensive comment about members of the Nigerian community in Sunderland and Mr Gibbins’ views as to their status, or otherwise, as human beings,' she said. Adam King, defending, said the post was 'an unpleasant joke/non-joke' – but noted that professional comedians such as Jimmy Carr had joked about gypsies in the Holocaust and they had not been prosecuted. Godwin Ejeh, a community leader for Nigerians in Sunderland, told the court that Nigerians in the city were 'distressed' for their safety and the safety of their children. The court heard Gibbins was approached by Reform UK about becoming a councillor in March this year and considered the post would be a source of income 'to tide me over' when business was quiet. His legal team said he plans to appeal against the decision at Crown Court.
