What Russell Brand’s life in prison will look like

Article text

Russell Brand risks being “targeted” in prison due to his celebrity status and the nature of his offences, experts have said. The former actor and comedian was sentenced to 10 years and six months in prison at Southwark Crown Court in London on Monday after being found guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault. Now, Brand will briefly be taken to a holding cell before being transported to prison, where, according to leading prison reform experts, his “celebrity status may be a factor leading to increased risk” of harm.Meanwhile, the Metropolitan Police has said he is under investigation over other allegations of rape made by two different women. What will happen to Brand now? It is not yet known where Brand will serve his sentence. Two possibilities are HMP Bullingdon in Oxfordshire and HMP Isle of Wight. Ben Blackwell, of the Prison Advice and Care Trust, told The i Paper: “When sentenced, he will be eventually moved to the correct category prison.  “Certain prisons tend to specialise in sex offenders, such as HMP Isle of Wight or HMP Bullingdon, and he will possibly be placed in one of those. If not, he will be located on a vulnerable prisoner wing of whichever prison he is placed in.” A vulnerable persons unit (VPU) is where some inmates are held if authorities believe they are at risk from others. However, not all prisons have a VPU, so it may be necessary to transfer Brand to another one if he is considered to be facing a serious enough threat. While awaiting sentencing, Brand had been held in a VPU at HMP Wandsworth in south London. How will Brand be treated? The treatment Brand is reported to have faced at Wandsworth immediately following his guilty verdict may give some insight. The Daily Mail reported that Brand was left “cowering in fear” in his cell as he faced a chorus of abusive chants from inmates shouting: “There’s a Brand new nonce on the wing! A Brand new nonce.” Asked if his celebrity status might afford him extra security, a prison reform expert told The i Paper: “It’s based on an individualised risk assessment, there is no blanket policy for celebrities. A prison vehicle believed to be carrying Brand leaves Southwark Crown Court (Photo: Alishia Abodunde/Getty) “Celebrity status may be a factor leading to increased risk, but other factors such as the nature of the index offence are also relevant.” Asked if Brand faced a greater threat of violence due to the nature of his offences, the expert said: “Sex offences are the most targeted.” In October last year, Ian Watkins, singer with rock band Lostprophets, was stabbed to death while serving a 29-year prison sentence for child sex offences in HMP Wakefield. Watkins had also been stabbed in prison in 2023, when he was taken to hospital with injuries which were not life-threatening. What is life like in Bullingdon and Isle of Wight prisons? HMP Bullingdon is a Category B/C prison located near Bicester in Oxfordshire. It has been flagged in several HM Inspectorate of Prisons reports for issues with overcrowding, safety, gang violence and drugs. Bullingdon once housed sex offender and former children’s entertainer Rolf Harris. HMP Isle of Wight is a Category B training prison in the town of Newport, spread across two sites, Albany and Parkhurst. It holds about 1,700 prisoners and most have been convicted of sexual offences. When HM Inspectorate of Prisons visited in March it rated safety, respect, and purposeful activity “not sufficiently good”. It noted that drug availability had almost doubled since the previous inspection, and that violence in the prison was rising. Asked what Brand’s life inside prison might look like, Blackwell said: “In general population, sex offenders are sometimes targeted, but if he is in a prison or wing which is largely made up of sex offenders it is less likely.” Blackwell added that Brand would “probably have the same level of family contact as most other prisoners. He will have to get the phone numbers of friends and family approved for him to ring them. Although, this is normally just a matter of prison staff contacting the named person and asking if they are happy to receive contact from the prisoner. “His family should also be able to visit him. Although, all this is on the assumption that there will be no additional restrictions being placed on who he can contact.”
