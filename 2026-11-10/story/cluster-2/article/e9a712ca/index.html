How Nvidia boss’s affair with ‘Chinese spy’ sent shockwaves across globe as UK pensions exposed & tech markets plunge

Article text

IT sounds like the plot of a classic spy thriller: A suspected Chinese agent has an affair with the American boss of the world’s most valuable company, sparking fears of a security breach. And US tech chief Jensen Huang’s fling with businesswoman Alice Huang might also have huge consequences for the future of artificial intelligence — as well as the global economy. That’s because his chip-making business Nvidia supplies two-thirds of the world’s AI computing power, and the storm over the company brought about by his conduct is likely to spook investors who already fear the boom in the new technology is coming to an end. Shares in the huge firm crashed eight per cent in early trading yesterday, wiping out $335billion from its market value, amid fears the tech titan could be forced to quit — with no obvious successor in place. Famed for his trademark leather jacket, 63-year-old Huang admitted to being unfaithful to his wife Lori and apologised for hurting his family, adding that he supports an investigation into his actions which his own board is now carrying out. In a message to staff on Sunday, Huang confessed: “I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry.” Read More on World News He added: “Throughout my life, my family has given me love and support. They deserve my honesty and loyalty. Instead, I let them down and damaged the life we built together. “To my wife and family: I love you. I know an apology cannot undo what I have done. I hope our family will heal and that, over time, I can earn back your trust.” Huang’s friend US President Donald Trump backed the microchip mogul as Nvidia insisted there was no evidence its security had been compromised as it battles Chinese rivals for global AI domination. The workaholic CEO — whose fortune is estimated at £116billion — has flown on Air Force One with Trump on Chinese trade missions. Most read in The Sun Marco Rubio, the US Secretary of State, was reported to have warned that dark–haired Ms Huang, aged in her late 30s, was a spy recruited by China’s Ministry of State Security. She was alleged to have gathered information regarding US artificial intelligence chips and encouraged the Nvidia boss to push for looser export restrictions on sale to China. Tech markets propping up the global economy were braced for a crash in trading later today which could hit investments, including UK pension funds. Shares in Nvidia, valued at a staggering £3.35trillion, crashed early yesterday but rallied later on Wall Street amid continuing market jitters. The shock news triggered an immediate share price drop, aggravating a sell-off already under way following the rout of Trump’s Republicans in last week’s mid-term elections as Democrats seek to place greater control on the development of AI. British tech firms are also braced for fallout from the sex scandal. Liz Upton, co-founder of British computer business Raspberry Pi, told a conference amid laughter yesterday: “I do hope Jensen’s wandering penis doesn’t bring down my share price.” Online wags were also quick to poke fun at the Nvidia boss’s fling with copycat memes of the famous Coldplay Jumbotron moment when the affair between US executives Andy Byron and Kristin Cabot was exposed in 2025. But serious concerns intensified that a burst of AI bubble stocks could trigger a global recession as the scandal played out yesterday. The International Monetary Fund and central banks across the globe fear over-investment in AI tech stocks could herald broader financial turmoil. Nvidia, Microsoft, Alphabet, Apple, Meta and Broadcom account for an unprecedented chunk of the market value of listed companies and prop up many investment and pension funds. The UK Pensions Regulator recently warned British retirement pots were at risk and urged investors to test for “hidden AI exposure”. US spy chiefs have been eyeing Ms Huang, who works for Singapore-based firm Megaspeed, which buys AI semi- conductor chips from Nvidia. Megaspeed is suspected of helping Beijing boffins to gain access to Nvidia’s world-leading technology built into microchips banned from export to China. Nvidia’s board ordered an independent investigation amid concerns that its charismatic boss may have fallen victim to a Chinese “honey pot” sting. He said that he was fully co-operating and would continue as chief executive until the investigation concluded and issued a statement in a bid to calm shareholders. Mr Huang said: “You deserve better from me. Leading Nvidia is the privilege of my life. My responsibility is to serve you and be worthy of your trust. I have fallen short of that responsibility. I am fully committed to Nvidia and will work to earn back your confidence.” China branded the spying claims “entirely groundless, speculative and intended to smear and malign China”. But Tory Shadow Foreign Secretary Tom Tugendhat warned yesterday that “it wouldn’t at all surprise” him if the Chinese state had recruited Alice Huang. He added that it “would not be beyond the Chinese state” to threaten sending Ms Huang’s family to prison if she did not co-operate. Ms Huang — no relation to the Nvidia boss — worked as a TV reporter in China before becoming a chip buyer at Singapore-based data centre company Megaspeed. It is unclear when the alleged spy met the tech supremo but they were photographed together in 2024 at a bar in Taiwan’s capital Taipei. Megaspeed swiftly became one of Nvidia’s biggest customers after the illicit affair was kindled. In the past two years, the company has bought more than £3.6billion worth of microchips from the US firm. Taiwan-born Huang, who co-founded Nvidia in 1993, has become one of Donald Trump’s closest “tech bro” pals and claims the US president often calls him late at night. He has also met British PM Andy Burnham and attended a summit with the King in September. Trump said yesterday that Mr Huang was “an honourable guy”, adding he had not spoken with him about the affair. He said: “I haven’t heard he’s done anything wrong. He dealt with somebody, but, you know, so does everybody else. I just know he’s a fine person, but you never know what’s going on.” Trump was reported to have been briefed in October 2025 that Ms Huang was spying for China. Asked by reporters whether he now wished he had “acted on the intelligence” at the time, Trump replied: “No, I don’t think so. I think he’s an honourable guy. He probably has an excuse for it.” Trump’s inaction last night is said to have “raised eyebrows” among former national security officials. Tracy Walder, a former CIA officer and FBI special agent, told the American cable network NewsNation: “It’s even more disturbing to me that President Trump knew about this information and personally allowed him to stay that close.” AI firm stocks were hit last week by the Republican mid-term election flop which triggered fears that Trump’s support for his big tech pals would begin to wane. £12bn hit to stake By James Flanders NVIDIA saw more than $335billion wiped off its market value as shares plunged in early trading on Monday. Boss Jensen Huang’s stake also took a $12billion hit as his 3.61 per cent holding, worth around $162billion at Friday’s close, dropped to roughly $150billion. The AI chipmaker’s stock fell almost 7.5 per cent after Wall Street opened, hitting its lowest level in seven months. Shares dropped from Friday’s closing price of $186.64 to $172.75, cutting the company’s valuation from roughly $4.5trillion to $4.16trillion. The stock was later trading down six per cent, extending losses from last week’s sell-off in AI shares. Investors were rattled by a probe into whether Huang had been targeted by foreign intelligence, after he admitted an affair with a businesswoman suspected of being a Chinese intelligence operative. Nvidia’s board said it remained confident in its boss – but the uncertainty comes as investors question the scale of spending on AI data centres, a key source of demand for Nvidia’s chips. Chris Weston, an analyst with Australian broker Pepperstone, said the investigation was hurting the stock but did not expect Huang to be replaced. He warned shares would face an “absolute bloodbath” if investors believed the chief executive was leaving. Some analysts said Nvidia had no obvious successor lined up. Meanwhile, major Asian suppliers recovered from last week’s losses. Taiwan’s TSMC closed 4.7 per cent higher, while Samsung rose 0.6 per cent and SK Hynix gained 0.4 per cent, avoiding Nvidia’s sharp fall as investors assessed the chipmaker’s leadership uncertainty.
