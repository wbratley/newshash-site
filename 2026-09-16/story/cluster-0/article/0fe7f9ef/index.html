Desperate search continues into second day to find partially deaf and non-verbal boy, three, who got away from his grandfather in a park: Officials say no sightings of Noah have been reported - as '1,000 locals' join police to scour area - See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source The search to find a non–verbal, partially deaf boy who vanished from a Suffolk playground is continuing into a second day. Local officials said at 3am that there had been no sightings of three–year–old Noah Woods since he outpaced his grandfather and vanished down an alleyway at 3pm on Tuesday. Hundreds of members of the public turned out to help police as the hunt continued overnight throughout the town of Brantham. Noah, who is autistic and unlikely to respond to his name being called, was described as white with curly ginger hair. He was wearing a t–shirt with cartoon character Bluey on it, shorts and white trainers when he went missing. The alleyway he disappeared down leads to a new–build housing estate, a local pond and a railway line. Noah's great uncle, Tony Malcolm, who was part of the search team, described him as 'a lovely little kid'. Residents were urged to check their gardens, sheds and outbuildings. A Coastguard helicopter was seen circling the village, which is near the estuary of the River Stour. And passengers on trains between Manningtree and Ipswich were told that their services would be run at a reduced speed because of the searches. More than 100 people have joined the search to find three–year–old Noah Woods, who went missing after visiting a playground in Suffolk Noah vanished after becoming separated from a relative in Merriam Close, Brantham, on Tuesday afternoon Emergency services are searching the Brantham area in a bid to locate him, while residents have been urged to check their gardens, sheds and outbuildings Just before midnight, volunteers who joined the hunt were told to go home for the night and rest, but many remained on the streets. Brantham Parish Council wrote on its Facebook page: 'We have been completely overwhelmed by the support received from the Brantham community and from visitors from far and wide and our thanks go to the emergency services and all the volunteers who have joined the search so far. 'The Village Hall will be open all night with toilet facilities and drinks if you need it. 'The emergency services will be continuing their search throughout the night.' In a late–night update, they added: 'So far there have been no sightings of Noah. 'The police are continuing their search through the night. They have asked us to let the public know that if you are asked to move by the police this will be to allow them to do specialist work so please be courteous. 'For all those still searching, the police would like to remind volunteers to take care, travel in pairs, wear high–vis and to keep away from dangerous sites. 'As a priority please check your own property; in gardens, sheds, under furniture etc.' Parish council clerk Sarah Keys estimated 'up to 1,000' locals had joined the search. Volunteers from Suffolk Lowland Search and Rescue used a boat to search Decoy Pond, the local beauty spot Noah was seen running towards. Police dog units joined the search overnight, and thermal imagine cameras were deployed. One officer manning the cordon urged the public to allow officers to get on with the job themselves. He told the Harwich and Manningtree Standard: 'We have been given information and we want to search this bit with our teams now that we have got the teams here. 'You've got dog units looking for any sort of tracks, and then we've got thermal imaging going throughout the wooded area. 'At this time of night with this light, it's harder for members of the public – we want to make sure we can do that with all of our equipment we've got. 'The more people we have got in there [in the woodland] the harder it is going to be for us, especially with scents – if there is a scent or a track or anything like that, we don't want that to be changed in any way. 'If there are people in there, the thermal imaging is going to pick up them as opposed to obviously all of our goal to find the little man.' Suffolk Police said anyone with information about Noah's whereabouts should contact them. The said: 'Police are appealing for help to locate a missing three–year–old boy in Brantham. 'Noah was last seen at approximately 3pm [on] Tuesday 15 September, when he became separated from a relative after visiting the playground in Merriam Close. The search continues in darkness for three–year–old Noah, who disappeared in Brantham Volunteers who joined the hunt have now been told to go home for the night and rest 'Noah is reported to have run off down the alleyway beside the playground towards the new build housing estate. 'Noah is described as white, with curly ginger hair. He is wearing a white Bluey t–shirt, shorts and white trainers.' A spokesman for HM Coastguard said: 'We are assisting Suffolk Constabulary with an incident at Brantham by sending an HM Coastguard helicopter and Coastguard Rescue Teams from Felixstowe and Holbrook.'
