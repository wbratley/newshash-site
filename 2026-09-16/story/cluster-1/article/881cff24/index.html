Macklemore shared a statement Monday alleging that he was kicked off Sheeran's US tour dates because of what he said on stage in support of Palestinians while opening for the singer in New Jersey earlier this month. Later that day, Sheeran, 35, addressed the 43-year-old rapper being removed from the tour with his own statement which read that Macklemore's speech had upset the owners of venues they were set to play while reminding fans he will never personally take a publicly political stance. However, that response did not seem to be good enough as musician and Billie Eilish's brother/music partner Finneas led the four remaining acts who put out separate statements announcing that they will not continue with their dates on the stadium Loop Tour. The 25-year-old Los Angeles native wrote: ‘Artists must not be silenced when they speak up for the oppressed. 'I have decided to withdraw from my upcoming tour dates with Ed Sheeran. I stand with Palestine and its people.' I could never thank him enough for this. ‘But as Irish people, we know all too well about genocide, forced famine and violent occupation.’ The Hey Ma hitmaker continued by saying he 'cannot stand by and allow billionaires to use their position of power to silence the rightful voices of those who speak up.' The third support act who pulled out was the group Lukas Graham who wrote: 'We should be able to speak about war, about civilians being killed, about children who deserve to grow up. Wherever they live. Whatever flag flies over them.' Lukas Graham said that their upbringing in a small Denmark community made it so speaking your mind was part of everyday life and having more money did not invalidate one's opinions. Graham concluded by saying: 'I love Ed, and I’m grateful for everything we’ve shared. That hasn’t changed. But I can’t put these words out into the world and carry on with this tour as things stand. Macklemore claimed that his long-term friend Sheeran had told him that Kraft personally called the singer to inform him that the rapper would not be allowed to perform at Gillette Stadium Macklemore released a statement on Monday after he was dropped over backlash sparked by the 'Free Palestine' speech. Responding with an Instagram statement after it was confirmed that he will not be the opening act for the remainder of the LOOP Tour, the rapper claimed Sheeran told him Robert Kraft orchestrated the move after 'rallying stadium owners and issuing an ultimatum'. The 43-year-old rapper, whose real name is Benjamin Hammond Haggerty, was due to open for Sheeran at eight of the remaining ten shows on his tour in America, which continues Friday in Philadelphia. Macklemore had faced a boycott petition to remove him from the tour after he delivered a politically motivated speech in support of Palestinians in Gaza and the West Bank during his performances at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey on September 4 and 5. In a statement on Instagram, Macklemore claimed that his long-term friend Sheeran had told him that Kraft personally called the singer to inform him that the rapper would not be allowed to perform at Gillette Stadium in Massachusetts. Kraft is the billionaire CEO of the Kraft Group, which owns the Gillette stadium along with NFL team the New England Patriots. He wrote that 'Ed also told me that Kraft had rallied some of the other stadium owners and collectively they gave him an ultimatum: if Macklemore stays on the tour, you will not be allowed to play in our venues.' In his 1,000 word statement the rapper went on to say that he did not blame Sheeran personally, writing that his friend was put in a 'f***ed up position'. 'He told me that the words ‘Free Palestine’ and the image of the Palestinian flag were hurtful to a lot of people he spoke with,' Macklemore continued. 'I told him that if those words were more offensive than tens of thousands of Palestinian children being killed by Israel, then there was a fundamental disconnect between where those people stood and where I stood. But he couldn’t get past his public facing, "I don’t take sides." And I get why people don’t.' 'Taking a side can cost you,' Macklemore added. 'Money, brand deals, sponsorships, festivals, private shows, relationships and access. I've lost all of those things. But there is no neutral position between the oppressor and the oppressed.' He noted that the pair had had 'some difficult conversations this past week' and he hoped that 'this isn't the end of my relationship with Ed. Thirteen years of friendship doesn't disappear because of one painful disagreement.' Kraft has defended his decision to block Macklemore from performing, saying the rapper 'crossed a line'. 'Gillette Stadium has a longstanding commitment to providing a welcoming environment for all guests and ensuring that events held at our venue do not provide a platform for hate speech,' Kraft said in a statement. Macklemore stated that every human being deserves the same freedom, and told the crowd he wanted people in Gaza and the occupied West Bank to know they had not been forgotten.
