Flydubai hijacker 'was suicidal' and 'had undergone Islamist radical indoctrination', Benjamin Netanyahu says

Article text

The co-pilot of a passenger jet who stabbed the captain in an apparent attempt to crash the plane as it flew to Israel had undergone radical Islamist indoctrination and was clearly suicidal, Israel's prime minister said Thursday. Disaster was averted on Wednesday's flydubai flight FZ1073 from Dubai to Tel Aviv after the wounded captain managed to unlock the cockpit door, allowing passengers to subdue the attacker. A reserve crew that had been aboard landed the plane, which had more than 170 people on board, at Tabuk airport in Saudi Arabia. 'We know that he underwent Islamist radical indoctrination. That appears to be something we are gathering from his personal information,' Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said in an interview with Fox News. Two Israeli security officials said their initial assessment is that the co-pilot, identified by as an Omani national, was likely acting alone, though the investigation was still ongoing. The flight plunged more than 14,000ft in less than 30 seconds during the incident as it flew over Saudi Arabia on the journey from the United Arab Emirates to Israel, according to the flight-tracking website Flightradar24. Musaev Shota, an Israeli doctor who was on board, described the horrific scene following the stabbing. Speaking to CNN, Shota said the pilot was covered in cuts and in terrible shape, adding: 'His head and his hands were practically sliced open, almost severed in two.' The man who stabbed his co-pilot had undergone radical Islamist indoctrination and was clearly suicidal, Israel's prime minister said Netanyahu said it was clear that the co-pilot who carried out the stabbing was suicidal. 'It looks very likely that he was going to down the plane,' Netanyahu said. Coupled with 'the Islamist tendencies, that radicalisation that are found in his background, I think that's... a pretty good clue,' he said, without elaborating. 'But again, I don't make final conclusions yet. But that's the indications we have now,' he added. The United Arab Emirates has started investigations seeking to establish the circumstances and motives and determine 'whether the incident was linked to any terrorist activity or purpose, or involved prior planning or direction', the UAE state news agency reported on Thursday. Israel's envoy to the UAE, Yossi Shelley, earlier said it was too early to draw conclusions and no motive had been determined. Another Israeli official said Israel was not receiving all the information it sought from the Saudis and that Israeli intelligence services, Mossad and Shin Bet, were investigating the incident as well. Israeli officials have identified the attacker as an Omani national. Omani authorities have not commented. In a separate interview with Fox News, Netanyahu said the attacker was being investigated in Saudi Arabia and he believed that he would be taken to the UAE. Asked if there was any indication of Iranian involvement, Netanyahu said it was too early to say. 'We'll get to the root of this attacker, whether he had accomplices, whether Iran was behind it. I think we'll find out very soon,' Netanyahu said. Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz said on Wednesday that Israel's preliminary information indicated that the co-pilot aimed to crash the plane, describing him as a terrorist, though Netanyahu only described it as 'a serious security incident'. The incident heightened security fears in Israel weeks before Israelis vote in the first election since the Hamas-led October 7, 2023, attacks, with Israel's security figuring prominently in the campaigning. The US and Israel launched strikes against Iran in February, in a conflict that quickly spread to the Gulf and Lebanon, where Israel has been fighting Iran-backed militant group Hezbollah. Netanyahu and Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi have hailed the flight FZ1073 captain and Indian national Smit Machchhar as a hero. The Indian ambassador to United Arab Emirates met with the wounded captain from the flydubai flight on Thursday, with the embassy saying he was in 'good health and recovering well'. Israel has also lauded the passengers who subdued the attacker. Israeli President Isaac Herzog said Thursday he would present 'heroism' awards to five of the Israelis who helped overpower the knifeman and stop him from crashing the plane. 'You put yourselves at risk to save others and prevented a terrible disaster. Today, the whole world saw the beautiful and courageous face of Israeli society,' Herzog, who holds a largely ceremonial role, said in a statement. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu meets Yaniv, who broke into the cockpit, at Ben Gurion Airport in Tel Aviv 'I am proud of you, and I thank you on behalf of the State of Israel.' Yaniv Hayoun, one of the men who incapacitated the attacker, described running to the cockpit as the plane nosedived. 'We pulled the assailant out, I pulled him out. After I pulled him out we took control of the plane, I pulled up the yoke (steering control of the plane), I straightened the plane out a bit,' he said, talking to reporters upon his return to Israel on Wednesday. Speaking to the Daily Mail, Hayoun said he knew what to do from watching plane documentaries. 'I leaned over the seat, reached forward with my hands and pulled the controls toward me because the aircraft was pointing downward. I pulled the controls back toward me. It was only for a few seconds.' 'There's a programme called Air Crash Investigation…The images are frightening and they make my wife afraid of flying, so I watch it secretly when she isn't watching with me. 'I saw on it that when an aircraft is going downward, you have to bring the controls back — pull them backward — to bring the nose level. It really was only for a few seconds. It wasn't as though I flew the plane.'
