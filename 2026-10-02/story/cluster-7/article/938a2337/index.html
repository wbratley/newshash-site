FlyDubai FZ1073 latest: Netanyahu says hijack pilot 'used axe' in cockpit attack The pilot who allegedly stabbed another pilot and tried to take over FlyDubai flight FZ1073 on Wednesday may have used an 'axe' in the attack, Israel's Benjamin Netanyahu claimed The co-pilot who allegedly stabbed the captain of FlyDubai flight FZ1073 in an apparent attempt to crash the plane may have used an "axe" in the attack, according to Israel's prime minister. Benjamin Netanyahu told CBS News yesterday: "As they approached Israel, one of the co-pilots stabbed or hit with an axe the other pilot, an Indian pilot. And the pilot that was hit, bleeding profusely, fighting for his life, managed to unlock the pilot's door." Airlines are generally required to carry crash axes in the cockpit for use in an emergency to break open a window to escape or break open a panel to access a fire. The Israeli Prime Minister's comments come as Saudi officials investigating the horror incident said initial findings show the co-pilot "assaulted the captain". Donald Trump has said he believed Iran may be linked to the co-pilot but a senior Israeli official said this had, so far, been ruled out. The flight issued an emergency hijacking alert yesterday morning, around two and a half hours into its flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv. The injured pilot - identified as Indian national Capt Smit Machchhar - and a number of passengers bravely fought back control and regained control of the plane, which then made an emergency landing in Saudi Arabia. One of the passengers who helped was Yaniv Hayoun, who Netanyahu described as a "great hero". Mr Hayoun said: "I grabbed the attacker, put him in a chokehold, and pulled him - first and foremost - outside." He then said he helped pull the aircraft's controls. Netanyahu meets passengers who helped save FlyDubai flight Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has met passengers who helped bring the FlyDubai emergency under control, praising their actions as an example of “Israeli heroism”. Netanyahu met Yaniv Hayun, Or Pardilov, Dr Shota Musayev and his wife Sigalit at his office after hearing their account of the struggle onboard the flight from Dubai. The group told him they had restrained the pilot involved in the attack using a headphone cable. Netanyahu told the passengers they had prevented a major disaster and saved many lives, adding that they would light a torch of civic heroism on Israel’s Independence Day. He also urged Israelis to remain vigilant, saying the country was in a “very sensitive time” and warning of continued threats. Machchhar appears to have been transferred from Saudi Arabia to the UAE for further medical care following the emergency landing in Tabuk. Mittal said it was “reassuring” to meet the captain and thanked the UAE government for the “support and medical care” being provided to him. The plane rapidly lost altitude, dropping from 33,000ft to 17,000ft in about a minute. It then circled close to the Jordanian border before landing in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia, about an hour later. Israeli intelligence services are investigating what happened and are working with other agencies, the envoy added. Here's what we don't yet know about the incident on the FlyDubai pilot We are still waiting for more information on a number of key details surrounding the attack on yesterday's FlyDubai flight FZ1073. The following details remain unclear: - The identity of the attacker, although Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has said he is an Omani national - The motive behind the attack - The extent of the injuries sustained by the two pilots, although the Indian embassy in Saudi Arabia yesterday said Capt Smit Machchhar, who was stabbed by a fellow pilot, is in a "stable" condition in hospital The UAE prosecutor general has ordered an investigation into the incident, the state news agency reported. The investigation will examine the circumstances and motives behind the attack, including whether it involved prior planning or direction. UAE authorities have jurisdiction over the incident because the aircraft is registered in the UAE and operates under its flag, the statement said, adding that UAE law "applies to crimes committed on board even if they occur outside the country’s territory". Here's what we know so far about the incident on yesterday's FlyDubai flight - A pilot stabbed another pilot, captain Smit Machchhar, on board FlyDubai flight FZ1073 as it was carrying 174 people from the UAE to Israel on Wednesday morning - Passengers rushed into the cockpit and wrestled with the attacker as the plane descended rapidly, according to witnesses and Israel’s government - About two and a half hours into the flight from Dubai, the flight descended from 33,000ft to 17,000ft in about a minute - "We’re going to die," passenger Naama Bershadski recalled thinking as she closed her eyes and prayed - The plane made an emergency landing in Tabuk City, Saudi Arabia - The shaken passengers from the flight landed in Israel on Wednesday evening on a replacement flight - Israel’s emergency services said they treated three passengers with minor injuries - It is not clear what motivated the attack, but Israeli Defence Minister Israel Katz called the incident a thwarted "terror attack" - Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said in a CNN interview that the attacker was an Omani national and was being interrogated in Saudi Arabia, where he was arrested - Aviation experts were surprised the plane did not crash after such an extreme descent, which left most of the rudder ripped from the tail, The Associated Press reports Eerie remarks made by FlyDubai pilot a year before incident Capt Smit Machchhar, who was stabbed on Wednesday's FlyDubai flight, made eerie remarks about cabin crew being able to "save your life" in a podcast recorded almost a year before the incident. In a podcast episode uploaded to YouTube on October 17, 2025, Capt Machchhar said he believed people often did not treat cabin crew properly. The captain told host Zalak Mehta on the Bona-Fide Banter podcast: "When we are up there and if something happens, who's going to save you? You'd be lucky enough to have a doctor on board, but then if not, then they [cabin crew] are trained ... they can save your life, you know?" He described the public's "outlook" towards cabin crew as "unfortunate", adding: "When things are going wrong, you need them."
