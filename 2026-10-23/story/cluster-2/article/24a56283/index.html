Storm Austen closes 66 UK schools as 'danger to life' warning issued

Article text

Storm Austen closes 66 UK schools as 'danger to life' warning issued A number of schools will not open as usual on Friday as the UK is expected to be battered by strong winds with several 'danger to life' weather warnings issued by the Met Office Schools in England are planning to close or open late today as Storm Austen has triggered a "danger to life" warning. The South West has been issued an amber weather warning from 1am until 10am on Friday, impacting the whole of Cornwall and parts of Devon near the border, including Plymouth. Storm Austen, the first named storm of the season, could bring gusts of up to 80mph to parts of south-west England, with 60-70mph winds expected widely in the region. A yellow wind warning is also in place for further areas as west to northwesterly winds will spread across southern parts of England and Wales, starting on Thursday evening. As a result, several schools have already planned to close or open late on Friday as a result of the stormy conditions. The majority of schools facing closures are in Cornwall where the weather conditions are expected to be the worse. The number of schools with impacted opening times or remaining closed altogether currently sits at 66, however this may change as weather conditions could worsen. Brits are also being warned of delays and cancellations to road, rail, air and ferry services, possible power cuts and building damage, while flying debris and large waves could pose a danger to life. The storm will “probably” bring some damage to buildings, such as tiles blown from roofs, the Met Office said. In coastal areas, large waves could pose a danger to life, as well as beach debris being thrown onto coastal roads, sea fronts and properties. Mobile phone coverage and the power supply could also be affected, the Met Office said. Garth Smith, head of resilience at the British Red Cross, has encouraged the public to take steps to protect themselves, their families and their homes. He said: “We have practical advice that can make a real difference – from checking in with people who may be more vulnerable, securing loose items in your garden to checking windows and doors, and making an evacuation plan for your household, including pets. “We also urge people to keep an eye on local weather reports and guidance from local authorities.” Full list of schools facing closures or time changes: -
