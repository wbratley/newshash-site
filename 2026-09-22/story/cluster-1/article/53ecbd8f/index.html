Royal family latest: Prince Harry ‘in talks to make documentary about Princess Diana’ after Earl Spencer’s memoir Princess Diana’s brother has insisted he is ‘telling the truth’ about his bombshell allegations - Bookmark - CommentsGo to comments Prince Harry is in talks to make a documentary about his mother Princess Diana, according to a report, as the fallout over Earl Spencer’s bombshell memoir continues. The Duke of Sussex wants to make a documentary to mark the 30th anniversary of her death and is “considering the most meaningful way to honour her legacy during this important anniversary year”, a source close to Prince Harry told The Telegraph. The documentary would be produced by Archewell Productions, the Sussexes’ production company, and would likely be bought by Netflix due to a ‘first-look’ deal between the companies, the outlet reported. “The Duke has discussed a number of possible projects to mark the 30th anniversary of his mother Diana, Princess of Wales’s passing,” the source said, adding that “nothing has been finalised or confirmed”. It comes after Earl Spencer has claimed he has “really nothing to do” with King Charles anymore ahead of the release of his memoir Swan Song which hits shelves on Tuesday. Spencer claimed Charles said “we’ll forget her soon enough” after Diana died, and “sounded giddily elated – like a lottery winner” after her death in 1997. Buckingham Palace responded by suggesting that “fraternal grief can cloud reason”. If Prince Harry wants police protection, let him have it Harry ‘completely fine’ with Diana memoir, Earl Spencer says The Duke of Sussex is “completely fine” with a new memoir chronicling the life of his mother, Diana, Princess of Wales, his uncle Earl Spencer has said. The earl has made a series of claims about the King in Swan Song: Diana, My Sister – including that Charles said “we’ll forget her soon enough” after she died – which has prompted a war of words between him and Buckingham Palace. A palace spokesman said last week that the King is “mindful that the pain of fraternal grief can cloud reason, affect judgment and colour memory in ways others do not recognise”. Asked about telling Harry and the Prince of Wales about the book, the earl told People magazine: “I can say that Harry is completely fine about it. “He said, I hope there’s nothing embarrassing about him in it. I said, ‘There isn’t’. “I think they were actually worried where I was going up to with the book. “It pretty much ends in 1997 with Diana’s death, and there’s a tiny postscript by the grave. “So I think there was this, ‘Gosh, what’s he written about?’ And I think it’s natural that people so in the public eye are concerned how they will come across in the book. “But I could reassure both of them that they’re of course in the book, but really as examples of two little boys who had the most exceptional mother, I’m not in any way compromising their privacy at all.” What is Earl Spencer's relationship with William and Harry? Earl Spencer has remained outwardly close with Prince Harry over the years amid the Duke of Sussex’s strained relations with the Windsors, while his relationship with his nephew Prince William remains a lot more private. The earl said in an interview with the BBC that he did not take sides in the row between the Prince of Wales and the Duke of Sussex. He told the BBC: “I’m on my deceased sister’s sons’ side, both of them. “And if either of them wanted anything then I’d be equally there for them. “And I never get involved in one camp or the other, except as a benign figure I hope.” Earl Spencer said there are a “lot of reasons on both sides” why the brothers’ relationship had “got to this stage”. He added: “I hope one day it’s resolved, but I don’t know all – I certainly don’t know what’s been done in the finer detail and so I would feel intrusive if I got more involved than to say of course a mother would want her two sons to be happy together and love each other.” In full: Prince Harry fights back tears as he praises parents caring for seriously ill children The Duke of Sussex became emotional as he praised "mums and dads" looking after seriously ill children during the WellChild awards.
