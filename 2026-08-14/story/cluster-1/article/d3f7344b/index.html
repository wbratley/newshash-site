Brits swelter in hottest day of the year so far as temperatures hit 38.1C: London bus drivers go on strike, wildfires burn down homes and amber 'extreme heat' warning extended

Article text

- READ MORE: Proof that Britain has beaten 'record-breaking' summer of 1976 - See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source Britain has experienced its hottest day of the year so far and the fifth warmest on record as temperatures soared to 38.1C in Kew Gardens. It is the second time the UK has reached 38C this year – with the first being in Lingwood, Norfolk, when the mercury reached 38.0C on June 26. The only other time temperatures have reached 38C twice in a year was in 2022 when the UK recorded its hottest ever temperatures, the Met Office said. Bus drivers are going on strike due to the heat and the Met Office has extended its rare 15-hour amber 'extreme heat' warning, which came into force at 9am this morning. The warning initially covered parts of central and southern England, but at 10am it was extended further west - where the highest temperatures are now expected. The whole of England is separately under a UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) amber heat health alert until tomorrow night, which warns of a 'rise in deaths'. Today is the ninth day of 2026 when temperatures have hit at least 35C; the fourth day of reaching 36C; and the third day of getting to 37C – all three being new records. The Unite union, which represents bus drivers, has said it is too hot too work and have claimed Arriva North London is not taking the issue seriously. More than 1,500 bus drivers will be taking industrial action tomorrow and multiple other days. Chiltern Railways and East Midlands Railway both told passengers to 'only travel if essential' with the UK's sixth heatwave of the year expected to cause delays. Reduced services were also confirmed by c2c, CrossCountry, Great Western Railway, Northern, Transport for Wales and West Midlands Railway as temperatures soar. Meanwhile a devastating wildfire ripped through six bungalows in Newbridge in Caerphilly, South Wales - with 20 residents evacuated to a nearby leisure centre. It comes as fire chiefs said 1,017 wildfires have been recorded in England and Wales so far in 2026, the same as the record-breaking total for the whole of last year. Prime Minister Andy Burnham chaired a meeting of the Government's emergency Cobra committee yesterday in response to the ongoing hot and dry weather. Met Office forecaster Alex Burkill said: 'Temperatures could reach highs of around 36C, 37C, possibly 38C. 'Now when you get these sorts of high temperatures, well that's when we could see impacts to our infrastructure, our railway lines, our roads, they are not designed for this sort of heat. 'And more people are likely to be impacted by how hot it is with regards to their health.' He added: 'It could be the hottest day of the year so far if we get above 38C, and if we get above 38.5C, well it could be the hottest August day on record, but that's actually pretty unlikely.' Meanwhile a yellow warning has been issued for thunderstorms in Northern Ireland, which is in place until 6am tomorrow. Confirming the bus strike today, Unite general secretary Sharon Graham said: 'The heatwave this week has caused even more problems for our members at Arriva North London, who have once again faced doing a demanding job in unbearable conditions. No worker should be made ill by their job. 'This problem is raised year after year and as the summers get hotter it can no longer be swept under the carpet. Arriva can afford to act on this serious issue, which puts both drivers and the general public at risk, and it must make this a priority.' Mo Wasim, managing director of Arriva London, said: 'Extreme heat is creating challenges across many sectors, including London's bus network. 'The wellbeing of our colleagues and customers is our top priority, and we are working closely with Transport for London to ensure appropriate support is in place. 'We have already introduced additional support for drivers during hot weather, including regular breaks and welfare checks - and are continuing to invest in long-term improvements, including upgraded air conditioning systems and new buses coming into our fleet. 'These upgrades are complex and being led by TfL as part of its extreme heat taskforce. We urge Unite to come back to the negotiating table and engage with us constructively so we can avoid further disruption.' Drivers are also being warned that the extreme heat is putting the UK's already deteriorating roads under strain. The AA said air temperatures in the low-to-mid 30Cs could translate into much higher road surface temperatures as the dark asphalt absorbs and stores heat, causing some roads to soften and leading highways authorities to go out with gritters to stabilise the surface. Edmund King, AA president, said: 'In prolonged hot weather, some road surfaces can soften, become sticky and, in places, start to rut under heavy traffic. The National Fire Chiefs Council (NFCC) confirmed England and Wales have already seen as many wildfires this year as in the whole of 2025. Crews from Hampshire and Isle of Wight Fire and Rescue Service have continued battling a blaze which hit protected heathland in the New Forest. The fire service said crews are expected to spend at least another three days at the scene, where a van caught fire on the A31 near Ringwood on Sunday. In a statement this morning, the service said: 'Once again firefighters have been working through the night at the scene of the New Forest wildfire.' Along with colleagues from other fire services, crews have been using techniques including mulching, tree felling and 'backburning', controlled burning to create firebreaks, the statement said. 'Our teams continue to make good progress, but we still expect to be on the scene for the next few days, with the A31 remaining closed,' the service said. Most major UK supermarkets have stopped sales of disposable barbecues in recent weeks in line with voluntary guidance from the NFCC. Mr Burnham has urged all shops to stop selling them and indicated an outright ban could be considered in future summers. Met Office satellite views show the transformed landscape from May (left) to Monday (right) The Prime Minister also said there had been a 'significant number of excess deaths from heat this summer already' and urged the public to follow health advice, including checking in on elderly friends and relatives. Baroness Smith said today the Government is doing more to stop wildfires than just requesting shops to stop selling disposable barbecues. Speaking to LBC, the skills minister insisted 'quite a lot more' came out of yesterday's Cobra meeting. She said: '(There is) additional funding for firefighters with equipment and training to help with some of the wildfires that we've seen. We've already invested in new reservoirs, for example, to help to protect our water supply.' The minister added that the Government was investing in nine new reservoirs and is working with farmers to help with irrigation issues. She continued: 'I think it is fair enough for the Prime Minister to highlight that all of us have got a responsibility in these really sort of quite difficult, hot times to look after our neighbours, to make sure that you know, we're keeping our homes cool, and there's advice about how to do that, and frankly, not having fires or using disposable barbecues where that might be setting fire to things.' NFCC chairman Phil Garrigan said: 'Most wildfires in the UK are caused because of human behaviours, careless, accidental or unfortunately intentional acts. 'But as climate change brings more frequent periods of hot, dry weather, the conditions for wildfires are becoming more dangerous, increasing their scale and intensity. 'Hot, dry weather and drought are turning parts of the UK into tinderbox landscapes, allowing fires to spread faster, burn longer and place greater pressure on fire and rescue services. 'Wildfires are becoming part of our new reality and we need to be honest about both the causes and the conditions that are driving them. 'While over 1,000 wildfires have been recorded so far this year, the incident numbers alone do not tell the full story. 'They do not capture the scale, duration and complexity of these incidents, or the impact they have on communities, the environment and fire and rescue services. 'As their impact grows, we must work with all stakeholders to ensure we have the right capabilities needed to protect our communities and to respond effectively when wildfires occur.' Major wildfires have hit areas such as the Pennines, the Cairngorms in Scotland, Llanwonno in the Rhondda Valleys, South Wales and Dunwich Heath in Suffolk. Two record-breaking heatwaves in May and June led to more than 2,800 excess deaths. The Royal College of Nursing has warned that nurses are collapsing at work and are being forced to care for patients in temperatures in excess of 30C. Others have reported seizures and 'dripping with sweat from head to toe', and nurses have said patients are being cared for in hospitals which have been described as 'unbearably hot' and 'not fit for humans'. On the trains, ticket restrictions were eased by some operators who told passengers to check for travel updates before setting off and carry water to stay hydrated. Heat-related issues affecting the railway can include buckled rails, sagging overhead electric wires and lineside fires. Steel rails can hit 50C in an air temperature of 30C. Chiltern Railways said temperatures are expected to reach highs of 38C (100F) on parts of its network. It will operate a reduced timetable between 11am and 8pm. Among the changes are: - Off-peak services between London Marylebone and Birmingham Moor Street will operate hourly instead of half-hourly. - Some services between London Marylebone and Oxford will be cancelled. - No Chiltern Railways services will operate from Birmingham Snow Hill from 10.15am until the end of the day. Michael Stewart, commercial and customer strategy director at Chiltern Railways, said: 'We are asking customers to travel only if essential on Thursday because of the exceptionally high temperatures forecast across our network. Philippa Cresswell from East Midlands Railway said: 'The hot weather means we'll need to make some temporary changes to our services to keep the railway operating safely. 'If you do need to travel and the journey is essential, we'd recommend travelling before midday and checking your journey before setting off. 'We appreciate these changes may inconvenience some customers and we'd like to thank everyone for their patience and understanding while we work to keep people moving safely.' It told passengers: 'High temperatures increase the risk of issues on the railway and with the reliability of our trains, which means we have to make adjustments to our usual timetable to keep trains running.' Some 45million people across England are now in drought-stricken areas, and 27million customers are under hosepipe bans as rivers and reservoirs run low. Parts of southern England including Hampshire, the Isle of Wight and West Sussex have now gone more than 60 days without rainfall, and there are no downpours in the immediate forecast. Nearly three-quarters of England and the whole of Wales are in drought, after a record dry July and very dry start to August has combined with high temperatures to put pressure on water resources. Southern Water has applied for a rare non-essential use ban drought order, which, if imposed, would mean businesses must stop using water for activities such as cleaning cars and filling non-domestic swimming pools. Record-breaking heatwaves in May and June led to the deaths of more than 2,800 people, while parts of the country were hit with further heatwaves last month as England and Wales endured their driest July since records began in 1836. A fifth heatwave of the year was declared early last week – and the Met Office has said summer 2026 is on course to be the hottest on record.
