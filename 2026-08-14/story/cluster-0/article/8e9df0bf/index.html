Nigel Farage says he has won the Clacton by-election victory after beating Count Binface with 'far more votes' than when he secured the seat - but stays away from the count after 'threat'

- See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source Nigel Farage has claimed an 'overwhelming victory' in the Clacton by-election with 'far more votes' than he won at the 2024 General Election, but says he will not attend the count after an alleged 'credible' security threat. The Reform UK leader said Essex Police had warned him of an 'organised campaign to disrupt and degrade the result in a few hours' time', making it unsafe for him to attend the declaration at Clacton Leisure Centre this morning. 'I’m not scared of these people, far from it. But I’m damned if I’m going to stand on a stage, having won a resounding victory, a ringing endorsement of all that I’ve tried to fight for, and you’ve tried to fight for, and be demeaned and humiliated by nobodies, and I will not sadly be attending the count,' he told supporters at a 'Farage Fest' party in the constituency. He added: 'Once again it shows you the sheer intolerance, the sheer loss of fundamental democratic principles that those that went before us, fought in world wars, to fight for and preserve.' Mr Farage is still waiting to discover whether he has beaten his rivals, including Count Binface, to retain the seaside seat he won at the 2024 General Election - but he said he has won. 'The verdict is now in, and it is a convincing, overwhelming win with more votes than we got in the General Election of 2024. Today the voters in Clacton have stuck up two fingers to the entire political establishment.' However, Essex Constabulary then denied that it had told any of the 34 candidates that they should not attend Clacton Leisure Centre, insisting security at the venue was 'robust'. The final result is expected at around 6am. Speaking to GB News at a party-organised event in Essex, called 'Farage Fest', Mr Farage was asked whether he had won, and said: 'Yeah, very convincingly, I've managed to beat the establishment candidate.' He added: 'Look, this is an overwhelming endorsement of me. I have had enough of being judged by mainstream media, being called a criminal. 'Do you know, if I made one sin, if I haven't filled in a piece of paper, I've broken no law, I've broken no tax law, I've taken no public money, I don't even own a motorhome. 'Maybe I haven't filled in a piece of paper. I'm confident that I'm right, we'll see where we go. I put this to the people of Clacton, said 'you decide my future', and tonight they will.' Nigel Farage says he has won the Clacton by-election with 'far more votes' than he secured when taking the seat in the 2024 general election, hours before the final result is in Watch Live as the ballots are counted: Count Binface speaks to Sky News as he makes appearance at the Clacton by-election polling station The verdict on whether Mr Farage's political gamble has paid off is not expected until after sunrise on Friday, due to the record 34 candidates in the contest. The first ballot boxes began arriving at the Essex counting centre in the minutes after polls closed at 10pm. Ian Davidson, acting returning officer, said turnout was 44.37 per cent - compared to a 58.7 per cent turnout when Farage was first elected in 2024. It is the largest number ever for a Westminster by-election and has caused logistical problems for the vote count - not least because the ballot papers are almost a metre long. The 62-year-old declared he was forcing a 'people versus the Establishment' contest, but the other political parties boycotted the race as they dismissed it as a 'stunt'. Actor-turned-politician Laurence Fox, three contenders from the Official Monster Raving Loony Party and an array of independents also stood in the constituency. 'Three guesses who I voted for!', he posted on social media shortly after he filled in his ballot paper. The success of the Reform leader's campaign is likely to be judged in comparison with the 46.2 per cent of the vote - and a majority of 8,405 over his Conservative rival - that he won in Clacton in 2024. He will be expected to comfortably beat that result in the by-election, due to the absence of any other main political parties. The 90cm-long ballot paper of all 34 by-election candidates has caused logistical problems for Tendring District Council and returning officer Ian Davidson. The size of the ballot paper means larger tables than usual are being required at the count and the result will be announced on the floor of Clacton Leisure Centre rather than on a stage due to the number of candidates who would have to fit on it. Photographers and TV cameras covering the count were advised by officials they will need a wide-angle lens to fit all the candidates into a single shot. Before quitting the House of Commons, Mr Farage was being investigated by a parliamentary watchdog over a £5million gift from cryptocurrency tycoon Christopher Harborne, which he received before he won his Clacton seat in 2024. Under Commons rules, new MPs are required to register any gifts worth more than £300 they received in the previous 12 months, except where the gift 'could not be reasonably thought by others' to relate to their political activities. That investigation was paused when Mr Farage resigned his seat but will resume if he wins the by-election - as is widely expected by bookmakers who have made him the firm favourite to win the contest. The Reform leader could also face an investigation over support provided by George Cottrell, a convicted criminal and long-term ally. Mr Cottrell reportedly recruited and paid three staff to work on Mr Farage's social media before the general election, and has continued to allow him to use a five-storey Georgian property he rented near Buckingham Palace. He continued: 'At the beginning of summer we called this by-election to give the people of Clacton the chance to have their say on the endless political attacks on me, my family and those around me. 'It's given us the opportunity to knock on tens of thousands of doors, making the affirmative case for what I've done for the area, and what Reform will do for Britain.' He added 'it's not just about me, it's about the people versus the establishment'. The 34 candidates standing in Clacton marks a new high for the most candidates standing in a single seat. The previous record was 26, set at the Haltemprice and Howden by-election in July 2008, which was triggered by Tory David Davis in his own seat and was similarly ignored by the main Westminster parties.
