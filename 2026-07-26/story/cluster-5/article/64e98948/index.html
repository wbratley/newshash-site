First wildfire death in Spain as blazes ravage Europe - with 270,000 people forced from their homes and France sends in the military

Article text

First wildfire death in Spain as blazes ravage Europe - with 270,000 people forced from their homes and France sends in the military - See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source A man has been the first victim to die from the wildfires ravaging Spain and France as more than 270,000 people have been forced to flee their homes. The man was found in a car in a ravine in the Spanish town of Manises near Valencia following a forest fire in the area, according to the local fire brigade. Spain's capital is being deluged in smoke and ash by huge wildfires to the west, which has led the country to declare a national emergency and the Prime Minister to warn of 'complex hours ahead'. As two blazes in Spain risk merging amid tricky firefighting conditions today, France has called in the military. France is set to roll out 'an unprecedentedly large-scale response' as raging infernos across the southwestern region have even seen some evacuate on boats. Some 500 French military personnel had been deployed yesterday to help combat the blazes and now a further 1,500 will be sent in tonight. The move comes after a 'crisis meeting', which was held at the armed forces headquarters in Paris earlier today. French Interior Minister Laurent Nunez said a hulking A400M military transport aircraft will join 18 other water- and retardant-dropping planes and helicopters waging an expected 'long and very difficult' battle against the blaze in the Gironde. 'I am in touch with my French and Spanish counterparts about the situation. 'British nationals should follow the advice of local authorities in the area, alongside FCDO travel advice.' With winds expected to shift in a west-to-east direction on Saturday afternoon, potentially driving the fire closer to Bordeaux, fire crews and authorities were digging trenches, using retardants and taking other steps to block it, Nunez said. He said the fire was about 30 kilometers west of the city and lost some of its intensity overnight. The government mobilised soldiers to join the battle and was rushing in 1.5 million face masks to protect against choking smoke. Authorities ordered people between the fire and Bordeaux to evacuate, including from parts of the city's western suburbs and other towns and villages. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu described the fires as unprecedented and said the Gironde blaze became so strong on Friday that it had begun generating its own winds. 'Our priority is clear: protecting human lives,' he posted on X, adding that evacuation orders 'must be respected without delay.' He said firefighting teams were being reinforced on Saturday with the addition of hundreds of soldiers. In tests, the French military transport aircraft carried and dropped 20 tons of water, around three times the capacity of widely used Canadair water-bombing planes. Some 197,000 people have been evacuated from south-west France amid a blaze approaching Bordeaux, with the 'unprecedented' blaze leading to a rare pyrocumulonimbus - a thunderstorm caused by a large fire - on Friday, which has never been seen in the country before. While in Spain, the figure has hit at least 70,000 with another 20,000 told to remain indoors. A blaze nearing the capital is 'at its peak' and is at risk of merging with a second today, something government official Nicanor Sen Velez said would be 'very bad news'. Meanwhile Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez said the priority was 'to save lives' as he visited the fire zone. The country has declared a national emergency and Sanchez warned of 'complex hours ahead'. The municipality of Villaviciosa has opened its public facilities to shelter and assist hundreds of evacuees, with many bringing what little belongings they could grab and scooping up their pets. In Hall 3 of the Parc Des Expositions De Bordeaux in France, around 1,000 camp beds had been made available, all of which were snapped up by 4am this morning. At least 5,000 people were crammed into the stuffy hall by 8am, some of whom had arrived seven hours earlier. The virulence of the French and Spanish wildfires, fueled by high temperatures and the long-term effects of climate change, caught fire crews and authorities off guard. Firefighters in Spain said the blazes spread so fast and violently that they could not be tackled head on. In France, some people even fled by boat when flames swept through touristic towns on the Atlantic coast. 'We've never seen anything like this,' said Javier Organista, a resident of El Tiemblo, west of Madrid, where fire charred a famed chestnut grove. 'When we saw the flames coming into our town, surrounding us, leaving us with practically no way out, all the neighbors joined together to try to stop its advance... It's just really sad,' he said. A British restaurant owner in Bordeaux said the city is 'full of smoke and ash' and residents are wearing masks if they go outside. Lance Attwood, 58, who owns Sweeney Todd's in Bordeaux city centre, told the Press Association: 'It's not really conducive to coming out. 'The town is full of smoke and ash, so there's a big drop in business, people are just walking around town in masks. 'Our biggest concern is people's safety and the safety of the emergency services.' Mr Attwood, who has lived in Bordeaux for 25 years, said friends who had been evacuated were focusing on recovering sentimental belongings and finding alternative living arrangements. He encouraged people to donate dry food with a long shelf-life to local charities to help those affected. As the French fire arrives just 12 miles from Bordeaux, the European Union has been drafted in to help and is deploying seven planes to the two countries to help firefighting efforts. Tomorrow's final stage of the Tour de France has been shortened due to the fires, and will now only be 89km instead of the planned 133km. 'The fires that are hitting our country have reached a level that has never been seen before,' Lecornu wrote on X on Saturday. So far this month, temperatures have averaged 32.2 degrees Celsius (90 Fahrenheit) across the broader Aquitaine region, which is 7.3C (13.1F) above the normal high for July between 1961 and 1990.
