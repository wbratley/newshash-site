← Wildfires Force Mass Evacuations in France and Spain

Military sent in to tackle wildfires as 270,000 are forced from homes by blazes across Spain and France and smoke and ash reaches Madrid - with Spanish PM warning of 'complex hours ahead'

Article text

- See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source France called in military reinforcements on Saturday as some of the worst European wildfires on record rampaged out of control. More than 270,000 residents and tourists have been ordered to leave their homes amid apocalyptic scenes both across the Channel and in Spain, where a national emergency has been declared. Flames continued to rage just 12 miles west of Bordeaux in south-west France while a wildfire near the Madrid was said to be 'at its peak' on Saturday evening as thousands were told to stay indoors. On Saturday, as the mass evacuations continued, it was announced: - The wildfires have claimed their first Spanish victim, a man trapped in his car; - President Macron has mobilised 1,500 troops to south-west France - Emergency plans were being drawn up to evacuate the city of Bordeaux; - Today's final stage of the Tour de France in Paris will be shortened so that security staff can be re-deployed; - And closer to home, hundreds of firefighters were battling a blaze in Scotland's Cairngorms. The French fires, which began on Tuesday in a pine forest west of Bordeaux, have so far burned through 390 square miles of woodland, incinerating homes and holiday villas. Officials say strong winds and hot embers have made the situation worse, igniting fresh blazes several miles apart. The situation, they admit with understatement, is 'far from under control'. Nearly 200,000 people, including British tourists, have been told to leave their homes in the Gironde and Landes region, including the Lege-Cap-Ferret tourist destination sometimes known as the 'French Hamptons', a popular spot for the rich and famous. On Friday, the authorities ordered the evacuation of the whole of the Cap Ferret peninsula with hundreds escaping by boat. Many found safety in the picturesque town of Arcachon across the water. Residents and visitors were given just 30 minutes to pack. With no taxis available and the road 'absolutely jammed', the only route out involved paying 250 Euros and boarding a boat across the bay to Arcachon. 'By the skin of our teeth we got out just in time,' he said. Parisian Noémie Eisenchteter, a half-British marketing executive, was forced to evacuate a luxury villa after receiving text alerts from the emergency services in the early hours of Friday morning. Ms Eisenchteter, 25, a keen polo player, was only there for 48 hours before plumes of smoke from the forest fires plunged the sky above into darkness. 'We arrived on Cap Ferret on Wednesday evening and there was some smoke on the distance but we didn't think too much of it. 'But when we got up on Thursday the smell of smoke was really powerful, it made the house smell of barbecue, and the sky became really dark, blotting out the sun,' she told The Mail on Sunday. On Saturday night, flames were threatening Bordeaux itself, where – with smoke drifting across the city – officials said they were considering the possibility of a mass evacuation. Residents in the western suburbs have already left, ordered out in the early hours of Saturday morning. British Airways has begun contacting customers 'to provide assistance' in case they need to change their plans and is offering alternative routes and times. Martin and Nicola Smith, from Jersey, were staying with relatives at the Holiday Inn in Mérignac, just north of Bordeaux Airport when they got a 3.30am wake-up call by staff and told to leave. With smoke billowing across from fires on the Cap Ferret peninsula, the hotel would have to close. Mrs Smith, 49, said: 'It was a shock to the system and quite dramatic, but I'd been getting alerts on my phone all night.' The Bordeaux region's famous vineyards sit several miles to the north of the city along the river Garonne and were not thought to be in danger. Around 60,000 residents have been evacuated in Spain, where fires are closing in on Madrid and the historic city of Avila. Another 28,000 were told to stay at home. The authorities fear the Madrid fire will merge with a second major blaze – something they describe as 'very bad news'. On Saturday the Spanish Prime Minister warned of 'complex hours ahead'. Some residents were left terrified by the evacuation order, with one saying: 'If you don't run from the fire, it devours you'. European nations are rallying around with supplies of aircraft and specialist equipment. The EU itself has sent five planes and two helicopters to France and four planes to Spain. On Saturday saw the first mission by a French military transport plane deployed to fight the wildfires. The aircraft, equipped with a dispensing kit that can release 20 tons of water or fire retardant, flew over a forest in Lacanau in south-western France. Wildfires have hit much of Europe in recent weeks with Italy, Greece, Portugal and Germany affected as well as parts of Scotland and Wales. More than 500 firefighters were on Saturday tackling a ten-day blaze in the Cairngorms that has been declared a major incident. On Saturday the first minister of Scotland, John Swinney, called for military help from the British government. Fire crews were also battling a wildfire in the Rhinogydd Mountains near Harlech in North Wales. Meanwhile, although thousands of tourists have been affected, Éléonore Caroit, French minister for international partnerships, urged visitors not to change their travel plans. She told Radio 4's Today programme that while the fires have been worse than in 2022, most of the country had not been affected.
