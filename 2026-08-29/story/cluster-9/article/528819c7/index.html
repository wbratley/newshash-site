Haakon's new role follows the passing of his father - a cousin of King Charles III - who died today after a brief stay in hospital. Harald was being treated for haemolytic anaemia, a disorder that causes red blood cells to be destroyed at an abnormally fast rate. The new monarch was announced alongside a photo of Haakon, 53, the former Crown Prince, who is now officially His Majesty King Haakon VIII. He shares two children with his wife Mette-Marit, 22-year-old Princess Ingrid Alexandra and 20-year-old Prince Sverre Magnus. With Ingrid now second in line to the throne, she would be the first female monarch in Norway for more than 600 years if she were to become queen - the last being Queen Margaret I, who died in 1412. Mette-Marit, 53, faced intense scrutiny following the release of the Epstein Files, which revealed she stayed at paedophile Jeffrey Epstein's Palm Beach residence in 2013 – after he was convicted for soliciting sex from girls as young as 14. One email released suggested she was still in contact with the billionaire financier just months before his death. The new queen claimed this year that she had been misled by the late sex offender as she sought to contain the fallout. Fresh scandal also came in June when Mette-Marit's son, Marius Borg Høiby, was convicted on two counts of rape and jailed for four years following a six-week trial that laid bare allegations of violence, drug abuse and sexual misconduct. He is a great-grandson of Edward VII and his godparents included Queen Mary and her son George VI (grandmother and father respectively of the late Queen Elizabeth). His early life was marked by the vicissitudes of the Second World War. In April 1940, when Harald was just three, Nazi Germany invaded Norway. Harald and his two older sisters, Ragnhild and Astrid, were swept up by their mother and taken first to Sweden. Whilst Harald's father and grandfather – the reigning King Haakon VII – held out in Norway as their heroic countrymen battled the German invasion, they too finally fled in June and headed to London with the Norwegian government-in-exile. In August that year, Harald and his mother and sisters then went to the United States, where they spent the rest of the war, living in Washington. Then Crown Prince Olav and King Haakon – whose wife Queen Maud (sister of George V) had died in 1938 – were initially guests at Buckingham Palace. A young Princess Elizabeth at one point found them 'both snoring away' on the floor of the royal air raid shelter. 'Though we love having them,' she told her grandmother 'it is rather a bore never to be alone.' In June 1945, Prince Harald – then aged seven – returned with his family to Norway after Nazi Germany's defeat in the war. During five years of occupation, the royal palace had been turned into a prison, whilst ordinary Norwegians had been starved. The prince, then just 17, would go on to name his firstborn Märtha in honour of her memory. Three years later, King Haakon VII died at the age of 85, after nearly 52 years on the throne. Harald, now the heir, went on to study at the University of Oslo and also enrolled in the Norwegian Military Academy, graduating in 1959. It was at his graduation ball that he brought along the woman who would become his wife. Harald had met Sonja Haraldsen, the daughter of an Oslo draper, earlier that year. It was her status as a commoner that ruffled royal feathers. The pair initially kept their relationship a secret but when it did become public knowledge, the Palace repeatedly insisted that the pair would not marry. But when Harald threatened to remain a bachelor forever if he did not get his way, his father relented and let him wed the woman he loved. Their 1968 wedding at Oslo Cathedral came after both had studied in England. Whilst Sonja read English at Cambridge, Harald went to Oxford to study history, economics and politics at Balliol College. But sailing was his sport, and he went on to represent Norway at the Olympics in Tokyo in 1964, Mexico City in 1968 and Munich in 1972. His and Sonja's first child, Princess Märtha Louise, was born in 1971, with her brother, Crown Prince Haakon, arriving the following year. But Prince Haakon would have a long wait before he became first in line to the throne. His father did not become king until January 1991, when he was 53. Harald's closeness to King Charles was demonstrated five years later, when he went to Sandringham for a shooting weekend. The monarch was also at Charles's 50th birthday party in 1998, when 250 guests descended on Highgrove. In 2011, Harald was among the dozens of European royals who attended the wedding of the Prince and Princess of Wales and was a guest at the Queen's Diamond Jubilee Lunch the following year. Like some of his British cousins, King Harald's family have had their own controversies. Mette-Marit faced intense scrutiny when a tranche from the Epstein Files revealed she stayed at Epstein's Palm Beach mansion in 2013 after he had been convicted of soliciting sex from girls as young as 14. One email suggested she was still in contact with Epstein just months before his death. Mette-Marit, 53, who was diagnosed with pulmonary fibrosis in 2018, later issued a grovelling apology in which she claimed the emails 'do not represent the person who I want to be'. Harald defended his daughter-in-law during a public appearance that month in which he insisted: 'We must remember that the Crown Princess has not done anything illegal. 'Unlike some others in Norway, she broke the connection very early. So I think we should give her credit for that.' Harald's later life had been marked by a series of health problems that left him unable to fulfil his royal duties during certain periods. In February, the 89-year-old was rushed to hospital while spending time with his wife, Norwegian Queen Sonja, 89, in Tenerife. The monarch's condition at the time was said to have been 'satisfactory' after he received treatment for an infection and dehydration at Hospital Universitario Hospiten Sur.
