Pope Leo XIV's doctors say no further treatment needed after lung cancer surgery

Unbiased summary

Pope Leo XIV, 71, underwent surgery on 13 October to remove the lower lobe of his right lung after a nodule was found. The Vatican announced on Wednesday that final histological and molecular analyses confirmed lung adenocarcinoma that remained localised, and that a subsequent oncological assessment found no further treatment is necessary. The pope was discharged from Rome's Gemelli Hospital on Saturday, four days after the operation, and on Wednesday held his first weekly general audience in St Peter's Square since leaving hospital. He has been gradually resuming duties and has spoken by phone with Vladimir Putin and Volodymyr Zelensky. It remains unclear whether a planned trip to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru from 6-17 November will proceed.
