Covid inquiry finds care homes were 'afterthought' and Hancock's 'protective ring' claim 'unfounded'

Unbiased summary

The UK Covid-19 Inquiry's sixth report, chaired by Baroness Heather Hallett, found that the adult social care sector was treated as 'something of an afterthought' during the early pandemic. It concluded that government failures, including discharging hospital patients into care homes without testing or isolation guidance, inadequate PPE, and a focus on protecting the NHS, created an environment where the virus could 'spread like wildfire'. The report rejected Matt Hancock's May 2020 claim that a 'protective ring' had been thrown around care homes from the start as 'unfounded'. Between March 2020 and July 2022, over 43,000 care home residents died involving Covid-19. Bereaved families accused Hancock of telling a 'cruel lie'.
