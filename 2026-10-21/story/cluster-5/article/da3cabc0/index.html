Kate entertains Omani royals in a £260 midi dress alongside William and Charles after Harry spoke out to say he wants reconciliation with family

Article text

The Princess of Wales pulled off a quick outfit change between engagements on the first day of the Sultan of Oman's state visit - on the same day Prince Harry said he wants to reconcile with his family. Both times, she delivered a masterclass in diplomatic dressing as she joined the King, Queen, and the Prince of Wales in welcoming the Omani ruler to Windsor Castle today. For her first engagement, Prince William's wife repurposed a gorgeous emerald Alexander McQueen coat and a matching Juliette Botterill fascinator in an apparent nod to Oman's flag. It came just hours after her brother-in-law, the Duke of Sussex, made a reconciliation plea to his family as he said he 'wants it to work out', before adding that 'some people don't want it to work out'. The remarks were initially viewed as an apparent dig at William, but a spokesman for the Sussexes later insisted it was not aimed at his older brother. Kate, meanwhile, showed once again today she was well practised in the art of sartorial diplomacy; her outfit also included a sentimental piece of jewellery as the future Queen paid tribute to her late mother-in-law, Princess Diana. Glistening on the lapel of her coat was the 160-year-old diamond and emerald brooch that once belonged to the 'People's Princess' and was gifted to Kate by King Charles in 2022. Her accessories also included a black quilted Chanel bag, Manolo Blahnik pumps and dazzling earrings from Cassandra Goad worth £3,830. But the future Queen changed her look completely by the time she was pictured viewing an exhibition in the Castle's Green Room. Kate let her bronde tresses down and switched her black lace blouse for a £260 Norma Kamali midi dress in the same deep green shade as her Alexander McQueen coat. Kate opted for a £260 Norma Kamali midi dress for her second official engagement with the Sultan of Oman today She also took off her fascinator as her bronde tresses fell in glamorous, loose waves The brooch was first given as a wedding gift to Princess Alexandra of Denmark in 1863 by the Ladies of Bristol when she married Bertie, Prince of Wales (later King Edward VII), and the couple became King and Queen. Following Alexandra's death, the jewel was reportedly handed down to her daughter-in-law, Queen Mary and then onto the Queen Mother, who subsequently passed it on to Diana on her marriage to Charles in 1981. The heirloom piece comprises a cabochon teardrop emerald pendant dangling from an oval of diamonds with the three-feathered insignia of the Prince of Wales inside. Below the diamond-studded coronet is a banner bearing the words Ich Dien (I serve). It was fashioned into a necklace by the late Princess of Wales, who wore it to a glittering gala evening in 1986 with a pair of matching emerald earrings. Diana was on a state visit to Austria with Charles when she wore the dazzling jewel to the Burgtheater in Vienna. It will have been handed back to the Queen upon Diana's death in 1997 as the brooch is considered the property of the monarch. King Charles gifted it to Kate in 2022 as a way of honouring the women who have held the Princess of Wales title before her - including Diana. Earlier, Harry, who has not spoken to his brother William for four years, admitted in an interview with GQ that he is grieving his 'complicated relationships' with the King and the Prince of Wales. Asked directly whether he really wanted to heal the rift with his brother and father, Harry replied: 'Of course I do. With everything else that's going on in the world, the most important thing is to reconcile. That's the key point.' But in remarks widely interpreted as a dig at William, he added: 'Some people don't want it to work out. I want it to work out.' Hours after the interview was published, a Sussex spokesman insisted that the remark was not aimed at his older brother as speculation mounted over who Harry was referring to. He said: 'It was absolutely not intended as a swipe at his brother or any other member of his family. 'The comment was made in a general context and was not directed at his family in any way.' In the evening, His Majesty, accompanied by the Queen and Members of the Royal Family, will host a glittering State Banquet at Windsor Castle for the Sultan - the highlight of which will undoubtedly be the ladies' gowns and tiaras. The King and His Majesty Sultan Haitham bin Tarik will deliver speeches at the beginning of the banquet. The present Sultan acceded to the throne in 2020 following the death of his cousin, Sultan Qaboos bin Said, becoming the fifteenth ruler of the Al Bu Said dynasty which has ruled Oman since the mid-eighteenth century. He has also been dubbed 'one of the most discreet billionaires alive'. The sultan is married with four children, although his wife is not accompanying him on this State Visit. The ruling Omani family has enjoyed good relations with the British royal family and the Sultan has met the King and Queen on several occasions previously. Oman is considered one of Britain's oldest friends in Arabia. One of the Sultan's main challenges is seen to be the need to oversee Oman's transition from over-reliance on oil revenues to a diversified economy.
