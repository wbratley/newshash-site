Andy Burnham says 'win-win' EU deal possible ahead of UK-EU summit

Unbiased summary

At the European Political Community summit in Dublin, UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham stated that a "win-win" deal with the EU could be achieved, describing it as potentially "more like the Brexit that people thought they were voting for in 2016." He said negotiations would continue over the next seven days ahead of a UK-EU summit on 20 November, where agreements on food and drink, emissions trading, and youth mobility are expected. Burnham also rejected comments from former Prime Minister David Cameron, who had argued that Britain's problems were unrelated to Brexit and that Burnham should not seek to rejoin the EU. The EU has linked the food and drink deal and emissions-trading agreement to a deal on tuition fees for European students. Burnham has ruled out major changes to the UK-EU relationship before the next general election, committing to Labour's manifesto pledge of no return to the single market, customs union, or freedom of movement.
