Judge BANS people from putting up St George's flags on lampposts and bridges in Birmingham because of 'safety risks'

Article text

Judge BANS people from putting up St George's flags on lampposts and bridges in Birmingham because of 'safety risks' A judge has banned people from putting St George's flags on bridges and lampposts in Birmingham - after councillors said they threatened public safety. Residents could face up to two years in prison for breaking the injunction, made against a co-founder of the Raise The Colours group as well as 'persons unknown'. It follows the display of thousands of flags around the city - with the local council arguing they pose 'safety risks, obstruct visibility, interfere with highway assets and contribute to tensions and disorder'. Judge Kelly ruled an interim prohibitory injunction would be granted against co-founder Ryan Bridge and Ben Cullen, who is associated with Raise The Colours. Police can now detain someone immediately without a warrant if they reasonably suspect the order has been breached. Mr Bridge, who attended the Birmingham Civil and Family Justice Centre earlier this month, opposed the injunction on the basis it would breach his right to freedom of expression and his right to protest under the European Convention on Human Rights. His lawyer, Liam Wells, told the court the activist admits attaching flags to street furniture but has only encouraged people to 'express their views'. Mr Wells had told the court the injunction would have a 'chilling effect' on the expression of Mr Bridge's rights. Multiple St George's and Union Jack flags are seen tied to lampposts around Weoley Castle in Birmingham Birmingham city council first sparked outrage in August last year by ordering the removal of British flags from lampposts The earlier hearing was attended by about 20 Raise The Colours members, who wore T-shirts bearing the organisation's name, while three protesters stood with Union flags outside the court. The council said its application for an injunction included reported incidents involving 'alleged intimidation, harassment and obstruction' connected to flags being put up in the city. The court heard on one occasion a man was run over by a cherry picker and his leg was broken while members of Raise The Colours were putting up flags, while others have been allegedly assaulted or threatened. In her ruling, Judge Kelly said 'very emotive language' has surrounded the application for the injunction. She said: 'The interim injunction needs to be understood for what it is. 'It is not the court nor the claimant banning the Union Jack or the display of pride flags or anything of the like. 'Whilst the interim injunction order will cover flags of all types, it is limited in scope to the restraint of unauthorised flags attached to highway street furniture or infrastructure, and to the restraint of associated conduct, within the administrative area of Birmingham.' Councillor Jane Baston, Cabinet Member for Equalities, Communities and Social Justice for Birmingham City Council, said: 'We are grateful that the court has granted the council an interim injunction against Ryan Bridge, Ben Cullen and persons unknown relating to the unauthorised attachment of flags to street furniture and highway infrastructure.' Birmingham city council previously said it was pursuing legal action to stop people raising the flags, after staff tasked with removing them faced harassment and intimidation Ms Baston added: 'Residents remain free to display flags on their own property. 'The injunction relates specifically to the unauthorised attachment of items to street furniture and highway infrastructure, and we ask residents and community groups to support this approach by ensuring any displays are placed only where permission exists.' The council said anyone who breaches the injunction could be imprisoned for up to two years, fined, or have their assets seized for contempt of court. A follow-up hearing is due to take place in Birmingham on Thursday.
