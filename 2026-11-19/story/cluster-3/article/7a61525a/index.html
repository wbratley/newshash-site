Laura Gallacher visits Russell Brand in cushy jail as he faces prison transfer and ban on speaking to his wife after protesting his innocence in message she read on YouTube Russell Brand's wife has visited her rapist husband in prison as he faces possible sanctions over a YouTube statement she read on his behalf. Laura Gallacher was pictured arriving at HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire on Wednesday, where she spent around two hours. The 39-year-old entered the visitors' car park in a khaki green Land Rover Discovery accompanied by her husband's bodyguard Joe McCann. After meeting Brand, 51, she spoke to prison staff for 20 minutes before getting into the car and leaving around 4.30pm. A woman who had been visiting her son said: 'Russell had a visit from his wife. They were talking for a good length of time. 'The stress was quite evident, though, something like this can be really hard on the family. 'I was quite surprised that they had met so quickly, especially so soon after the trouble Russell got himself into for asking his wife to read out the message.' Laura declined to comment when approached by the Daily Mail. Laura Gallacher was seen arriving at the visitors' centre at HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire on Wednesday, where she spent around two hours The 39-year-old arrived in a khaki green Range Rover accompanied by her husband's bodyguard Joe McCann Mr McCann - seen with Ms Gallacher - accompanied the Brands throughout his trial On Sunday, she read out a message Brand had composed behind bars in which he claimed to be innocent. In the statement, the disgraced comedian said: 'As you know, I'm not guilty, I am not guilty for the offences I have been jailed for, so something strange is happening. 'The good news is that Christ is with us when we choose him, and I do.' The Ministry of Justice called the message 'completely unacceptable' and launched an 'urgent investigation'. Prison sources said Brand had 'won the lottery' when he was transferred from HMP Wandsworth - one of Britain's toughest jails - to Littlehey, which has a far more relaxed regime and offers activities including yoga, gardening and football. Officials are said to have met yesterday to discuss whether Brand should be transferred from the prison to another jail. Separate reports suggested Brand faces being banned from phoning or taking visits from Laura while an investigation into his conduct is underway. But former prison governor Ian Acheson has played down the prospect of a total ban. 'That would be swifty overturned by the courts as draconian and disproportionate to the act,' he told the Daily Mail. 'But there are parameters that can be put around what he can do if it is decided that he is unnecessarily traumatising victims, for example. 'What's going on here is in a bit of a murky zone. If he were causing a breach of prison security or inviting others to violence or trying to continue criminality that would be much more straightforward.' Mr Acheson added: 'Brand would tie up any attempt at a blanket ban in litigation that would, A – be successful, and B – feed his narcissistic desire for publicity whatever the cost to his victims.' Laura read out a message on his behalf on their Sunday Service YouTube show, in which the convicted rapist maintained his innocence HMP Littlehey. Prison sources said Brand had 'won the lottery' after he was transferred there from HMP Wandsworth – which is known as Britain's toughest jail Inmates at HMP Littlehey have phones in their cells to call family and friends until 11pm Officials are establishing how Brand's statement was communicated from inside prison and investigating whether it was simply a declaration of innocence or an attempt to influence his accusers amid a police investigation into further allegations. Prison governors have the power to restrict the ability of inmates to communicate with people on the outside if it is deemed necessary to protect victims, public safety or prison security. Inmates are also warned they cannot ask anyone else to share messages on their behalf that they wouldn't be allowed to do themselves. Justice Secretary Alex Norris said: 'Prisoners are rightly banned from making statements that cause distress to victims. 'This is being urgently investigated and I expect action to be taken if he is found to have broken the rules.' Downing Street officials and campaigners have also reacted with fury towards Brand's statement being shared on YouTube. He and his wife have also been posting on Rumble, a largely Right-wing 'free speech' alternative to YouTube. Their videos have a link that allows viewers to tip them directly, while their account links viewers to a Russell Brand-themed shop selling stickers of the rapist with the caption 'Russell Brand Stay Free'. YouTube accounts make money through advertisements and views on their videos. Brand's account has 6.6 million subscribers and more than 1,704,000,000 views. Creators earn around 55 per cent of the revenue from adverts with 45 per cent given to YouTube. They can earn up to £4 per 1,000 views, and Brand's wife's latest video on their channel has racked up more than 400,000 by midday on Monday. Victims' campaigners have criticised social media sites for allowing Brand to keep posting. Mia Latter, who alleges she was raped by Brand when she was 16 and has waived her right to anonymity, said his message was an 'insult' and questioned why YouTube had allowed the video to be shared. She told The Jeremy Vine Show: 'It was an insult to all the women that stood trial... it's an insult to the criminal justice system.' Meanwhile, a leading criminologist has claimed Laura may be being 'used' and 'coercively controlled' by her partner. Laura Richards, a former Met detective and expert on stalking, coercive control and sexual violence, said: 'The fact that he's prepared to use his wife like this, the caretaker of their three children, means he is putting her in jeopardy. 'He used her as a shield throughout his trial. I've seen coercive controllers use women all the time. 'She was important to him all the way through, knowing that this trial would be coming. And even more so now, to get his cult-like messages out to people and into the mainstream.' Videos of the couple interacting on their Christian podcast before Brand was jailed for rape and sexual assault have since been widely shared online. In one clip, captioned 'Laura Brand pls leave,' the comedian sarcastically applauds and appears to rebuke his wife after she challenged him over a passage of scripture. Ms Richards said of Brand: 'He is devious and diabolical. He's a shapeshifter. Now he's trying to influence people outside prison. That needs to be shut down. YouTube needs to shut him down. Rumble needs to shut him down.' Following his conviction, Brand's wellness firm Reborn began advertising a 'Free Russell' T-shirt. And now both Reborn and Brand's personal website are selling a range of T-shirts and caps bearing the message 'Free Russell Brand', which are promoted as 'fundraising apparel'. The items, all priced at $34.99 (£26.50), are promoted alongside the message: 'Free Russell. Silenced for speaking out against injustice and corruption.' Prison rules state that convicted prisoners are prohibited from conducting business transactions. The investigation may also consider whether Brand, who is serving a ten-and-a-half year sentence, has benefited in any way from his crime. Last week, a petition calling for Brand to be freed from jail was shared from the convicted rapist's X account. The link was shared on Thursday afternoon alongside the hashtag '#FreeRussellBrand'.
