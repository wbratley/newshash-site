PM's Brexit surrender is the 'worst of both worlds', Kemi says: Tory leader warns deal will hand the EU control of our money, laws and borders

Article text

PM's Brexit surrender is the 'worst of both worlds', Kemi says: Tory leader warns deal will hand the EU control of our money, laws and borders Andy Burnham's Brexit reset plan will make Britain a 'rule-taker run by EU courts', Kemi Badenoch warns today. Writing in the Daily Mail, the Conservative leader says the deal, due to be signed in Brussels tomorrow, will take the UK 'backwards'. She dismisses the Prime Minister's claim that the deal will deliver a modest boost to growth – while warning that it will fuel low-skilled immigration and drive up energy bills, as well as drag the UK back into the EU's orbit. 'Andy Burnham is about to deliver us the worst of both worlds when it comes to Brexit,' she writes. 'Out of the EU, but paying them money. A rule-taker run by EU courts and having to take more immigration.' Mrs Badenoch warns that the Prime Minister's desperation to cosy up to Brussels will lead to the UK getting a worse deal. She acknowledges that successive governments 'haven't made nearly enough' of the opportunities presented by Brexit. But she says Mr Burnham has no mandate for his stated ambition to put Britain on the road to rejoining the EU. 'Keir Starmer did, on a promise not to reopen our relationship with the EU. 'Andy Burnham stabbed him in the back and is now pursuing his own divisive agenda.' Mr Burnham will attend an EU summit in Brussels tomorrow to sign off an agreement aimed at unpicking part of the Brexit settlement. Next month he will set out 'options' for Britain's future relationship with the EU, including rejoining, with or without a referendum. This week's deal, originally negotiated by his predecessor Sir Keir, is focused on food and farming. In return for Brussels scrapping post-Brexit paperwork on British food exports, the PM will agree to permanently align with EU rules governing the sector. The move will require the UK to adopt hundreds of Brussels laws each year over which it will have little if any input. And Mr Burnham will sign up to a 'youth experience scheme' which will allow up to 100,000 EU nationals a year to live and work in the UK for up to three years at a time. But former foreign secretary David Lammy has dismissed the potential benefits of this week's reset as 'small beans' He will also signal he is ready to slash university tuition fees for EU students studying in the UK, potentially costing the taxpayer up to £485million a year. Diplomatic sources said he would agree to pay tens of millions of pounds a year into funding infrastructure projects in poorer EU countries in return for access to the bloc's electricity market. Ministers have suggested the deal could improve energy supplies. But Mrs Badenoch warns that higher EU green levies could push up bills by £230 a year. The PM has claimed unpublished Treasury analysis suggests that the deal could boost food exports to the continent and add £12billion to GDP in a decade's time. Sir Keir had pressed for a new 'youth experience scheme' allowing EU youngsters to live and work in Britain to be capped at 50,000 a year and limited to 12 months. But, despite the youth jobs crisis at home, diplomatic sources told the Mail Mr Burnham is ready to accept almost 100,000 a year for three years at a time in order to get the deal over the line by tomorrow. Sources said EU leaders have warned the PM that Britain will have to rejoin or submit to full common market rules if he wants to get further concessions. During angry clashes in the Commons, Mr Burnham described opponents of his Brexit plans as 'zombies'. He said he would be 'fighting in Brussels on Friday for a new arrangement to improve the Tory Brexit deal'. But former foreign secretary David Lammy dismissed the potential benefits of this week's reset as 'small beans' and called for a referendum on rejoining the EU.
