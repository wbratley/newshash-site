Andy Burnham's biggest myth busted

Article text

Andy Burnham's claim that Britain's economy has suffered as a direct result of leaving the EU has been torn apart by figures released by the Office for National Statistics (ONS). According to productivity data, the UK has outperformed all of its counterparts in the bloc, only trailing behind the US on output per hour among G7 countries since 2015. The figures emerge just days after Andy Burnham delivered a speech at the Lord Mayor's Banquet amid the Government's push for closer ties with Brussels. Claiming the UK had "lost more than we have gained in the last 10 years", the Prime Minister told attendees: "Taken as a whole, the OBR (Office for Budget Responsibility) says Brexit has meant a four per cent hit to the British economy. "Others put it higher at up to eight per cent of GDP." Before Britain formally withdrew from the EU, the Treasury watchdog warned such a move could slash "potential productivity" of the UK economy by four per cent after 15 years. But the fresh ONS data suggests the economy has been more efficient than previously believed. By updating the way they measure how much workers produce per hour, researchers found that since 1997, the UK has grown faster than Germany, France, and Italy. The UK held onto that lead over the last 10 years - meaning only the US beat Britain's efficiency growth among the world's seven largest advanced economies. Upon releasing the figures, the ONS said: "While international comparability between productivity growth rates should be treated with some caution, because of differences in measurement methodology, the UK's growth since 1997 being the second highest among this group suggests that productivity growth over the past three decades has been stronger than previously perceived. "However, it is still materially lower than that observed in the US." Simon French, chief economist at Panmure Liberum, said the data presents "a rather awkward data backdrop" to Friday's UK-EU summit in Brussels. ANDY BURNHAM'S BREXIT 'BETRAYAL' - READ THE LATEST: He wrote on social media: "It now reveals that since the Brexit vote in 2016 the UK's productivity performance has accelerated away from comparable EU economies - having largely tracked them (2016-2025) in the previous iteration of this data. "It must call into question the validity of the (widely cited) NBER estimate that Brexit triggered a three per cent to four per cent reduction in UK productivity. "We have consistently believed the Brexit impact to be closer to half this level." On Friday, the Prime Minister will seek to lock in deals on food, emissions trading and youth mobility at the gathering in Brussels. At Mansion House earlier this week, Mr Burnham "if we are going to be serious about growth" there must be a "conversation" surrounding closer alignment with the EU. "It’s like the emperor’s new clothes, where no one is prepared to say the obvious truth. "Well, I do try to say things as I see them, and to be frank, we have lost more than we have gained in the last 10 years. The hard Brexit we were given is not good enough for Britain." He also argued that a new agreement with the bloc may bolster economic growth, warning against turning its back on the potential benefits of a closer partnership. “We are not in a position to turn our back on a potential deal that could add up to 0.4 per cent to GDP," he said.
