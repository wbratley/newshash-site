Kemi Badenoch blasts Andy Burnham's Brexit reset plan as 'worst of both worlds' in scathing attack

Article text

Kemi Badenoch has launched a blistering assault on Andy Burnham's EU reset deal, warning it will leave Britain in the "worst of both worlds" on Brexit. The Conservative leader argues that the agreement the Prime Minister is set to finalise in Brussels tomorrow, Friday, will drag the country "backwards." Mrs Badenoch has rejected Labour's claims that the deal will generate a modest economic uplift. Instead, she warns it will encourage low-skilled migration, raise household energy costs, and pull Britain deeper into the EU's regulatory sphere. Writing in the Daily Mail, she said: "Andy Burnham is about to deliver us the worst of both worlds when it comes to Brexit. "Out of the EU, but paying them money. A rule-taker run by EU courts and having to take more immigration." The deal Mr Burnham is expected to sign centres on food and agriculture, with Britain agreeing to permanently follow EU rules in the sector in exchange for removing post-Brexit paperwork on exports. This would oblige the UK to adopt hundreds of Brussels regulations annually with minimal say over their content. A youth experience scheme allowing up to 100,000 EU nationals to live and work in Britain for up to three years is also part of the package, significantly more generous than the 50,000 cap Sir Keir Starmer originally sought. Diplomatic sources indicate the Prime Minister will also commit to paying tens of millions of pounds annually into EU infrastructure funds to secure access to the bloc's electricity market. Mrs Badenoch warns that EU green levies could add £230 a year to household bills. Mr Burnham is also expected to signal his willingness to reduce university tuition fees for EU students in the UK, a move that could cost the taxpayer as much as £485million annually. LATEST DEVELOPMENTS The Tory leader's sharpest barb targets the Prime Minister's democratic legitimacy on the issue. She points out that Sir Keir Starmer, not Mr Burnham, fought the last general election and did so on an explicit pledge not to reopen Britain's relationship with the EU. "Andy Burnham stabbed him in the back and is now pursuing his own divisive agenda," Mrs Badenoch writes. While conceding that successive governments "haven't made nearly enough" of the freedoms Brexit offered, she insists Mr Burnham has no mandate for his stated ambition of steering Britain back towards EU membership. European capitals are making clear that the era of piecemeal reset agreements is drawing to a close. EU diplomats have warned that once Friday's deal is concluded, they will not entertain further sector-by-sector access to the single market. "Don't come back until you know what you want. Work out what your options really are and then come to negotiate," one EU diplomat said. Brussels has already turned down a British proposal to align with all single market rules on goods in return for fewer border checks. Several EU capitals believe any additional concessions would amount to cherry-picking the advantages of full membership. Ireland's ambassador to the UK, Sonja Hyland, put it plainly: "If you want full access to the single market, then that's a slightly different thing." Mr Burnham has shown no sign of backing down, branding Conservative critics "zombies" during heated exchanges at Prime Minister's Questions on Wednesday. He told MPs he would be "fighting in Brussels on Friday for a new arrangement to improve the Tory Brexit deal." Next month, the Prime Minister plans to lay out four possible paths for the UK-EU relationship — from maintaining the status quo to full rejoin — as part of a 10-year plan. The government is reportedly considering a year-long "national conversation" in 2027, featuring town halls and citizens' assemblies, before settling on a direction. Senior Labour figures are already pushing further. Former foreign secretary David Lammy described the reset's potential gains as "small beans" and called for a referendum on rejoining, while ex-business secretary Peter Kyle urged Mr Burnham to fight the next election on a rejoin platform.
