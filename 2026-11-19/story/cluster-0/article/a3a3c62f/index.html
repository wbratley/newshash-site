‘Most extraordinary virtual world’: critics hail Grand Theft Auto VI as masterpiece

Article text

Good things clearly come to those who wait. After 13 years of anticipation, Grand Theft Auto VI is finally here – and, according to critics, it’s a masterpiece. The trailer for the video game, which returns players to Vice City, Rockstar’s fictional version of Miami, attracted more than 90m views in 24 hours when it was released in December 2023. Excitement continued to mount, with two release delays and a steady stream of leaks and speculation keeping the game in the headlines. Now, Metacritic, which aggregates reviews from critics, averages it at 99 out of 100 – tying with Nintendo’s The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, which has held the record for the best reviewed video game since 1998. In a five-star review, the Guardian’s Keza MacDonald said: “In GTA VI, Rockstar has created the most extraordinarily, indulgently detailed virtual world that we have yet seen.” MacDonald praised the fictional state of Leonida as an extraordinarily detailed world, populated by incidental encounters and activities that make it feel alive. But for all its violence and grotesque humour, what stood out was the relationship between Jason and Lucia – GTA’s first female protagonist. She said: “I was rooting so hard for Jason and Lucia by the end that I felt quite sick with worry about what was going to happen to them, pacing back and forth in front of my TV throughout the final missions. Among all the expectations I had for GTA VI, I did not expect it to make me so emotional.” In a 10-out-of-10 review, IGN’s Luke Reilly called it “the single most impressive video game I have ever played”. He praised its graphics, driving mechanics and the sheer amount of detail in its world. He said: “I see no real limit to the amount of time I could spend here, endlessly exploring and interacting with Vice City and the massive area surrounding it. While the well-paced story effectively nudged me around the map, there are still vast sections of it that I’m yet to even visit – despite hitting the credits after almost 120 hours of hijinks. “Frankly, I’ve never played anything that’s hit quite like GTA 6 – and right now I have zero plans to stop playing it.” In its five-star review, Eurogamer said Rockstar had “pushed back the horizon of video games once again” with its graphics, storytelling and world building. Chris Tapsell wrote: “It’s about big ideas, themes, places – America, society, love – and also about individuals. It’s ridiculous and puerile, and also sometimes mature. Tender and violent. Deep and shallow. Novel and, in places, surprisingly familiar.” For the New York Times, the game was “about love and relationships, their joy and heartache, and the work required to make them successful”. Yussef Cole’s review said this reflected a maturation of a series that had “generally only told stories about men solipsistically chasing after their own success”. Whether you’re controlling Jason or Lucia, he added that “much of what you can do – apart from activities routine to GTA like stealing cars and robbing banks – is taking action to care for and nurture your romantic partner”. The Washington Post’s Gene Park awarded the game four stars, saying it “raises the bar at every level”. He highlighted its unusually convincing portrayal of human behaviour, with more than 33,000 individual non-playable characters and interactions shaped by race, class, gender and money. Writing for Forbes, Paul Tassi singled out the relationship between Jason and Lucia as one of the game’s greatest achievements, saying: “I can’t say I’ve ever seen a better video game relationship than the one they share.” Bloomberg’s Jason Schreier gave the game five stars, calling it a “masterpiece”. “It’s hard not to be astonished by what Rockstar has done here. There’s nothing else like it,” he said. GamesRadar+ also awarded it full marks, calling it “the ultimate time vampire”, and VGC suggested its influence will be felt across the games industry for years to come. Reviewer Jordan Middler described Rockstar as operating at “the absolute peak of its power”, adding that GTA 6 was “one of the best video games ever made”. Grand Theft Auto VI officially launches on Thursday. Rockstar has not revealed the cost of the game, but some estimates put the figure north of $1bn (£760m). In the UK, the standard edition costs £69.99, rising to £89.99 for the Ultimate Edition. The research firm Sensor Tower estimated that the game had generated $1.54bn (£1.2bn) in digital pre-orders by 17 November, while analysts have forecast that it could generate between $3.25bn and $5.2bn in sales during its first week.
