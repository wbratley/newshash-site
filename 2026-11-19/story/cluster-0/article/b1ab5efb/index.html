Schools fearing Grand Theft Auto 6 release will result in empty classrooms offer pupils £10 vouchers and warn parents not to let them skip classes to play - even though it's rated 18+

Schools fearing Grand Theft Auto 6 release will result in empty classrooms offer pupils £10 vouchers and warn parents not to let them skip classes to play - even though it's rated 18+ Schools are offering £10 vouchers to coax pupils into attending as they brace for empty classrooms in the wake of the release of Grand Theft Auto 6. After more than a decade since the series' last release, it has fast become one of the most eagerly anticipated video games of all time. But it has led to concern from teachers that pupils may stay up late and will not be able to concentrate in class. Schools have been forced to take drastic measures to try and prevent a mass absence of pupils. These range from offers such as a £100 raffle or Amazon vouchers, to threatening to use a minibus to pick up non-attenders, or issuing fines to parents. And it is not just pupils who could be bunking off, as brazen parents prepare to call in sick to join their skiving children for what could be one of the least productive days in British history. An estimated 2.8million UK adults are expected to play the much-anticipated GTA 6 on launch day alone, after it releases at midnight on November 19. Tens of thousands of workers have booked leave, and some companies have given employees the day off. Now, with just two days to go before Grand Theft Auto 6 finally drops, experts have revealed the impact this could have on Britain's economy One Lincolnshire school is offering a total of £100 in vouchers in a prize draw to persuade children to go to school on Thursday - the day the game is released. At Oasis Academy Immingham, students have been urged to attend school as normal on Thursday and Friday. Ten £10 vouchers are up for grabs in a prize draw for those attending school on Thursday morning. Ninestiles, An Academy in Birmingham will be picking up students who are not in school in its minibus and bringing them in, and pupils who attend class every day this week will be entered into a draw to win vouchers. The secondary school has also adjusted its mock exams schedule to extend into this week as part of its efforts to drive attendance when GTA 6 launches. Ellesmere Park High School, in Eccles, Salford, has sent a warning to parents and carers to remind them that 'taking a day off school to play a newly released game would not be considered an authorised absence'. It also pointed out that the game has an 18+ age rating and is not meant for school age children. The message, sent by the school's attendance team, states: 'Please be aware that taking a day off school to play a newly released game, or for any other leisure activity, would not be considered an authorised absence. Any absence without a valid reason may be recorded as unauthorised.' It added: 'We recognise that many students are excited about the release of Grand Theft Auto (GTA). However, parents and carers may wish to note that the game carries an 18 certificate due to its mature content and is therefore not intended for children of school age. 'We ask for your support in ensuring that your child attends school as normal and continues to make the most of their learning opportunities.' In a letter to parents and carers ahead of the highly anticipated release of GTA 6, the school said: 'We would encourage parents to discuss not only whether their child is accessing the game, but also when they are accessing it and how this may affect their well-being, sleep and readiness for school. 'We would also encourage families to review parental controls and monitor gaming activity where appropriate.' Parents in England can be fined if their children have unauthorised absences from school. School absence fines in England start at £80 per child if paid within 21 days, but a parent who receives a second fine for the same child within a three-year period can receive a £160 fine. St John's Catholic School and Sixth Form College, in Bishop Auckland, County Durham, has said pupils who attend school on Thursday will be entered into a one-off prize draw to win a £65 Amazon voucher. In a post on Facebook, the secondary school said: 'We know many of our students will be counting down the days until the highly anticipated release of the GTA video game on Thursday. 'But before you start your next mission, there's one objective left to complete: Attend school on Thursday!' In a post on social media, Felixstowe School and Sixth Form said a new video game release is 'not an authorised reason to miss school'. The game's release could be so popular that experts warn it could have an unexpected side-effect – damaging the economy. Research from Opinium suggests that a staggering 4.1million working days could be lost across Britain, as avid gamers take sick days and bunk off work. GTA 6 is the latest entry in the franchise and is set to arrive on November 19, more than 13 years after GTA 5, the second best-selling video game in history. The action-adventure game is thought to be the most expensive ever made – with estimates putting development costs at more than $1billion. Ahead of its release, Opinium surveyed 2,000 Brits to find out their plans surrounding the game. The results revealed that one in ten Brits who are planning to buy GTA 6 will take a sick day to do so. Another 12 per cent will play it while they're supposed to be working from home, while 15 per cent have proactively booked annual leave to get a proper gaming session in. Those who are taking time off – whether it's as annual leave or as a sick day – say they'll need to take two days. That equates to 4.1 million lost working days, according to Opinium. The news comes shortly after broadband operators warned that the imminent release of Grand Theft Auto 6 (GTA VI) could bring the internet to its knees. Several firms, including BT, are understood to have purchased additional capacity from major internet exchanges in anticipation of a huge surge in traffic when the game launches on November 19. They have also ramped up their use of caching, restricted scheduled maintenance and stepped up network monitoring to prepare for the influx of data. Grand Theft Auto developers Rockstar North are based in Edinburgh and are set to re-establish their world dominance in the gaming world with the latest instalment, which is among the most anticipated entertainment releases in history. GTA 6 is priced at £69.99 with an ultimate version for just under £90. The franchise launched in 1997 as a fairly straightforward, top-down game that let players roam fictional cities, steal vehicles, dodge police and engage in criminal activity. The 2001 release of Grand Theft Auto III revolutionised open-world gaming. It offered players the ability to explore a three-dimensional city, tackle missions and engage with their environment. Pepe Di'Iasio, general secretary of the Association of School and College Leaders (ASCL), said: 'Grand Theft Auto has an age certificate of 18 and therefore has been deemed unsuitable for school-age children to play. 'Schools are focused on ensuring good attendance at all times of the academic year, as they know how important this is for learning and outcomes, and many have existing measures in place to promote this. 'However, school absences are often the result of external factors that are outside of a school's control. 'We'd encourage parents to work with schools in promoting the benefits of attendance alongside other good habits such as having a wide range of age-appropriate hobbies with limited time spent on screens.'
