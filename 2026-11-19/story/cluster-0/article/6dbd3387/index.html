Grand Theft Auto staff ‘caught red-handed’ breaching confidentiality rules, tribunal told

Log in Workers who were sacked by Grand Theft Auto (GTA) developer Rockstar Games were “caught red-handed” flouting contractual policies and obligations, an employment tribunal has heard. The tribunal for a group of former staff who are claiming unfair dismissal concluded its evidence hearings on Wednesday in Glasgow. It comes ahead of the release of Rockstar’s highly anticipated Grand Theft Auto VI game on Thursday. Some 34 people were dismissed by Rockstar in October last year across the UK and Canada over what the firm described as “the sharing of confidential company information”. The company has accused them of breaching confidentiality rules by discussing sensitive information on a Discord server which had hundreds of members. Twenty-three of them, who are being represented by the Independent Workers’ Union of Great Britain (IWGB), are taking part in an employment tribunal, claiming they were unfairly dismissed for trade union activities. Thomas Cordrey, representing Rockstar, said employees made disparaging comments about the company on a semi-public Discord server and shared confidential information, including messages which could be taken to refer to unreleased game features for Grand Theft Auto VI and the timing of its release. In his closing submission, he said moderators of the Discord server did not want to “over police” it and chose to “let the taps of disparagement and confidential information flow”. He added that the claimants “were caught red-handed” while Rockstar was under “enormous pressure”. He said the company was on the “precipice of announcing further delays” which had wiped a substantial amount of money off the share price. The alternative to the dismissals, he said, would have been 34 extended disciplinary hearings with multiple rounds across multiple jurisdictions, all in the face of “inevitable dismissals for gross misconduct”. In light of those considerations, he said Rockstar’s approach to issue letters setting out the basis of dismissals was “within the band of reasonable responses”. He urged Judge Lucy Wiseman to take into account the impact of going through those lengthier processes. Mr Cordrey also said the claimants did not appear apologetic about their conduct and described them as “blameworthy”. Referring to the claimants’ conduct, Mr Cordrey told the tribunal: “This was a case of ‘we know the policies, we know our employer would be opposed to the use of a semi-public Discord to disparage and breach confidentiality, but we did it anyway and we would do it again’. “The reason why these individuals were dismissed was for that reason – flouting of contractual policies and obligations.” Mr Cordrey said managers were “vilified” in some posts on the server. He said references to unreleased game features which could have been taken to refer to GTA VI were discussed while work was under way on the game. He told the tribunal: “This is why it was so grave – the most vitally protected resource which the company is so incredibly careful to protect was being liberally discussed in this environment.” Mr Cordrey said the employer was “obsessed by confidentiality”. He said the context of comments on the server can be understood more clearly by taking account of reports by journalists describing how the stock value and reputation of the game “can be hugely impacted by even minor news and rumours”. Mr Cordrey referred to evidence from the games developer’s head of licensing, Jennifer Kolbe, who described being “shocked and appalled” at what was discovered on the Discord server in October 2025. He said union membership and activities had nothing to do with the dismissals. In a statement, a spokesperson for Rockstar Games said: “Rockstar Games’ position has been clear and consistent throughout: individuals across the UK and Canada were dismissed for gross misconduct following the sharing of confidential company information, not because of alleged trade union membership or activity. “The tribunal has heard our evidence and seen the facts, and the hearing has not yet concluded. We remain confident of a positive outcome.” Judge Wiseman concluded the evidential hearings after hearing closing submissions from Rockstar on Wednesday afternoon. She said: “I cannot give a timeframe for producing a judgment in this case for obvious reasons I don’t need to rehearse. “I will do my best to endeavour to get (the judgment) out as quickly as I can.” The company’s Rockstar North studio is in Holyrood Road, Edinburgh.
