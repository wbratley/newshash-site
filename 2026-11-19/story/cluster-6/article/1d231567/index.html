Crew unaware of risks before Bayesian yacht accident, say investigators

Article text

Crew unaware of risks before Bayesian yacht accident, say investigators - Published The crew of the Bayesian superyacht did not know about a critical safety risk that left it vulnerable in powerful winds before it sank off the coast in northern Sicily coast, killing seven people, British investigators say. British tech tycoon Mike Lynch, 59, and his daughter Hannah, 18, were among seven people who died when the vessel sank on 19 August 2024. The sinking of the Bayesian unfolded with "extraordinary speed" when a violent downburst of freak hurricane-force winds knocked it onto its side in less than 15 seconds, the UK's Marine Accident Investigation Branch (MAIB) final report said. Safety information provided to the crew did not identify this type of risk, the MAIB said. - 'For two seconds I lost my baby in the sea' - Sicily yacht survivor - Published19 August 2024 - Tributes paid to 'UK's greatest tech entrepreneur' - Published23 August 2024 "It was very dynamic, very quick," said MAIB principal inspector Simon Graves. "The speed of events overtook the crew and prevented them from taking any other action." The accident also led to the deaths of Morgan Stanley International bank chairman Jonathan Bloomer, 70, and his wife, Judy, 71; American lawyer Chris Morvillo, 59, and his wife Neda Morvillo, 57; and crew member Rick Thomas, 59. Rob Loder, chief inspector of marine accidents said: "Our thoughts remain with the families and loved ones of the seven who lost their lives, and with all those affected by the loss of Bayesian. This accident developed with extraordinary speed." Fifteen survivors escaped from the 56-metre (184m) luxury yacht and managed to get to a life raft that had been launched by a crew member. The marine investigation found that the Bayesian "had a vulnerability to high winds that was not recognised, because the yacht had never been assessed at anchor with its sails down". In addition, "the extreme winds that night were also not explicitly forecast, so the crew had no warning of the risk", Loder added. In the early hours of 19 August 2024 the Bayesian was anchored outside of Porticello port, on the north coast of Sicily. With thunderstorms rapidly coming in, wind speeds went from 8 knots to about 70 knots (80mph) within minutes. The weather forecast had only predicted gusts of up to 27 knots (31mph). The MAIB report stated that with no sails set Bayesian was vulnerable to being knocked over in severe winds. A study commissioned by the MAIB indicated that, in the conditions assessed, Bayesian was vulnerable to extreme winds at the vessel's widest point, known as the beam. It also found that any knockdown - where the boat was suddenly tipped sideways - would be "irrecoverable once the yacht passed the angle of vanishing stability", assessed in the study as being 73 degrees. Once the vessel passed this angle it could not right itself. In addition, the centreboard, which provides stability when a ship is under sail, was raised. The report said the centreboard was not needed when the Bayesian was using its motors and was rarely lowered at anchor. The way the boat was loaded, and the angle of the wind, also contributed to the accident. Inspectors said a "downburst of hurricane-force winds" was the most likely cause of the ship keeling over. They happen when cold, dense air plunges rapidly, sending out violent winds across the surface. The MAIB said that none of risks were covered in the UK training syllabus, nor in published guidance available to crew. Once the vessel was on its side, it rapidly filled with water and sank. The MAIB's final report into the sinking, published on Thursday, appeared to contrast with recent reports from Italy, where prosecution experts have reportedly blamed errors by the crew rather than problems with the yacht. The investigation was carried out alongside a criminal probe in Italy, where captain James Cutfield, chief engineer Tim Parker Eaton and deckhand Matthew Griffith are under investigation over potential charges of multiple manslaughter and culpable shipwreck. British investigators said that Italian prosecutors had restricted access to some witnesses and evidence. "It is possible that the circumstances depicted in this report may alter following disclosure of evidence withheld by the Italian authorities," the report says. However, the MAIB's Graves said he was satisfied with the British investigation. "We are content with our investigation, the investigation that we've undertaken, and the information we've used to feed into it." The report also found that alcohol consumption had not contributed to the sinking. The MAIB report makes seven recommendations to four agencies. It calls for new regulations to be set for single-hulled sailing vessels, and new rules around life rafts. It also urges collaboration between the Met Office and its global counterpart to improve information-sharing of severe weather forecasts with mariners.
