Apec summit live: Trump praises ‘great relationship’ with Putin ahead of historic trilateral talks with Xi US president meets Russian counterpart before the two leaders meet Xi Jinping in their first trilateral, with trade, AI and global conflicts the key topics Xi Jinping describes Apec members as 'passengers riding the same ship' Here’s the Guardian’s Rebecca Bailey on Xi Jinping’s speech today, in which she writes that he reiterated themes of openness and innovation emphasised throughout the summit. “China is an advocate, promoter and forerunner of Asia-Pacific economic cooperation,” he said. He announced China would give US$20 million in funding “to support APEC’s practical cooperation”, and would establish a Apec-focused research centre in Shenzhen. “Efforts should be made to align various regional economic and trade agreements, boost trade and investment facilitation and digitalisation, and reduce barriers to trade and investment,” he said. The region should promote connectivity and innovation, he added. Green energy, energy and food security and digital infrastructure were all among areas mentioned for more coordination and collaboration. Toward the beginning of his speech he had this poetic flourish: Members of Apec are like passengers riding the same ship. To navigate the waves of the Pacific, we must unite as one and look out for each other. Before meeting Xi, Trump said the meeting would be “productive” and the two countries have now split tariffs in a “fair way”, Reuters news reports. Trump said the Board of Trade that he and Xi agreed to form at their summit in Beijing in May had brought balance to the relationship between the two countries. He also described the Chinese president as a “friend” saying the pair had a special relationship. Yalta became a byword for betrayal. Will Trump, Xi and Putin enact another carve-up? That is the question the Guardian’s diplomatic editor Patrick Wintour explores in his piece in the lead up to today’s historic trilateral. Some have likened the impending three-way talks (pictured below) to the 1945 meeting between Stalin, Churchill and Roosevelt that that in effect carved up postwar Europe, between a communist-ruled east and a democratic, capitalist west. Leaders of the Ukrainian community in Australia say they are deeply disappointed at a decision by the prime minister, Anthony Albanese, to pose in photos with Russia’s Vladimir Putin. Albanese joined two group photos at the Apec summit, posing among leaders standing alongside Putin. Albanese said before arriving at the Shenzhen talks that he would respect the wishes of China as the host country and join the leaders’ “family photo”. The Australian Federation of Ukrainian Organisations said the decision put diplomatic courtesy ahead of Australia’s national values and moral responsibility. Chair Kateryna Argyrou accused Albanese of handing the Kremlin “a propaganda win”, saying: There is nothing normal, diplomatic or acceptable about standing shoulder to shoulder with Vladimir Putin, an indicted war criminal, for a celebratory photograph while Russia continues to slaughter innocent people on a daily basis and wage the largest war in Europe since the Second World War. A photo like this normalises a man who should be condemned and tried in a court of law, not celebrated on a podium. 'A lot of wonderful things' to come, Trump says before meeting Putin US President Donald Trump met with Russian President Vladimir Putin in China’s Shenzhen on Thursday on the sidelines of the Apec summit. He said a lot of ‘wonderful things’ could come out of their talks, and described Putin as a “friend”. We’ve had a great relationship for many years. We know each other for a long time, and I think a lot of wonderful things can come out of this, or at least start. But I think they’ll come out of this,” he said. Russia is an integral part of the Asia-Pacific region, President Vladimir Putin told Apec leaders in China on Thursday, as he urged regional nations to use Russian logistics infrastructure. “I would like to emphasise that constructive partnership with Apec economies is Russia’s strategic course. We are an integral part of the region and make a significant contribution to its economic prosperity,” Putin said. Apec should set a unifying tone and build a constructive agenda for cooperation, which he said the draft of the group’s final communique aims for, adding that Moscow supports the document. Putin has been shunned by the West since launching Europe’s worst conflict since the second world war with his 2022 attack on Ukraine. Addressing the Apec gathering on Wednesday, he said he expected the trilateral summit to be “highly useful”. Donald Trump has used his comments at an Apec session to thanks Xi Jinping for hosting him, describing China’s leader as an “incredible leader.” This is an incredible country. You’ve done an unbelievable job and you’re and incredible leader. Thank you very much. In an earlier interview with Fox News, Trump highlighted the pomp surrounding his arrival, which contrasted with the more muted greeting of other leaders, including Vladimir Putin. Xi greeted Trump at the airport, the first time he has done so with any visiting leader since becoming president, reciprocating his counterpart’s gesture at Joint Base Andrews in September when they last met. The leaders also briefly hugged. “This is a great president, one of the great presidents of China, and he’s never gone to the airport to meet somebody,” Trump said, when asked what he wanted to get out of the trilateral. And not only that, the fanfare, the beauty, the pageantry, the thousands of people, the young children, the soldiers, the whole thing. It was great respect, and I appreciate that. That’s a big win for us. It’s a big win for China too.” There has been both warm welcomes, and muted greetings on the red carpet rolled out at Apec, writes the Guardian’s Japan correspondent, Justin McCurry. Military bands played, dozens of cheering young men and women waved colourful bouquets, and senior officials offered hugs and handshakes on the tarmac. Along with the coterie of world leaders at Apec, hundreds of business leaders are also in Shenzhen, with executives from BYD, Baidu, Boeing and Google set to attend. Elon Musk is also expected to be there, along with Visa CEO Ryan McInerney, Mastercard CEO Michael Miebach and Deloitte CEO Joe Ucuzoglu. Despite the high-profile roster of names, Nikkei Asia has reported that the number of prominent US executives appears “relatively light” compared to previous years. One official told the publication that executives had not been provided with clear agendas in advance and many were caught up in scheduling conflicts. One major name present at the event in South Korea last year, but noticeably absent from this year’s summit, is Jensen Huang, who resigned from Nvidia last week after a scandal over an affair with a woman suspected of spying for China. South China Morning Post reports that the chaos that has engulfed the chipmaker in the last week has played a role in discouraging some US business leaders from attending the summit, as they “worry about being perceived as close to China”. Zak Dychtwald, the founder of BridgeWorks Global, a China-based consultancy told the paper that “CEOs had spent the last 18 months returning to China with more confidence, and this sets that back”. The Guardian’s correspondent Rebecca Bailey is in Shenzhen, where she is covering the events of the day. Describing the mood on the ground and what we can expect today, she writes: There’s a sense of anticipation in the air, and traffic restrictions on the road, as we kick off the final day of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (Apec) summit. Today will be a blockbuster day of geopolitics, featuring both multilateral discussions and back-to-back big power negotiations. Leaders from Apec’s 21 member states will shortly gather for a family photo, before going into a plenary session, according to a White House schedule. Host Xi Jinping will then meet Donald Trump at a state guesthouse beside Shenzhen’s tiger safari park in the early afternoon. Just under two hours later Vladimir Putin will join them for unprecedented trilateral talks, according to that schedule. In addition, it has been reported that Trump and Putin might meet separately. Also potentially on Thursday’s agenda is an Apec joint statement that, an official told Reuters on Tuesday, delegates had been “working around the clock” to find agreement on. Hello and welcome to our live coverage of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (Apec), taking place today in the Chinese tech hub of Shenzhen. It’s a big day for global diplomacy, with a hotly anticipated meeting between US president Donald Trump, China’s leader Xi Jinping and Russia’s Vladimir Putin. It is the first time ever the three leaders have held trilateral talks – with the meeting even drawing comparisons to the 1945 Yalta meeting, shorthand for great-power deals imposed on smaller countries. On the agenda is Russia’s war with Ukraine, the US war in Iran, as well as global trade, AI, and nuclear weapons. Speculation also abounds about the possible appearance of North Korean leader Kim Jong-un, with Trump saying he is open to engagement.
