Anthropic IPO: how can I buy shares, and is it worth it?

Article text

The company is a leader in artificial intelligence, and is best known for its chatbot Claude. The float, which is expected to value the business at $1.4tn (£1tn), comes at a volatile time for tech stocks, yet it has been reported that the shares could be six times oversubscribed . How much are the shares? When a company floats it issues a prospectus for would-be investors that details its activities and lays out the share offering, including an estimated price. Anthropic’s prospectus explains that the shares on sale will be 825m of class A common stock, and that the initial public offering (IPO) price will between $70 and $78 a share. However, the final price will be set on Wednesday. The shares will come with voting rights, but, if all the shares’ votes are combined they will only represent 49.9% of the total vote available, so will not allow buyers to be able to force any changes in the way the company is run. How do I buy shares? The shares will be listed on the Nasdaq in New York on Thursday, and will be available to individual investors alongside big institutions. In the UK, several investment platforms are offering customers the chance to subscribe to shares, including AJ Bell, Hargreaves Lansdown, Robinhood and eToro. At Robinhood and eToro the minimum investment is $250 (£189), at Hargreaves Lansdown the minimum investment is £250, and at AJ Bell investors will need at least £1,000 to apply. AJ Bell is closing applications at 1pm on Wednesday, while Hargreaves Lansdown and Robinhood will close at 3pm. All say they could close the doors early without notice. Once the IPO has happened and the shares have been listed they can be bought at any point, and through platforms that have not been involved in the IPO. Will they be available in funds? Yes – in fact, there are already funds which own stakes in the company. In the UK they include the Baillie Gifford US Growth and Scottish Mortgage investment trusts , while Fidelity has investments held in funds available to US investors. Once the IPO is over, the stock will ultimately find its way into some tracker funds – although some will be quicker than others – and managers of actively invested funds could also choose to buy shares. That means people might well end up investing in Anthropic even if they do not attempt to buy any shares directly. Should I buy the shares? Anthropic’s prospectus includes 92 pages on potential risks to the company and its industry , so it is not an investment for the faint-hearted. Recent weeks have highlighted the fragility of the US markets, and tech stocks in particular, so this is one to consider very carefully. Nils Pratley, the Guardian’s financial editor, has raised questions about the company’s governance structure , saying it “represents an extraordinary concentration of power in the hands of a few individuals”. Jason Hollands, the managing director of the investment platform Bestinvest by Evelyn Partners – which is not participating in the IPO – highlights some other things investors need to consider. “Anthropic would give most retail investors the first opportunity to have a direct stake in a frontier AI developer. But it remains a capital-intensive business, and it has yet to establish a consistent path to profitability,” he says. “Investors will need to judge whether the company can turn demand for its models into durable earnings.” Hollands says if demand for shares exceeds the limited supply, “that could support the price initially with a ‘pop’, but it does not guarantee a strong debut or lasting gains over the coming months”. He adds the SpaceX listing in June “is a reminder that a hotly anticipated IPO and early enthusiasm can quickly be followed by volatility: its shares have traded below their IPO price at points since listing”. What does Claude think? When we asked Claude whether to buy shares in Anthropic it was upfront about why it is not a great source of investment advice: “I can’t tell you whether you should buy. I’m not a financial adviser, and I’m also made by Anthropic, so I have an obvious conflict of interest here.” It went on to present a few facts about the IPO, and some useful questions that any investor should be considering before buying an individual share in an IPO. Can you cope with a loss if it goes down in value? How much of your portfolio will an investment in it represent? Will you already have exposure to the share through a tracker fund?
