'I am pleased to announce that Mr. Stephen Miller, the current Deputy Chief of Staff of the Trump Administration, and one of my most trusted advisors, will become the Chief of Staff starting Monday, November 9, 2026,' Trump wrote on Truth Social. 'Susie Wiles, who has done an outstanding job, will be moving over to the Trump Political Operation. I want to thank her for her service as Chief of Staff — She has done a great job!' The shake-up came Friday afternoon, just days after the Democrats' massive election victory handed the president and Republicans a stunning defeat. The margins of the left's victory earlier this week were so large that it may end up keeping Republicans out of the congressional majority for the better part of the next decade – a tough pill to swallow for many on the right who have called for Trump to make changes. Although the sudden switch was shocking, the Daily Mail had reported in June that Wiles was quietly prepping an exit plan for after the midterms. At the time, the White House had denied it. Pressed on the shake-up Friday afternoon, Trump told reporters: 'I wanted to make a change. I think Susie's done a very good job, great job, but ... we gotta make moves. Sometimes in life, you have to make moves.' His account cuts against that of a senior White House official, who told the Daily Mail that Wiles 'gave her resignation last night.' While Trump says publicly that he needed to make a change, the official says that the departure was Wiles' decision, stoking controversy over which account to believe.
