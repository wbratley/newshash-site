Burkas represent 'security risk', says Reform Scotland deputy leader Thomas Kerr

Article text

Reform's deputy leader in Scotland has said burkas represent a "security risk" that he finds "extremely intimidating". Thomas Kerr argued that Muslim women ought not to be permitted to wear niqabs and burkas in public across what he described as a "Christian nation." The MSP insisted that full-face coverings are incompatible with contemporary Scottish life. "I just find it extremely intimidating. I don't think it's right. I find it very, very wrong that in our society, in this day and age, that people can walk around and make sure that their faces aren't seen. I think that's wrong," he said. Reform has initiated two separate Holyrood debates in recent weeks pushing for a prohibition on face coverings, though its most recent motion singled out the burka and niqab by name. Roughly one in every hundred Scottish Muslim women is estimated to wear one of the garments. Mr Kerr explained: "When you are covering your entire face, it becomes a security risk because you never know who is behind that." He continued: "I think that we are in a Christian nation which is built on Christian values. "I think that if you're going to integrate in our society properly, then you should live by the rules that we have, and that means learning the language, that means speaking how we speak, that means integrating properly with our society." The Reform deputy leader dismissed accusations of racism, noting that 24 nations - among them EU member states France and Denmark - have already enacted burka bans. Mr Kerr also stood by his regular use of words such as "strangers," "invaders" and "fighting-age men" when discussing migrants reaching British shores, saying the language reflected how his constituents speak. "I don't know what else you would call hundreds of young men coming across the English Channel other than an invasion of our borders," he stated. When challenged on whether his rhetoric was calculated to heighten fear, he replied: "There's nothing scarier than hundreds of young, fighting-age men coming to your country who you don't know. "There's nothing scarier than seeing the city that you love changing dramatically overnight. There's nothing scarier than seeing Glasgow city centre becoming lawless, or seeing people being housed over your own kids." LATEST POLITICS NEWS He maintained that his true target was not Muslim women but what he termed "the hypocrisy in the political establishment," contending that politicians were too eager to punish working-class young men who conceal their faces at protests over immigration. First Minister John Swinney was among those who levelled accusations of racism at Reform over its campaign. Green MSP Kate Nevens said: "We saw it with Enoch Powell, we saw it with the BNP and UKIP and now we're seeing it from Thomas Kerr and Reform." She added: "Nobody will seriously believe that Thomas Kerr is genuinely intimidated by women wearing burkas or niqabs. "We can all see exactly what he's doing, and there is nothing remotely original or subtle about it." She branded the Holyrood face-coverings debate "another desperate, cynical and deeply irresponsible attempt to manufacture division for political gain." Mariam Ahmed, chief executive of Amina The Muslim Women's Resource Centre described Mr Kerr's stance as both misogynistic and racist.
