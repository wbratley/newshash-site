Man, 76, is arrested for 'concealing his identity' amid angry clashes at anti-migrant protest in Portsmouth after police warned against face coverings

Article text

- See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source Police have arrested three men, including a 76-year-old for 'concealing his identity', after the gathering of hundreds of anti-migrant protestors in Portsmouth on Saturday ended in violence. After a march through the city, skirmishes took place as around 400 protesters and activists from a rival demonstration organised by Stand Up To Racism left Guildhall Square. Though originally claimed to be a peaceful protest, anti-migrant protesters were filmed shouting 'scum' before scuffles broke out as officers sought to prevent them breaking through police lines. The demonstration followed protests by Right-wing groups last weekend, which saw roughly 2,000 men dressed in black and wearing balaclavas block access to the port of Dover. The organiser of that protest, Danny Thomas, took to social media on Friday to admit that he would not attend today's event. Yet he said 'as many people as possible' should still turn out in the coastal town. Hampshire Police had warned those attending today of powers in place that make it illegal to wear a face covering to conceal identity while attending protest activity. It means officers can arrest anyone in a designated area for hiding their face during the demonstration using powers under the Crime and Policing Act. After violence broke out between anti-migrant protesters - some of whom were wearing face coverings - and police, officers were seen detaining several people. police warned anyone using coverings to hide their identity could be arrested Police were seen speaking with the man and asking him why he was wearing the face covering Hundreds of anti-migrant protesters had gathered in Portsmouth by mid-afternoon Anti-migrant protesters are marching in Portsmouth today, as police warned attendees not to cover their faces and vowed to make more arrests Police speak to the co-ordinator of the South Coast Patriots march before it set off in Portsmouth An anti-migrant protester wears a black face covering, despite police warnings this could lead to arrest At least three people have been arrested: a 33-year-old man from Portsmouth on suspicion of obstructing a highway, and a 16-year-old male from Portsmouth on suspicion of violent disorder, in relation to violent public disorder at Eastney Marina on Sunday, September 6. A 76-year-old man from Southampton was also arrested on suspicion of wearing/using an item to conceal identity at a protest. The man, wearing what appeared to be black scarves tied across his face and head, was seen in handcuffs on Saturday afternoon. A police officer at the scene also said one man who was arrested had been detained on suspicion of criminal damage. Protesters initially gathered in the city centre at All Saints Church in Commercial Road at 12.30pm, with protestors carrying placards with the victims of the Southport stabbings and singing football chants, before the group moved to Guildhall Square. But chants quickly included: 'deport, deport, deport the f****** lot' and 'whose streets, our streets'. A man with a megaphone addressing the anti-migration protest began chants including 'stop the boats'. He said the protest was against 'illegal men invading the shores' and said it was not about having an attitude of 'f*** the police'. Hundreds also gathered in a counter-demonstration organised by Stand Up To Racism - the two sides were kept apart in Guildhall Square by police officers. Protests and counter-protests are also taking place in other UK cities including Newcastle and Glasgow. Earlier on Saturday, Hampshire Police said: 'We will support those who want to express their views peacefully and lawfully, but we will not tolerate those whose only aim is to create fear and incite violence.' The anti-migrant protestors had to follow a designated route, and organisers Portsmouth Patriots and other aligned groups were allocated a specific location to gather near All Saints Church. Failure to comply with conditions could lead to arrest, the force said. A counter demonstration planned by Stand Up To Racism gathered in a neighbouring car park adjacent to the protest, holding signs including 'RNLI are heroes, no-one is illegal'. But as the counter protestors left the Guildhall, they were followed by anti-immigration protestors who attempted to block their exits. Scuffles then broke out between the two groups, with police trying to break up the fights and keep the factions separate. Many of the protestors then allegedly covered their faces, in violation of the police order. Despite the arrests, Police and Crime Commissioner for Hampshire & Isle of Wight Constabulary Donna Jones said: 'I'm pleased that today's protests in Portsmouth took place without major issue. 'Thankfully we didn't see repeated scenes of Sunday night with assaults on police officers and criminal damage to police vehicles and people's property. 'Hundreds of people took part in today's protest, and with the exception of a few people, it was peaceful and conducted in accordance with the new law calling for their faces to be uncovered. 'The people of Portsmouth deserve to go about their business without fear and disruption. 'While some may think it was arbitrary to use the new power directing people to remove face-coverings, the police ensured it was implemented equally and impartially for everyone involved. 'I will ensure this power is used proportionately in the future for both sides of the spectrum of protestors. 'Hundreds of officers were needed to ensure today's event passed peacefully. Earlier in the afternoon, the majority of protesters did not wear face coverings, although one man on the anti-migrant side - who appeared to be wearing a covering in the style of an Islamic niqab - was filmed being spoken to by police. Asked why he was wearing the face covering, he claimed: 'I am converting'. A man on a mobility scooter wearing a plastic face mask was also stopped by police at the Guildhall Square. He was spoken to by officers about the policing powers in place and then removed the mask and continued on his way. Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood met police on Thursday for reassurance they are ready to move quickly in dealing with 'far-right groups' seeking to intimidate communities after the events in Dover and Portsmouth last weekend. Overnight last Sunday, several hundred people gathered at Eastney Marina after a dinghy's arrival across the English Channel in a bid to prevent the 120 migrants leaving the area on coaches. Official vehicles were damaged and a small number of police officers were injured, Hampshire Police said. About 200 people were dispersed early on Monday. In Dover, crowds of masked men blocked road access to the port last Saturday, in scenes which were condemned by politicians of all parties. Yesterday, the force announced that a man has been charged with violent disorder and assaulting a police constable in the execution of their duty in relation to Sunday's protest in Portsmouth. They said the arrest of Del Leon Somerville, 42, of Paulsgrove, Portsmouth, was the first of what they anticipated to be 'a number of' detentions regarding the 'violent public disorder' and hit back at claims online that the event was a peaceful protest. Somerville appeared at Portsmouth Magistrates' Court, sitting at the crown court, this morning. He did not enter any pleas at the short hearing to charges of violent disorder between September 5 and 8 in Portsmouth, and assaulting a constable on September 11 in Waterlooville. Prosecutor Lucy Paddick told the court he was accused of throwing a bottle towards a police line and was allegedly heard telling officers to 'f*** off' as the bottle was thrown. Somerville is also alleged to have kicked out at PC Peskett, making contact with the officer's right thigh, when he was arrested, the court heard. He was remanded into custody at the hearing, supported by members of his family in the courtroom, ahead of his next appearance at Portsmouth Crown Court on September 16. Ms Mahmood said: 'These scenes have caused disruption for people and, given that people are dressed in black wearing balaclavas, there is a fear factor and obvious malign intent being communicated to our residents. 'This makes it different from other protests, so we need to make sure we are learning lessons.'
