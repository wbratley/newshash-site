Reform's £36m donor Ben Delo: Oxford-educated crypto king who was thrown out of three primary schools, pardoned by Trump and became UK's youngest self-made billionaire

Article text

Reform's £36m donor Ben Delo: Oxford-educated crypto king who was thrown out of three primary schools, pardoned by Trump and became UK's youngest self-made billionaire - See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source He dresses in modest Uniqlo, until recently lived in the same tiny rented flat in Hong Kong for well over a decade and was expelled from three primary schools before being voted the undergraduate most likely to become a millionaire by fellow students at Oxford University. British crypto billionaire and free speech philanthropist Ben Delo, 42, has hit the headlines for his staggering £36million donation to Reform UK, matched - as The Mail on Sunday reveals today - by fellow crypto investor Christopher Harborne. So moved was he to make the mega-payment, Delo moved back to the UK earlier this year after the government put a cap on expat donations when he accused Sir Keir Starmer of 'stacking the political deck against the most popular opposition party'. Delo promised at the time to 'donate millions more to Reform' and 'build a war chest' to help its leader Nigel Farage fight the next election. But who is the man whose gift is intended to swell Reform's coffers by £1million a month in the run-up to the last possible date for a General Election, donated in one lump sum to avoid any further further tightening in donation rules? Maths prodigy Delo grew up in Oxford where his early education seemed to mark him for a rather different life with one of his three expulsions for vandalising the teachers' toilets at his primary school. But after being diagnosed with Asperger's syndrome, a form of autism, he received the specialist help which saw his talent for maths recognised and nurtured, leading him to state his ambitions in a school project at the tender age of 16 as 'computer programmer. Internet entrepreneur. Millionaire'. Ben Delo, the co-founder of the crypto trading platform BitMEX, has donated a record £36million to Reform Nigel Farage (pictured on September 6, 2026) said he was 'honoured' and 'humbled' by the gift His drive and talent propelled him to a place in the hallowed halls of elite Worcester College, whose alumni include Rupert Murdoch, Harry Potter star Emma Watson and the Queen's son, Tom Parker Bowles. Nevertheless he was never far from the edges of trouble, narrowly escaping being kicked out of Oxford for internet piracy, an episode which also led to fellow students dubbing him 'the second most likely to go to jail'. After graduating with a double first in maths and computer science in 2005, he worked for global tech and consulting firm IBM and then alongside the legendary Russian-born mathematics genius Alex Gerko, one of Britain's richest men, at trading and investment experts GSA Capital. Delo soon became involved with the early days of Bitcoin after moving to Hong Kong to work for JP Morgan and becoming fascinated by the technology, regularly, he has said, meeting other 'geeks in a pub' to discuss it. In 2014, he resigned to co-found the Hong Kong-based crypto exchange platform BitMEX, which now trades billions in cryptocurrency futures every day, with two American expats, Arthur Hayes and Samuel Reed. His meteoric success saw him become the UK's youngest self-made – and Bitcoin - billionaire by 2018, the same year he married his wife, entrepreneur Pan Pan Wong. By 2019, BitMEX's annual trading volume had reached $1trillion with Delo owning a 30 per cent stake in the company, which was valued at $3.6billion. But controversy followed when in 2020, along with his fellow founders, he was charged by the US government with failing to introduce anti-money laundering controls. They accused the company of targeting American users even though they were based off-shore. And although at the time Delo pleaded guilty and paid a $10m (£7.5m) fine two years later as part of a settlement, he has vehemently defended his behaviour. He claims that early crypto pioneers were unfairly targeted and prosecuted with what was a 'non crime' while his detractors say the conviction is a further sign that Reform's funding is coming from 'dodgy sources'. Pro-crypto Trump revoked Delo's and others' convictions last year as part of a loosening of regulations which its founders believed unfairly stifled the industry. Delo welcomed the pardon and said he was 'grateful to Trump', but he expressed his frustration that it had detracted from his 'actual achievements and actions' and called it 'some spurious blip running a start-up'. Others in the industry have also heavily criticised what they saw as unjustified US crackdowns. 'We were pioneers – and I think as pioneers we took the brunt of regulators who didn't understand,' he said. 'We ran our business very straight. I built BitMEX to be mathematically perfect. I took immense care to build a system that never, ever lost a single penny.' Hand in hand with his stunning financial success and burgeoning wealth came a fervour for defending free speech which he says was triggered by his belief that Paul Chambers, a man who was prosecuted for a 2010 tongue-in-cheek tweet that he would blow up an airport if his plane was cancelled, should never have been pursued. Since the landmark case, he has spoken out against the arrest of thousands of people who have been arrested for social media posts calling it 'authoritarianism' and has vowed to help prevent the truth being threatened by political correctness. He has already used his wealth to help fund free speech movements in the UK to combat this, including Lord Toby Young's Free Speech Union and the Committee for Academic Freedom, which campaigns against speech restrictions in universities. A friend of renowned free speech proponent and academic Jordan Peterson, he has passionately defended free speech and spoken out about 'an absence of common sense'. He has previously explained he felt driven to defend something which should be a 'constitutional principle', becoming a 'reluctant philanthropist' in the process. 'The Government should be defending this, not attacking it,' he said. 'It unfortunately comes down to individual philanthropists to help defend it.' 'I'd always just taken for granted that as a British citizen, we had rights .. freedom to enquire, freedom to think, freedom to speak.' 'I very much hope that these issues are resolved via a democratic process and we move away from this kind of authoritarianism and policing of opinions and policing of thought. Then I can focus my resources on other, more needy, areas.' Despite his professed philanthropic reluctance, Delo has in fact already donated more than £100million to fund research and education in maths and science, to support autistic children and of course to champion free speech. He has funded a series of mathematics initiatives, from the advanced London Institute for Mathematical Sciences to Numberphile, the hugely popular YouTube channel. He has also passionately criticised Labour's cuts to advanced maths programmes as 'short-sighted'. Speaking to the Telegraph earlier this year, he said he was 'still more of a maths geek than a business tycoon'. 'I enjoy the challenge of building things but getting rich has never been my driving motivation. I always knew that however much money I made, I would give the bulk of it away.' After their high-profile UK return, workaholic Delo is also still promising his wife a much overdue honeymoon, having previously dubbed her a 'BitMEX widow'. That time may come soon as BitMEX will close down permanently at the end of this month following a strategic review. For now, he has defended the UK as a 'great country' and says people need to 'believe in Britain'. And while the focus is on his vast donation to Reform UK which he hopes will indeed lead to greater things for the country, one other recent donation may also say just as much about Delo. He has apparently donated funds to renovate the staff lavatories at the primary school in Oxford which he once vandalised.
