Kimi Antonelli becomes youngest champion in F1 history! Third-place finish at Mexico Grand Prix secures title triumph for 20-year-old superstar in race won by Max Verstappen

Kimi Antonelli becomes youngest champion in F1 history! Third-place finish at Mexico Grand Prix secures title triumph for 20-year-old superstar in race won by Max Verstappen Sobs and wailing, and neither he nor we could fully explain what someone so youthful had done. ‘Champion of the World,’ Kimi Antonelli was told over the Mercedes radio by his race engineer Peter Bonnington. And the crying started. For once the tension, he admitted, had got to him this weekend. That is how Antonelli felt going into the Mexico City Grand Prix, an incident-laden one, a controversial one, but one from which he emerged emotional as the youngest winner of the Formula One title in history, at 20 years and 68 days. Nessun Dorma played after he was hailed on the podium, for he was also the first Italian to achieve the feat since Alberto Ascari in 1953, and the operatic master Puccini bowed his knee. Then, having finished third on the day, behind winner Max Verstappen and Lewis Hamilton, those pair of giants, with 11 titles between them, lifted the newest member of their exclusive club on to their shoulders. This was a few minutes before Antonelli had put his gloved hands inside his helmet and all the lad’s emotions poured out after he crossed the line. Max Verstappen and Lewis Hamilton lift the new champion Kimi Antonelli on their shoulders after his third-place finish secured the title at the Mexico GP Antonelli celebrates becoming the youngest world champion in F1 history Antonelli embraces his team having secured the title with a third-place finish in Mexico But he was, agonisingly, made to wait here on Sunday after a high-speed crash involving Valtteri Bottas delayed his route in the hot sunshine and thin air of this sprawling city, the honking horns of its roads a contrast to the calm he has exhibited all season – well, until the end and it was all over. While Ferrari squabbled – more of which later – the details of Antonelli’s coronation being punctuated by 33 minutes: Bottas’ front left wheel was suddenly half torn away from its hinges, still attached yet askew, and his Cadillac was suddenly transporting him as a passenger, a big snap. It looked like a suspension failure. The race was suspended as the Finn received treatment and the fencing was mended. He hobbled away. So, a red flag was waved and Antonelli prepared to go through a second dose of lights-out torture from fourth place at Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez. A further delay followed when Racing Bull’s Arvid Lindblad was punted into the air in a tussle with McLaren’s Oscar Piastri. A safety car was deployed. Even from the original start, long before the Bottas mishap, the drama had been set up in the most intriguing way. Antonelli started on the second row alongside Ferrari’s Charles Leclerc with the only two men who could possibly delay his elevation to champion status in front of him, pole-sitter Hamilton and George Russell. One of the front-two pair had to win or Antonelli was champion. And even then only if he finished outside the top nine in the case of Russell winning, or outside the top 10 if Hamilton were victorious. It is a massive 1,000 yards into the first corner and experience suggests that pole here is not always the advantage it usually is. Slipstreaming from pursuers had been key in the past here, and highly dramatic, and it raised the question whether Antonelli would try to impose himself at this early point. Or sit back. He had, as it were, a three-foot putt to win The Open, but in this case a mighty right-hander waited to test his race-craft and nerve. Antonelli, pictured during the Mexico GP, did enough on the day with a composed drive Antonelli's family embraces him after he crossed the finish line to complete his ambition He looked calm on the grid in the immediate build-up, chatting to his dad Marco under a back umbrella protecting them from the intense heat. He kissed his mother Veronic on the cheek. His little sister Maggie was also in attendance. The good news for Antonelli is that he was not snared at that opening corner. He held on to fourth position. Verstappen, a past master at that very place and already a five-time winner here, was the big bolter. Russell fell back to sixth, an emblem of his miserable season. But the real drama was at the front as the two Ferraris tangled at Turn 4, after Leclerc used Russell’s slipstream down the opening straight to press his claims. They were wheel-to-wheel going into Turn 4. Leclerc went down the outside. The Monegasque could not make the corner and cut across the grass into the lead. They did not quite touch. Hamilton, who made it around the bend, albeit with a slight wobble, fell back to third. Verstappen cashed in on the internecine squabble to go second. ‘Pushed off by my team-mate,’ complained Hamilton. The stewards opened their investigation. ‘What is happening about Charles?’ asked Hamilton. A five-second penalty came Leclerc’s way. Hamilton, who later passed Leclerc at a restart, thought this unfair. He had a point. Leclerc’s questionable lunge had granted him free air at the front. Hamilton asked his pit wall for them to be switched around. This was not so easy because Verstappen was sandwiched between them. Antonelli is draped in the Italian flag as he takes in the magnitude of his accomplishment The new world champion receives adulation from the crowd in Mexico City Yes, there was self-interest in Hamilton’s comments but it is hard to argue with his logic. Though, as it turned out, once Leclerc had served his penalty Verstappen just jumped him in the pits for the lead. Russell, meanwhile, had run into Isack Hadjar on the eventful opening lap and sent the Red Bull No2 to the back of the grid. The stewards’ prosecution workload doubled. He was landed with a 10-second penalty while running fifth. It never rains on George’s side of the garage. Ha, but meanwhile, the boy prince Kimi was running fourth, and Russell, his bag man, was going to fall further behind once he had sat out his punishment, finishing sixth. A brittle cocoon of calm was created around the likely-to-be champion. Though teetotal, Antonelli promised a night with family and friends they would ‘remember for ever’. Who could begrudge it? Four races, or four laps of honour, remain.
