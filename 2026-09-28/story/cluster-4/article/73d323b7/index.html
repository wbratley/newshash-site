Labour conference live: Healey vows to take Britain into ‘new age of industrialisation’ in face of growing uncertainty over Budget

Log in John Healey will vow to take Britain into a “new age of industrialisation” in the face of growing uncertainty over the October budget. As he sets out an economic vision in his address to the Labour Party conference for the first time as chancellor on Monday, he will insist that Britain’s “industrial past is being remade now for the modern world”. He is also expected to confirm more support for Britain’s shipbuilding industry as part of his pledge, which will include investment in the Royal Navy’s Scottish submarine base. It comes after Louise Haigh announced on Sunday that the government was investing £115 million into a state-of-the-art marine research vessel commissioned to “meet the new challenges we face”. Mr Healey’s speech at lunchtime comes amid speculation he could raise taxes to combat severe economic headwinds. Andy Burnham had already acknowledged he and the chancellor face a “challenging” set of circumstances, and are prepared to take tough decisions to deliver his change agenda. On Monday, the Confederation of British Industry said private sector firms saw falling activity in the three months up to September, and expect activity to continue to decline for the rest of the year. Recap: Highlights of Sunday's opening day Kicking off the first day of Labour’s conference yesterday, Andy Burnham declared that a free social care service in the style of NHS will be in Labour's next manifesto as he promised to “grasp the nettle” of reform in England. He described social care in England as being as “unfair as American healthcare, where the most vulnerable in society can lose everything.” The prime minister rejected suggestions a free care service could cost taxpayers £18 billion a year, telling BBC “it’s not that high”. Transport secretary Heidi Alexander revealed that Avanti West Coast services will be under public ownership from March next year. Meanwhile, Louise Haigh announced that the government was investing £115 million into a state-of-the-art marine research vessel commissioned to “meet the new challenges we face”. Throughout the conference, Mr Burnham was receiving updates on the major incident near RAF Fairford, which he described as “deeply concerning”. More than 250 people staged a protest in support of Palestine Action at the conference on Sunday, with police arresting dozens of demonstrators. Scores of police stood by before moving in to start making arrests, telling protesters they were holding up a sign supporting a proscribed organisation. Speaking at a fringe event at the Labour Party conference in Liverpool, Sir John, professor of politics at the University of Strathclyde, said it was a “message that the prime minister seems to have quite clearly learned”. Asked if there should be an early election, he said: “No.” Earlier he said: “You have heard a lot about the mega polls in recent days, and these, in a sense, put a clear sense of reality on the fact yes, the party is ahead. “Yes, it’s now ahead of Reform, but that in itself… is not enough for anybody to contemplate the idea that this party is currently, at least, in a position where it could contemplate an early election.” He also said Reform UK peaked in the polls “12 months ago” and if anyone would succeed in eliminating leader Nigel Farage “it’s going to be the Conservatives” not Labour.
