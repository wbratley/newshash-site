Counter-terror police arrest 'car bomb plot' suspects near RAF base that launched Iran strikes after member of public calls 999

- See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source Counter-terror officers were seen arresting five men after in dramatic pictures from a foiled attack at RAF Fairford, which was first reported to police by a local. The heroic resident is understood to have phoned the air base to no avail before she dialled 999 at 12.45am to report men 'acting suspiciously' around three white vans. Footage showed the men being arrested and sitting on the road shirtless while handcuffed, indicating officers may also have suspected they were wearing suicide vests. Viable incendiary devices and chemicals were found inside one of the vans, police said. The men are in custody on suspicion of preparation of a terrorist act and offences under the Explosives Act. A bomb disposal robot from the Army's specialist explosive ordnance disposal team was later seen moving between the three parked vans. The rear doors of two of the vehicles were open and what appeared to be large black barrels could be seen scattered outside. RAF Fairford, in Gloucestershire, is the US Air Force's command outpost for operations from Europe and has been used for American strikes on Iranian missile sites that have been attacking ships in the Strait of Hormuz. It was the subject of a warning from Iran's Revolutionary Guard in July, with the regime warning Britain not to allow US bombers to fly from the base. Today's arrests are not believed to be part of a pre-planned operation, meaning police were previously unaware of the plot. Around 85 homes were evacuated from the nearby Cotswolds countryside as armed police and the SAS swooped in while the Army's specialist explosive ordnance disposal team searched for more bombs. A Government spokesman said Andy Burnham was being 'kept updated' from Liverpool, where the Labour conference is getting under way today. The incident will raise fresh questions over decisions to be made on defence by the Prime Minister and his Chancellor John Healey at the Budget next month. Mr Burnham's predecessor Sir Keir Starmer initially refused to permit US bombers to use British bases after the outbreak of the Iran war in February, drawing the ire of US President Donald Trump, who said the former Labour leader was 'no Winston Churchill'. Sir Keir soon relented and gave the US permission in March for the 'specific and limited defensive purpose' of destroying Iranian missiles 'at source'. Speaking this afternoon Mr Trump said the operation to arrest the plotters was 'fantastic'. He said: 'Working with Britain, it was an amazing job. We had them under investigation, they were looking to do big damage to our fort and working with the British worked out great. 'We had them under view for a long time.' RAF Fairford has reportedly been put on Delta alert, the highest threat level used by Washington's Department of Defense. Meanwhile security has been tightened at other US bases in the UK, with reports that RAF Lakenheath in Suffolk has been placed on Charlie alert, the second-highest threat level. A spokesman for the 48th Fighter Wing told the Daily Mail: 'We constantly assess a variety of factors that determine what measures are implemented or modified. 'Please be assured that the Tri-Base area (RAF Lakenheath, RAF Mildenhall, and RAF Feltwell) remains vigilant and will take appropriate action to ensure the safety and security of US service members, US and UK civilians, contractors, and their families.' 'Evidently, they phoned the airbase police, who didn't answer the phone,' he said. 'It just rang out so she dialled 999.' Road blocks and armed police have been in place near the base in recent days and emergency vehicles are parked nearby. Having lived in the village for three years, he said he found it 'pretty safe' but added some other residents had noticed increased police patrols recently.
