Moment teenage Norwegian hitman is arrested in his boxer shorts for plotting to assassinate UK target for Iran-backed gang as he is jailed for 20 years

Article text

Footage shows the moment a teenage Norwegian hitman was arrested in his boxer shorts – as he was today jailed for 20 years after plotting to assassinate a target in the UK on behalf of an Iran-backed gang. Johannes Natland, 18 at the time, flew from Stavanger, south-west Norway, to Britain to kill a target in return for £21,000, a trial heard. He was arrested in a hotel room in Huddersfield, West Yorkshire, on March 19 last year, hours before he was due to carry out the hit – and footage of the raid has now been shown. Police found a semi-automatic pistol, a revolver and 12 rounds of ammunition in his possession, as well as £2,000 in cash. He was convicted of conspiracy to murder following a two-week trial at the Old Bailey. Sentencing today, judge Mrs Justice Cheema-Grubb said Natland was 'thrilled' by his involvement in the plot. Johannes Natland, 18 at the time, was arrested in a hotel room in Huddersfield, West Yorkshire, on March 19 last year, hours before he was due to carry out a planned assassination Natland, 19, was found guilty of conspiracy to murder following a two-week trial at the Old Bailey. He had already admitted possession of firearms Addressing the teenager at Woolwich Crown Court, she said: 'At the heart of this case is a stark fact. You agreed to assassinate another human being, a stranger to you, in a foreign country.' She added: 'Although the fact that no one was shot dead or physically harmed is important I am sure that had the intervention of the police not prevented the plan from proceeding, you would have done what you had agreed to.' The court heard how the murder plot was hatched by a Swedish gang called the Foxtrot Network, a ruthless organised crime group used by the Iranian regime. The UK sanctioned Foxtrot in April last year for their involvement in violence against Jewish and Israeli targets in Europe. Natland became involved in the Huddersfield plot when he received a message on Signal about a 'murder in England' from a user called 'Unknown Hustler', the Old Bailey was told. Natland – who is now 19 – was then added to a group chat with a Norwegian recruiter and a senior Foxtrot recruiter, Swedish-Iraqi gangster Ali Shehab, 22, known as 'Agent 47' – a name taken from the video game Hitman. The jury was not told that Shehab is a close ally of Foxtrot boss Rawa Majid, who allegedly struck a deal with Iran to target the state's enemies abroad. Under Shehab's instructions, Natland flew from Stavanger to Manchester on March 17, the court heard. Johannes Natland, 19, travelled from the oil-rich town of Stavanger, south-west Norway, to assassinate a target in the UK 'in return for money', the Old Bailey was told He was questioned by suspicious Border Force officials and ultimately refused entry to the UK but was told to return to the airport for a flight home four days later. This meant Natland was free to enter the country and he took a taxi to a hotel in Manchester. The following day, he was directed by his recruiters to a wood in Huddersfield to collect the guns and ammunition. After checking in at a nearby hotel, he messaged his girlfriend to say: 'In the bag is bang'. At 10.39pm that evening a friend asked Natland: 'U dun it?' and he replied: 'No. tomorrow.' But at 5.15am on March 19, firearms officers arrested him in his room at the Briar Court Hotel in Huddersfield. He came to the door with his hands up, imitating holding a firearm, and pretended to shoot one of the officers. Natland admitted possession of the firearms but denied conspiracy to murder. Giving evidence, he said he was 'terrified' and planned to sabotage the plot at the last minute by shooting himself in the foot. But the prosecution suggested Natland's plan was a 'lie', arguing that he had boasted to his friends about the mission. The court heard the teenager also told his then-girlfriend Sara Solbi that 'it will be a bit cool to really kill someone'. Mrs Justice Cheema-Grubb said the teenager 'had been a good student from a financially secure and supportive professional home, with every prospect of a successful life'. Addressing Natland, she said: 'To the horror of your parents and those who knew you well, the trajectory of your adolescence was seriously disrupted by cannabis and then class A drugs.' Jurors heard how the defendant – while not quite 'a hitman from a Hollywood movie' – was precisely the type of vulnerable teenager preyed upon by the Foxtrot Network. Natland's mother, journalist Ellen Kongsnes, has previously told the Mail that she did not believe her son would have gone through with the killing. She said: 'He could never harm anyone, he wouldn't have been able to do it.' Commander Helen Flanagan, head of Counter Terrorism Policing London, said: 'Natland was clearly being tasked by someone in the Foxtrot Network, an organised criminal group sanctioned by the UK and the US due to its involvement in orchestrating violence against Jewish and Israeli targets in Europe on behalf of the Iranian regime. 'The intended target of this specific assassination has never been identified, and we found no evidence of any direct link to Iran in this case. 'One of the tactics increasingly used by foreign states seeking to cause harm to the UK is the use of criminal proxies. They often recruit people online with promises of significant financial reward. 'But the reality is that those carrying out these tasks are viewed as disposable assets by those directing them. They are used when convenient and abandoned when circumstances change.'
