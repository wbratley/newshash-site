UK's hottest summer EVER: 2026 on course to become warmest on record - as ghost village emerges, reservoirs dry up in drought and 'extreme heat' warning over 38C temperatures

UK's hottest summer EVER: 2026 on course to become warmest on record - as ghost village emerges, reservoirs dry up in drought and 'extreme heat' warning over 38C temperatures - See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source The UK is on course for its hottest summer ever - with only average temperatures needed for the rest of August to beat the record, the Met Office says. The mean temperature for the summer so far, from June 1 to yesterday, stands at 16.48C – which is 1.88C above the 1991 to 2020 average, provisional data shows. To equal the 16.12C seen only last year – the UK's warmest summer on record – the rest of August would need to be just 0.21C below the long-term average, and any warmer than this would establish a new record for the country, meteorologists confirmed. For England and Wales, which have seen temperatures more than 2C above average so far this summer, it is even more likely that this summer will be record-breaking. It comes as the record-breaking drought revealed the remains of a fascinating lost village which was demolished and flooded half a century ago to create a reservoir. Stonework and the outlines of buildings in Nether Hambleton can now be seen because of the low water levels at Rutland Water reservoir in the East Midlands. More than 3,000 acres of land were submerged in the 1970s to create what was then Europe's largest man-made lake, with residents rehoused in the surrounding areas. Among the lost buildings were 18th century homes and farmland growing barley to be sold to Germany for brewing lager, and wheat which Hovis bought for breadmaking. The remains of Nether Hambleton were left in the bottom of a valley between what is now the Hambleton peninsula in the north and the hamlet of Lyndon to the south. People across drought-hit England have been told to expect no rain in this week's forecast with some areas now having been completely dry for two months. Almost three-quarters of the country is now in drought - and you can use the Daily Mail's exclusive new tool below to find out when your area last experienced rain. Some parts of Hampshire and West Sussex have not had rain for 59 days. The parched landscape will only deteriorate as Britain's sixth heatwave of the year builds from today towards one of the hottest days on record this Thursday with a 38C peak. The Met Office has issued only its second extreme heat warning in four years, in an alert saying the 'wider population is likely to experience some adverse health effects'. The 15-hour amber warning for central and southern England will run from 9am until midnight on Thursday. Another extreme heat warning was also issued in the June heatwave, but that was the first time such an alert had been issued since 2022. Separately, UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) today activated amber heat health alerts for much of England, which will run until Friday - warning of a 'rise in deaths'. The Government has urged people to stay safe by drinking plenty of water, checking on vulnerable friends and family, and avoiding the sun at the hottest part of the day. Highs of 26C in London, 28C in Manchester and 29C in Cardiff are expected today, before the mercury soars in some areas to 34C tomorrow and 38C on Thursday. The UK could also have 'tropical nights' where temperatures stay above 20C through the night. Friday is likely to hit at least 30C, before a dip to 26C across this weekend. If 38C is observed, it would see Britain experience its joint fifth hottest day on record - tying with that same temperature recorded at Lingwood in Norfolk on June 26. The hottest-ever UK day was July 19, 2022 when Coningsby in Lincolnshire hit 40.3C. It comes as the Environment Agency said 71 per cent of England is experiencing a 'flash drought' as a dry start to August followed the country's driest July on record. While there is no meaningful rain in this week's forecast for drought-hit areas of England, meteorologists have spotted early signs that showers could move across the country on Sunday – bringing the first proper rain to southern areas in two months. The Met Office amber warning states that 'adverse health effects are likely to be experienced by those vulnerable to extreme heat', while the wider population is 'likely to experience some adverse health effects'. These could include sunburn and heat exhaustion - which has symptoms such as dehydration, nausea or fatigue - as well as other heat-related illnesses. The warning said some 'changes in working practices and daily routines are likely to be required', adding: 'Some heat sensitive systems and equipment may fail, with the potential for some power cuts and the loss of other services to some homes and businesses.' Forecasters added that more people are likely to visit coastal areas, lakes and rivers 'leading to an increased risk of water safety incidents'. There was also a warning over possible delays to road, rail and air travel - with 'potential for welfare issues for those who experience prolonged delays'. The Met Office also said: 'Following a warm night, a spell of very hot weather will affect England on Thursday. 'The highest temperatures will be in parts of the East, South East and the Midlands, where 37C or even 38C could be reached. This may lead to impacts on transport, infrastructure and health.' UKHSA amber heat health alerts have been issued for the Midlands, South East, North West and South West of England, while a yellow alert will be in force for the North East. The amber alerts warn of 'significant impacts' across health and social care services due to the heat. This includes a 'rise in deaths, particularly among those aged 65 and over or with health conditions'. William Spencer, climate and first aid expert at the British Red Cross, said: 'While periods of hot weather have become more familiar in recent years, they can still pose serious risks to health. In fact, after prolonged dry and warm conditions, it is more important than ever to follow heat safety advice. There are warnings older people are more at risk from the extreme heat, with Caroline Abrahams, Age UK's charity director, saying that as people age, their bodies find it harder to adapt to extremes of heat and cold. 'Even younger and otherwise healthy people can feel the effects of very hot weather, but with temperatures forecast to reach up to 36C in some areas, the risks can be much greater for older people,' she said. She said most homes in the UK were not designed to cope with extreme heat, so it is important to keep them as cool as possible. Keeping curtains and windows closed during the hottest part of the day and opening windows overnight when temperatures fall can help. She urged people to check in on older neighbours, friends and relatives to see if they need support such as picking up the shopping or lending them a fan. She said: 'The increasing frequency of heatwaves, alongside droughts, wildfires and hosepipe bans, shows how important it is that we do more to protect those most at risk as climate change continues to bring more extreme weather.' This summer has been one of extreme heat and dry conditions, with two record-breaking heatwaves in May and June that led to the deaths of more than 2,800 people. This was followed by two further heatwaves in July, and a fifth was confirmed last week. The UK has been struggling with the impact of extreme weather including drought, heatwaves and wildfires. Some 45million people are in drought-stricken areas, and 27million customers are under hosepipe bans as rivers and reservoirs run low. Rainfall in July ranged from 1 per cent of the average in the South East to 26 per cent in the North West. Farmers have warned of a 'brutal harvest' for crops and a lack of feed for livestock, and there have been warnings of food price inflation shocks later in the year as a searing summer across Europe feeds through from fields to supermarket shelves. Meanwhile firefighters continue to battle a series of fires across the UK, including one in the New Forest National Park which began in a van and spread to nearby heathland. Families were forced to abandon their cars on Sunday and flee for their lives after police feared the fuel-laden vehicles could become 'bombs' if caught in the flames, and told occupants to flee to 'stay alive'. Dozens of fire crews fought to control the blaze, which leapt through acres of 'tinderbox bracken' by the A31 near Ringwood in Hampshire. The area hit by the blaze is protected heathland and home to a number of rare species. Hampshire Fire and Rescue Service said crews had worked overnight as more than 120 firefighters attended the scene. Elsewhere, South Wales Fire and Rescue Service said crews were attending 'multiple wildfire incidents', including at Cwmavon Woods in Blaenavon, Wattsville in Sirhowy Valley and Abernant in Aberdare. The North East and Yorkshire have moved to 'prolonged dry weather' status, the stage before drought, and nowhere in England is in 'normal' status, the EA said. Downing Street defended the Government's response to the heatwave and wildfires, insisting it was focused on the immediate response and long-term resilience to increasing extremes, from working with the National Drought Group to backing major investment in infrastructure such as reservoirs.
