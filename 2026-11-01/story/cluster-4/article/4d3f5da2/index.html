Judge tosses Trump's $10bn lawsuit against Wall Street Journal over Epstein birthday card just days before midterms - Which US president are YOU? Take Daily Mail's quiz and find out Donald Trump was told by a federal judge that he was 'grasping at straws' as he threw out the President's $10billion lawsuit against the Wall Street Journal over his alleged 'birthday card' to Jeffrey Epstein. Trump had sued the paper and media mogul Rupert Murdoch after it published a letter last year, allegedly signed by the President and featuring a lewd drawing with the message: 'May every day be another wonderful secret.' US District Judge Darrin Gayles in Miami dismissed the suit with prejudice, ruling that the president did not prove the defendants made defamatory statements with actual malice as required by law. The ruling said the lawsuit had attempted to 'manufacture' flaws in the Journal´s investigative reporting. When a case is thrown out 'with prejudice,' it cannot be refiled again and the dismissal is permanent and incontrovertible. A spokesperson for Dow Jones, which owns the Wall Street Journal, told The Daily Mail: 'We're pleased with the court's decision.' Meanwhile, when reached by The Daily Mail, a spokesman for President Trump's legal team said the President intends to appeal the ruling. 'President Trump will continue to press forward in his powerhouse lawsuit against the Wall Street Journal and all of the other defendants. The President will continue to hold those who mislead the American People with Fake News and smears accountable for their actions.' Former Executive Chairman of Fox Corp Rupert Murdoch was also sued by Trump In Saturday's ruling, the judge cited the Supreme Court's landmark 1964 ruling in New York Times Co. v. Sullivan. That decision made it harder for public figures to win libel lawsuits because it requires proof that a media outlet knowingly published something false or showed a reckless disregard for the truth, an 'actual malice' standard. 'The Amended Complaint falls short of pleading actual malice,' Gayles wrote. 'Indeed, the allegations regarding malice are conclusory, vague, sometimes inconsistent with the Article, and often irrelevant to whether Defendants knew or should have known that the statements in the Article were purportedly false.' The judge also noted that the Journal's story did not ignore or minimize Trump's denial, 'Rather, it informed readers that President Trump decried the Letter as a fake and denied writing it.'
