Romps just twice a month, porn habits & ‘virgin surge’ — 7 surprise findings from UK’s biggest sex survey in ten years

Article text

YOUNG people are having half as much sex as they did in the 1990s, a major study has revealed. The latest results from the National Survey Of Sexual Attitudes And Lifestyles all but confirm that Gen Z and young millennials in the UK have shrugged off the boozy, fast-living youths enjoyed by their parents. They show under-45s make love twice a month, on average, compared with once a week for those who were the same age in 1990. Experts suggest physical intimacy has become a casualty of the “digital revolution” of the past three decades. Pulling on a night out now seems less likely than matching on an app, and study director Soazig Clifton, from University College London, says: “Online sexual activity is now part of everyday life for many.” Most men and a third of women watch porn, the surveys found, and just under half have sent or received a sexual photo. Three quarters of all adults — 77 per cent — have had sex in the past year, but intimacy is also on the slide among older age groups. The average frequency for 35 to 44-year-olds has declined from three to two times per month. A typical woman over 45 has not had sex in at least a month. Most read in Fabulous Prof Clifton added: “Britain’s sex lives are changing. While most adults are having sex, people are doing it less often and more young people have never had sex.” The study questioned nearly 19,000 16-to-59-year-olds between 2022 and 2024 to compare with studies from 1990, 2000 and 2010. When the research was first conducted most of us had only four television channels, no internet and a simpler nine-to-five. Fast forward to the 2020s and Netflix, TikTok and 24-hour news are in our pockets, while many young adults still live with their parents and struggle to find work or afford homes. Professor Kirstin Mitchell, from the University of Glasgow, said: “Compared with ten years ago, we live in much more of a digital age. “That has an impact on so much, including how we meet people, how we interact and where we source information. It’s a huge change.” Prof Clifton added: “It’s possible that housing and economic factors play a role, as well as changes in how people socialise and high rates of mental health problems. It’s likely to be a mixture of factors.” A report published this month by The Centre for Social Justice said there is a “relationship recession” among young people in the UK. It found men in their early 20s are significantly more likely to live with their mum than a wife. The report studies internet porn — but experts and ministers are busy tackling a raft of challenges faced by millions of young people. Government adviser Alan Milburn last week said “too many young people lack opportunity” in his blueprint for reducing the number not in employment, education or training (NEETs) from its current record of 13 per cent. The Health Foundation said last year that 17 per cent of women and 11 per cent of men under 35 have mental health problems. And research by the Institute For Fiscal Studies found the proportion of 25-to-34-year-olds who own their own home fell from 59 per cent in 2000 to 39 per cent in 2022. The new sex study is one of the most thorough of its kind in the world. It includes in-depth interviews and medical test results from thousands of people. And the results offer a glimpse into the most private details of our lives. They show that young women are significantly less likely than previous generations to want kids. The proportion who said they “definitely” do not want to be a mother surged from eight to 17 per cent between 2010 and 2022, with a further 36 per cent undecided. Young women are increasingly turning their backs on the contraceptive Pill, once seen as a sign of sexual freedom and women’s rights. The proportion of women who say they have had a same-sex experience has doubled since 2010, to 14 per cent. For men, the figure has tripled to 12 per cent. Importantly, the study found most people feel largely safe and satisfied with their sex lives — 86 per cent scored “high” or “moderate” on tests of sexual wellbeing, which was measured for the first time. Revealing their study to journalists on Monday, scientists could not imagine what they will find in the next survey ten years from now. Prof Pam Sonnenberg, of University College London, said: “We’re focusing on less sex but more than 75 per cent of the population have had sex in the past year. “When we think about our digital lives, we must remember most people are intimate with a partner.
