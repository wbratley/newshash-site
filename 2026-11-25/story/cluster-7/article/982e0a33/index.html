How triple murderer and rapist with 'nothing to lose' made a mockery of our 'high security' jails by battering Soham killer Ian Huntley to death after evil pair's feud - before boasting 'I'm proud of it' in prison's yard

Article text

When Anthony Russell was convicted of three murders as well as the rape of his heavily pregnant final victim, his own barrister felt compelled to acknowledge that his client was a 'rather sad and pathetic individual'. Little has changed in the four years since then, apparently. Appearing in the dock of Court 1 at Newcastle Crown Court on Tuesday, the second day of his trial for the murder of Soham monster Ian Huntley, Russell's legal team asked for the charge to be put before him again. The bald and portly 44-year-old, wearing a prison-issue grey sweatshirt and guarded by four police officers, stood. In a barely audible voice, he responded: 'Guilty.' It brought an abrupt end to proceedings which exposed the unrelenting danger of life behind bars in our 'high security' jails and the tit-for-tat feuds which can explode into extreme violence. It also offered a revealing insight into the real Huntley: a 'cocky, arrogant… narcissist' whose notoriety over his appalling crimes led him to believe he was 'big time' on HMP Frankland's A wing. As the trial was opened on Monday, Russell cut just the 'sad and pathetic' figure his barrister first alluded to back in 2022. Gone was the gaunt, high-cheekboned, heroin-addled face which snarled menacingly for a mugshot captured by West Midlands Police after he was arrested following his week-long crime spree in October 2020. Anthony Russell, seen shortly after the attack, has pleaded guilty to the murder of Ian Huntley Child killer Huntley had been assaulted on a number of occasions in prison owing to his appalling crimes A rare whole-life sentence was subsequently imposed for the murders, meaning he had no prospect of release. In his place was a short, tubby, dishevelled man with a high-pitched voice who waved at reporters as he was led back to the cells, having been handed a second whole-life sentence for Huntley's murder on Tuesday. It's not often one sits a matter of metres away from a convicted quadruple murderer, able to see the whites of his eyes. But as Russell stared blankly at the judge, Mr Justice Hilliard, it was difficult to reconcile this meek figure with the man who had strangled a mother and her son to death, raped and murdered a woman in woodland and later battered Huntley over the head with a metal bar, boasting that he was 'proud of it.' He didn't instil fear. Again, he looked rather pathetic. Russell was handed his whole-life prison tariff for the brutal murders of Julie Williams, 58, and her son David Williams, 32, in 2020. Mr Williams was assaulted and strangled with a lanyard in Russell's flat because he believed he had been sleeping with his girlfriend. As his appalling criminal history was read out by prosecutor Matthew Bean KC on Tuesday, Russell repeatedly shouted, 'he did!', from the dock when it was suggested he 'mistakenly' believed Mr Williams had slept with his partner. Russell also admitted murdering pregnant Nicole McGregor, 31, who was found in woodland near Leamington Spa. She was attacked just three hours after they had met on the premise of taking drugs together. He was further convicted of raping Ms McGregor, whom he had assaulted shortly after she showed him a picture of her baby scan. The court heard Russell's lengthy criminal history started from the age of just 13 with convictions for criminal damage, burglary, theft, assault, affray, robbery and battery. Unfortunately, Huntley, who was 52 when he was killed, needs little introduction. The former school caretaker was jailed for life for the murders of ten-year-olds Holly Wells and Jessica Chapman in Soham, Cambridgeshire in 2002. Huntley claimed the girls' deaths had been accidental, telling jurors during his 2003 Old Bailey trial that they entered his home because Holly was having a nosebleed. She then fell into a filled bath and drowned while Huntley 'panicked and froze', he claimed. He said he then accidentally suffocated Jessica while attempting to stifle her screaming. Their bodies were found in a ditch 13 days after their disappearance, having been burned by Huntley. Prosecutors alleged that Huntley had a sexual motivation for killing the girls. This was accepted by jurors and he was sentenced to life imprisonment. His girlfriend, Maxine Carr, once branded 'the most hated woman in Britain', provided Huntley with a false alibi saying the pair had been at home together when the girls disappeared. She was actually 100 miles away in Grimsby. She has since been released from prison and lives under a new identity. For much of the last 20 years, Huntley has been held at Durham's Category A Frankland prison, with his notoriety and the nature of his crimes making him a marked man. Huntley was serving a life sentence for the 2002 murders of Holly Wells and Jessica Chapman Anthony Russell, pictured in 2020, was handed a second whole life term for the murder of Huntley In 2005, he was attacked by fellow murderer Mark Hobson while held at tough HMP Wakefield. Hobson, who was just months into his sentence for killing four people, singled out Huntley and threw boiling water over him. In 2010, he was nearly killed by armed robber Damien Fowkes who slashed his neck while Huntley was working as a cleaner in Frankland. And in a leaked recording of a phone call he made from Frankland in 2018, Huntley said he had been attacked by another inmate with a razor blade the previous year. He said the prisoner 'tried to kill me' and 'tried to cut my throat'. It is against that backdrop, then, that Russell and Huntley found themselves just a few cells apart on A wing – which caters for so-called 'vulnerable' inmates. From at least March of last year, the evil men didn't see eye to eye. That month, the pair were seen by prison officers arguing and threatening each other with Russell later claiming that Huntley had 'got in my face'. But he said he 'bit his tongue' because he didn't want to lose his 'enhanced' status on the wing. In return for good behaviour and compliance with rules, 'enhanced' prisoners are allowed extra 'privileges' such as more visits, in-cell TV and higher canteen spending allowances. In January this year, Russell was seen with a black eye, believed to have been caused by Huntley. Not wanting to be seen as a 'grass', he told prison officers he had fallen over. But around this time, Russell decided that he was going to kill Huntley. This was not, as one may assume, to gain a certain amount of infamy from attacking such a high-profile prisoner. But instead over a series of 'wholly trivial incidents,' according to Mr Bean. On February 26, Russell, Huntley and around 10 other inmates went to work in the prison's recycling workshop. It had been closed for months until the day before the attack and the prisoners in attendance were said to be in a 'happy' mood. Russell had earmarked a 4kg metal stillage post – used for stacking items on pallets – as his weapon of choice, stashed it near him as he did his cleaning duties and lay in wait for the 'perfect opportunity' to attack Huntley. When the Soham monster's back was turned, Russell began bludgeoning him around the head. The ferocity was such that when a member of prison staff rushed down to intervene, he witnessed Russell rain down a further six blows, the court heard. Russell made frank admissions about the attack when questioned by police shortly afterwards Russell previously pleaded guilty to the murder of 58-year-old Julie Williams Russell also murdered Julie William's son David Williams, 32, in Coventry Russell also admitted the murder of pregnant 31-year-old Nicole McGregor One civilian worker who witnessed the bloodshed is understood to have been traumatised by the incident. As Russell was led through the prison yard in the aftermath, video footage captured him shouting: 'Ian Huntley is dead, Ian Huntley is dead.' He then adds: 'I'm proud of it. Sorry, I'm not, but I am.' Huntley was taken to Newcastle's Royal Victoria Infirmary but never regained consciousness and died on March 7. Given the overwhelming evidence against Russell, it was difficult to understand the grounds of his defence, albeit a short trial allowed a few days outside of the confines of the prison. Indeed, shortly following the attack, Russell made full admissions. He told a prison officer in the immediate aftermath of the assault that Huntley had been 'winding us up' and 'pushing my buttons for months.' When two other prison officers arrived at the workshop, Russell was asked whether he had punched Huntley, and replied: 'No, I've hopefully killed him.' He said he had 'smacked him over the head with one of them metal bars.' He later told the prison governor that Huntley had been 'putting it on me', adding: 'I'm not getting out and I'm a natural lifer [a reference to his whole life term] so I thought, 'F*** it.' In police interview, he said: 'My mindset was, I'm not getting out, I'm not gonna just hurt him because I'm gonna lose everything that I've built up in the last five years for a little fight… that's not worth it. 'So I thought, 'f*** it, I'll kill him.' He said that since the incident in March last year, Huntley had been 'getting really arrogant, cocky, in my face, winding me up, just little smiles as if to say, 'yeah.' He added: 'I'd just had enough of it… I thought, 'f*** you, you ain't getting away with it'. I've got nothing to lose, I'm never getting out' Russell, who had pleaded guilty to manslaughter on the grounds of diminished responsibility but initially denied murder, intended to claim he had been hearing voices which told him to attack Huntley. However he abandoned this defence on Tuesday. His barrister, Tyrone Smith KC, further elaborated on his reasoning for killing Huntley. Russell said Huntley was 'manipulative' and he had been trying to 'bully and intimidate him over a period of time.' The killer told police he believed Huntley was 'protected' in the prison and thought he was 'big time.' He said Huntley was a 'horrible narcissistic person' who 'walks around the wing like he is someone big and hard.' 'He knows if something happens, it's only one person who is going to lose, and get kicked off the wing, and it's not him because of how high profile he is,' he added. He also told police how he decided when to attack Huntley. 'I picked my moment, when the officers weren't looking, and I picked up a metal bar and I wrapped it round his head,' he said. The case exposed the dangers of dealing with those serving whole life sentences who admit they have 'nothing to lose' and realistically cannot be punished further, other than having privileges withdrawn in jail. In November last year, whole lifers Lee Newell – who had previously killed an inmate in custody – and gangland assassin Mark Fellows, stabbed child killer Kyle Bevan to death in his prison cell at high-security HMP Wakefield. Former prison governor and Home Office advisor Ian Acheson said the cases should 'concern us greatly.' 'Today's verdict shines a light on the difficulty of managing people whose crimes are so grave they will die in custody,' he told the Mail. 'I can understand why there is no sympathy for either the perpetrator or victim of this horrendous attack. 'They rightly deserved to rot in jail for their crimes. But this misses the point. Frankland was once regarded as the best-performing high-security prison in the country. 'So how two hyperviolent and notorious prisoners with a history of antagonism between them ended up in a prison workshop and one armed with a large metal bar must be alarming.' He added: 'The risk between these men was foreseeable and very high. The only explanation here is a catastrophic failure of human security and intelligence.' Sentencing Russell, Mr Justice Hilliard remarked that his whole life term was a factor in the offence. 'All that could happen is that you could lose your enhanced status and, to be worth that, you would have to kill your fellow prisoner,' he said. 'You then murdered him.' He added: 'This was premeditated murder by a man who had murdered three times before.' As he returned to HMP Frankland today to see out the rest of his days from a rudimentary cell, Russell, through his barrister, admitted he still has 'no remorse' over Huntley's death. In this case, no one will shed a tear over the grim fate of either of these evil men.
