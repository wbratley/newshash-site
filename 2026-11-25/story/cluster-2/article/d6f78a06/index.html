Nvidia shares slam into reverse as euphoria over Jensen Huang comeback fades

Article text

Nvidia shares slam into reverse as euphoria over Jensen Huang comeback fades Nvidia shares slammed into reverse as the euphoria sparked by the return of Jensen Huang gave way to concerns over the fallout from the scandal gripping the company and his reinstatement. On yet another day of turmoil on global markets, shares in the AI chipmaker fell 8 per cent on Tuesday having jumped 31.5 per cent on Monday after the 63-year-old was dramatically handed his job back. The stock is still down 35 per cent from its peak last month and 17 per cent since Huang admitted cheating on his wife before resigning two weeks ago. The latest slide in the Nvidia share price dragged down other tech companies and the wider market with the Nasdaq 100 some 2 per cent lower in New York. Jensen Huang was reinstated as Nvidia boss on Monday after Donald Trump intervened Jensen Huang was given a hero's welcome on his return to Nvidia HQ in California Anthropic faced a fresh bout of selling, with shares falling more than 11 per cent to below $78 - lower than the $83 they listed at in last week's record-breaking initial public offering (IPO). It marks a dramatic bump back down to earth for the Claude AI chatbot developer after shares soared nearly 40 per cent to $115 in the minutes after the float on Thursday. The turmoil came as investors continue to fret over AI funding, profitability and resistance to data centre development – all issues that dogged the market before the Nvidia scandal and Anthropic IPO. And while the FTSE 100 edged higher, the turmoil on Wall Street is a blow to British savers with money tied up in the US stock market through their pensions, Isas and other investments. 'We very well may see more selling pressure after this relief rally runs its course,' said James St Aubin, chief investment officer at Ocean Park Asset Management in California. 'Jensen's return doesn't answer the big questions investors were grappling with.' In a move that stunned investors worldwide on Monday, Nvidia reinstated Huang less than two weeks after he resigned as chief executive following a string of affairs, including with a suspected Chinese spy. It came after pressure from investors and the White House – including an intervention from Donald Trump – despite ongoing investigations into Nvidia and Huang over ties to Beijing. Analysts warned that questions over whether Huang was targeted by Chinese intelligence, Nvidia's accounts and possible breaches of rules surrounding the export of advanced AI chips to the People's Republic still posed risks for investors. 'The immediate business risks for Nvidia may have diminished, but the political and legal risks could prove more enduring,' said Charu Chanana, chief investment strategist at Saxo. 'The circumstances surrounding Jensen's reinstatement raise uncomfortable questions about board independence, political interference in corporate governance and the rule of law.' Huang will face questions from Congress next week as members of Trump's Republican party as well as Democrats voice concerns over his comeback. Republican Tom Cotton, chairman of the intelligence committee, sounded the alarm over the smuggling of chips into China, adding: 'It's not resolved because Jensen Huang has his job back.' Fellow Republican Michael Baumgartner raised the prospect of Huang swapping his famous black leather jacket for 'orange prison pants and an orange jacket'. Duncan Ferris, an analyst at Freetrade, said: 'Jensen's return is not a full-stop at the end of the story. It answers the question of who will head Nvidia, but there is still plenty of scope for the share price to swing as more details emerge.' Russ Mould, investment director at AJ Bell, said: 'Once the party poppers have been put away, and the chants of 'Jensen, Jensen' have faded within the walls of Nvidia's HQ, investors may be mindful of a hangover as they start to weigh some of the potential political and governance implications of Huang's return to the hot seat.'
