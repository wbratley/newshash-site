Trump floats pardon for Jensen Huang as Nvidia CEO prepares to testify before Congress over Chinese ‘spy’ scandal Donald Trump said pardoning Jensen Huang was 'an option' on Tuesday but believes the Nvidia CEO will survive his testimony before Congress next week. The microchip giant's board announced Huang's reappointment on Monday after spending Sunday grilling him about his multiple affairs. Huang's dramatic return to the top of his company, orchestrated with the help of the White House, has ignited a bipartisan backlash on Capitol Hill over fears that American technology critical to national security could be falling into Chinese hands. Trump offered some advice for Huang before the tech tycoon goes before a closed-door interview with three House committees next Tuesday and public testimony before the Senate Banking Committee a week from Thursday. 'I think I want him to just tell the truth, and I mean, his lawyers are going to have to advise him, but I've always found him to be 100 percent aboveboard, and you know a very smart person,' Trump told The New York Post. In the worst case scenario for Huang, Trump said he was open to granting Huang a presidential pardon. 'It's always an option, I guess, but I don't think it'll be necessary,' Trump said. Trump also doesn't believe that Huang will need to use his Fifth Amendment privileges when he testifies.
