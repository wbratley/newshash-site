'Spy' Alice Huang's looks and background now subject of gossip in Chinese weapons factory town where she grew up after her affair with Jensen Huang was exposed

'Spy' Alice Huang's looks and background now subject of gossip in Chinese weapons factory town where she grew up after her affair with Jensen Huang was exposed Chinese businesswoman and alleged spy Alice Huang has become a sensation in the small village where she grew up after her affair with Nvidia boss Jensen Huang was exposed. The Nvidia chief, 63, had admitted earlier this month to having an affair with Ms Huang, 43, a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed whom authorities in the US have flagged as a potential Chinese spy. The revelation sent stocks plummeting and sparked national security concerns before President Donald Trump pushed to have Mr Huang reinstated at the company over the weekend. But even as both US and Singaporean authorities seek to question Ms Huang over her alleged plot to smuggle Nvidia chips into China, she has become a local celebrity in her hometown of Nanzhushan. 'We are all gossiping about it,' a neighbor who once made hand grenades at the weapons factory where Huang's father worked told The New York Times. Another said many had been discussing Ms Huang's looks, while in nearby Xingtan, a man surnamed Han said he looked up to the alleged spy. He said that times have been tough in the hills of southern China's Hunan Province, and it is becoming ever more difficult for residents to make money. For someone from the area to get rich was impressive, Han said. 'I don't care if she is the mistress of Jensen Huang,' he said. 'She is great because she is a successful businesswoman.' Chinese businesswoman and alleged spy Alice Huang has become a sensation in the small village where she grew up Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has admitted to having an affair with Ms Huang. The two are pictured dancing together at a tech conference in Taiwan in 2024 Ms Huang had grown up in the factory town, attending a school for children of workers at the weapons factory, which was owned by a state-run company the United States government has repeatedly placed under sanctions, The Times reports. She later enrolled in nursing school in the nearby city of Xiangtan, before looking for better opportunities in cities like Beijing and Shanghai. By her 20s, Ms Huang had left Nanzhushan, working for state television at the flagship channel of China's state broadcaster. She then went on to attend Hong Kong Baptist University in 2006 to pursue a master's degree in media management, but a classmate told The Times Ms Huang often wore designer brands and skipped classes – leading many of her fellow students to suspect that her connections to officials in China kept her in school. Ms Huang's first business venture then came in 2010, when she and her older sister, Huang Yu, registered a company at the address of a building inside a members-only golf course in Beijing that was a magnet for government officials and the businessmen who courted them. It is unclear what the Huang siblings' business venture entailed, but Ms Huang eventually transferred her share to her older sister. She later joined Hexi Capital in 2015, which had been founded five years earlier by Kang Wei. Kang would later reportedly introduce himself as Huang's husband to Chinese employees and employees of her company, Megaspeed, people familiar with the power couple previously told the Times. At those meetings, Kang would brag about Ms Huang's connections to the Nvidia founder, with whom she shares no familial relation, and use those ties to raise money for their business, one of the sources said. By the spring of 2020, Kang and Huang's network landed a major project – helping build and operate an AI computing center in Shanghai for the internet giant Tencent, and the couple were able to reinvent themselves as AI entrepreneurs. Ms Huang's Singapore-based company imported Nvidia chips, which US authorities claimed it was sending to China despite trade regulations When the Yangtze River Delta AI Advanced Computing Center was then finished, it featured Nvidia chips and leased computing power to companies. The data center and a company set up to acquire Nvidia servers and rent out computing power, called Century Litong, soon became a key link in the chip supply pipeline – with Nvidia chips flowing to a network of companies linked to Chinese military contractors like the Aviation Industry Corporation of China, China's state aerospace and defense conglomerate, and an AI lab linked to the People's Liberation Army. As the world then opened back up following the COVID pandemic in 2022, Nvidia's sales team introduced Ms Huang to Chinese tech giant Alibaba, according to The Times. Shortly thereafter, Ms Huang and Kang reportedly began pitching investors on a plan to buy Nvidia chips, install them in southeast Asia and sell the AI computing power to Chinese companies. By 2023, Ms Huang used a British Virgin Islands company she and Kang had invested in to buy a Singaporean subsidiary of 7Road, a gaming company, which she renamed Megaspeed. Then, as the United States imposed export controls on AI chips to China, Ms Huang started to cultivate an image of herself as an international executive. Mr Huang was seen joining Ms Huang for drinks at the conference in Taiwan It is unclear when Huang then struck up a relationship with the Nvidia boss, but reports detail how she would openly boast about how close she was to him as far back as June 2024. At a computer industry trade show in Taipei at the time, sources told the Wall Street Journal that she displayed her cozy relationship with Mr Huang to executives by summoning him to a bar where they were drinking. She reportedly told the group: 'Jensen's a great friend of mine,' and he arrived just a few minutes after she texted him to join them. The artificial intelligence chief then showed up in his trademark leather jacket, and held court with the executives in the bar for some time as a picture was snapped of the Nvidia CEO and the alleged spy sat closely beside each other. By the following year, Alice Huang pitched an AI chips deal to Chinese-based investors, touting her ability to secure Nvidia's coveted graphics processing units and her commercial relationship with Alibaba, two investors pitched on the deal told the Financial Times. Trade records obtained by Bloomberg News showed Megaspeed's imports of Nvidia products eventually topped $4.6 billion. The US government asked Nvidia to audit Megaspeed last year As Megaspeed continued to grow, though, officials in the US were briefed that Huang Le was working for Beijing's Ministry of State Security as part of an effort to learn about American-made AI chips and possibly persuade Nvidia to make its most sophisticated semiconductors available in China, according to The Times. The US officials noted that the Megaspeed executive speaks English well and has relationships with executives at the highest levels of the AI chip industry. It has since been revealed that Ms Huang maintained contact with an officer from China's Ministry of State Security (MSS) between spring 2025 and fall 2026. Separately, Washington was investigating Megaspeed over concerns it may have been involved in smuggling hardware to China – prompting the government to ask Nvidia to audit the firm last year. In its initial report, the chipmaker said the hardware was fully accounted for, but records obtained by Bloomberg News show that Megaspeed had imported 50,000 more chips than Nvidia found in its data centers. The AI chip company then pledged in December 2025 to visit Megaspeed's data centers again, but it is unclear whether that happened. President Donald Trump insisted the federal government has 'no reason to believe' Mr Huang's return to Nvidia posed a national security risk The DOJ is now leading two separate probes of Nvidia customers who may have smuggled the coveted AI chips into China. Lawmakers are also scheduled to hear from Mr Huang next week, and have called for their own investigations. But President Trump has insisted the federal government has 'no reason to believe' Mr Huang's return to Nvidia posed a national security risk as he questioned whether Ms Huang was actually a Chinese spy. 'As far as the woman's concerned, every time you have a Chinese woman that's good looking or beautiful, they say she's a spy,' he said on CNBC Monday. The president then went on to argue that if Nvidia did sell one of its coveted graphics processing units to Megaspeed, 'that would be standard' business. 'If they then took it and you know, gave it to China, that wouldn't be standard,' Trump said of the Singaporean company. 'But that wouldn't necessarily - and probably wouldn't be because I don't think he'd do it - that won't be Jensen's fault.' Still, the president urged Nvidia's board to continue its 'thorough internal investigation' of its compliance with US export controls to ensure it is protecting national security.
