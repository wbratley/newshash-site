Trump says he could pardon Jensen Huang as Nvidia CEO hauled before Congress over Chinese 'spy' affair

Article text

Trump says he could pardon Jensen Huang as Nvidia CEO hauled before Congress over Chinese 'spy' affair Donald Trump was asked if Jensen Huang may have had any more affairs with suspected foreign intelligence agents but joked 'I really didn't get into that, I got into the business of chips, much more than the business of sex' Donald Trump has said pardoning Jensen Huang is 'an option' as the disgraced Nvidia CEO prepares to appear before the United States Congress next week. The US president said he could pardon Mr Huang, but believes the boss of the tech giant should survive giving testimony to Congress. This week, Mr Huang dramatically shot back to the top of his longtime company reportedly with the help of the White House, which ignited fury across the floor of Capitol Hill over fears that American technology critical to national security could have found its way into the hands of China. Trump offered some tips to Huang before the tech tycoon goes before a closed-door interview with three House committees next Tuesday, and public testimony before the Senate Banking Committee a week on Thursday. "I think I want him to just tell the truth, and I mean, his lawyers are going to have to advise him, but I've always found him to be 100 percent aboveboard, and you know a very smart person," Trump told the New York Post. Trump said he was open to granting Mr Huang a presidential pardon, but did not think he would have to. "It's always an option, I guess, but I don't think it'll be necessary," Trump said. Trump also doesn't believe that Mr Huang will need to use his Fifth Amendment privileges when he testifies. "I don't think he needs to take the Fifth. I think it's an interesting story for people," he added. Trump was questioned if Mr Huang, who has confessed to several affairs following the scandal that one of them may be a Chinese spy, may have had more entanglements that could have been suspected foreign intelligence agents. "No, not from what I understand. I didn't really get into it very much, to be honest with you. But no, from what I understand, no," Trump replied. "I really didn't get into that. I got into the business of chips, much more than the business of sex," Trump joked when asked if Huang told him how many affairs he'd had. Mr Huang, who married wife Lori in 1985, stepped down from the top spot of one of the richest companies in the world following reports that he had a relationship with suspected Chinese spy and former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, whom he is not related to. He was also accused of an affair with Nvidia's vice president of healthcare Kimberly Powell and another unnamed company staffer, according to reports. No details on the identities of the fourth or any further affairs have been revealed. As he was being reinstated, Mr Huang said on Monday: "I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment. I can't wait to get back to work." "The Nvidia board voted unanimously to bring Huang back as president, chief executive and board member, effective immediately, after a series of meetings left directors satisfied with his 'candor and ability to lead," the company said in a statement. Nvidia did not name the government officials it consulted or say what they told the board. As Mr Huang was reappointed, US President Donald Trump said the government had “no reason to believe” Mr Huang’s return to Nvidia posed a national security risk. Mr Trump has a close personal relationship with Mr Huang and called for his reinstatement as recently as Sunday night. The president said attorney general Todd Blanche confirmed that the government “does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct”. Mr Trump called the reinstatement the “correct and proper decision”, writing that the country “needs Jensen’s leadership at Nvidia in order to ensure the success and prosperity of not only the company, but the American economy”. He also called for Nvidia to continue its internal investigation. Billionaire technology boss Huang stepped down as president, chief executive and a member of the company's board after 33 years at the helm earlier this month. Chief financial officer Colette Kress was named as interim CEO while the company searched for a permanent replacement. In an email apology to Nvidia employees he said: "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry." Nvidia has said it had "no reason to believe" that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, but that its inquiry would examine all available information.
