Log in Downing Street said it was right that A TikTok influencer had taken down a video of him miming to a song with explicit lyrics outside the door of No 10. Max Balegde filmed himself mouthing "bomboclaat p****wagon chicken nuggets", a viral meme song, having been invited to Downing Street as part of an outreach drive by prime minister Andy Burnham to connect with online celebrities. Bomboclaat is a Jamaican profanity denoting a menstrual cloth, while p**** is a slang word for female genitals. His post was “clearly not acceptable”, the prime minister’s official spokesman said. The spokesman declined to say whether Downing Street had asked Mr Balegde to remove the video. Asked repeatedly whether No 10 had made that request, the spokesman said: “It is right the creator took down that content. “It’s clearly not acceptable and that is reflected by the fact that it has been taken down. “Content creators are obviously responsible for what they choose to post.” He added it was “right that the PM listens to” social media content creators who “reach millions of people who don’t get their news from traditional media”. Earlier on Tuesday, safeguarding minister Satvir Kaur was among government and Labour figures who criticised the video. Ms Kaur, whose remit includes tackling violence against women and girls, said: "Would I use that language? Absolutely not. "Do I want to hear that kind of language anywhere, wherever it is? No, I don’t." However, speaking on LBC Radio, she defended Mr Burnham’s engagement with TikTok influencers. “I think it’s right that the prime minister talks to lots of different people and lots of different media outlets,” she said. “I’m sure you wouldn’t want me saying that I can control, or the prime minister should control, what broadcasters say outside No 10.” Her comments came after Tory leader Kemi Badenoch condemned Mr Burnham for turning No 10 into a joke following the reception for online celebrities. Influencer Luke Hamnett posted a video recreating Hugh Grant’s prime ministerial dance from the film Love, Actually inside No 10 to the Pointer Sisters’ ‘Jump (For My Love)’. He stops when he suddenly sees the prime minister waiting to sit down with him for an interview. Mrs Badenoch said on X: “When I was a new MP, we had to hand in our phones as we stepped into 10 Downing St, so it’s jarring to see a Labour PM turn it into a bit of a joke. “Hugh Grant could dance through No 10 in Love Actually because he was pretending to be prime minister. Andy Burnham actually IS the prime minister. “At a time when Trump, Xi and Putin are projecting power on the world stage, and people across Britain are anxious about the future, this looks extremely unserious. “Being relatable doesn’t mean treating the public as though they are children who need entertaining.” The prime minister’s official spokesman said: “The prime minister has been very open about his desire to communicate with the public in a variety of different ways and on the channels that they use and you’ve seen that reflected in (the) prime minister’s comms since coming into office. “And I think that particular piece of content was part of an interview, as you say, but the prime minister… engages with communications on a variety of different channels, which is a reflection of how the public gets this information these days.” Join our commenting forum Join thought-provoking conversations, follow other Independent readers and see their replies Comments
