Clip by clip, the social media influencers who are turning Burnham - and Downing Street - into a laughing stock

Article text

Clip by clip, the social media influencers who are turning Burnham - and Downing Street - into a laughing stock For Luke Hamnett, it was a 'pinch-me moment'. The former insurance salesman turned TikTok star with 1.3 million followers could never have guessed his career would take him all the way to No 10 Downing Street. Luke had been invited to the Prime Minister's official residence to interview Andy Burnham on everything from LGBTQ issues, music, the NHS, cost of living and even the PM's tattoo – a small bee on his right bicep in honour of his Mancunian stronghold. With a small crew on hand, including Mr Hamnett's publicist and his parents, Janet and Dave, the TikTok star arrived at the famous black door one day in October. Mr Hamnett and Mr Burnham already had an existing relationship, having met at an Oasis concert. And the PM's army of spin doctors thought him perfect to promote the new 'down-to-earth' premier's centrist-dad personality. The interview went without a hitch, despite Hamnett's initial nerves – not least because the army of influencers being weaponised by the PM's propaganda officers have yet to ask him a single tough question. Speaking to his local BBC radio station afterwards, Hamnett recounted how daunting it had been for a working-class boy who grew up on a council estate in Stoke-on-Trent to meet and interview the Prime Minister. But the 34-year-old could never have anticipated the almighty row into which he was about to be dragged, nor how he would be accused of debasing one of Britain's most venerable institutions. Controversial: Max Balegde filmed a lip-sync video outside No10 to a song featuring the deeply offensive and misogynistic lyrics 'Bomboclaat pussywagon Chicken Nuggets' Luke Hamnett filmed himself twerking his posterior and thrusting his hips to the Pointer Sisters' song Jump, before being 'caught' by Prime Minister Andy Burnham Andy Burnham impersonator Stuart Pidman was also invited along to Downing Street His decision while in No10 to recreate Hugh Grant's infamous dance scene from Love Actually could not have been more poorly judged. He was filmed dancing around Downing Street's gilded interior, twerking his posterior and thrusting his hips to the Pointer Sisters' song Jump, before being 'caught' when Mr Burnham suddenly appeared. The scene was meant to mirror the way that Grant's character, fictional British prime minister David, was caught by an aide dancing exuberantly within No10. Mr Hamnett's dance was released as a promotional clip for his interview with Mr Burnham. But matters quickly escalated, with claims that the PM's obsession with social media meant he was happy to demean Downing Street in order to court support among the young. Yesterday, Mr Hamnett's response to the growing row was a new TikTok post saying, simply: 'Well that escalated…' But this was far from the only incident involving content creators. On November 9, the Prime Minister threw open No10's doors to a whole host of influencers for a session of mutual back-slapping and to thank them for 'inspiring us to do things a bit differently in Downing Street'. In personally signed letters which the PM handed out as gifts to the TikTokers, he stated: 'Voices like yours are now more important than they've ever been. Hope you enjoyed your evening.' The wine and nibbles-fuelled soirée was given the Hollywood treatment, complete with a hi-tech camera called a Glambot, more commonly seen on the red carpet filming Hollywood A-listers. Beneath the grand chandeliers of No10, it provided an opportunity for influencers to make videos about their historic visit. Mr Burnham, meanwhile, was 'on the decks' as DJ, sporting headphones and playing tunes to the attendees. They included such individuals as Max Balegde, Daddy May Cooks, Paul and Phoebe Gillett (aka 'PuwTok'), as well as Andy Burnham impersonator Stuart Pidman and Sarah Burwin. Max Balegde, 27, an influencer with 3.6 million followers on TikTok and around 640,000 on Instagram was arguably the most famous person in the room after the Prime Minister. Given a prime speaking slot, he encouraged those in attendance to 'give yourself a round of applause' for having made it to Downing Street. For his part, Daddy May Cooks, a food influencer who has 1.9 million followers on TikTok, offered Mr Burnham a free pint the next time he visits Chequers – Daddy May happens to own the nearby Russell Arms pub. Essex influencer Paul Gillett also filmed his own version of the Love Actually Hugh Grant dance, once again shimmying around the famous No10 staircase adorned with the portraits of Winston Churchill and Margaret Thatcher. But the event would shortly spark one of the biggest rows since Mr Burnham became Prime Minister, thanks to Mr Balegde's decision to film a lip-sync video outside No10 to a song featuring the deeply offensive and misogynistic lyrics 'Bomboclaat pussywagon Chicken Nuggets'. Mr Burnham was interviewed by TikTok star and A&E doctor Maddy Lucy Dann about the NHS Influencer Simon Squibb filmed an apparently spontaneous encounter with Mr Burnham in Westminster, in which they discuss Britain's 'broken' education system After 124 days of the No10 team proudly boasting about a social media-focused messaging strategy, Mr Balegde's video would unleash furious cross-party condemnation in Westminster. Mr Burnham set out to use social media to win over young people, and entrusted the strategy to top aide Abby Tomlinson. Ms Tomlinson first rose to fame in 2015 after masterminding the 'Milifandom' Twitter movement, which sparked a surge of pro-Ed Miliband posts in the hope of swinging the election in the then-Labour leader's favour. Despite it failing, Ms Tomlinson remained in Westminster and rose through the Labour ranks, before proving herself invaluable to Mr Burnham during the Makerfield byelection. In attempting to bypass traditional media outlets, which might dare to ask difficult questions of the Prime Minister, the strategy allows Mr Burnham to enjoy numerous softball interviews courtesy of these TikTok stars, while claiming to still be making himself accountable to voters. At the same social media event at No10, Mr Burnham was interviewed by TikTok star and A&E doctor Maddy Lucy Dann about the NHS, during which the PM cited his previous time as health secretary in 2010. While Ms Dann appeared pleased to show off her knowledge that this had been during Gordon Brown's premiership, a political journalist may have used the opportunity to ask Mr Burnham about his record on the Mid Staffordshire hospital scandal involving hundreds of avoidable patient deaths, or the private finance initiative hospital contracts outrage which left taxpayers with bills of billions of pounds. Just eight days later, another influencer called Simon Squibb filmed an apparently spontaneous encounter with Mr Burnham in Westminster, in which they discuss Britain's 'broken' education system. The question Mr Burnham's team will be asking today, after two days of their wider political messaging being drowned out by thoughtless influencers, is whether the strategy is working. Previous prime ministers were much more deferential where No10 was concerned. It can be revealed that both Rishi Sunak and David Cameron turned down opportunities to parody the Love Actually dance scene themselves, out of respect for the historic building. In the meantime, Mr Burnham will no doubt plough on. According to research by Knox, a digital communications consultancy, he is by far Britain's most successful TikTok politician, pulling in over 77 million views on his own account in the past 90 days. Nigel Farage, by contrast, is in second place with just 19.7 million. This will give the PM reassurance that the risk of putting social media front and centre of his communication strategy is worth the reward.
