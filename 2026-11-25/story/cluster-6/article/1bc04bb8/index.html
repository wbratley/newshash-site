← Unknown substance at Manchester Co-op Live concert prompts evacuation; police investigate possible link to jewellery thefts

Police probing links between 'unknown substance' being released in crowd at US rapper Don Toliver's concert and jewellery robberies

Article text

Police probing links between 'unknown substance' being released in crowd at US rapper Don Toliver's concert and jewellery robberies The release of an 'unknown substance' into the crowd during a rap concert could be linked to robberies and reports of jewellery being stolen at the venue, police have said. Thousands of fans were evacuated from the Co-op Live in Manchester on Sunday and 88 people needed treatment at the scene. In a statement on Tuesday night, police said they had received nine reports of jewellery being stolen during the Don Toliver concert. They added 'similar robberies have been reported at two European concerts for the same artist'. No arrests have been made at this time, Greater Manchester Police said. Assistant Chief Constable Tom Harding said: 'We know that what happened on Sunday night and the imagery seen on social media will bring back painful memories of 2017. 'Thankfully, we are not dealing with an incident of such a devastating nature or motive and we maintain our belief that, based on the information available to us now, there is no wider ongoing risk to the public. 'We are aware similar robberies have been reported at two European concerts for the same artist and will be linking in with our European counterparts to better understand the wider picture of this suspected offending.' Don Toliver was halfway through a show in the Co-op arena on Sunday when an unknown substance was released Police maintained a cordon around Manchester's venue as dozens of concertgoers accused security at the venue of failing to check bags Fans pictured standing in a bright concert hall after an 'unknown substance' set off in the crowd brought the show to a halt On Monday, Toliver postponed his O2 Arena gig in London following the incident in Manchester. Footage on social media appeared to show people coughing and covering their mouths as they left the venue on Sunday. Some said there was a 'horrible smell' and were treated outside by emergency services for 'eye and respiratory symptoms'. One person was taken to hospital for treatment. Police initially thought the substance was tear gas but later described it as an 'unknown substance'. Bobbie Frizzell-Andrews, 20, who attended the concert told the Daily Mail that once the substance was released it was as if 'a stampede' had gone off and the crowd 'spread like wildfire in all different directions'. She added that she saw 'around 15 people being wheeled off in wheelchairs or with oxygen masks' and a number being 'trampled' as the crowd dispersed. 'I saw a child being carried out, held in someone's arms. I'm surprised there weren't more serious injuries. 'It was heart-breaking seeing how many people got hurt. It was just really awful...the whole thing was really quite scary and intimidating,' the carer from Suffolk said. Security at the venue have since been accused of failing to search bags. Ms Frizzell-Andrews said that she had a 'huge tote bag filled to the brim with stuff' but security 'didn't ask once to look inside it', although said there were 'metal detector scanners'. She and her friend found it 'interesting' but 'wandered in as they had been to Co-op Live multiple times'. Concertgoer Joey Howe told BBC Radio Manchester: 'I saw a lot of people streaming out and you go into this panic mode and you see a lot of people running out of the venue. 'The lights have come on and he is being rushed off stage. That horrible smell just hit you.' He said it was like 'time stopped in motion' when 'there was nothing going on'. 'We then decided to go out. People were coming through in wheelchairs, choking – it was really distressing. 'We then decided to leave the venue. It was just really distressing scenes and not something you want to see.' Mr Howe also claimed security was 'poor' and 'very relaxed' and people were 'walking straight in'. A spokesperson for Greater Manchester Police said: 'A police scene has been lifted at Manchester's Co-op Live arena as our investigation into the release of an unknown substance continues. 'As part of progressing enquiries, detectives are exploring the possibility that release of the substance could be linked to reports of a small number of robbery offences at the arena on Sunday evening. 'It comes after we received nine reports of jewellery being stolen during the same concert. No arrests have been made at this time.'
