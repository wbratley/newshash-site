BBC to reinvent Christmas telly with 24 festive specials dropping EARLY in bold bid to win ratings war

Article text

IN one fell swoop, the BBC might just have reinvented Christmas telly as we know it. The corporation has revealed that it is releasing 24 festive programmes on iPlayer on November 27. It means viewers can enjoy an array of yuletide-themed shows for almost a month before the big day. BBC iPlayer boss Dan McGolpin said: “This year, we are putting the audience in control of when they watch, with many of this year’s Christmas specials available early on BBC iPlayer. “Alongside these brand-new programmes, audiences can also enjoy cherished festive favourites from the BBC archive, making iPlayer the perfect place to begin the countdown to Christmas.” It’s a move that doesn’t just see the Beeb competing with the streamers, but actually puts them ahead of the curve. Read More on TV And other channels will soon start following suit. Among the shows the Beeb is putting out early are festive specials of Death In Paradise, Beyond Paradise and The Great British Sewing Bee, plus Mary Berry’s Christmas Countdown, The Repair Shop At Chrismas, and A Ghost Story For Christmas: The Bride’s Chamber, starring Martin Freeman. There will also be a dozen children’s shows dropping on CBBC in time for the festive season, including Dexter Procter And The Case Of The Disappearing Doctor as well as Gladiators: Epic Pranks. The corporation’s new director general, Matt Brittin, is very much a “digital-first” boss and this is certainly a sign of things to come. Most read in TV But it remains to be seen just how warm a reception the move receives this Christmas. Otherwise it might be another one of those unwanted festive gifts. UH-OH, IT’S A HARRIGAN FAMILY DO… UNDER normal circumstances, seeing MobLand’s Harrigan family and friends assemble usually leads to someone meeting a grisly ending. Thankfully the likes of Helen Mirren and Pierce Brosnan – who play gang leaders Maeve and Conrad Harrigan – were gathering in the calming surroundings of Soho House Mews in Mayfair, London, ahead of filming starting on the thriller’s third series. So it was a far more civilised affair, and it remained that way, even with two of the Paramount+ show’s most fiendish characters turning up – Eddie Harrigan (Anson Boon) and Paul O’Donnell (Emmett J. Scanlan). Mind you, show boss Guy Ritchie was there to oversee the proceedings, which probably also helped to keep everyone in line. Mobland became one of the biggest shows on Paramount+ when it debuted on the streaming service last year, with Tom Hardy in the lead role as the Harrigan’s fixer, Harry Da Souza. Its third outing was commissioned before the second season had even aired. SURANNE IS SAYING SO LONG SURANNE JONES says it was time to say farewell to two of her biggest characters. The third series of Vigil, in which she played DCI Amy Silva, aired in September. And the star says the return of Doctor Foster to the BBC this winter, a decade on from season two, was the perfect opportunity to say goodbye to that character, too. She told Women’s Health: “My gut said yes. Gemma (Foster) is getting married and hasn’t had a relationship with her son in the 10 years we’ve been away. She’s got all this stuff to unpick.” Series two ended with Gemma and Simon’s teen son Tom running away amid the couple’s toxic divorce. “I wanted to see how the adult Tom would react to what his parents did,” added Suranne, who posed in gym gear in a photoshoot for the magazine. “I’ve just turned 48 and I wanted to say goodbye to that character in a way. All the big things that I’m known for are coming to an end. I’m entering my new era and I wanted to do something new.” HBO Max will air documentary The Slightest Touch on November 26. It stars Colin Farrell and his close friend Emma Fogarty, who has a rare condition that leaves her skin so fragile it blisters and tears at the slightest touch. They attempt to tackle the Dublin Marathon together. BADDIEL CAT SHOW ON PAWS DAVID BADDIEL’s moggy miniseries is hanging in the balance – after its sponsor pulled out. He revealed the second season of Channel 4’s Cat Man, celebrating the nation’s feline friends, needs a lifeline amid the funding blow. The three-parter has been dubbed a kitty version of long-standing hit show For The Love Of Dogs. David told Virgin Radio: “Presently, series two has been greenlit, but the sponsor has dropped out and we need a new sponsor. “So, anyone who’s got some kind of cat product . . . I know this is a bit pathetic, this call out . . . please get in touch. “The last sponsor was Everypaw – they’re an insurance company – and Everypaw has dropped out.” OWAIN WYN EVANS raised £5,139,588 for BBC Children in Need with his 4 Nations Cycle Challenge. WILL TEAS UP BREAK WILL MELLOR says he would hide from the cameras on BBC drama Casualty – so he could sneak off for a cuppa. The actor, who played hospital receptionist Jack Vincent, would dodge under a desk to escape the action. Will – Jack Harte in Channel 4’s A Woman Of Substance – told the Life’s A Beach podcast: “I’d think, ‘How can I get out of the back of them shots so I can go and have a cup of tea or chill out?’. “I’m a bit lazy unless I’ve got dialogue . . . I’d duck behind the desk.” I might try that.
