BBC to unleash raft of Christmas programming earlier than ever before on iPlayer

Article text

BBC to unleash raft of Christmas programming earlier than ever before on iPlayer For the first time, a whole raft of festive shows will be available to watch in the month leading up to the big day Christmas is coming earlier than ever before on the BBC - with a stack of programmes going live on iPlayer from November 27th. Bosses hope that giving viewers access from the end of this month will allow more of them to watch some of the festive programming in the run up to big day. The programmes on offer include the Christmas specials of hit drama Death in Paradise, factual entertainment favourite Mortimer and Whitehouse: Gone Fishing, The Christmas Repair Shop, Mary Berry’s Christmas Countdown and MasterChef: Celebrity Chef Showdown. It will also cover Diane Morgan’s comedy The Mandy Who Knew Too Much, which was pulled from the schedule at the last minute amid problems last year. And BBC iPlayer will also be stacked with “familiar favourites” from previous years for audiences to watch again if they choose. But the earlybird policy does not stretch to all shows and does cover some of the BBC’s biggest programmes, likely to air on Christmas Day, which include comedy Amandaland, dramas Call the Midwife prequel Sisters in Arms and The Other Bennet Sister plus Strictly Come Dancing and author Julia Donaldson’s latest animation The Baddies. BBC iPlayer boss Dan McGolpin said the idea behind letting audiences have access to shows earlier, was to allow people more choice and control with their festive viewing. “For generations, the BBC has delighted viewers with Christmas programmes that have helped to define each festive season. This year, we are putting the audience in control of when they watch, with many of this year’s Christmas Specials available early on BBC iPlayer,” he said. “Alongside these brand-new programmes, audiences can also enjoy cherished festive favourites from the BBC Archive, making BBC iPlayer the perfect place to begin the countdown to Christmas.” Other shows going live early are Beyond Paradise, Stacey and Joe: Crafty Christmas and the Great British Sewing Bee Celebrity Special. There will also be festive offerings for the children from CBeebies and CBBC, including Dexter Procter and the Case of the Disappearing Doctor, Rafi the Wishing Wizard and What’s in Your Plant Pot? Last year’s Christmas Day offering from the BBC was branded “stale”, with almost eight million viewers tuning out compared with 2024. The Kings’ Speech at 3pm was the most watched programme with 4.6million, compared with 12.3million for Gavin & Stacey: The Finale the previous year.
