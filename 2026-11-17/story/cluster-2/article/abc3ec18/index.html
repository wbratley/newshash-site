Rapist Russell Brand's behind-bars bulletins breach prison rules and will be investigated, minister says

Article text

Rapist Russell Brand's behind-bars bulletins breach prison rules and will be investigated, minister says Russell Brand is being investigated for breaking prison rules after sending a message from behind bars professing his innocence. The Justice Secretary and victims' campaigners have reacted with fury after the convicted rapist claimed to be 'not guilty' in a statement read out on his YouTube channel by wife Laura. Less than a week after Brand, 51, was jailed for raping one woman and sexually assaulting three others, Laura passed on a message from the comedian, which read: 'I am not guilty of the offences I have been jailed for.' Under prison rules, anything intended for broadcast to the media requires a Governor's permission and prisoners cannot ask another person to communicate something on their behalf that they would not be permitted to do themselves. Prisoners found to have broken the rules can face disciplinary action. Justice Secretary Alex Norris said: 'The broadcast of a statement from Russell Brand is completely unacceptable. 'Prisoners are rightly banned from making statements that cause distress to victims. This is being urgently investigated, and I expect action to be taken if he is found to have broken the rules.' Victims' campaigners have criticised social media sites for allowing Brand to keep posting. She told The Jeremy Vine Show: 'It was an insult to all the women that stood trial... It's an insult to the criminal justice system.' The outcry came after a 'Sunday Service' show on Brand's YouTube, during which his wife said, 'Russell has asked me to read something to you today', before passing on this message from her husband: 'As you know, I am not guilty for the offences I have been jailed for, so something strange is happening.' Brand, who has positioned himself as a Christian influencer, continued: 'As for my fellow prisoners, I now know that I am in no position to judge anyone. I don't know which other men here are not guilty, and the ones that are, they are people that Christ died to save, like you and me.' Following his conviction, Brand's wellness firm Reborn advertised a 'Free Russell' T-shirt. But now both Reborn and Brand's personal website are selling a range of T-shirts and caps bearing the message 'Free Russell Brand', which are promoted as 'fundraising apparel'. The items, all priced at $34.99 (£26.50), are promoted alongside the message: 'Free Russell. Silenced for speaking out against injustice and corruption.' Prison rules state that convicted prisoners are prohibited from conducting business transactions. The investigation may also consider whether Brand, who is serving a ten-and-a-half year sentence, has benefited in any way from his crime. It is understood that officials are now establishing how Brand's statement was communicated. It follows prison bosses coming under fire for moving Brand to a cushy jail where he has access to a landline telephone in his cell. Prison sources said Brand had 'won the lottery' after he was transferred from HMP Wandsworth in south London – known as Britain's toughest jail – to HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire, regarded as 'one of the best'. Inmates at Littlehey – the largest sex offenders prison in Europe – have phones in their cells which they can use until 11pm to ring family and friends. Last week, Brand used X to launch a fightback from jail, posting a link to a petition calling for him to be freed. Meanwhile, amid suggestions that the age of consent could be staggered to bar adults over a certain age from having sex with 16 and 17-year-olds, Kemi Badenoch on Monday said it should be made 18 'for everything'. The Tory leader told Good Morning Britain: 'It is very confusing to see Labour ministers running around saying that they want to stagger the age of consent, because 16-year-olds are too young, and at the same time saying they want to lower the voting age to 16.'
