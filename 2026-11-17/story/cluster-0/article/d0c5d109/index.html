Sadiq Khan has failed London, as Mayor I will show City Hall is on your side

Article text

Sadiq Khan has let down a generation of Londoners by failing to build houses and create jobs. As Mayor I will give businesses the freedom they need to start, scale and grow, says James Cleverly London is one of the greatest commercial cities in the world. It produces more than a fifth of the UK’s economic output and is home to millions of people whose businesses, ideas and hard work drive our national prosperity. That is prosperity driven by big international firms and small local businesses alike, from cutting edge emerging technologies to the most traditional skills. But London is being held back. Instead of backing the people who create jobs, invest in businesses and take risks, Labour is making it harder and more expensive to live, work and do business. Whether in Whitehall or City Hall, Labour see businesses as nothing more than a source of tax revenue.&nbsp; Labour neither respects business or understands business. I spent decades in the private sector before entering politics and I’ve started a business in London too. My views on the importance of business aren’t theoretical, they are born out of the direct experience I have had.&nbsp; All businesses, big or small, high-tech or traditional need people. Staff and customers. And at the moment both are being priced out of the city. We need to build homes in London. Without housing, businesses cannot recruit, workers cannot afford to live near their jobs and young people cannot build a future in our capital. And without a pool of talented future employees, many businesses can’t hope to get off the ground. Under Sadiq Khan, housebuilding has collapsed to its lowest level since the Second World War. Khan missed his house building target by 95 per cent, so his response was to lower the target, not build more homes. He then missed his new target. It is an extraordinary failure of leadership. Priced out It’s not just residential building that has stalled, office construction starts in central London fell by 35 per cent last year, and city prime rents have passed £100 per square foot for the first time. A firm that wants to open or expand in London cannot find the space, or pays a record price. Some of Britain’s biggest builders are turning their backs on London. Taylor Wimpey will not pursue new schemes once its existing projects are complete, describing the economics of development in the capital as “fundamentally broken”. But it goes far beyond the construction sector. Londoners are priced out of the city entirely, employers struggle to recruit, and the next generation cannot afford to put down roots.&nbsp; As Mayor, I will rewrite the London Plan, restore confidence among developers and make it easier to build the homes and create the opportunities London desperately needs. It is a shocking indictment of Labour’s leadership that Britain’s economic powerhouse is failing the next generation so badly. London now has the highest youth unemployment rate of any UK region. At the very moment young people need a foot on the career ladder, Labour’s jobs taxes and new, stricter red tape is making it more expensive for businesses to take them on. Businesses do not need another government-designed hand-out scheme. They need the freedom and confidence to grow, invest and employ. Kemi Badenoch is setting out how we will bring down the cost of doing business, from financial services to the high street. And as Mayor, I will review every cost imposed by City Hall and cut the cost of doing business wherever I can. But this is only half the job. I will also sell London to the world, to bring investment in and show why London is the best place on earth to start a business, to invest and to grow. As Foreign Secretary, I travelled the world selling Britain, and London was always at the heart of that pitch. I saw first-hand how hard other cities work to attract the same investors. London is not competing with Manchester or Birmingham. We are competing with New York, Paris, Singapore and Dubai. We cannot take our position for granted. We must make London a city where people want to live, work and build their futures. What I hear from young, ambitious Londoners and the international business community is a growing question mark over the capital. Is it safe? Is it affordable? Is it still a place where ambition is rewarded? For the first time in decades, London’s population is falling. People are voting with their feet. Whether it is crime, the cost of living or the cost of doing business, too many people are questioning whether London is still the place for them. I am a Londoner. London made me. I love this city and believe passionately in its future. But loving London means being honest about what is going wrong and having the ambition to put those things right. I will not accept decline when London has everything it takes to succeed. London has all the right ingredients. What it needs is a Mayor who shares its ambition, understands what makes businesses tick and is prepared to fight for them. I will be that Mayor. From the smallest independent business to the world’s largest companies, I want every business in London to know that City Hall is on its side. James Cleverly is standing to be the Conservative candidate for Mayor of London
