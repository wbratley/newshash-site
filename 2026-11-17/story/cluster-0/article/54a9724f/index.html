Burnham to tell sceptical City that Brexit has failed UK

Article text

Andy Burnham will tell a sceptical City of London audience that the UK lost more than it gained from Brexit ahead of a summit this Friday.&nbsp; The Prime Minister is set to argue that Brexit has failed the country in his Lord Mayor’s Banquet address at the Guildhall on Monday evening.  He will say that closer trade with the EU was a “win-win” for the UK, even as closer alignment on financial services has been ruled out at the upcoming summit on 20 November.&nbsp; He will say: “It’s like the Emperor’s new clothes – where no one is prepared to say the obvious truth. Well, I say it as I see it. And to be frank, the Emperor has nothing on. “The Brexit we were given has not worked. We have lost more than we gained.” City&#8217;s EU fears The address at Mansion House will be offered to a sceptical City audience that has raised an eyebrow at Burnham’s push for closer alignment and access to the EU. Morgan Stanley researchers said that while studies have shown that Brexit dampened the UK’s growth prospects, there were “multiple uncertainties” hanging over closer alignment with the EU, such that projecting a boost in investment “cannot be viewed with any conviction”.&nbsp; TheCityUK, the main industry body for financial services, has called on the government to pursue closer relations on areas such as security yet it has said the UK should ditch some EU regulations on risk and data.&nbsp; Other City figures said firms had already invested in rebuilding organisations after Brexit as further debates around moving closer to the EU could be a distraction.&nbsp; Burnham gets Brexit pushback Brexit campaigners from around 2016 have also remade groups hitting back at Burnham’s push for closer alignment.&nbsp; Lord Michael Gove has backed a ‘Stay Out’ research group that reported rejoining the EU would cost the UK nearly £40bn a year in contributions.&nbsp; Some Labour backbenchers, whose own constituencies backed Brexit, have warned that re-alignment with the EU comes with risks amid the prospect of far right politicians taking power in France, Germany and Spain over the next year.&nbsp; Burnham’s address at Mansion House will re-iterate that the government remains committed to manifesto “red lines” on Brexit, which state the UK will not rejoin the customs union, single market or rejoin before the next election.&nbsp; In his Labour Party conference address in September, Burnham suggested he would look to negotiate the possible future terms of closer trade with the EU, which could provide the basis of Labour’s campaign in the next General Election.&nbsp; The upcoming summit is expected to sign off a food standards trade deal, alignment on energy markets and provide further details on a possible youth mobility scheme.&nbsp;
