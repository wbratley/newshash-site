All the key points to expect in Alan Milburn's 'Neets' review including benefits shake-up

Article text

All the key points to expect in Alan Milburn's 'Neets' review including benefits shake-up The Mirror looks at what to expect in Alan Milburn's long-awaited report into tackling the almost one million young people not in education, employment, or work Alan Milburn will set out a national 10-year mission to slash Britain’s ‘Neet’ rate as he publishes his long-awaited report on Tuesday. The ex-Cabinet minister will insist a “lost generation is not inevitable” as he sets out his long-awaited plans to transform the welfare system. He will first present his recommendations on how to tackle the thorny issue of one million young people classed as ‘Neets’ — those not in education, employment, or work — to Andy Burnham’s Cabinet on Tuesday morning. Mr Milburn said: "Britain has had a youth Neet problem for 25 years and never a plan to end it. We are at a fork in the road and half measures will not do. “A complete system reset is needed. A lost generation is not inevitable. A work generation is possible. This is not simply a cause for young people. It is a cause for the country. We have to make the next generation a national priority. Youth participation has to run through public policy like a stick of rock." Here The Mirror looks at what is expected to be in Tuesday's long-awaited report. Cutting Neet rate to 5% A new “national ambition” to reach a Neet rate of 5% within a decade is central to the Milburn report. Currently, just shy of one million young people between the ages of 16 and 24 (13%) are not in employment, education, or training. Mr Milburn has previously noted that only Romania has a higher Neet rate in Europe than the UK. The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) said the report’s modelling shows reaching this target would mean an extra 600,000 more young people in education, employment or training each year. Around 440,000 of them would be in sustained work, it added. Legal guarantee of job or training One of the key recommendations will be that young people should be given a legal guarantee of a job or training. Mr Milburn revealed at the weekend the report will call for an “opportunity guarantee” — placing a legal duty on the government to help youngsters. He said: "It should be a guarantee for a young person. Because at the moment the only guarantee they've got is a very simple thing. To get a benefit. What sort of life is that?” But he also warned his call for a legal duty on the government would be a “straight-forward deal” for young people in Britain. He said: “If there’s more opportunities for young people there’s got to be more obligations on their part to take up that opportunity. What we can’t have is a generation living on benefits. It’s a disaster for them and its a disaster for taxpayers.” Huge investment in job support schemes In order to achieve a reduction in the Neet rate, Mr Milburn is expected to call for a huge increase in investment into schemes helping young people into work or education. While this will come with an upfront cost, the report is expected to argue it will also help to bring down the overall welfare bill in the long-term. Mr Milburn has been on fact-finding missions to Denmark and Norway in recent months where both countries have much better Neet rates. In Norway, for example, the country spends around 2% of its entire GDP on helping people into the workforce — compared to just 0.3% here in the UK. Benefits shake-up for under 25s Last week, Mr Milburn floated a contentious shake-up of the welfare state, with some young people receiving less financial support under his proposals. Under his plans, under-25s would no longer be able to claim the health element of Universal Credit or Personal Independence Payments (PIP). They would instead move on to a new system, paid at the same level as the standard rate of universal credit - but it is likely that fewer people are entitled. A total of 453,495 16 to 24-year-olds in England and Wales were entitled to receive PIP as of July 2026, according to the latest DWP figures. Mr Milburn admitted last week the plans would not be "pain-free". ‘Work allowance’ for young people The Mirror first revealed the recommendation earlier this month to give young people a new “work allowance” to stop their benefits being reduced from the first pound they earn. Under existing rules, only those with children or claimants assessed as having limited capability for work, including health conditions, get such an allowance. But everyone else loses 55p of benefit for every £1 they earn - starting from the first £1 of earned income. Under Mr Milburn’s proposal every young person currently in receipt of benefits would get a work allowance - set at a value of 16 hours a week at the minimum wage. His report will argue that for some 10 or 15 hours a week will be a stepping stone to full-time work. New national scheme to prepare young for apprenticeships The Mirror also reported last month the review will call on Andy Burnham's government to introduce a national scheme to get young people ready for apprenticeships. It will argue a new pre-apprenticeship route should be delivered in colleges and combine English and maths, technical training and substantial workplace experience with an employer. They would typically be available for 16-18-year-olds who lack qualifications, confidence, and workplace experience, and be taken instead of routes including A Levels, BTECs and V Levels. National insurance cut for firms hiring young people The report is expected to propose a gradual cut to National Insurance for firms employing under 25s. The BBC reported the move to cut the costs will start with retail and hospitality firms, which are responsible for hiring a large chunk of young people. Mr Milburn is also expected to call for the doubling of the threshold at which businesses start paying NI for 21 to 24-year-olds from £5,000 to £10,000, the BBC added.
