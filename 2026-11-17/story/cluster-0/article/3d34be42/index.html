Britain is a land of opportunity – as Lord Mayor, I will help the City unlock it

Article text

As the new Lord Mayor of the City of London, Alderman Bronek Masojada aims to unlock the UK&#8217;s economic potential by connecting talent, businesses, and investment across the country I believe the UK is a land of opportunity. That belief comes from personal experience. I arrived here from South Africa, built a career in insurance and found people who recognised my potential and gave me opportunities to succeed. At Hiscox, we raised capital on the London Stock Exchange and grew the business tenfold, creating thousands of jobs, developing skills and generating wealth along the way. My journey was not preordained. It depended on hard work, but also on people willing to open doors. As I begin my year as Lord Mayor, I want to help create those same opportunities for others by connecting talent and capital to opportunity across the UK. We have extraordinary strengths as a country. But too often, the connections between our talent, businesses and investment are missing. Addressing that challenge will be central to my year in office. It starts with championing opportunity We should be far more confident about what the UK has to offer. We are home to more than 44,500 scale-ups employing 3.9m people, alongside more than 200 unicorns – the third-largest cohort globally. We also have over £750bn of planned infrastructure investment over the next decade, spanning energy, transport, housing and regeneration. These businesses and projects have enormous potential to create jobs and drive growth. The challenge is connecting them with the investment they need. The City of London Corporation&#8217;s InvestConnect platform has already identified an initial £31bn pipeline of potential investment opportunities, with investors representing $3.8 trillion in assets under management signed up. I intend to champion these opportunities at home and overseas, making the case for investment in the UK and helping connect investors with businesses and projects across the country. But we also need to unlock more of our own capital for growth. Here lies one of the great paradoxes of our economy. The UK has one of the world&#8217;s largest pools of domestic savings, yet growing businesses and infrastructure projects still struggle to access long-term investment. International investors recognise the opportunity. In 2025, international pension funds invested approximately 16.5 times more in UK-managed private equity and venture capital funds than UK pension funds. That raises an obvious question: what does international capital see in the UK that we sometimes fail to see ourselves? The Mansion House Accord has established a clear direction, and the government is playing its part through the National Wealth Fund and British Business Bank. But the private sector needs to move faster. Employers should take greater interest in where their workplace pension contributions are invested. Trustees and investment managers should recognise the long-term opportunities available in UK businesses and infrastructure. This isn&#8217;t about sacrificing returns for patriotism. UK-managed private equity and venture capital funds delivered annualised returns of 15.8 per cent over the decade to December 2025, compared with 8.4 per cent for the FTSE All-Share. We need to create the conditions for more businesses to start, scale and stay in the UK. And we cannot talk about opportunity without talking about talent. Financial and professional services support 2.5m jobs across the UK, around two-thirds of them outside London. Yet too many talented people still see the City as somewhere that isn&#8217;t for them. This is a perception I intend to challenge, building on the work of my predecessor, Dame Susan Langley. I want to help more young people see what is possible, develop the skills and experience they need, and find genuine routes into employment and career progression. We will shortly be hearing more from the government on these shared aims too. Through my involvement with the East End Community Foundation, I&#8217;ve seen how people living just minutes from the Square Mile can feel a world away from its opportunities. That is something we need to change. Talent is everywhere, but access to opportunity is not. The City has spent centuries connecting people, businesses and investment. It has the networks, expertise and international reach to make a real difference. My aim over the next year is to put those strengths to work for more people, more businesses and more communities across the UK. Because the opportunity is already there. Our job is to make the connections that help people and businesses realise it.
