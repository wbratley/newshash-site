From personalised jobs plans to welfare cuts, Milburn's advice to Burnham

Article text

From personalised jobs plans to welfare cuts, Milburn's advice to Burnham The anticipated report will put pressure on Andy Burnham to act. These are set to be its main recommendations: BENEFITS Most likely to be the most contentious point on the Government backbenches, young people should no longer get the main sickness and disability benefits. In a major shake-up of the welfare system, under-25s would be given a new Youth Allowance rather than claiming Personal Independence Payment or the health element of Universal Credit. Mr Milburn has said he does not want 'crude cuts' to benefits but some Labour MPs will still rebel against any withdrawal of support. NATIONAL INSURANCE The amount of NI paid by firms for young staff should be gradually lowered. Over five years, the Government should reduce NI paid by firms for 21 to 24-year-olds, starting for those working in shops and pubs. The threshold at which companies begin paying the tax for this group should be doubled from £5,000 to £10,000. Prime Minister Andy Burnham listens to the speech by Lord Mayor of the City of London Bronek Masojada during the annual Lord Mayor's Banquet at The Guildhall yesterday GUARANTEED HELP Young people should be given an 'opportunity guarantee' of a job, training or employment support. It would put a legal duty on the Government to ensure under-25s have a chance of getting into work, rather than just having the right to claim benefits. Youngsters not employed or in education would lose their benefits if they reject the help offered by a new Youth Opportunity Service, which would include a personalised plan by a named adviser. EDUCATION Schools should be measured on how many of their pupils secure jobs rather than just on exam results. New numeracy, writing and speaking tests should be introduced for 13-year-olds so those who need extra help can be identified. And the target for reception-age children to be ready for primary school – meaning they can eat with cutlery and are out of nappies – should be increased from 75 per cent to 90 per cent. Former health secretary Alan Milburn speaks to the media on the publication of the interim Milburn Report into Young People and Work in May NHS Young people suffering from mental health problems should be given immediate support rather than being left waiting by the NHS. Those with mild ADHD, anxiety or depression should get counselling services or online support without having to get a formal diagnosis first. Mr Milburn has said there should also be a maximum waiting time for access to Child and Adolescent Mental Health Services for under-18s. SUBSIDIES Taxpayers' money should be given to companies who agree to take on young workers. Mr Milburn believes the Government should pay businesses as much as £7,000 for every under-25 they employ, as it will work out cheaper than giving them benefits. He said the state should 'subsidise wages' as part of ways to make it 'more financially attractive for employers to take on young people'.
