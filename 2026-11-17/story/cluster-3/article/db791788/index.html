A right royal pairing! Kate hails 'wonderful' return to Mumbai as she enjoys game of street cricket with William after couple arrive in India for first joint tour in four years

Article text

The Princess of Wales said it was 'so wonderful' to be back in Mumbai a decade after her last visit with Prince William – but admitted they needed a bit more practice when it comes to cricket. The couple were bowled over by a vibrant welcome for the start of their three-day visit to India as they were greeted by cricketing all-time great Sachin Tendulkar. Schoolchildren gathered in the streets hoping to catch a glimpse of the royal visitors and their sporting hero as they arrived. Signing autographs and posing for selfies alongside Tendulkar, William asked the students: 'Is Sachin your favourite? He's like a God or a King round here isn't he?' The princess thanked the children for coming to watch them play cricket, telling them: 'Thank you so much, it's so wonderful to be here again.' Dubbed the 'Little Master', Tendulkar kicked off the royal tour with a game of street cricket at the city's Ballard Estate, an area known for its impromptu games of 'gully cricket', for their first public engagement of the visit. He met the prince and princess as they arrived, walking down a side street of the estate before stopping off at a pop-up chai tea stall. William, wearing a KARDO handwoven cotton waistcoat made by artisans in Bengal, could be seen asking Tendulkar for some batting tips before striding out to the makeshift wicket on the closed street. Gully cricket is an informal version of the sport played by Mumbai locals in the narrow residential streets and alleyways, or gullies, that run through the city. Games spring up wherever there is space, with makeshift wickets and rules that change from street to street. William was bowled out on his third ball of the day by a student who steamed in from the north end of the street. The Princess fared better when she came in to bat, connecting with a slow delivery to cheers from the crowd who could be heard over the sounds of the Swaranjali Band of drummers, adding to the carnival atmosphere. However, she also succumbed to a looping delivery from William, nicking the ball before she was caught behind. Her husband put his hands up in triumph, clearly unbothered by the prospect of getting told off later. The Prince and Princess scheduled the cricket match at the start of their three-day visit, which will see them attend William's Earthshot Prize awards on Tuesday night, to 'highlight the power of sport to bring people together', Kensington Palace said. At the end of the visit, Tendulkar – who also played cricket with William and Kate during their 2016 trip to Mumbai – told the British media he was impressed with their batting skills and praised the prince's Earthshot movement. He said: 'However much we praise him and talk about this fantastic initiative, it's not enough, because the whole world is rallying with him. This is a beautiful journey, to be able to protect the planet and create something really special.' On the couple's cricket skills, he added: 'I was impressed with the freehand batting skills, that was there ten years ago as well. These are all lasting memories.' Cricket is the most-watched spectator sport in India and millions of people play it at a recreational level. Royal aides said the match was chosen to celebrate the shared passion for the game which has long connected the UK and India. Through the Sachin Tendulkar Foundation, he now supports initiatives focused on sports, health and education for underprivileged children. The couple also met representatives from organisations using cricket to support young people and encourage greater participation from women and girls in sport, before joining Tendulkar and Poonam Raut, a former Indian women's international, for a game with local children. Later there were a series of engagements centred around Earthshot – an initiative William started six years ago to capitalise on positive solutions to global environmental challenges. There is huge excitement in India about the awards coming to their country, with the country having the largest number of Earthshot finalists out of any country in the world. But there's no doubt that having his wife with him will sprinkle a little extra royal stardust on proceedings. Catherine hasn't travelled overseas with William for official duties since 2022 because of her health issues, but is conducting a number of joint engagements with her husband to highlight Indian creativity and its links with the UK. In a message released publicly to coincide with their arrival, the prince said: 'Catherine and I are delighted to be in India for this year's Earthshot Prize. 'India has made an extraordinary contribution to Earthshot over the past six years and is home to more Earthshot finalists and winners than any other country. 'It has also become one of the most exciting places in the world for environmental innovation, with people across the country developing practical solutions to some of the greatest challenges we all share. 'Throughout this week, we will celebrate the innovators and entrepreneurs driving that progress, as well as the young people whose creativity and determination give us so much optimism for the future. 'We look forward to meeting the innovators, entrepreneurs and young people whose ideas are helping to shape a more sustainable future.' Later this week there will also be a dash of Bollywood glamour on the green carpet at the Earthshot Prize awards as well as a high-level meeting with the Indian prime minister, Narendra Modi, arguably one of the most powerful politicians in the world. This may be a short trip, three days in all, but the Waleses are clearly determined to pack a punch. Aides say that despite the noise around family drama in recent months, this is what the real work of the Royal Family is all about: representing Britain PLC on the world stage. And there's no doubt that William and Catherine – particularly on joint engagements such as these – remain a huge draw. The royal couple had a ticket to ride on the Mumbai metro today to highlight green transport – and even Catherine's bag fitted the environmentally-friendly theme. They travelled on its newest and fastest line to show how investment in public transport is helping to support sustainable urban growth in one of the world's largest cities. Having changed from a more casual blazer, trousers and flats to play cricket this morning, the future queen looked elegant in a favourite royal blue dress, heels and a particularly eye-catching handbag. It was made by British luxury label DeMellier, who have teamed up with SurpRice to celebrate homegrown design and pioneering material innovation, transforming rice straw into a leather-inspired alternative. The bag is a special edition of their small Hudson, made especially for the Princess of Wales, celebrating the 2026 Earthshot Prize. Bringing together British design and innovative British and Indian material technology, the collaboration reflects a shared commitment to sustainability, responsible production and positive social impact. SurpRice is a next-generation material developed as an alternative to synthetic materials using rice straw and husk, by-products of rice cultivation. The material aims to reduce agricultural burning while creating additional income opportunities for farming communities in India. SurpRice, like Demellier, is London headquartered and is the first of its kind globally to use state-of-the-art European technology to manufacture at industrial scale, with material science developed in India. The couple got on at Vidhan Bhavan Metro Station and were given tickets to go through the barriers before travelling downstairs on an escalator to catch their Aqua Line train for the short journey to Bandra Kurla Complex. They also met women working as train pilots on the Metro to hear about efforts to create a more inclusive workforce. Women make up around a third of employees across the Mumbai Metro Rail Corporation, significantly higher than the national average. William spoke to representatives from British businesses supporting rail and infrastructure projects across India, a sign of important Anglo-Indian trade links are. He also chatted to participants from the British Council Climate Skills Programme, which is being delivered in India. On the Mumbai Metro the couple paid 60 Rupees (50p) for tickets to take the Aqua Line from Vidhan Bhavan Metro Station across the city. And the Princess revealed she was a huge fan of travelling on the railway at home – speaking about taking Charlotte on the Elizabeth line last month. They spoke to Stephen Long, Director and Head of Strategy at Crossrail International, and Dr Sameer Singh, managing director of UK-based Rail Vision. Rail Vision has worked in the Delhi Metro and now helping infrastructure in Mumbai, while Crossrail International has been working with Mumbai Metro Rail Corporation for the past three years and sharing best practises from London's Elizabeth Line. Speaking afterwards Stephen said: 'She (Catherine) was saying she loved the Elizabeth line and had taken it with Charlotte and was really taken with it as it was used by people from all parts of society such as lads on the way to work and people out for a night out in Covent Garden.' William spoke to a female train driver and cleaner saying 'this is your line' and 'are more women working on the line nowadays'? Speaking about getting more people to use the Metro, he said: 'The traffic is notorious.' Catherine added: 'There is so much infrastructure change since our last time here.' They last visited in 2016 and the Mumbai Metro was completed six years ago, while the Aqua Line they travelled on opened 13 months ago. While commuters boarded the train at the multiple stops as the couple hurtled underneath Mumbai, no one entered the doors of the carriage where the Waleses sat. Later William called in his first speech of the trip for urgent action to protect the planet, warning that the world 'cannot wait for everyone to agree before we act'. Calling on business and government leaders and investors to throw their weight behind solutions to environmental problems offered by Earthshot Prize finalists, he told them the future would be transformed by 'those who dare to show us what is possible'. The heir to the throne addressed the Earthshot Prize Impact Assembly after travelling with the Princess of Wales by metro to the JIO World Convention Centre in Mumbai. Six years after launching the £50million programme to find 50 solutions to the world's most pressing environmental problems, William highlighted the extraordinary progress being made but said more needed to be done to turn innovative ideas into practical solutions on a global scale. 'We know that there are still great challenges. But we cannot wait for everyone to agree before we act,' he said. 'Over the past six years, Earthshot has shown me that the people with the answers are already out there. Our task is to find them, back them and help them scale. 'So I ask everyone here to support these finalists. Give them your ideas, your influence and your investment. Help them take their solutions to the world. Because the future will not be transformed by those who simply describe what is wrong, It will be transformed by those who dare to show us what is possible.' Watched on by his wife, William opened his speech with the Hindi greeting 'Namaste' and ended it by saying 'Dhanyavaad,' which means thank you. He hailed India as an example of 'what happens when bold ideas are backed and given the chance to grow. They create jobs, improve lives and change what we believe is possible.' He added: 'Too often, the world's emerging economies are presented with false choices: growth or the environment; prosperity or protection. But India shows us a different picture. A picture full of colour, complexity and possibility. 'There is a phrase in Hindi: rang-birangi duniya - a world full of colour. 'Perhaps that is how we should see the future, not presented as false choices between people and planet, prosperity or nature… but in all its colours. A world in which solutions can come from anywhere and progress benefits us all.' Pointing out that the country reached a target for 50 per cent of installed electricity capacity to come from renewable sources five years early, he said: 'This transformation is about more than energy. It is about people finding better ways to live.' William and Catherine then sat in the front row as the session heard updates from former winners and current finalists, including Mahindra Last Mile Mobility, which is now partnering with Uber, and UK-based Matter, which is partnering with IKEA. There was laughter as Nvidia's Head of Sustainability, Josh Parker, admitted it had been 'a busy week' for the tech giant as he announced a partnership with Pula, a firm providing farmers with financial protection against climate crises. Earlier, William and Catherine were reunited with Princess Chimi Yangzom Wangchuk of Bhutan, half sister of King Jigme Khesar Namgyel Wangchuck, who hosted them on a visit to the Himalayan kingdom in 2016. Bhutan is nominated in the Protect and Restore Nature category for writing the protection of nature into its constitution, keeping nearly 70 per cent forest cover which in turn helps protect Himalayan water systems and the people living downstream. 'It's so lovely to see you again,' William told the Princess, who was joined by her husband Dasho Sangay Wangchuk. 'We haven't seen you in ages.' The royal couple also spent time with Dame Jacinda Ardern, former prime minister of New Zealand and an Earthshot Prize Trustee, and met Dalton Tagelagi, Prime Minister of Niue, a Pacific island nation which has a population of just 1,700 people. 'You've travelled a long way to get here!' William told him. Niue Ocean Wide Trust is nominated in the Revive Our Oceans category for protecting a section of ocean bigger than the UK, boosting fish stocks by 60 per cent. 'It's been amazing and you truly deserve to be a finalist,' the Prince said. 'It's incredible. The size you have to do is just vast.
