Last week, Pattinson was suddenly pegged as the front-runner in the race to play Amazon MGM's Bond by a man many consider Hollywood's go-to source for insider information. Puck's Matthew Belloni reported Friday that the terms offered to Pattinson seemed to suggest The Batman star was the top dog. Each potential Bond signed pre-negotiated employment contracts locking in the financials related to the role, and Pattinson was offered 'a whole lot more money than the others', according to Belloni. Belloni predicted Amazon wouldn't base 'their Bond on who's cheaper', given the 'stakes' of taking a new stab at the series. The offer to Pattinson, meanwhile, was also said to include a commitment to three movies as opposed to four, three sources familiar with the talks said. O'Connor and Lowden would have to agree to four. Pattinson's package was framed as the most appealing. According to Belloni, its contents, when compared to the others, 'suggests the studio is willing or even eager to sacrifice a bit to get a 'name' star to front its most important franchise'. It's thought that the original shortlist failed to impress during their auditions, which led to big names such as Robert entering the running. Robert's casting given his current involvement as Batman may seem like an odd one, but the publication has reported he is unlikely to carry on wearing the famous cape and cowl when he finishes shooting the much-delayed second film, which is currently slated for release in February 2028. Batman star Andy Serkis shared on Monday that filming for The Batman sequel had already been pushed back into 2027, as he was yet to film a scene as Bruce Wayne's butler Alfred Pennyworth. 'So, immediately after Christmas we'll be going.' Filming for The Batman 2 had begun in June, but was paused after director Matt Reeves stepped away due to personal reasons, with Steven Soderbergh later joining the project.
