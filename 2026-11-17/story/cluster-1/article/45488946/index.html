How Robert Pattinson finally got the Bond role: Callum Turner and Jack Lowden failed to impress bosses but Batman star won them over with his talents, friendship with director and his ability to deal with scrutiny It has been one of the biggest questions of the showbiz year: who will be the next James Bond? And fans were finally given the answer on Monday as Robert Pattinson was announced as the new 007. The actor, 40, best known for his roles in Twilight and Batman, will take over from Daniel Craig as the iconic British spy. Pattinson's name only really came into the mix last week, with Jack Lowden, Callum Turner and a 'mystery actor' previously said to be top of the shortlist to take over. How Pattinson has ended up landing the role of a lifetime comes down to several factors. It appears other favourites Turner and Lowden failed to eventually impress in screen tests, with Pattinson and Skins star Nicholas Hoult joining the race for the coveted part as late contenders. There were other names also floated around as potential leading men too – including Joe Alwyn and The Crown star Josh O'Connor but it appears something was missing until they saw Pattinson take on the screen tests. Journalist Matthew Belloni wrote on Monday that bosses 'wouldn't have gone through the trouble of bringing in established stars like Pattinson and Hoult if they were happy with the less well-known guys'. It has since been reported that the process to cast Bond was so secretive, it wasn't until Sunday morning that Pattinson learned he'd got the part. According to The Hollywood Reporter, it was director Denis Villeneuve himself who told Robert he would play the next Bond, after the pair previously worked together on the upcoming Dune 3. His friendship with Villeneuve must have done him huge favours in the casting, with Belloni writing in his Puck News piece that Pattinson was 'always a name the director wanted to see'. At 40, Pattinson is also the perfect age to star as the next Bond as his appeal is widespread. For millennials, his role as Edward Cullen in Twilight will bring nostalgia, while he will also be sure to attract a younger audience too thanks to Batman. Belloni also points out that Pattinson, being a well-known heart-throb, is used to scrutiny – something which anyone taking on such a well-known role will need to be accustomed to. He wrote: 'Pattinson is used to it. He's been a star since 2007. He survived the initial backlash to his Twilight casting and the infamous Entertainment Weekly cover.' The cover he mentions was released in July 2008 ahead of the premiere of Twilight and was heavily criticised by fans who claimed the styling and tone clashed with Stephenie Meyer's books.
