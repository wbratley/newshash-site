Iran plotting terrifying 'dirty bomb' attack targeting Trump administration officials in Washington DC, intelligence report says

Article text

Iranian officials are reportedly developing plans to target senior Trump administration officials by detonating a 'dirty bomb' or a chemical weapon in Washington DC. Sources familiar with the terrifying intelligence told CNN that the plans have been very specific, including locations and targets. Some of the Iranian chatter even claims that the country has stashed bomb-making materials inside the United States. US officials now believe it would be very easy – and is even likely – that Iran has pre-positioned nuclear weapons or chemical agents that could be added to a conventional weapon to create a dirty bomb, according to the CNN report. 'As porous as our border has been for the better part of 10 years, it would have been extremely easy to get operatives inside the country' who could acquire and place the bomb components,' one of the sources said. Another added that many national security officials assume that pre-positioned nuclear materials have already been in the US for years – if not decades. Dirty bombs, which package radioactive materials into a conventional bomb, are relatively easy to assemble, and officials say the US is incredibly vulnerable to such an attack. Officials have since held several meetings over the past week to discuss how to counter the threat, two of the seven sources told the outlet. Iranian officials have reportedly developed plans to detonate a 'dirty bomb' targeting Trump administration officials in Washington DC Sources familiar with the terrifying intelligence told CNN that the plans have been very specific, including locations and targets. Washington DC is pictured A billboard that reads 'We will kill Trump' is pictured in Tehran on Monday One of the sources noted that a dirty bomb is 'not a weapon of precision,' comparing it instead to 'a suicide bomber blowing themselves up.' But dirty bombs can still disperse dangerous airborne radiation for blocks or even miles, making residents sick and leaving certain areas uninhabitable. A spokesperson for the Iranian mission to the United Nations did not explicitly address the potential dirty bomb plot in a statement to CNN, but said the country 'considers the use of chemical weapons under any circumstances to be wholly illegitimate.' The spokesperson then went on to say Iran 'categorically rejects and unequivocally refutes the false and fabricated allegations circulated in this regard.' White House spokesperson Lauren Bis also told CNN that while the US is 'monitoring any and all potential threats... at this time there are no credible threats to the homeland.' For now, those familiar with the intelligence agree that Iran is just giving itself options, as they suggest the chatter about a dirty bomb is not an indicator of an imminent attack. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has been moved out of his Washington DC home amid threats on his life from Iran Still, American officials have viewed the intelligence as a signal of Iran's willingness to escalate tensions amid stalled negotiations to end the war Separately, US officials are also said to be monitoring another stream of intelligence that suggests Iran may use chemical weapons against American troops stationed across the Middle East, two officials told CNN. Several said that the troops do not have adequate protective equipment or training to guard against such an attack – prompting a parallel contingency planning effort focused on ensuring diplomatic and intelligence personnel have the necessary equipment and training to protect themselves, one of the sources said. It comes after threats from Iran prompted the Secret Service to move Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent into military housing. Israeli officials have also claimed over the weekend that Iran is plotting to assassinate Trump using explosive drones. One regional source told CNN that the next several weeks will now be critical for the US and Iran to reach any sort of agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and end the war – during which time, Iranian hardliners could be incentivized to launch an attack to keep the war going. Yet Iranian plots against current and former US officials, including a murder-for-hire plot against Trump in 2024, have been ongoing since the president's first term and have largely concentrated in the past on opportunities to kill US officials while they were traveling abroad. President Trump is now en route to Manila in the Philippines and then on to Shenzhen, China, to attend the ASEAN and APEC conferences. US and Iranian officials remain deadlocked on negotiations to reopen the Strait of Hormuz (pictured) and end the war But when Trump was asked on Monday whether he was concerned about an Iranian drone attack targeting him, he replied: 'No I'm worried about you, because if they get me, you're gone too. 'Be careful,' he warned. Efforts to learn more about the plans may be hindered, as a surveillance law that allows officials to collect foreigners' communications expired in June amid a standoff between the White House and Congress over Trump's then-acting director of national intelligence, Bill Pulte. Surveillance orders approved prior to June are grandfathered in until March, but unless Congress acts, national security officials have warned they are poised to lose one of their most effective tools for catching terrorist plots, according to CNN. In a letter sent to Republican and Democrat leaders of both chambers last week, Attorney General Todd Blanche, Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton and CIA Director John Ratcliffe urged reauthorization of the law. They argued any delay would put 'the lives of both Americans in the homeland and our troops stationed abroad at risk in the heightened threat environment briefed to the Gang of 8.' It came after lawmakers in the so-called Gang of 8, including the four bipartisan leaders of the House and Senate Intelligence committees, were briefed on Iran. It is unclear whether that briefing addressed the dirty bomb or chemical weapon threat in DC.
