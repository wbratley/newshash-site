Argentina bids Messi farewell in final national team match

Article text

LIONEL MESSI described playing for Argentina as “the most wonderful thing that can happen to you” after ending his international career with a goal in a 3-0 win over Benin in Buenos Aires. The 39-year-old ends his international career with 208 senior caps, the most of any player in the team’s history, with his 126 goals for Argentina also a record. Messi won the 2022 World Cup and two Copa Americas during his 21-year spell on the international stage. On an emotional night, Messi also created the first two goals, assisting first another departing Argentina player in 38-year-old centre-back Nicolas Otamendi and then Nico Paz. “I feel so many emotions,” Messi, who announced his international retirement following this summer’s World Cup and the death of his father, said in quotes reported by the Argentinian FA. “The first thing I think about is my old man. I can only express my gratitude; it was a tremendous honour to wear this jersey for over 20 years. “I never stopped fighting, never stopped giving my all. I swear to you that I’d like to come back for life, because playing for the Argentina national team is the most wonderful thing that can happen to you.” After a goalless first half in which Benin threatened not to stick to the script, Otamendi fired home after the visitors failed to clear a Messi corner. Paz doubled Argentina’s lead from the edge of the area after 61 minutes and inevitably Messi had the last word when Tamimou Ouorou’s clumsy challenge on Agustin Giay resulted in a penalty, which the Inter Miami forward blasted into the top corner. Argentina boss Lionel Scaloni said of his captain: “It’s been a dream come true. I never thought I’d be the coach of the best footballer in history. “The feeling of having him [Messi] on your side is incomparable… I’ve never seen that aura in anyone else in my life.” After the final whistle, Messi’s eyes glistened as his teammates gathered around him, applauding. A rumbling chorus of “Ole, ole, ole, ole, Messi, Messi” rose from the stands as tens of thousands of fans jumped, sang and bowed toward him. A banner at midfield proclaimed him the “Eternal Captain.” Football may not solve the working class’s problems, but it does matter — and politicians know it, writes BERT SHOUWENBURG
