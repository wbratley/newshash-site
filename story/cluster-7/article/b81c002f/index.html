Raya injured but Madueke rescues point for Arsenal in seesaw Newcastle draw

Article text

If Mikel Arteta was delighted to see the back of Eddie Howe as Newcastle’s head coach, the bad news is that another similarly awkward technical area adversary has been installed at St James’ Park. Arsenal’s manager never seemed to relish trips to face Howe’s old team and he certainly did not enjoy this latest trip to Tyneside, where Matthias Jaissle’s initially overwhelmed team fought back commendably to earn a point courtesy of a stellar second-half performance. At half-time the crowd had felt close to turning against Jaissle but he held his nerve and, thanks to a triple substitution and subtle tactical rejig suggested he is no mug after all. It left Arsenal – who lost their outstanding goalkeeper David Raya to a potentially significant second-half shoulder injury – still top of the Premier League, six points clear of Brighton and Manchester City, and Arteta with a face like thunder. Just last week Bruno Guimarães claimed he was “beloved” by Newcastle fans but the club’s former captain turned Arsenal midfielder found himself serenaded by a soundtrack of boos of the sort usually reserved for Sunderland players. Newcastle’s problem is that, despite spending about £275m this summer, they have struggled to replace last season’s departed star trio – Guimarães, Sandro Tonali and Anthony Gordon. That reality perhaps left Howe’s successor – who arrived just before the start of the season – making a not so subtle point with his team sheet here. Of Jaissle’s starting XI only the goalkeeper, Lukas Hornicek, the right-back Amar Dedic and the midfielder Sean Steur were close-season signings. The other five new faces were either benched (Nico González and Matias Fernandez-Pardo) or did not make the 20-man matchday squad (Aladji Bamba and Bazoumana Touré). Admittedly Ewen Jaouen is injured but, given Bamba and Toure cost a combined £78.5m, their omission represented quite a statement. Ditto the decision to bench González and Fernandez-Pardo and their individual £50m plus price tag. Meanwhile the jury remains out on Hornicek. The Czech international has made some excellent saves this season but, initially here, his kicking proved pretty much uniformly poor and he was fortunate to be reprieved after hesitating too long as the impressive Kai Havertz closed in. Jaissle’s frustration with his goalkeeper proved well apparent as Arsenal took increasing control of a first half that saw Newcastle’s lone striker, William Osula, isolated and even their normally imperious England left-back Lewis Hall given a thorough work out by Noni Madueke. Much to Arteta’s chagrin, Lewis Miley, preferred to González in the midfield holding role, endeavoured to slow the tempo but the red tide could only be stemmed for so long. When Havertz dispossessed Hall after the latter miscontrolled a long ball, he cut inside and slid a highly intelligent, beautifully calibrated, pass across to the on-rushing Eberechi Eze. Without breaking stride, Eze exhibited perfect balance and unleashed a gorgeous first-time shot that arrowed, inexorably, past Hornicek. As the home fans fell eerily silent, Arteta and his staff celebrated a goal sufficiently special to rebuke suggestions that his side are all about efficiency at the expense of artistry these days. With Declan Rice always available to reinforce his defence and smother any flickering home attack it proved a struggle to recall Newcastle making a meaningful attacking touch inside Arsenal’s box. Fans stationed near the home dugouts told Jaissle what they thought of it all. Arsenal’s pressing was initially so good that Newcastle were left almost incapable of playing short passes and, instead found themselves reduced to aimless long balls. With Rice and Martin Ødegaard apparently operating on a different midfield planet an equaliser felt like a distant dream. Yet that seemingly ambitious vision was something Harvey Barnes proved fully capable of buying into. When, early in the second half, Ødegaard made an uncharacteristic error, his heavy touch sliced the ball in Osula’s direction and the Dane played it into Barnes’s path. All that remained was for the left winger to take a single, steadying, touch before an assured finish left the hitherto redundant David Raya picking his shot out of the net. Jaissle’s second-half introduction of Tino Livramento, Jacob Ramsey and Anthony Elanga – all at Newcastle last season – at the expense of Dedic, Steur and Jacob Murphy was paying dividends. Suddenly, almost astonishingly, Arsenal were the team in danger of being pressed into submission. The home side were closing their guests down far higher up the pitch and had morphed into an infinitely more menacing team. Soon, they were ahead when a Hall corner was headed down by Dan Burn, allowing Malick Thiaw to stab the ball beyond Raya. Shortly afterwards Arsenal’s goalkeeper was helped off the pitch in evident pain after sustaining a nasty left shoulder injury during an ostensibly benign collision with Osula. On came Kepa Arrizabalaga in a triple substitution also featuring Guimarães’s exit. Madueke though was still around and he reminded everyone of his presence by meeting the counterattacking Ødegaard’s fine pass, dummying two defenders and watching his slightly deflected shot evade Hornicek’s grasp. No matter; Jaissle had done enough to win plenty of hearts and minds. Not least the chair, Yasir al-Rumayyan, over from Riyadh to watch his first match of the season and reportedly “take the club’s temperature”.
