Parents urged to vaccinate children for flu as hospital cases surge in England

Article text

Parents are being urged to make sure their children are vaccinated against flu before Christmas amid a surge in hospitalisations, with pharmacies being asked to administer them for the first time. The NHS in England is pushing to vaccinate thousands of children who missed out when jabs were administered at schools. More than 6,000 high street pharmacies are offering appointments, with bookings opening on Monday through the NHS app or the NHS find a pharmacy webpage, with appointments from Tuesday. Dr Suzanna McDonald, the flu vaccination programme lead at the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA), said: “Vaccinating schoolchildren against flu not only helps protect them, but crucially helps protect younger siblings and other more vulnerable family members at greater risk, like grandparents. “Ensuring all children from age two receive a flu vaccine every winter is so important to help prevent the thousands of unnecessary hospitalisations we see among unvaccinated children. “Please don’t dismiss the importance of a flu vaccine for your children. It will help keep them out of hospital and even save lives. Don’t put your child needlessly at risk; book that appointment with your GP surgery or at some community pharmacies offering the vaccine.” Pharmacies are also offering nasal spray flu vaccinations for two- and three-year-olds. Children can fall very ill with flu and can spread it to more vulnerable people. More than 3.5 million children from reception to year 11 have already had their vaccine. The rate of flu admissions in England stood at 9 in 100,000 people last week; up from 5 in 100,000 in the previous seven days, according to the UKHSA. Admissions stood at 4.9 in 100,000 in the equivalent week in 2025. UKHSA data shows the rate has not been at such a high level at this point in November in any previous year this decade. It confirmed the number of attendances at hospital emergency departments for flu-like illnesses was “above seasonally expected levels”. The NHS England national medical director, Prof Frankie Swords, said: “The run-up to Christmas can be such a busy time for parents, so it’s great news that for the first time, it’s now really easy to book your child’s flu vaccine at your local high street pharmacy if they missed it in school.” “Winter viruses like flu are on the rise again, which puts real pressure on the NHS and could also ruin your family’s plans for Christmas. This offer is another way to protect children, families and communities, so if you need an appointment for your child, please book now.” McDonald told BBC Radio 4’s Today programme on Friday: “Hospital admission rates have increased 80% in the latest data that we’ve published this week compared to last week … What we’re seeing is that sharp rise is coming in a few weeks earlier than we saw this time last season, but last season was the earliest season that we’d seen for over 20 years. This is very early to be seeing such high demand in hospitals.”
