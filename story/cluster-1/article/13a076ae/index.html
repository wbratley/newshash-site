Poundland collapses into administration putting as many as 12,000 jobs at risk weeks before Christmas

Article text

Poundland collapses into administration putting as many as 12,000 jobs at risk weeks before Christmas Poundland has collapsed into administration, with as many as 12,000 jobs across the discount retailer's 600 stores at risk just one month before Christmas. It comes after a month of crisis talks, with experts at corporate finance firm AlixPartners already lined up to be appointed as administrators of the company led by managing director Barry Williams. The retailer - which has been trading since 1990 - could not secure a solvent rescue deal, with a period of breathing room to help agree a takeover now having expired. For now, Poundland said all stores and offices would remain open today and tomorrow as usual. But its failure marks one of the biggest High Street collapses since BHS shut in 2016, resulting in 11,000 job loses and 164 store closures. The announcement will also reignite fury towards the retailer's controversial US owners Gordon Brothers from politicians, unions and Poundland's own management. Gordon Brothers has faced pressure to protect the chain and MPs have accused it of deliberately cutting off funds and pushing the retailer to the brink of insolvency. MPs on the business and trade select committee are keen to demand Mark Newton-Jones, the head of Gordon Brothers's European operations, returns to Parliament. Politicians have already met with the firm behind closed doors - but they will now be eager to grill the company at a public hearing as soon as possible. Some 12,000 jobs across discount retailer Poundland's 600 stores are now at risk Poundland said all stores and offices would remain open both today and tomorrow Poundland managing director Barry Williams Billionaire retail magnate Chris Dawson was among those said to be considering a rescue deal - having done the same with Wilko and Homebase in recent years Earlier this month, the group issued a second notice of intention (NOI) to appoint administrators, allowing it ten working days extra to hold talks over a rescue deal without the risk of creditors forcing the business into administration. However, the retailer was not able to strike a deal over the period. It is understood owner Gordon Brothers had been in discussions over whether another NOI could be filed, which would need to have taken place this afternoon. But the US investment firm instead prepared to appoint administrators and was advised that it had 'no choice but to file for insolvency' today. Poundland's ownership had received a number of offers, including from Irish real estate firm Martin Property Group, investment firm Xyra Holdings and German investment firm Zachert Private Equity. 'Del Boy billionaire' Chris Dawson - the founder of CDS Superstores, owner of The Range, Wilko and Homebase - had also entered the race to buy some stores. Prime Minister Andy Burnham called for executives to work with the Government to find a solution that saves shops and jobs. Bally Auluk, Usdaw national officer, said today: 'This is devastating news for our members, who were only recently praised by management for helping to turn the business around, and now face the threat of losing their jobs. 'There is never a good time for this to happen, but just before Christmas has to be the worst. This decision has been made by the owners Gordon Brothers, despite failing to engage with Usdaw, which is a disgraceful way to treat our members. 'We are now seeking urgent meetings with the administrators, where we will be looking for an open dialogue about how we can save as many stores and jobs as possible. 'We know that Poundland could have a future; the company has turned around its fortunes and there are a number of buyers who have shown interest, including a management team buyout.' He added: 'Our members are grateful to Government ministers and MPs who have tried to engage with the owners, and we have had useful meetings with them, along with Poundland management. 'We are calling on the Government to continue to support our campaign to save jobs and do what they can to give Poundland a future.' AlixPartners said the appointment of Clare Kennedy, Daniel Imison and Kevin Wall from its firm as administrators were 'made at the request of the directors of Poundland following their filing for insolvency at the High Court'. The global consultancy added that this 'follows a longstanding and public sales process for the business'. It added: 'Despite the efforts undertaken in this period, which spanned an extended period of moratorium, this sales process did not identify a viable solvent solution for the business as a whole.' 'The administrators and AlixPartners will now assume responsibility for M&A activity and will pursue all options including sales of the business and its assets, either in whole or part, from administration. 'Poundland will continue to trade as those options are examined and all stores and distribution centres remain open at this time.' Poundland launched in 1990 in Burton-upon Trent with a business model of selling items for £1 - but inflation over the decades since has put pressure on this price point. In 2019, it dropped its 'Everything's £1' slogan and introduced multi-tier pricing across its UK stores in a response to inflation over the decades. The retailer - which has been trading since 1990 - could not secure a solvent rescue deal The retailer has also come under intense competition from rival discount businesses during the cost-of-living crisis - a period when it might have been expected to thrive. Barry Williams and Shaun Wills from the Poundland board of directors said in a joint statement today: 'The board of directors has today taken the difficult decision to appoint administrators to the Poundland business. 'It is with sincere sadness and intense regret that we have to tell you that we had no alternative but to take this decision, bringing to a close 36 years of history at Poundland and Dealz in Ireland as we know it. 'Following the removal of all borrowing facilities and access to our bank accounts by our lender and majority shareholder on Tuesday 27 October, the Board has explored - and sadly exhausted - all options to have adequate funds released that would allow the business to trade solvently. 'Furthermore, we have been informed today, despite our positive engagement with prospective bidders, there is no prospect of an acceptable solvent offer, and consequently there is no prospect of our owner and lender continuing to fund the business. 'Under these circumstances, it is impossible for the Board of Directors to see how Poundland can continue to operate as a going concern. Therefore, mindful of our legal obligations as directors, we believe we have no alternative but to take this action.' Mr Williams and Mr Wills added that they would carry out a series of virtual and in-person briefings tomorrow. They continued: 'We would like to take the opportunity to put on record our unreserved thanks and gratitude to all our colleagues, who have worked tirelessly in returning the business to positive sales and profit over the past 18 months. 'We also extend those unqualified thanks to our suppliers and other stakeholders who have remained supportive, even though the circumstances have put incredible pressure on their own businesses. 'We regret, deeply, that their hard work and commitment has not been rewarded as we would have all hoped.' Reform UK's Treasury spokesman Robert Jenrick accused Prime Minister Andy Burnham of having 'stood by as the Greedy Brothers have stripped out a high street store and risked 12,000 jobs, which could have been saved by multiple buyers'. He added: It's an outrage. These shysters starved Poundland of cash and have cost thousands of workers and suppliers their jobs just before Christmas. ;They should never be allowed to invest in British businesses again.' Tesco previously held talks over proposals to buy up to 150 Poundland stores, while Poundstretcher owner Fortress also showed its interest in purchasing around 250 store. Other grocery chains such as Iceland and Lidl were also previously in the frame to acquire some stores. A spokesman for the administrators said: 'We appreciate this is a deeply concerning time for all involved - customers, suppliers and, in particular, Poundland colleagues. 'Our priority now is to work with all stakeholders, including representatives from government and USDAW, as we identify the best possible route forward. 'We would encourage anybody interested in acquiring the business in part or as a whole, as well as UK retail operators with open employment opportunities, to contact The Administrators.' And a spokesman for Gordon Brothers said: 'Poundland Limited has entered administration. First and foremost, our thoughts are with Poundland colleagues. We recognise how difficult the continued uncertainty is for them. Efforts to preserve as many jobs and stores as possible will continue, led by the Administrators. 'This appointment follows an extensive three-month M&A process, which explored every avenue to secure a sale of the whole company. 'Commercial negotiations with interested parties for both the whole of the company and with established retailers interested in acquiring substantial portions of the business will now continue, led by the Administrators, and conclude through the administration process. 'Poundland has faced several years of instability. In July 2025, Gordon Brothers supported the company's Restructuring Plan - stepping in when no one else was willing to finance the business - and provided £85million in new financing and contributed substantial efforts to turn around and stabilise a business that was in free fall. 'Supported by Gordon Brothers, the management team and colleagues at Poundland worked hard over the past 12 months to improve the underlying performance and achieve £100million of cost savings. 'Despite a reduced cost base, looking forward, the business is still vulnerable to inflationary pressures or any downturn in sales and margin. We recognise and we are thankful for all the hard work and effort by management and colleagues throughout.'
