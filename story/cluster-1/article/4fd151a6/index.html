Poundland collapsed after owner withheld funds, bosses say

Article text

Poundland’s owners withheld the funds the retailer needed to stay afloat, its management has said, leaving the company with no option other than administration.&nbsp; The now-collapsed retailer’s directors told its 12,000 staff that they were forced to trigger an administration after they &#8220;exhausted all options to have adequate funds released that would allow the business to trade solvently”. Poundland’s collapse is one of the biggest high street failures in years. It has been described as “deeply concerning” by Andy Burnham and “devastating” by the UK’s retail workers’ union.&nbsp; Barry Williams and Shaun Wills, the retailer’s board of directors, told staff that this collapse was triggered after it was starved of cash by Gordon Brothers, the US investment firm which owned it. In a memo seen by City AM they wrote: “It is with sincere sadness and intense regret that we have to tell you that we had no alternative but to take this decision, bringing to a close 36 years of history at Poundland and Dealz in Ireland as we know it. “Following the removal of all borrowing facilities and access to our bank accounts by our lender and majority shareholder on Tuesday 27 October, the Board has explored &#8211; and sadly exhausted &#8211; all options to have adequate funds released that would allow the business to trade solvently. “Furthermore, we have been informed today, despite our positive engagement with prospective bidders, there is no prospect of an acceptable solvent offer, and consequently there is no prospect of our owner and lender continuing to fund the business.” Williams and Wills added that it became “impossible” for management to see how it could continue to operate Poundland as a solvent business without access to more funds. They added: “We would like to take the opportunity to put on record our unreserved thanks and gratitude to all our colleagues, who have worked tirelessly in returning the business to positive sales and profit over the past 18 months.” In collaboration with Alix Partners, who were appointed as the administrators to Poundland, the retailer’s management will be holding a number of in-person and virtual meetings with staff on Thursday, sources said. During Gordon Brothers’s sale of Poundland it faced repeated accusations that it had been stopping the retailer from accessing vital funds needed to keep trading. Liam Byrne MP, chair of Parliament’s business select committee, has accused the firm of “starving” Poundland of access to funds. After the retailer’s management filed their intention to appoint administrators in late October, Gordon Brothers agreed to release the funds the company needed to continue trading. The investment firm decided that it would not provide the further funds Poundland’s management say they needed to continue trading for any longer under the notice to appoint administrators, it is understood. “This was a decision between either putting in more money or finding a buyer. They were the only ways to move forward. They elected not to put in more money, and they couldn&#8217;t find a buyer,” one person with knowledge of the matter told City AM. A spokesperson for Gordon Brothers said it “continued to approve funding for payments which were necessary for the trading of the business during the NOI period.” After failing to find a viable bid to take over the entire company, Gordon Brothers and Poundland’s directors decided that administration was the only option remaining for the retailer. The spokesperson for Gordon Brothers said: “This appointment follows an extensive three-month M&amp;A process, which explored every avenue to secure a sale of the whole company.&nbsp; “Commercial negotiations with interested parties for both the whole of the company and with established retailers interested in acquiring substantial portions of the business will now continue, led by the Administrators, and conclude through the administration process.” The spokesperson added that Poundland has faced “several years of instability”. Gordon Brothers propped the retailer up with £85m in new financing to “stabilise a business that was in free fall,” they added.
