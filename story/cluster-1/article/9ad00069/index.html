Meghan's father Thomas Markle 'cut off ankle monitor after arrest' amid child abuse material charges

Article text

Thomas Markle, the father of Meghan, has been accused of cutting off his ankle monitor before a court appearance over child abuse charges. Prosecutors say the 82-year-old, who was arrested for allegedly keeping child abuse material inside storage units in California, allegedly ripped off the tracking device early on Tuesday morning. US Attorney Bill Essayli said Mr Markle, who denies the charges against him, was told he must wear the device on his ankle before appearing before court. However, the prosecutor alleged that at 1.45am on Tuesday morning, he cut the ankle monitor off his body. On social media, Mr Essayli urged the court to revoke his bail and hold him in custody pending trial. "Release conditions are court orders, not suggestions," he wrote. Judge Lindsay Griffin granted the US Attorney's request today, saying Mr Markle had "tampered" with the monitoring device and stressed he must be taken back into custody as his living situation had become "untenable". Following his arrest on Friday, Mr Markle was placed under house detention at his assisted living facility in Florida, where he has been residing while recovering from a leg amputation. He had relocated to the Florida rehabilitation centre earlier this year after living in the Philippines, where he had moved following a series of strokes. The FBI executed search warrants on storage units in Los Angeles that had been registered in Thomas Snr's name since 2008, with the most recent payment recorded in October this year. Agents recovered approximately 78 items from the units, comprising film negatives, slides and books that allegedly contained child sexual abuse material. The US Marshals Service, which is responsible for tracking the data from Mr Markle's ankle monitor, said it was urgently working to establish the circumstances surrounding him allegedly taking the device off. LATEST DEVELOPMENTS A spokesman said Thomas Sr had been blocked from returning to his senior living facility on Friday after being bailed. He was subsequently "released to the custody of his other daughter" Samantha, they added. Judge Griffin said he would be willing to release Mr Markle from custody if he is able to find alternative accommodation which would be suitable for his home detention. Following his arrest on Friday, FBI director Kash Patel said the bureau would deploy every available resource to safeguard children, warning no predator would find "safe haven in this country or anywhere they try to hide". Philippine authorities obtained a warrant last month to examine devices that had allegedly been removed from Thomas Sr's former residence in the country. He had been living in Cebu until May, having moved to the Asian nation the previous year after suffering multiple strokes. Separately, the Metropolitan Police revealed in October it had received a letter in 2019 containing a "number of claims" about Meghan's father. The force acknowledged passing the information to the Sussexes' office but confirmed it had not forwarded it to other law enforcement bodies. When the allegations first surfaced in October, the Duchess of Sussex said: "I am deeply distressed at the news that has come to light in the last 24 hours, and my thoughts are with any children who may have been harmed."
