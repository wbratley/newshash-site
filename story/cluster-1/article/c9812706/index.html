Man City charges news: Triple punishment agreement as Premier League chiefs speak out Manchester City boss Enzo Maresca faced the media for the first time since the club were found guilty as they prepare to take on Liverpool in the Premier League Welcome to Mirror Football's live blog for Friday, October 9, as Manchester City prepare to return to Premier League action for the first time since being found guilty of breaking Premier League financial rules. The club have already confirmed their appeal after an Independent Commission found them guilty on 114 of 115 charges levied against them between 2009-10 and 2017-18. They revealed that more than £830million of owner funding was used to inflate revenues through 'sham' agreements. In addition to City's appeal, there is now also a punishment to await. The range of sanctions can go from a fine to a points deduction or even expulsion from the Premier League. Enzo Maresca held his first press conference following the news as he previewed City's clash against Liverpool. The head coach presented a defiant response as he was probed on his and the club's position. City are set for a frosty atmosphere at Anfield on Sunday, with the Reds being one of four clubs to have reserved the right for a legal challenge against their rivals. Follow all of the updates below... Key Events Thanks for joining us! That brings our live coverage to an end this evening. We'll be back tomorrow morning with all the latest news and opinion on the charges case. So be sure to join us then. You can read all the latest news HERE, in the meantime. Man City 'held settlement talks with Premier League' BEFORE guilty verdict on charges Manchester City reportedly held settlement talks with the Premier League prior to the independent commission delivering their guilty verdict last month. City, still strongly deny any wrongdoing and have appealed the decision after they were found guilty of all but one of the alleged charges. Now, in a report by Bloomberg, the Premier League side held talks with the top-flight's governing body to discuss an 'early-stage settlement' but those discussions broke down. The report claims that any punishment would have been a hefty financial payment, rather than the stripping of titles and relegation, according to those sourced. It's claimed those talks quickly fell apart with City adamant about their innocence in the matter. The sheer amount of charges means the reported financial proposal would have only reduced the size of the case, rather than resolved it. Man City whistleblower Rui Pinto goes BACK into witness protection after 'fearing for his life' Rui Pinto has had his protection restored by the Portuguese authorities in a rapid U-turn. Pinto’s Football Leaks website was key in Manchester City having 115 Premier League charges levelled against them. City have since been found guilty of 114 of those, with a number of potential punishments on the table should their appeal be dismissed. Pinto in the meantime has also been left uncomfortable, having seen his witness protection revoked by the Portuguese authorities. He had previously received protection since 2020. But the 37-year-old whistleblower has now had that restored for the time being, with a statement signed from the Ministry of Justice stating that Pinto had not been adequately informed of the grounds for terminating the programme or given the opportunity to exercise his right to challenge the decision. Vincent Kompany makes first statement on Man City guilty verdict as Enzo Maresca convinced Vincent Kompany has admitted he is 'not too occupied' by former club Manchester Citybeing found guilty of the majority of the 115 charges brought against them by the Premier League. An independent commission found City guilty of all charges relating to breaches of the Premier League's financial regulations between the 2009-10 and 2017-18 seasons. The Etihad club, though, have appealed against the verdict and claimed in a statement the commission's ruling contained "clear material errors, of law, principle and fact and is unsafe". Kompany was at City throughout the period in question, having joined the club in 2008 before spending 11 years there. The current Bayern Munich boss was also named City captain in 2011, a role he held until he departed in 2019, but he refused to be drawn on the case involving his old club. He said: "I'm only thinking about my work at FC Bayern. We have important games coming up, and that takes a lot of time." Premier League executives name one Man City punishment they're 'not expecting' Premier League club chiefs are not expecting Manchester City to be directly thrown out of the league if the guilty verdict against breaches of financial rules is upheld. City are appealing the verdict, which rules them as guilty of 114 breaches over a nine-year period. The three-person panel ruled that City artificially inflated their revenue through ‘sham’ sponsorship deals that were funded by the club owners, something that is now allowed under the financial regulations. It’s something that City strongly deny. There has been a lot of talk over what sort of punishment could be handed to City should their appeal against the verdict be unsuccessful. The sanction will be decided by the same three-person panel that delivered the initial verdict, with the Premier League able to give their recommendation. Lawyer surprised Sports lawyer Dan Chapman is surprised that Manchester City's chiefs had yet to take a 'damage limitation' approach to their appeal. "We now know that the Independent Commission, based on the Premier League's investigations and not simply the Der Spiegel leaked documents, have made multiple findings of fact based on actual evidence and we no longer need to speculate as to the authenticity of documents," Chapman told TEAMtalk. “And so for me the strategy deployed by City from the outset has always been high risk at best, or extremely dangerous at worst. If City cannot win this appeal, or win out via subsequent legal processes, they are making the sanction stage of these proceedings more difficult for themselves. But they have been nothing if not consistent in their approach. "Time will ultimately tell, but I see no imminent sign of damage limitation mode being deployed yet. A punishment for seven seasons would be fitting." Rules must be upheld Sunderland boss Regis Le Bris refused to comment on Manchester City's situation but believes the rules of competition must be upheld to ensure fair competition. "There are still ongoing legal proceedings so it’s not for me to comment on that," he said. “My position is simple: football has rules on and off the pitch and you have to respect those rules. When they are broken, you should have consequences otherwise it’s not fair, and we want fair competition, we want fair play. So the relevant authorities have to be relentless, otherwise you lose the value of your competition. "Even it is hard to understand exactly what the charges are as it happened ten years ago, more or less. We were not here in Sunderland, for example. But it is about respecting rules, so it’s key because if you don’t have fair competition it’s a bit weird. That’s more or less the feeling we have in the building." What if you lose the appeal? And what happen if the appeal is won? We are predicting the future. I am worried about tomorrow and two days. I know Liverpool is a nice pitch, it will be more work in terms of environment. Games like this is why I became a manager."
