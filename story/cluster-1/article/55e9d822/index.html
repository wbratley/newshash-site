Only six beds for trans victims of domestic abuse in UK, report finds

Article text

There are only six beds specifically for trans victims of domestic abuse in the UK, a report has found, with services saying it is “almost impossible” to find refuges for trans people since the supreme court ruling on single-sex spaces. Galop, an LGBTQ+ anti-abuse charity, called on the government to create an emergency national fund for specialist trans refuges, as many people were being turned away from mainstream services. Prior to the supreme court ruling on single-sex spaces in 2025, trans people could be placed into some mainstream domestic abuse refuges, Galop said. However, since that ruling determined same-sex spaces referred strictly to “biological sex”, many refuges that previously accepted trans people are now no longer able to. Galop said it has been unable to secure non-specialist refuge accommodation for any trans women in the UK in 2026 – it has only been able to find space at specialist LGBTQ+ or trans providers. Jasmine O’Connor, co-CEO of the charity, said: “Finding housing suitable for trans survivors has been very challenging for a long time, but since the supreme court ruling, that challenge has become absolutely acute. It’s devastating. “What was a very limited supply of housing has shrunk to virtually zero. For us as a service, it’s desperate to have to turn individuals away, week in, week out, because there is nowhere for them to go.” Across England and Wales, there are nine LGBTQ+ specialist accommodation services for domestic abuse victims providing a total of 106 bed spaces – of these, only six are specially reserved for trans people and they are all based in one refuge. Data suggests that demand far outstrips the supply. LGBTQ+ people are twice as likely to experience domestic abuse as heterosexual and cisgender people, and government data showed that 500 trans or non-binary adults were supported in domestic abuse safe accommodation in 2024-25. Galop said that 40% of its domestic abuse clients were trans+ in 2025/26, and that many were having to make the “unimaginable choice about whether to stay living with their alleged perpetrator or having no home”. “We’ll never know the amount of people who are making the choice to stay in a really abusive relationship because there is not a safe route out until, sadly, they’re in the headlines,” she said. In a landmark case last year, the supreme court ruled that the terms woman and sex in the Equality Act referred to a “biological woman” and “biological sex”, which meant that transgender people could be excluded from same-sex spaces. In October, the NHS announced that trans people would no longer to be placed on same-sex wards. The report says many domestic abuse services only place trans people in dispersed accommodation, self-contained spaces in the community, which are often most in demand and most difficult to access. Others only place trans people in mixed-gender homelessness accommodation, where they are more likely to experience further abuse. One trans woman quoted in the report, who has lived as a woman for over 20 years, said she was turned away from a women’s refuge because of the supreme court ruling, and Galop staff said many people were reluctant to approach services because of they fear the humiliation of being rejected. O’Connor said she hoped the government would speak to them about ringfenced funding for increasing trans services across the country: “We’d like a conversation with the government and a commitment to create this fund so that there is the right money available to commission the right sorts of services. “We see this very much in line with the government’s commitments on domestic abuse and wanting to ensure that LGBTQ+ survivors are supported within that strategy.” In the UK, call the national domestic abuse helpline on 0808 2000 247, or visit Women’s Aid. In Australia, the national family violence counselling service is on 1800 737 732. In the US, the domestic violence hotline is 1-800-799-SAFE (7233). Other international helplines may be found via www.befrienders.org
