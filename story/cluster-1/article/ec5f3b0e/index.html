Burnham is blasted over Piddington migrant camp approval by council boss: ‘Any sexual violence will be on your head’

Article text

ANY migrant sexual assaults in the tiny village of Piddington will be on the PM’s head, its furious council boss hit out last night. The chilling warning came as the Government used emergency powers to green-light housing 1,250 foreign men in the sleepy Oxfordshire hamlet of just 350. It was given the go ahead despite ministers admitting they cannot “eliminate all risk” to the villagers, who have claimed they are “sitting ducks”. Local parish council boss Tim McNally blasted: “I’m sorry, Andy Burnham, you’re responsible for this. If any sexual violence happens, it will be on your head.” Mr McNally, who has been leading the campaign against converting RAF Bicester near his village to migrant housing, said he was “massively disappointed” by the decision announced by local government boss Angela Rayner last night. He warned “this whole thing is just a major mistake. It’s a bad, mad calculation.” Read more News And he accused the PM of being quick to call out sexual assaults in the showbiz world in the wake of the Russell Brand trial, while abandoning ordinary Brits. He told Sun show Harry Cole Saves the West: “Andy Burnham, the great Prime Minister of the United Kingdom, has come out and basically accused the media industry of sexual assaults against women, and yet he’s actually giving the approval to open up a site from where we’re going to have a whole number of problems.” Ministers admitted last night that residents at the camp will be free to roam the local area. But they claimed the small country lanes would hinder their movements, writing: “Service users would be free to enter and leave, but the site’s relatively isolated location and limited pedestrian connections are likely to constrain journeys on foot.” Most read in The Sun But local Conservative MP Greg Smith, from the neighbouring Mid-Buckinghamshire constituency, said that was nonsense. “There are 11 villages in my constituency where it’s straightforwardly walking distance,” he told The Sun’s show. And he said officials are already planning to hand the migrant residents high viz jackets “because the site goes out onto a 50 mile per hour road… it’s just an absurdity.” The furious Tory added: “I’m genuinely scared for the safety of my constituents, particularly my female constituents. “We’re talking about a cohort of people who even this government’s Home Office has deemed they have to give a leaflet to informing them that rape is not acceptable in the United Kingdom, and they’re going to be walking distance from my constituents.” He added: “It’s not a risk worth taking. It is an absolute abdication of the government’s responsibility to keep their citizens safe by saying that they can’t eliminate risk.” And he urged Andy Burnham to “follow the plans that my party are now putting out that we need to deport those that are illegally entering the United Kingdom, not housing them in the middle of the countryside where they pose a real and present danger to my constituents.” Mr McNally also accused the Labour government of totally abandoning his village, despite promising to discuss their plight. “We’ve been engaging with the politicians at every possible level, we’ve been in Parliament. Not once have they actually engaged with us directly. They’ve promised to come. They’ve never come,” he said. “The only people who have come have been the opposition… but not a single Labour MP has come to visit Piddington, and that is really, really disappointing.” A Home Office spokesperson said: “Planning permission allows us to continue to progress the use of MOD Bicester. However, a final decision to proceed is yet to be taken. “The government is working on a fairer distribution of asylum accommodation across the country, with over £1 billion saved in costs since the election.” In September, the quintennial English village voted for independence from the UK in a symbolic local referendum. A whopping 285 villagers voted for “independence”, with just 26 against and one spoiled ballot on a turnout of almost 92 per cent. Parish council vice chair Susannah Parden, who announced the result, said: “We are Piddington — the village that roars.” The referendum was held in protest of the government’s proposal, reflecting local concerns about the impact of a significant influx of new residents. Village officials previously estimated that male asylum migrants will outnumber women in Piddington by 10-1. Fearful women in the area have resorted to self-defence lessons ahead of the influx of asylum seekers. After the independence vote, Prime Minister Andy Burnham said he understood the “strength of feeling” displayed by residents. Local mums told The Sun they would be too scared to take their children to the park if plans were approved — a fear that has now become reality.
