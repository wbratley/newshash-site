Andy Burnham finalises EU agreements as former PMs criticise reopening Brexit debate

Unbiased summary

Prime Minister Andy Burnham finalised agreements with Brussels on Friday covering food checks, a youth mobility scheme allowing 100,000 young EU citizens to travel, work or study in the UK for three years, and carbon emissions. Burnham told MPs the deal would add £17bn a year to the economy by 2040 and represented a choice between a 'broken status quo or change', while accusing Conservative predecessors of letting the country down. Former prime ministers Theresa May, David Cameron, Boris Johnson, Liz Truss and Rishi Sunak, along with former Labour PM Tony Blair, warned against reopening the Brexit debate. May said she was 'aghast' and urged Burnham to 'accept Brexit and move on'. Reform UK published analysis claiming potential costs from EU student loan defaults, and Kemi Badenoch demanded details of the deal.
