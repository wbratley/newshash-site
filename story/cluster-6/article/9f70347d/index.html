Breaking Bad actor RJ Mitte's cause of death as star dies aged just 34 Breaking Bad star RJ Mitte has died aged just 34, with a spokesperson for the late actor sharing that he took his own life. Just days before his death, the late actor opened up in his final interview Breaking Bad star RJ Mitte took his own life, his representative said. The Breaking Bad actor was just 34. The Brownsville Police Department received a call at 12:50pm local time, and sadly, Mitte was found dead upon arrival. "The cause and manner of death are still pending, as there is currently an ongoing investigation," they said. Cameron County Justice of the Peace Cyndi Hinojosa later declared Mitte dead at his home at 1:32pm local time. A medical examiner will now determine the official cause of death, following an autopsy. However, there was no sign of foul play. The actor, who starred as Walter White Jr. in the hit show alongside Bryan Cranston and Aaron Paul, died in Texas. In a statement, his spokesperson said: "It is with profound sadness that we share the tragic passing of beloved actor, philanthropist, and passionate disability rights advocate RJ Mitte. Drawing from his own experience living with cerebral palsy, Mitte brought authenticity to the role and built a platform on disability awareness and inclusion." Over the course of his career, it was rumoured that RJ had a net worth of between $3million and $5million. However, this was far from the case. RJ previously revealed: "I think it's what I've earned in my entire life." But the amount he earned from Breaking Bad saw the actor becoming "the main breadwinner" for his family. National disability charity Sense's chief executive pays tribute Following the death of RJ, who was extremely open about living with cerebral palsy and inspiring millions, Chief Executive of Sense, James Watson-O'Neill, said: "We're deeply saddened to hear of the death of RJ Mitte. "RJ's portrayal of Walter White Jr in Breaking Bad was an important moment for disability representation on screen. His character's cerebral palsy was part of who he was, but it didn't define him or dominate his storyline. It showed that disabled actors and characters belong in mainstream television, with the same range of stories, experiences and opportunities as anyone else. "But there is still a long way to go. Our research shows that more than a third (39%) of disabled people rarely or never see people like themselves or hear their experiences reflected in the media. Representation matters. Seeing disabled people on our screens, telling their own stories and playing a wide range of roles helps challenge stereotypes, break down barriers and create a more inclusive society." Actor's comments on estranged dad RJ's parents divorced when he was just nine months old. After this, he didn't have much contact with his dad. Speaking in a previous interview, RJ said: "He remarried when I was quite young, and they had their own little family. He does his own thing. We are kind of estranged, but I don't feel like he's missing from my life." RJ 'didn't care' about being adopted At the age of eight, RJ was told by his step-brother that he was adopted. However, he "didn't really care". In an interview, he said: "It didn't seem relevant, because I never once in my life felt unloved or like I was a burden." RJ's heartbreaking comment on family life In a 2016 interview, RJ opened up about his life outside work, growing up with his mother and his sister, Lacey, who was 12 at the time. He had been looking after his mother, who was paralysed due to a road accident, the month after Lacey was born. Speaking to The Guardian, RJ said: "She was in a car accident – a car ploughed into her at 40mph – and ruptured her spine. Afterwards, she couldn't lift Lacey because of the operations she'd had. So I took care of Lacey. "There's a 12-year gap between us and her dad wasn't really around, so I guess I was a bit of a father figure to her as well as a brother. I changed her diapers, made up her bottles, fed her, rocked her, bathed her – everything, I did it." Shocking confession about Breaking Bad pay Just under a year before he died, RJ made a shocking admission about just how much he had earned from his role in Breaking Bad. He explained that he had "paid more in taxes" than he racked up for his role on the show. Speaking on The Iced Coffee Hour podcast, he explained that he was far from becoming a millionaire. A Coogan Account, a trust set up to protect a minor's earnings, was created, meaning 15 per cent of his earnings would be set aside until he reached the age of majority. He said: "Just the name of the game with it, you know. I was paying to keep pushing forward and to keep doing that ... and everything went back into the business." When asked if he made a profit from the show or earned any "excess" after paying his bills, he shook his head. RJ said he was paid "minimum" as it was his "first speaking job, ever." He commented: "Breaking Bad was not, like, very lucrative as far as like across the board. "Every season, we were told that we were cancelled. We just did not have the viewership until streaming. You know that was our big moment, when streaming came in and people binge-viewed Breaking Bad,' he added. 'But up until that point, it was a struggle every year. It wasn't much." When asked if the rumours that he was worth between $3million and $5million were true, he confirmed this was just pure speculation. "I think it's what I've earned in my entire life," he said. RJ's health battle RJ was diagnosed with cerebral palsy at just three. At the time, doctors put his legs in casts for six months to straighten his feet. He was later fitted with leg braces and used crutches throughout his early years. Over the years, RJ built up strength through sports and exercises and by the time he reached his teens, he no longer required any walking assistance devices. When he was born, via emergency caesarean, he was not breathing, which sadly resulted in brain damage. Life after Breaking Bad After his final appearance in Breaking Bad back in 2013, RJ continued to land a string of high-profile roles on both the small and big screens. In the same year his final scene aired, RJ appeared as Tim in the movie House of Last Things. He later landed roles such as Who's Driving Doug, Rivers Run Red, and Standing Up for Sunny on the big screen. In 2021, he portrayed Mike in Triumph, for which he also served as an executive producer. Meanwhile, although he had recurring roles and appeared in several episodes of various TV shows, his last small screen credit came in 2022, playing the role of Mind Master in the live-action animated series, The Guardians of Justice. RJ never liked watching himself on TV The actor previously said he didn't enjoy watching himself on TV, admitting: "It doesn't help me." He commented: "I dwell in it. I'm someone that when I mess up or like something happens, I dwell and I spiral. Where it's like, what could I have done differently? How could I have done that differently?" Final heartbreaking interview Just weeks before his death, RJ Mitte spoke about his role in Breaking Bad. The actor made an appearance on The Joe Vulpis Podcast just a week ago and spoke of his "most complicated" scene on the show. The American show followed high school teacher Walter White as he was diagnosed with stage-three lung cancer, before producing and selling methamphetamine to pay for his treatment and leave behind money for his family. RJ played Walter's son. Reflecting on a difficult scene, he recalled Walter's final call with his dad, during which RJ said he felt "a lot of pressure". He said: "I really think for me one of the most complicated scenes that I've had to do was that final phone call. "It's the last time he talks to him. In reality, I didn't understand it as much as I do now. But I still had a lot of pressure and felt a lot of pressure in trying to make sure that was right. And where it set, and the tones, and the confusion, and the different levels of that. Because he's now hearing this in his school. He's hearing it from people. He's been stained by this reputation of Heisenberg. And it's like, I don't know who Heisenberg is. Wait, that's my dad. That's my father, and he killed my uncle. Like he's a murderer. "And so that realisation for him was something that I really wanted to make sure that I met those criteria. And you know, listening to the directors, listening to Vince [Gilligan], and really Bryan [Cranston] was there that day as well. He was in the room when we did that scene. Co-star Bryan Cranston leads tributes His on-screen father, Bryan Cranston, shared his heartbreak at the news, revealing that the pair had been texting in recent weeks and had arranged to meet for lunch. The actor wrote: “I’m in deep despair as I write this tonight. RJ Mitte, my sweet son on Breaking Bad, has died. Born with Cerebral Palsy, he never – ever – allowed that challenge to be an excuse for anything. He met the demanding shooting schedule head-on with great determination.” Cranston addded: “Always listening to the advice that his TV parents (Anna Gunn and myself) gave him – but being a teenager, sometimes mischievously ignoring the counsel in order to learn the hard way… He was one of the stars of a hit TV show – but still a kid. All that promise and potential now is such a loss.” The actor added: “It is especially devastating to hear that he took his own life. Why?
