Nearly 80% of Black Friday ‘deals’ are cheaper or the same price at other times of the year, Which? warns

Article text

SHOPPERS are being warned not to get misled by Black Friday discounts after new research found nearly eight in ten deals were cheaper or the same price at other times of the year. Research by consumer champion Which? found that just 22% of products on offer during last year’s Black Friday sales period were at their lowest price of the year. A further 44% had already been available for the same price earlier in the year, while others were actually cheaper outside the sales. The findings come ahead of Black Friday on November 27, when retailers are expected to slash prices on everything from TVs and laptops to beauty products. Many retailers have already begun cutting prices, and plenty will keep their deals on until Cyber Monday. Which? analysed more than 250,000 daily prices across 756 products at eight major retailers, including Amazon, Argos, Currys and John Lewis. More from Sun Money It found shoppers could sometimes save hundreds of pounds simply by waiting a few weeks or months, as prices often dropped even further after the event. For example, an LG 48-inch OLED Smart TV cost £999 at John Lewis on Black Friday last year. But shoppers who held out until Christmas Eve could have bought it for £749 thanks to a price match, saving £250. Meanwhile, Bose QuietComfort Ultra headphones were advertised as a Black Friday deal at Amazon for £399. Most read in Money However, their price dropped to £299.95 in March, almost £100 cheaper. At Argos, a Dyson Airwrap i.d. was on offer for £429.99 during Black Friday, down from £479.99. But the price fell to £399.99 on December 4, before dropping as low as £349.99 in April. And at Very, Samsung Galaxy Pro wireless earbuds cost £149 during the Black Friday sales period, but were available for £109 in February. Which? also found that an LG 75-inch 4K Smart TV was advertised at AO for £1,800, with a claimed saving of £899. However, researchers could find no evidence that the retailer had sold the television at its stated £2,699 recommended retail price in the three months before the sale. Which retailers offered the best deals? Which? found the proportion of products hitting their lowest price during Black Friday varied significantly between retailers. Very performed best on this measure, with 33% of the products examined at their cheapest during the sales period. Amazon followed at 27%, while John Lewis and Currys both achieved 23%. Boots and Argos each recorded 21%, followed by AO at 18%. At Richer Sounds, just 6% of the products studied were at their lowest price during Black Friday. However, the rankings changed when Which? included deals that matched their lowest price at other times of the year. On this measure, Argos came out on top at 86%, followed by Currys and Boots at 79% each, and John Lewis at 74%. Richer Sounds rose to 59%. Which? said the findings showed that shoppers should not feel pressured into rushing purchases, as many products would be available for similar prices later. Reena Sewraz, Which? retail editor, said: “Our research shows there are definitely bargains to be had in the Black Friday sales period, but they might be hard to find. “Most of us will need a helping hand to separate the real deals from the hype – with the best prices on many products actually appearing at other times of the year.” She added: “If you do have your heart set on a product you’ve seen in the sales, make sure you check the price history first, so you know you can trust you are getting a good price. “You don’t need to rush into it, however, as more often than not we’ve found you’ll still be able to get the same deal or a better one towards Christmas and beyond.” How to make sure you’re getting a bargain Shoppers can use free price comparison websites such as PriceRunner and PriceSpy to check whether a product has been cheaper previously. For Amazon purchases, CamelCamelCamel allows customers to track historical prices and set alerts when products fall below a certain amount. It’s also worth comparing prices across different retailers rather than assuming a Black Friday label guarantees the cheapest deal. Be aware that some shops offer price guarantees on selected products. For example, Argos promises that products displaying its Price Guarantee badge won’t fall to a lower price before Christmas Day. Which? found that none of the products carrying this guarantee in its study dropped in price before that date. Meanwhile, Currys guarantees that products labelled as Black Friday deals were not cheaper in the preceding six months, and Which? found no breaches of this guarantee among the products it examined. Finally, shoppers should set a budget and avoid buying something simply because it has a large discount. After all, a bargain is only worth snapping up if it’s something you actually need. An Amazon spokesperson said: “Each item shared with us by Which? was cheaper during Black Friday than in the previous 30 days.” John Lewis said its prices can change outside of our planned promotions due to its never knowingly undersold guarantee. Boots said it runs promotions throughout November to give customers time to make “informed purchasing decisions”.
