I’ve been covering Black Friday deals for seven years – here are the rules that stop me from overspending

Article text

I’ve been writing about Black Friday deals since 2019, and while you may think spending hours scrolling online retailers’ deals would turn me into a shopping addict, it has (fortunately) done the opposite. Retailers know that, for many of us, this is the time of year we finally buy that hefty investment we’ve been debating, or else blitz through the kids’ letter to Santa. If you play your cards right, it’s an excellent way to save cash. I’d like to think I’ve got my shopping strategy down to a fine art, so here’s how I make Black Friday work for me. Make sure you go in with the right mindset. Relentless discounts and non-stop advertising are designed to make you feel frenzied. When I panic that something will sell out and feel myself rushing to buy it, I find it helpful to close my browser, take a breather, and remember that retailers need my custom more than I need that new coat. I was once the type of shopper who would run to buy that trending item or limited-edition lip balm for fear of missing my chance. What got me out of that anxious, impulse-buying habit was one simple mentality shift: remembering that these companies want me to buy their products and will create as many opportunities as possible for me to give them my money. In the words of corny dating coaches: remember – you are the prize! Learn to love your lists I bet most of us can think of 10 impulse buys we regret before we can name one thing we wish we’d bought but missed out on. That’s why it’s important to stay on track. The sooner you start writing down a list of things you’re tentatively interested in buying, the better. Consult reviews and buying guides (ideally, check out the Filter’s!) in the run-up to the event to make sure you’re not just buying that dehumidifier because it’s 40% off, but because it’s good. In the Filter’s guide to the best Black Friday deals, we’re only sharing savings on products we’ve tested and rated, which means you won’t get any dud recommendations. Last year I kept a note on my phone with links to the items I wanted, and I slowly whittled down the ones I’d lost interest in before Black Friday. When the big day arrived, I was left with a list of things I’d been considering buying for weeks and months. If I’d waited until Black Friday itself to consider my shopping list for the first time, I’d likely have spent twice as much. If you are already going to buy something and it happens to be discounted, I’d count that as an actual saving. That’s why I always do my Christmas shopping on Black Friday, compiling a list of recipients and their wishlists like it’s Mr Claus’s nice list. My list includes gift ideas, prices and my total budget, and on Black Friday I’ll cross off what I’ve bought (usually the ones I found at a good price) to make sure everyone is accounted for. And if you find yourself short of gift inspiration, I’d recommend you consult the Filter’s mega guide to the best Christmas gifts, which features more than 360 products. I also use Black Friday as an opportunity to stock up on bulk orders of things I buy regularly – Amazon usually has good deals on cleaning products, beauty restocks and electric toothbrush heads. It’s against trading standards to list a product at a reduced price unless it’s been sold at the listed RRP for a meaningful period of time. However, the limited-time deals and “flash sales” you see often aren’t as impressive as they look. There are some product categories we rarely recommend buying at full price – electric toothbrushes, TVs and some coffee machines, to name a few, can frequently be found at hefty discounts throughout the year. A better metric for whether you’ve found an unusually good saving is to check CamelCamelCamel, which shows historic pricing for products on Amazon and also keeps a record of its lowest ever price. It’s a tool I use constantly when writing about the savings I recommend to readers. Seeing just how frequently you can find savings on a product can temper that Black Friday frenzy or reassure you that if you wait a few more months, there’s every chance you’ll be able to find it on sale again.
