WW2 veteran who lied about his age to get into British Army dies aged 101 Donald Turrell forged a letter from his parents in order to enlist Don't Miss Most Read Latest A veteran who fought in the Normandy campaign in World War II after lying about his age has died aged 101. Donald 'Don' Turrell was just 17 when he enlisted in the Army, having forged a letter from his parents. Originally from London, he joined the Cameronians (Scottish Rifles) and in the following months was stationed at various places around the UK, including Bury St Edmunds, Newquay, Newcastle and Fort William in the Scottish Highlands. His battalion crossed over to France in June 1944 in the days following D-Day, and Mr Turrell landed on Sword Beach before taking part in fierce fighting against occupying German troops. The Taxi Charity for Military Veterans said in a post on social media it was "deeply saddened" to share that Mr Turrell had died on Friday, surrounded by his family. Paying tribute to their father, siblings Andrew Turrell and Linda Baker said in a statement the veteran had led a "truly wonderful and remarkable life." They added: "He was an extraordinary man, a loving father and someone who will be deeply missed by all of us who had the privilege of knowing him. "While our hearts are broken at losing him, we take great comfort in knowing just how many people knew, loved and admired him, and in the wonderful memories they share of him. Dad’s life was full, meaningful and filled with experiences that few could ever imagine." In an interview last year, Mr Turrell told reporters how he forged a letter from his parents in order to enlist. He said: "I did lie about my age. I pushed it up a bit, gave all the right answers, and I was in. I was happy.” "I was 17. I told them I was 18. I wrote a letter out saying that 'To whom it my concern, the bearer of this letter is my son, Donald Turrell blah, blah, blah'." Mr Turrell served as a rifleman in the Scottish Cameronians |GETTY The veteran described feeling "excited and apprehensive" during the crossing to the Normandy coast, and said his "introduction" to the war was seeing bodies from earlier fighting washing back up on the beach as he landed. His war came to an end a few weeks later on July 10, when he was seriously injured during fighting near the town of Eterville, and later medically discharged. Mr Turrell spoke movingly of the close friends he lost during the campaign, some of whom he buried in the field, and who are now buried in Commonwealth war cemeteries on the continent. The war also claimed his older brother Leslie, who had been a telegraphist on HMS Penelope and died when she was sunk off the Italian coast near Naples in February 1944. The veteran previously lived in Frinton-on-Sea in Essex.
