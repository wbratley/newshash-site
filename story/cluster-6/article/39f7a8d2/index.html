Schools ordered by Labour to 'make students read for pleasure' as part of education overhaul

Article text

Schools have been ordered by Labour to leave time in the day for students to read for pleasure. The move will be made as part of a national overhaul of the national curriculum to be rolled out next week by Education Secretary Lucy Powell. Primary schools will have to set aside time within the curriculum every day, and secondary schools will have the flexibility to make sure students have regular time to read throughout the week, the Department for Education (DfE) said. Secondary schools will also be told to encourage pupils to read entire books, poems and other literature. And pupils will be tested on their reading ability in Year 8 to identify students who need extra help. The Government said young people's daily reading levels were too low, with books losing the battle for attention to screens. The Education Secretary will also set out the programmes of study for the new curriculum in England. This is expected to include slimmed-down GCSEs, which could lead to shorter exams in some subjects. The overhaul will be the biggest since Michael Gove's reforms more than a decade ago. Ms Powell described the decline in reading as a "ticking time bomb for our future generations", adding that too many pupils see reading as "a chore or missing out altogether". She added: "We cannot afford to stand idly by and let this continue. Reading fluently is the gateway to lifelong learning, opening up every subject in school and creating opportunities throughout life. "Reading is not just a skill that sets children up for success throughout their lives. The Education Secretary said she was determined to "make sure every child in this country can become confident, fluent readers", but opponents have criticised the decision to cut down on GCSEs. Ms Powell previously announced that schools should stop teaching GCSEs before Year 10, with the change ensuring that the qualification can be taught comfortably within two years. History, geography, and foreign languages are considered the courses most likely to be trimmed by the Government. Education will place an increased focus on oracy, or speaking skills, Ms Powell has previously announced, with an overall emphasis on human-led skills as artificial intelligence takes over more of working life. Laura Trott, the Shadow Education Secretary, told The Times she welcomed efforts to encourage children to read more but said it "must go hand in hand with a serious effort to reduce screen time in our classrooms". Of the wider reforms, she said: "The Education Secretary is on a crusade to dumb down standards. "Hard won, evidence-led reforms to build a knowledge-rich curriculum have meant English pupils are now in the top ten in the world for reading, maths and science. "Any move to water down knowledge would be utterly bonkers and a backwards step for standards. "In Wales, Labour took this approach and a generation of children have been let down as a result.
