The Grinch of Middlesbrough: Moment mobility scooter-riding spoilsport takes scissors to family's Christmas lights

Article text

For most of us, Christmas is a time for celebration, joy and wishing well upon your neighbours... But not for one elderly woman in Middlesbrough, who took it upon herself to ruin a family's festive display by cutting their lights with scissors. Riding a mobility scooter on the pavement past Beth Wilson-Croft's home, she was seen on CCTV stopping briefly next to a set of railings covered in white Christmas lights. The woman then reached towards the lights with an implement, believed to be scissors, already in her hand. After several seconds of grappling with the wires, the lights suddenly turned off. The woman then simply carried on down the pavement. Mother-of-one Mrs Wilson-Croft, 31, was left shocked by the incident, which took place at around 11pm on Wednesday where she lives with her husband Kieran and their six-year-old daughter. The woman has been berated on social media and her actions have been reported to local police, the Daily Mail understands. The elderly woman was seen approaching Beth Wilson-Croft's house before appearing to cut the lights' wire with scissors After the woman was seen fumbling with the wire for several seconds, the festive lights abruptly turned off Mrs Wilson-Croft originally said on social media she wanted the woman to repay her for the cost of replacing the broken lights. Fortunately, they could be fixed without needing to be replaced. She told the Daily Mail: 'When we got up yesterday morning to take my daughter to school, we noticed the lights had been turned off and when we checked they had been broken. 'I initially thought it was kids who grabbed them when passing by and then when I watched the cameras back it wasn't, it was a little old lady on a mobility scooter which is absolutely crazy. 'It's laughable because you don't expect anyone to ruin your lights. Especially not a lady of that age and for no reason.' Mrs Wilson-Croft added that in her ten years of living at the home, she has never met the woman in her life and would like to know why she did it. 'If you don't like Christmas, that's fine. You do what you want with your time of year, but you don't need to spoil everybody else's fun,' she said. Sharing the video on social media, she added: 'If you don't like my Christmas decorations that's an absolute shame for you because they'll be back up today alongside a singing Santa and a snow machine and anything else I can get my hands on.' After the footage went viral, a 'very nice gentleman' offered to repair the snipped lights by soldering them back together. Mrs Wilson-Croft added that in her ten years of living at the home, she has never met the woman in her life and would now like to know why she did it Last night, Mrs Wilson-Croft's house was restored to its festive glory After fixing the decorations and putting them back on show, Mrs Wilson-Croft said: 'We are back in action… gangster granny didn't dim my sparkle for long.' Last night she shared photographs of her house redecorated with lights, a giant inflatable gingerbread man and snowman wearing top hats, as well as a Father Christmas and Rudolph decoration. She also paid a visit to a woman who firmly denied any involvement in cutting the lights. 'I found out where she lived within about half an hour of the lights going down and went round and asked her why she did it,' she explained. 'She said she didn't do it. So I said "you can either explain why you've done it or I'll call the police" and she said "call the police then".' The family put up the Christmas decorations outside their home to bring a little extra magic to the festive season for their daughter. 'I've got a six-year-old, so the more magical you can make it while they're still young, that's what it's for, isn't it?' Mrs Wilson-Croft said. The decorations have also attracted visitors from around the local area, with families bringing their own children to see the incredible display. Do you know who the Christmas grinch is?
