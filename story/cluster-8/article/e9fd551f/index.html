Australia's sandpapergate probe to also include 2017-18 Ashes

Article text

Australia's sandpapergate probe to also include 2017-18 Ashes - Published Cricket Australia's fresh independent probe into the sandpapergate scandal has been widened to include the 2017-18 Ashes series against England. The terms of reference for the independent forum have been published and the scope will include "ball-management practices in the Australian men's team during the 2017–18 season", which includes the Ashes series Australia won 4-0. Also included are all four Tests of the subsequent South Africa tour and the investigation which followed when the ball-tampering scandal emerged. Cricket Australia has pledged to publish the full independent report and recommendations within 30 days of receiving them. All those involved in Australia's 2018 tour of South Africa will be invited to speak to the independent forum, though none will have to do so and no retrospective sanctions will be handed out. The scandal was reignited by the release of former batter Usman Khawaja's autobiography. In it, Khawaja claimed the entire team knew about ball-tampering in the third Test of their series against South Africa in Cape Town. The four frontline bowlers in that 2018 team - Josh Hazlewood, Mitchell Starc, Nathan Lyon and current Test captain Pat Cummins - have always denied knowledge of the plan to use sandpaper on the ball. Cummins has called Khawaja's recollection of events "nonsense". However, former team-mates David Warner and Cameron Bancroft - two of three players who were banned for their part in the scandal, alongside Steve Smith - have backed Khawaja's version of events. "All participants in Australia's 2018 tour of South Africa, including players and support staff, will be invited to take part in the Independent Forum, as will CA management and directors at that time," Cricket Australia said. "While all involved will be encouraged to participate, there will be no compulsion to do so. All materials supplied will be treated as confidential unless otherwise stated by the participant and no sanctions will be retrospectively applied. "Participants can choose to attend the Independent Forum in person, online and/or provide information via written accounts or through relevant documents and materials." The forum will be open for six weeks. "This is an opportunity to provide their accounts in a safe environment and to provide the CA Board with relevant information including about the events of that tour and resultant investigation," CA chair David Boon said. "We are acutely aware of the enormous toll the renewed focus on this tour eight years ago has had on those involved and their welfare remains our main priority. "This forum is the best opportunity for people to speak and be heard, and for CA to listen and learn. "Publishing the report in full is part of our commitment to transparency, and to be open with cricket fans about what we learn. "It is also very important to understand that it will not seek to sanction or recriminate." - Published6 days ago - Published18 November - Published14 November
