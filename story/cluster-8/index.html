Thirlwall inquiry into Lucy Letby case finds failures at hospital and wider NHS system

Unbiased summary

The Thirlwall Inquiry's final report into the Lucy Letby case was published, finding a 'complete failure' to protect babies at the Countess of Chester Hospital, where Letby murdered seven and attempted to murder seven others. The report blamed dysfunctional management, an over-focus on reputation, and failure to involve police promptly. It also criticised wider NHS culture and regulation, issuing 14 recommendations. Health Secretary Yvette Cooper promised a tracking hub and a new maternity commissioner. Former hospital executives apologised for the delay in contacting police, while the judge said some babies' deaths could have been prevented. The inquiry did not re-examine Letby's convictions but focused on institutional failures.
