Former Hamas bodyguard convicted of Birmingham knife attack; wanted in Germany over alleged terror plot

Unbiased summary

Mohammed Alfatih, also known as Mohammed Naim, a 40-year-old British citizen born in Germany, was found guilty by a majority verdict at Birmingham Crown Court of unlawful wounding, possession of two knives, and criminal damage after attacking a bailiff outside his home in Birmingham in 2025. He was cleared of wounding with intent. The bailiff had come to repossess his wife's car over unpaid parking fines exceeding £2,250. Alfatih, who was under counter-terrorism surveillance at the time, could not be named during the trial due to reporting restrictions. He had previously worked as a bodyguard for former Hamas leader Ismail Haniyeh and is the son of senior Hamas official Bassem Naim. He was granted asylum in the UK in 2015 and British citizenship in 2020 or 2021 (sources differ). While detained for the knife attack, he was rearrested on a German extradition request alleging he was involved in a Hamas plot to attack Jewish and Israeli targets in Germany and Austria around the second anniversary of the 7 October attack. German authorities allege he played a prominent role in transporting and storing weapons and pre-recorded a video claiming responsibility on behalf of Hamas's military wing. The plot was disrupted by German intelligence after a Mossad tip-off. Alfatih is expected to face extradition proceedings after serving his UK sentence.
