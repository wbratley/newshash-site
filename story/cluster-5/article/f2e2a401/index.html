Jeffrey Epstein's death was not suicide, top forensic scientist finds in new report: 'That's not a hanging mark'

Article text

Jeffrey Epstein’s death was not suicide, top forensic scientist finds in new report: ‘That’s not a hanging mark’ Seven years after it was formally ruled that Jeffrey Epstein killed himself, a leading forensic scientist is challenging that official narrative. For years, questions have swirled, and conspiracies have abounded about the circumstances surrounding Epstein’s death. According to the New York City Office of Chief Medical Examiner, the FBI and the Department of Justice (DOJ), Epstein hanged himself in a federal jail cell in 2019 while awaiting trial on charges of sex trafficking minors. But Dr Michael Freeman, a forensic scientist with two doctorates in epidemiology and forensic medicine who spent a year examining Epstein's autopsy file at the behest of the dead pedophile's brother, has argued that Epstein did not, in fact, kill himself. Freeman told The New York Times that his first thought upon seeing the autopsy photos that show Epstein's injuries was: 'Wait a minute, that doesn’t look like a hanging mark.' The forensic scientist explained that typical hanging injuries are characterized by a smooth, consistent line that slants to a peak on the neck, while Epstein's neck bruise was deep, ragged and branched into a Y-shape on one side. To Freeman, the injuries are far more consistent with homicidal strangulation, rather than hanging. The forensic scientist's assertion is consistent with the public view of Epstein's brother, Mark Epstein, who has repeatedly claimed that the disgraced financier was murdered. Epstein's cell is pictured after his body was found Dr Michael Freeman, a leading forensic scientist who spent a year examining Epstein's autopsy file, has argued that Epstein did not kill himself It was Mark who gave Freeman access to Epstein's autopsy file, which for years was kept away from third-party investigators and forensic scientists and pathologists. As Epstein's next of kin, Mark was one of the only people to have access to the file. Mark gave the file to Freeman in November 2025, and since then, the forensic pathologist has studied it extensively. Freeman reached his conclusion with the help of a cognitive neuroscientist named Itiel Dror, and a forensic pathologist named Anders Eriksson. The three have been trying to find a peer-reviewed journal willing to publish their determination that Epstein did not take his own life. Freeman's assertion is sure to add fuel to skepticism about the official narrative of how Epstein died, which has been mired in mystery and speculation due to a series of suspicious circumstances. The two guards responsible for monitoring him, who had been assigned to his cell following an earlier alleged suicide attempt, reportedly fell asleep and failed to check on him for around three hours. At the same time, cameras outside his cell malfunctioned. The footage the cameras did record showed a flash of orange moving towards Epstein's cell shortly before his death. Inmates are issued orange clothing and bedclothes, yet this sighting occurred at 10.39pm when prisoners should have been locked up for the night. Less than eight hours later, Epstein's corpse would be discovered in his cell. Orange fabric identified at the scene of Epstein's death. The noose the financier allegedly used has never been officially identified A shadowy, orange object could be seen moving up the stairs to Epstein's cell block at the New York prison at around 10.40pm, the night before he was found dead After the body was found, evidence was sloppily catalogued, and the medical examiner was not allowed to inspect the cell. Epstein was found hanging from a makeshift noose made from a sheet, but the guard who found him later told the FBI: ‘I don’t recall taking the noose off... I don’t recall taking the thing from around his neck.’ And although a noose was found in the cell, it was later discounted and didn’t match Epstein’s injuries, according to a different forensic pathologist, Dr Michael Baden, who was also hired by Epstein's brother. ‘The markings [on Epstein’s neck] would have required a different type of material,’ Baden said. In 2023, the DOJ concluded that there had been 'job performance failures' by prison officials, but maintained that Epstein had died by suicide. The New York City medical examiner's office also said this week that it 'stands by its determination' of suicide. The disgraced financier had displayed a pattern of behavior in the weeks before his death that suggested a desire to kill himself, and authorities recovered an apparent suicide note. Even so, questions over the official account have persisted, with some arguing that Epstein's knowledge of his powerful connections could have given others a motive to silence him. A medical tag on Epstein's wrist after he was found hanging in his cell Freeman told The New York Times that he is not interested in who may have killed Epstein and is solely focused on what he views as failures in the death investigation and autopsy report. Kristin Roman, the medical examiner who performed the original autopsy and has since retired from the medical examiner's office, told federal investigators that she was certain Epstein had died by suicide. She cited a lack of bruises on the body or debris under his fingernails and claimed: 'This doesn’t look anything like a strangulation,' in a statement to authorities. But Freeman has asserted that Epstein did have injuries on his body, which suggests there was a struggle. He has also questioned why investigators did not test Epstein's fingernails for DNA. On Monday, Mark uploaded all of the photos from his brother's autopsy file online for the public to see. Freeman claims that the photos show fingernail marks above the ligature line on Epstein's neck, an abrasion at the base of the neck and a fresh hemorrhage in his left shoulder muscle, which all point to strangulation from behind.
