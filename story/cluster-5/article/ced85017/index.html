Ian Huntley's fellow inmate 'plotted Soham killer's alleged murder weeks before fatal attack'

Article text

Ian Huntley's alleged murder was "cold-blooded" and had been "planned weeks in advance," a court has heard. Anthony Russell, 44, is currently on trial at Newcastle Crown Court, having been charged with the murder of child-killer Huntley, which he denies. Russell, who is serving a whole-life sentence after murdering three people, including a pregnant woman, is said to have beaten Huntley to death with a metal bar in a "brutal" attack. Prosecutor Matthew Bean KC urged the jury to put aside their feelings about Huntley's own infamous crimes and instead "try this case on the evidence and the evidence alone". He told the court Russell "murdered Ian Huntley by striking him repeatedly to the head with a heavy metal bar" shortly after 9am on February 26, 2026. "The prosecution's case is that this was a cold-blooded murder that had been planned weeks in advance of its execution," he said. Mr Bean said Huntley's crimes "aroused feelings of disgust and anger" and insisted he was not looking to the jury for sympathy. "The prosecution do not ask for sympathy," he told the court. "Ultimately, this case is not about the appalling actions of Ian Huntley during his life, but the manner of his death. Mr Bean said there had been previous tensions between the pair, who were both housed in the A wing at HMP Frankland, in Durham, and in March 2025, officers had been forced to separate the men after an argument and threatening behaviour. The two "continued to make threats towards one another as they were taken back to the wing," Mr Bean said, but they were later spoken to and "agreed nothing further would come of it". Less than a year later, Russell was seen sporting a black eye. Though he told officers he had fallen, Mr Bean said some members of staff believed "it may have been caused by Ian Huntley". These were the only incidents between the two inmates on prison records, the court was told. Mr Bean said the mood was "happy" until a "commotion" broke out. A prison officer immediately ran over and said he saw Russell hit Huntley at least six times with a heavy metal bar, the court heard. The attack was described as "brutal", and those in the room said Russell was "out to intentionally kill" Huntley. The Soham murderer was left unconscious in a pile of his blood. He later died on March 7, after suffering multiple skull fractures and brain injuries. Mr Bean said Russell told officers Huntley had been "pushing my buttons", adding: "Hopefully I've killed him". Russell is also said to have said to police he "caved" Huntley's skull in because he was "arrogant" and "cocky". The court heard Russell told officers he had "nothing to lose" and was "not getting out" of prison anyway, and stated: "So I thought 'I'll kill him if I can'." Mr Bean expressed his understanding for "the emotions that may be stirred during this trial" but urged the jury to remain impartial, despite Huntley's crimes. "It is essential, however, to be true to your oath or affirmation, to put emotion to one side, to try this case on the evidence and the evidence alone, and to come to verdicts based on that evidence."
