ITV are accused of 'creating drama' as I'm A Celeb race row with AJ Odudu and Mariella Frostrup comes weeks after Big Brother show she hosts was hit by hundreds of Ofcom complaints ITV has come under fire after two of their valued reality shows became embroiled in race rows just weeks apart. On Sunday, I'm A Celebrity... Get Me Out Of Here! made headlines after journalist Mariella Frostrup, 64, accused campmate AJ Odudu, 38, of being 'aggressive and dramatic', during a clash over cooking duties. Both were left in tears after the interaction, with some viewers accusing Mariella of using a 'micro-aggression', which is a subtle, often unintentional comment or action expressing prejudice toward a marginalised group (such as a racial minority). While some viewers stated that Mariella had a similar exchange over cooking with Joanna Page, and that this was just the latest of her bust-ups and not race-related at all, it has drawn comparisons to the latest series of Big Brother – which coincidentally AJ hosts. This year's Big Brother was considered to be one of the most controversial series ever, racking up a record number of Ofcom complaints and featuring several explosive clashes between the Housemates. One such incident came when Rochelle Rackham – who went on to win the show – clashed with black Housemates Johanne Cade and Pauline Adeyemo, over comments the pair made about their experiences with micro-aggressions. I'm A Celebrity made headlines after journalist Mariella Frostrup, 64, accused campmate AJ Odudu, 38, of being 'aggressive and dramatic', during a clash over cooking duties It has drawn comparisons to the latest series of Big Brother in which Rochelle Rackham (left) clashed with Housemates Johanne Cade and Pauline Adeyemo over 'microaggression' claims Prior to the altercation, British carer Pauline was having a discussion with American influencer Johanne about how racism presents itself differently in the US and the UK, with Pauline stating that racism seems more overt in the States but presents as 'microaggressions' in the UK. Johanne then referenced a row she had with Rochelle that week after she complained about being served undercooked chicken, questioning how the situation might not have escalated if a white Housemate, such as Rhianna Collins, had made the complaint instead of her. She mused: 'My situation with Rochelle felt like a microaggression because I feel like if anybody else was over there heating up their food, say Rhianna, would [Rochelle] have reacted like that? 'And that's how you can really decipher a microaggression a lot of times – is just put anybody there that looks like that person, would their behaviour still be the same?' Rochelle overheard the conversation but incorrectly relayed the conversation to her fellow Housemates, saying that Pauline and Johanne had labelled herself and Rhianna as 'microaggressive', and asking them: 'Would you say, since we've been in here, that Rhianna and I have been racist in any way, shape or form?' The fallout left Rihanna in tears as she believed she had been labelled 'racist', with the confusion sparking a fresh argument between Rochelle and Johanne. Johanne branded Rochelle 'stupid, dumb and a trouble maker' in the clash, while Rochelle hit back F*** off! F*** off!' before Big Brother called Johanne to the Diary Room and gave her a formal warning. Viewers were furious that Rochelle was not also given a warning following the fiery spat and also called on Johanne's conversation with Pauline to be broadcast back to the House so the Housemates could hear it accurately. Following the episode, Big Brother was slapped with 493 Ofcom complaints. Many echoed the racism accusations – although others say the incident was not race-related at all. The conversation was sparked by camp leader Wayne Rooney, who brought AJ and Mariella together to discuss their differences after several clashes over cooking. But Mariella stuck to her position, telling AJ: 'I think you are making a drama out of a crisis. I was thrilled someone was able to step in and cook.' 'You have a funny way of showing it,' AJ retorted, before Mariella hit back: 'Now that's just aggressive and dramatic.' Later, the two were seen trying to make amends but after AJ asked Mariella not to call her aggressive, the journalist replied: 'Well, only if you're not being aggressive.' A shocked AJ replied: 'So you stand by that?' Mariella said: 'I can't even remember what you said that made me say it, but I apologise if it upset you.' As AJ tried to explain, Mariella said: 'If you just stop talking for a sec... I just said if it upset you, I apologise so can you bank that so you know that I've said that because I want to be very clear if it upset you, I'm sorry. 'But I can't remember what you said to me that made me say it so I can't even argue my case which is why I'm apologising.' Campmate Leomie Anderson explained the connotations to fellow campmate Joanna Page, saying: 'Black women are always painted to be aggressive when we're just expressing ourselves and I don't think that's the right term to use in this instance at all.' The Daily Mail revealed that following the row, ITV bosses spoke to Mariella, with a show insider confirming that producers had a private conversation with her about her ‘offensive, outdated and inappropriate terminology’. Tuesday's episode also saw Mariella attempt to clear the air with AJ, while questioning why she found the use of the word 'aggressive' to be 'triggering.' ITV has been contacted for comment.
