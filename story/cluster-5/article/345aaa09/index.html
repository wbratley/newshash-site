Hamas chief’s bodyguard ‘planned to kill Jews’ after being granted UK asylum

Article text

A Hamas bodyguard targeted the Jewish community last year after being handed British citizenship in 2020. Mohammed Alfatih, who is also known as Mohammed Naim, was found guilty in the UK of wounding a bailiff over his wife's unpaid parking fines while on bail with anti-terror police. German authorities are also in the process of building a case against Alfatih in a plot to murder Israeli and Jewish institutions, such as synagogues, in Germany and Austria, on the second anniversary of the October 7 massacre. Alfatih was the bodyguard of the terror organisation's assassinated leader, Ismail Haniyeh - and has been wanted in Germany for over a year. Alfatih, who was born in Erlangen, Germany and grew up in Gaza, which he described as "very, very tough", is the son of Basem Naim, a senior Hamas official. Speaking in court last week, he said: "When you get raised under occupation you don’t have control of your life. I remember soldiers coming to the door and they started hitting me with the butt of the gun." He claimed his older brother was murdered by Israeli forces in 2003, during an altercation which left his with a gunshot wound to the leg. But other sources have suggested his brother was actually a Hamas militant who was shot down in the Shuja’iyya neighbourhood of Gaza in January 2003. Alfatih first left Gaza in 2014, breaking into Britain in the back of a lorry before lodging an asylum claim in the UK. The 40-year-old was then granted asylum the following year, and the Government subsequently granted him citizenship five years later in 2020. Now, Alfatih has been found guilty of unlawful wounding, possession of two knives and criminal damage for the attack against the bailiff. He was found not guilty of wounding with intent. The incident to which the charges related took place in Birmingham in 2025, while a counter-terrorism unit monitored Alfatih's movements. The court was shown images of Alfatih brandishing two large kitchen knives outside his home in a suburban area of the West Midlands city. His successful asylum claim - and later citizenship - has raised questions for the Home Office over why Alfatih was permitted to stay in the UK. Hamas was proscribed in 2021, and had been partially outlawed in the UK since 2001. A Home Office spokesman said: “While we do not routinely comment on individual cases or operational matters, we take all steps necessary at all times to protect the nation’s security.” LATEST DEVELOPMENTS: In the meantime, German authorities have been building their case against Alfatih over what could have been the first Hamas attack on mainland Europe. The terror plot was set for October 2025, targeting synagogues and other Jewish buildings across Berlin as well as the Austrian city of Vienna. It would have taken place on the second anniversary of the Hamas massacre in Israel, where the militants murdered more than 1,200 civilians. Germany's intelligence services had received a tip-off from Israel's Mossad intelligence over the plot. Alfatih travelled from his home in the West Midlands to Hasenheide Park in Berlin to meet an accomplice, gathering five loaded HS-9 semi-automatic pistols. He concealed the firearms as he crossed over into Austria, where he stashed them away along with balaclavas and gloves. German prosecutors said Alfatih had filmed a video in which he was donning a face mask, sitting in front of two pistols. He then returned to the UK, where he continued plotting. However, a week before the planned attack, German officers arrested his accomplices, while West Midlands Police arrested him on suspicion of expressing support for a proscribed organisation. After he endured a grilling by British cops, he was placed under surveillance by security services. Mere days before the German authorities geared up to submit an extradition request, Alfatih then engaged in the violent brawl which led to his second arrest. The man in question was alleged to have obtained an automatic rifle, 13 pistols and more than 700 rounds of ammunition. German prosecutors are reported to have acquired a clip, during which Alfatih could be heard outlining the terror plot, vowing to deliver a "message in blood to despicable war criminal" Benjamin Netanyahu. On November 2, the 40-year-old was taken into custody before he was found guilty of his crimes in Birmingham. Now, he is face-to-face with extradition - after the attempt was put on hold during his criminal trial for assault - over terrorism offences. He will be sentenced for his criminal convictions on January 11. During the trial, Alfatih opened up on his life before his arrest, explaining how his younger brother was killed in Gaza just a few days before attacking the bailiff. While he claimed his brother was murdered by the Israel Defence Forces (IDF), insiders claimed he had been killed through Palestinian gang violence. He said: "On October 12, I learned that my youngest brother had sustained a wound in his head and his injury was very serious. "I talked to my uncle, a doctor, and he told me that I don’t think he will survive. The next day I was told he had died." Growing up in Gaza, Alfatih’s father was appointed the territory's Minister of Health, and was a key ally to Haniyeh. Alfatih said he suffered from post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) following his damaging time in Gaza. Once he reached the UK, he said he started working for Dunlop Aircraft Tyres in Birmingham - despite an online CV, which appears to be his, suggested he had reached Britain as early as 2009. The same document suggested he was enrolled at the West Midlands branch of the London School of Science and Technology (LSST) to study Logistic Management - despite a spokesman for the institution saying it was unable to find any record of him. The same CV says he graduated from college in Birmingham in 2013, after which he proceeded to work for Direct Line, Samsung Electronics and Virgin Trains as a network engineer. Then the father moved to Birmingham permanently, with his wife and children, and was said to have been a "nightmare" tenant for his landlord. "He just seemed to be in a bit of a situation with his housing, so I agreed to let him rent my place. I didn’t know anything about his background or how long he’d been in the UK," his ex-landlord told The Telegraph. "He never paid his rent on time and it became a nightmare, having to chase him for the money all the time," he added.
