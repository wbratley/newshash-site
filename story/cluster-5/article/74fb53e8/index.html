'I'm not getting out so I thought f*** it, I'll kill him if I can': Murderer says he 'caved in' Soham killer Ian Huntley's head 'six or seven times' with a four-kilo stillage post, court hears

Article text

'I'm not getting out so I thought f*** it, I'll kill him if I can': Murderer says he 'caved in' Soham killer Ian Huntley's head 'six or seven times' with a four-kilo stillage post, court hears A triple killer battered Soham murderer Ian Huntley around the head with a metal bar because he had no prospect of release and was being 'wound up,' a court heard today. Anthony Russell, 44, bludgeoned Huntley 'six or seven times' in a 'cold-blooded murder' after the pair had argued in Durham's Frankland prison. Huntley, 52, serving a life sentence for the 2002 murders of ten-year-olds Holly Wells and Jessica Chapman in Soham, Cambridgeshire, was set upon by Russell in the prison's recycling workshop on February 26, Newcastle Crown Court was told. Russell had been arguing with Huntley for at least a year and at one point the men had to be separated after they threatened each other, prosecutor Matthew Bean KC said. Having been convicted of the murders of three people in 2020, Russell was serving a whole life sentence, meaning he had no prospect of release, the court was told. In a 'carefully planned and executed act', Russell then plotted for weeks to murder Huntley over 'wholly trivial incidents,' which included Huntley 'getting in [Russell's] face', Mr Bean said. He said he had 'nothing to lose' and was sick of 'cocky' Huntley, the court heard. Using a 4kg metal bar – or stillage post – used for stacking items on pallets, he lay in wait for the 'perfect opportunity' to attack Huntley in the recycling workshop where they both worked before 'brutally' battering him around the head and leaving him unconscious. He told a prison officer who intervened in the immediate aftermath that Huntley had been 'winding us up' and 'pushing my buttons for months,' the court heard. When two other prison officers arrived at the scene, Russell was asked whether he had punched Huntley and allegedly replied: 'No, I’ve hopefully killed him.' He then explained that he had 'smacked him over the head with one of them metal bars,' the court heard. Later, Russell told prison governor Craig Phipps that Huntley had been 'putting it on me', the court was told. He added: 'I'm not getting out and I'm a natural lifer, so I thought "f*** it."' Russell allegedly said his intention was to kill Huntley, adding: 'That's why I caved his head in about six, seven times with a metal steel bar.' Russell denies the murder of Huntley, who died at the Royal Victoria Infirmary in Newcastle on March 7, having never regained consciousness following the attack. On the first day of his murder trial, the jury was told that Russell will claim he had been 'hearing voices in his head' in the run-up to the attack and suffered a 'sudden loss of self-control' when Huntley shoulder-barged him and then grinned. He will claim he is not guilty of murder by reason of diminished responsibility owing to PTSD from childhood incidents. Anthony Russell is on trial for the alleged murder at HMP Frankland near Durham Ian Huntley was taken to the Royal Victoria Infirmary in Newcastle where he died on March 7 Heavily tattooed Russell, sporting a dark goatee beard, sat alongside four police officers in the dock as the case was opened. Mr Bean told the jury that in March last year Russell and Huntley were seen arguing, threatening each other and 'had to be separated by prison staff.' In January, Russell was seen with a black eye. He told prison officers he had fallen over but jurors were told that it 'may have been caused by Ian Huntley.' On February 26, both men were working in the recycling workshop which had been closed for months until the day before the attack. Civilian instructor Mark Staplin and prison officer Mark Patterson soon became aware of a 'commotion' on the workshop floor, the court was told. Opening the case, Mr Bean said: 'There is no issue in this trial that this commotion had been caused by the defendant, Anthony Russell, repeatedly striking Ian Huntley to the head with a long orange metal bar. 'The prosecution case is that this was a cold-blooded murder that had been planned weeks in advance of its execution.' He added: 'The name of the man he killed, Ian Huntley, will be known to many, if not all of you. 'He had been convicted of the murder of two girls, Holly Wells and Jessica Chapman, that took place in the village of Soham in Cambridgeshire in August 2002. 'These were offences that, for entirely understandable reasons, aroused feelings of disgust and anger. 'Due to the nature of his offences, you may feel little or no sympathy for what happened to him. More than that, the prosecution do not ask for sympathy. 'Ultimately, this case is not about the appalling actions of Ian Huntley during his life, but the manner of his death.' Russell claimed Huntley had been 'bullying him for weeks,' the court heard. A sketch of Russell appearing via videolink at Newton Aycliffe Magistrates' Court in March Huntley was serving a life sentence for murdering Holly Wells and Jessica Chapman in 2002 Mr Bean said that Mr Staplin, who was in an office overlooking the workshop, saw Russell 'deliver at least 6 blows with the iron bar to Ian Huntley's head.' He added: 'He will describe these blows as being, to use his words, "brutal", and how the defendant continued to swing the iron bar despite being told to stop. 'His view of what he saw was that the defendant appeared out to intentionally kill him.' After the attack, Russell told a governor he was 'sick' of Huntley, adding: 'He put it on me about six months ago and I walked away, but when he sees weakness in people he tries to bully ‘em and he’s been getting in my face the last few weeks and my head [so I] just thought "f*** it", I ain’t putting up with it anymore. 'I’m not getting out, I’m a natural lifer, so I thought ‘f*** it’.' He later told police: 'He’s been getting really arrogant, cocky, in my face, winding me up, just little smiles as if to say "yeah" sort of thing, and I’d just had enough of it. 'I thought, "f*** you, you ain't getting away with it. I've got nothing to lose, I'm never getting out.' He added: 'My mindset was, I'm not getting out, I'm not gonna just hurt him because I'm gonna lose everything that I've built up in the last five years, for a little fight, that's not worth it, in my head... so I thought, "f*** it, I'll kill him, if I can.' The court heard when Huntley was taken to hospital he had multiple skull fractures and bleeding on the brain. He had a section of his skull removed to allow the removal of blood that had collected between the skull and surface of the brain. Life support was withdrawn on March 6 when the medical team treating him concluded that he could not survive the serious brain injury that he had suffered, and he died the following morning. The trial continues.
