Hundreds of police mourn hero officer killed in A66 crash - as man arrested on suspicion of assault at service for passenger in car which killed him

Article text

- See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source As the hearse carrying PC Matt Blades, bearing the word 'Dad', made its way down Hutton Avenue in his hometown of Hartlepool today, the dignified silence was almost unnerving. For minutes at a time, the only sounds audible above respectful whispers were the buzz of a drone filming this ceremonial funeral and the clip-clop of hooves from the four horses that led the procession. Given the chaos which has gripped this part of north-east England in the weeks since PC Blades, 37, and his colleague at Cleveland Police, PC Tom Clough, 38, were killed in a head-on smash on the A66 in Middlesbrough, the respect shown by the hundreds of members of the public who gathered to pay their respects restored a little of one's faith in humanity. How different this scene was to those over recent days celebrating the lives of the five young men driving a car on cloned number plates who also died in the crash that killed the two officers. During a 'memorial' service to Michael Cahill, 23, last month, five vehicles were set alight in Haswell, County Durham, and fireworks and flares were set off. Other funerals to the five have seen young men hiding their faces behind balaclavas or hanging out of car windows. Sadly, the tasteless tributes continued today. Just as more than 1,000 police officers quietly lined the streets to watch PC Blades' coffin, draped in the Union Jack, carried into St Joseph's Roman Catholic Church, a service for Cole Worthy, who died in the same crash, was being held nearby in Middlesbrough. A 39-year-old man was arrested on suspicion of assault before the service resumed. There were no such issues at the funeral for married father-of-two PC Blades, an armed response officer and former teacher who had served with Cleveland Police since 2015. Many officers hugged before the procession arrived and even touchingly fixed one another's lapels and straightened ties. Some wore identity patches bearing the words 'PC 2443 BLADES' and 'PC 3234 CLOUGH'. Standard bearers waved flags representing every force from across the country before four pipers and a drummer processed in front of the funeral car and played Amazing Grace as the coffin was led inside the church. Emergency services representatives from the North East Ambulance Service and Great North Air Ambulance were also in attendance. Among those paying their respects were Cleveland Police Chief Constable Victoria Fuller, policing minister Sarah Jones, Home Office minister Anna Turley, Conservative MP for Stockton West Matt Vickers and Tees Valley Mayor Ben Houchen. Cleveland Police Federation paid tribute to PC Blades, who was known as Matty, and to the courage of his family. Lauren Somerville, chairman of Cleveland Police Federation, said: 'The police family came together shoulder to shoulder today, arm in arm to remember our brave colleague PC Matthew Blades. 'It was an honour and a privilege to attend today's funeral on behalf of our colleagues across Cleveland. PC Blades, a football coach for youth teams, was a Manchester United fan and a regular at Old Trafford. His two young sons were mascots for the club's first home game of the season. A funeral service for PC Clough will be held on Thursday.
