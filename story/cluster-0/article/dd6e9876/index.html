William Chapman - latest: Horror at 'offensive' Trump tiger video after fatal mauling Former child actor William Chapman, 23, was killed after he jumped into the tiger enclosure at Yorkshire Wildlife Park with his family pleading for people to stop circulating 'deeply offensive media content' online The family of a man who was horrifically mauled to death by a tiger at Yorkshire Wildlife Park have called on people to stop sharing "deeply offensive media content" online - as anger grows over an "offensive" post shared on social media by Donald Trump. William Chapman, who was a former child actor, jumped into the tiger enclosure at the zoo as terrified onlookers urged him to "play dead". The Amur tiger, named Altai, then viciously attacked Mr Chapman, 23, and he was later pronounced dead at the scene. He had filmed chilling footage of himself approaching the tiger, breathing heavily in the moments before the mauling. His family said he suffered from "significant mental illness over a number of years". In a statement they said: "The family are utterly devastated by their loss and are struggling to come to terms with what has happened." The family has specifically asked for people not to share footage of the tragic incident on social media but, in a bizarre turn of events, US President Donald Trump has done just that, posting a video showing the animal biting and clawing at the man, though not the actual killing. He then went one step further, sharing an AI video of himself wrestling with a tiger with 'In The Jungle' playing in the background. In the video, posted on Truth Social, Trump chases the tiger before they end up embracing. The president's posts have been heavily criticised, with the Jeremy Vine show's Carole Malone among those calling it "offensive". Piers Morgan said: "This is appallingly tasteless. Delete it, President Trump." South Yorkshire Mayor Oliver Coppard said the President had used AI to "mock the incident and the pain felt by the family". Key Events Inside William Chapman's child acting career The man who was killed by a tiger at Yorkshire Wildlife Park was a former child actor. William Chapman, 23, starred in the ITV production May Contain Nuts alongside Shirley Henderson, best known for playing Moaning Myrtle in the Harry Potter films. He also played a young Edward Jenner in the BBC series True Stories. An old blog post written by William's mum offers a glimpse into his life years before the fatal attack. Published just over 12 years ago, the post described William as "passionate about acting and drama" having attended a performing arts school in High Wycombe since the age of four. The Jackie Palmer Stage School in High Wycombe posted a heartfelt tribute on its Facebook page. The post reads: "We are deeply saddened to hear of the passing of our former student, William Chapman. Our thoughts and heartfelt condolences are with William’s family and friends during this incredibly difficult time. "We loved watching William grow, both as a person and as a performer, during his time with us. It was a privilege to be part of his journey, and he will be remembered with great fondness by everyone who had the pleasure of knowing him. "William will be truly missed, and he will always hold a special place in our hearts. Rest in peace, William." Inquest into William Chapman's death to open tomorrow The inquest into the death of 23-year-old William Chapman will open at Doncaster Coroner's Court at 10am tomorrow. Mr Chapman died on Saturday after being fatally attacked by a tiger at Yorkshire Wildlife Park. Emergency services were called shortly after 1.30pm after Mr Chapman, who police said was visiting the park alone, entered the animal's enclosure. He was pronounced dead at the scene. City of Doncaster Council, which is responsible for licensing the park, said on Monday that it had launched a health and safety investigation. New photo of William Chapman released Unclear when Yorkshire Wildlife Park will reopen Yorkshire Wildlife Park remained closed today for a fifth day following the attack on Saturday. The park has not yet confirmed when it will reopen. It previously said members of staff were "deeply affected" by the incident and were receiving specialist support. South Yorkshire mayor condemns Trump's 'cheap laugh' AI video as Chapman experiences 'suffering' South Yorkshire Mayor Oliver Coppard has condemned Donald Trump's actions, saying the Chapman family had contacted his office. In a statement, Mr Coppard said: "They spoke to my office yesterday, sharing the pain and suffering it’s causing them knowing that videos and pictures of his death are being shared across social media. "The president of the United States now using AI to mock that incident and the pain felt by the family of Mr Chapman, for little more than a cheap laugh on social media, should be greeted with the contempt it deserves. "So I’m asking you, please, not to add to this family’s pain. If someone shares the content with you, please don’t pass it on." The mayor said: "What happened at Yorkshire Wildlife Park on Saturday was tragic, not least because it’s a place so many people associate with a family day out." He said: "The family of Mr Chapman remain utmost in my thoughts. I know all decent people across South Yorkshire will be sending them their love, thoughts and empathy.” Downing Street says people should respect the family's position Downing Street has said people should respect the views of William Chapman's family but would not directly comment on Donald Trump posting the videos online. 'If they really want to get in, they're going to find a way' Dr Campbell Murn, a lecturer in Conservation Science at the University of Reading, explained that despite UK zoos having to follow set regulations for enclosure designs, incidents like the one on Saturday cannot be eliminated. The expert, who is also a former member of the government's expert committee for zoos, said: "There’s an enormous amount of effort that goes into the securing of public safety for people who are visiting zoos, in addition to the weight that's given to animal welfare in those settings as well. "So it's certainly something that is a very big and integral part of the zoo standards in this country." Dr Murn added that staff at zoos which house dangerous wild animals are trained in emergency responses, explaining that he does not believe the incident at Yorkshire Wildlife Park was a result of a safety failure. Rather, he believes "it was not possible to get there before the visitor was injured and subsequently died". The expert said sophisticated enclosures that make it more difficult for unauthorised people to enter them "would minimise or reduce the risk, but it's never going to truly eliminate it". He said: "Even the most elaborate enclosure, if they really want to get in, they’re going to find a way".
