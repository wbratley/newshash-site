Esther Rantzen, TV presenter and Childline founder, dies aged 86

Article text

Esther Rantzen, the Bafta-winning TV presenter and founder of Childline, has died aged 86. In a statement, her family paid tribute to “the most wonderful mother” and “cherished grandmother”, whose “legacy of compassion and understanding will live on in her charities Childline and the Silverline, and through those of us who were lucky enough to love and be loved by her”. “We are grateful she is finally at rest with her beloved husband, Desmond Wilcox, but we will miss her immeasurably. She was our sunshine. We will always remember her endless capacity for joy, her ability to comfort every heartache, her beautiful way with words and her expert guidance in all things,” said her three children. Rantzen announced a diagnosis of stage four lung cancer in 2023, and became a vocal campaigner to change the law around assisted dying. In September 2026, she announced she had to abandon plans to end her life in Swiss clinic Dignitas, due to being too physically frail. “Unfortunately, when I was strong enough to make the flight and go to Dignitas, my life was not unbearably painful; indeed, I was able to relax in my beloved cottage in the New Forest,” she said in a statement. “And by the time life did become unbearable, I was too physically fragile to make the journey and go through the Dignitas process.” “So even though I had spent the money and made the plans, I was not ready to end my life back then, and did not realise that would be my last chance.” The prime minister, Andy Burnham, said: “Very sad to hear that Dame Esther Rantzen has died. Through her decades of campaigning, including founding Childline and The Silver Line, she changed countless lives and gave a voice to people who often went unheard. “And I will always admire the characteristic courage and openness with which she spoke about her illness, making us all think about the difficult questions we face at the end of life. My thoughts are with her family and all who loved her.” Kim Leadbeater, the Labour MP who introduced a bill to legalise assisted dying, said: “Esther was a strong, warm and compassionate woman and a fighter to the end. “Her positivity and determination never to give in gave me strength, but more importantly helped all those others like her who were confronting a terminal illness to keep going in the knowledge that they were not alone.” Rantzen was born in Berkhamsted, Hertfordshire, in 1940 and began her career in 1963 as a BBC radio studio manager. Having moved into television as a researcher, she became a presenter in 1968, as part of the consumer affairs show Braden’s Week, before being appointed as the main host of its replacement programme, That’s Life!, in 1973. Part magazine, part consumer show, with hard-hitting investigations and jokes, That’s Life! drew more than 20 million viewers at its peak – and ran for 21 years with Rantzen as anchor. After she successfully pitched an episode about child abuse to the then BBC controller Michael Grade, a set of phone lines was opened for 48 hours, which enabled children to call in and speak to social workers. The volume of responses led her to turn the service into a permanent fixture, launching the children’s charity Childline in 1986. In 1988, Rantzen created Hearts of Gold, a series focusing on people responsible for outstanding acts of kindness, and hosted her own BBC Two chatshow, Esther, from 1994, when That’s Life! came to an end, until 2002. Further TV projects included the consumer affairs show Old Dogs New Tricks, as well as competing in Strictly Come Dancing’s second series and the 2008 edition of the ITV reality show I’m a Celebrity … Get Me Out of Here! She received an OBE for services to broadcasting, Bafta’s Richard Dimbleby award for factual programming and a lifetime achievement prize at the Women in Film and Television awards. Her wide-ranging charity work saw her act as a patron or vice-president of numerous organisations. In 2010, she launched the Silver Line – a free confidential helpline for older people, which led to her being made a dame for services to children and older people. In 2010, she stood as an independent candidate for Luton South in that year’s general election, coming fourth after winning 4.4% of the vote. She wrote numerous books, including a murder-mystery novel. In later years, she was a regular contributor to the One Show and hosted a podcast with her former That’s Life! colleague Adrian Mills. On Instagram, the television presenter Anna Williamson described Rantzen as “somebody truly iconic, who really cared, and who has left behind one hell of a legacy”. Fellow presenter Lorraine Kelly also paid tribute to Rantzen on social media for being a “pioneer and a force of nature – [who] helped so many people – funny, forthright and fabulous”. One of Rantzen’s former co-hosts of That’s Life!, Howard Leader, said: “Over the time that I was with her, through her fantastic generosity, she taught me how to be a journalist. “I think Esther’s skill was that she really understood how to use the platform she had to create an effect for, we hope ... the improvement of people in our country.” Her husband, Desmond Wilcox, whom she married in 1977, died in 2000. Rantzen is survived by their three children, Miriam, Rebecca and Joshua – as well as five grandchildren.
