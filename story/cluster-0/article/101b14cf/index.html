The moment I never thought I'd see my family again: Huntingdon attack victim shares chilling footage of hero passengers fighting back against knifeman during train rampage

Article text

A father-of-four has revealed how he put his life on the line to join a desperate fight back against the Huntingdon train stabber. Raza Aslam was sitting quietly, looking at his phone on the LNER service from Doncaster to London on November 1 last year when someone shouted: 'Everyone run, there's been a stabbing.' Just two minutes later, he found himself armed with a pan and taking on Anthony Williams, who stabbed a total of ten people on the train in addition to four victims he attacked in the previous 24 hours. Williams was yesterday found guilty of 14 counts of attempted murder. Mr Aslam, 45, who had to retreat from the melee when he was stabbed in the arm and now faces a long recovery as well as suffering from PTSD and anxiety, told the Daily Mail: 'I decided I wasn't going to survive. The train was moving very fast, outside it was dark. I was really scared. We were on our own.' Mr Aslam - who recorded some of the shocking scenes on his mobile phone - was travelling home to north London after delivering a car to Nottingham for the dealership he worked for. He had been due to travel to Pakistan the previous day but the flight was cancelled due to technical problems and he decided to take the job. 'I was on my return journey, started doing my prayers and by the time I reached Peterborough I had finished,' the devout Muslim said. 'I was on my phone when all hell broke loose. A young man started saying: 'Everyone run, there's been a stabbing. Run, run'. Raza Aslam, 45, had his radial nerve slashed when he confronted Anthony Williams Mr Aslam's mobile phone footage shows fearful passengers waiting tensely for the approach of the knifeman, who had already attacked several people 'I looked back – there was a whole group of people, young, old, black, white, everybody, and they were all overtaken by something, they were all scared. 'I didn't believe it initially, but I saw that people had lost the colour in their faces.' No warning or advice was issued over the train's tannoy and he recalls shouting at passengers to run for their lives – before having the presence of mind to realise some might be injured in a 'stampede' and urging them to help others if they fell. 'I couldn't see anyone behind me yet but grown men, strong men, everyone was running,' said Mr Aslam, who is married to Nagina, also 45. 'We all met up in the last carriage, carriage M, which was the first-class carriage. I'd still not seen him [the knifeman]. 'A young man said he'd seen what happened, that someone had started some sort of argument and had slashed a man's arm. Another man had the knife scraped on his forehead. 'I realised this was indiscriminate and something was very, very wrong – worse than the worst-case scenario. 'This madman is coming. I decided I wasn't going to survive. The train was moving very fast, outside it was dark. I was really scared. We were on our own.' CCTV from the train shows Williams in carriage H, with heroic LNER employee Samir Zitouni in a white shirt at the front trying to fend him off. Injured Mr Aslam had retreated to the back of the carriage at this point The panic among passengers is plain in Mr Aslam's mobile phone footage, which begins with a terrified woman saying: 'Lock the door, lock the door, lock the door', as another person gasps: 'Oh my god.' Samir Zitouni, an LNER employee who the trial at Cambridge Crown Court heard had created a barricade using two metal food trolleys, can be heard saying: 'I don't think you can lock that door.' Mr Aslam addresses the other passengers and says: 'I don't think he's going to come and stab everybody but who knows. Did anyone see it? Is it random?' A young man tells him: 'He said: "Be a man." Then the guy said "sorry".' Despite the fear and confusion gripping those trapped in the carriage with nowhere to go, Mr Zitouni, known as Sam, calls for 'strong men' to help him fend off Williams and handed out pans he had grabbed. The screen goes black at this stage but there are crashing noises and the tension in Mr Zitouni's voice can be heard as he says: 'Stay back, stay back, stay back.' At this point, Mr Aslam, who has one of the pans, bravely tries to hold Williams back but is stabbed in the left arm, severing the radial nerve. Describing the terrifying moment, he said: 'I had to take a risk [alongside Mr Zitouni] because I thought I wasn't going to make it home that night. Mr Aslam told the Daily Mail: 'I decided I wasn't going to survive. The train was moving very fast, outside it was dark. I was really scared. We were on our own.' The scar from an operation to re-attach his radial nerve. Mr Aslam has been told he will never recover the full use of his left arm 'He walked in and looked perfectly relaxed – no sweat on his brow, no stress. It's like nothing [out of the ordinary] had happened to him. 'He said "Who's man enough to take me on?" and I said "No one's going to hurt you." 'It was designed to try and appease him but I looked in his eyes and he had this crazy, demonic look. 'I think he saw that I had something in my hand and he lifted the knife in the air and plunged it right into my elbow. 'It severed my nerve. I knew he'd done it straight away. My arm felt weak and useless and the pain exploded. It was burning.' In the footage, Mr Aslam can be heard shouting 'No, no, no' before shouting out 'Ah' in pain. A woman screams as he utters: 'I've been stabbed, I've been stabbed. I'm bleeding badly.' There are more banging and crashing sounds as the chaos continues before the recording suddenly ends after nearly three minutes. Mr Zitouni then intervened and began throwing items at Williams, aided by another LNER worker who had a frying pan. 'I was really scared and started running to the back [of the carriage], Mr Aslam said. Williams seen moving between carriages with the ten-inch knife during his marauding attack on innocent passengers Anthony Williams was convicted of 14 counts of attempted murder following a five-week trial 'The man was holding the shining, metal knife. He was doing a figure of eight with it and smiling while he did it. 'I looked at Sam and the other guy to see if they were injured. They were both as strong as rocks and I assumed they were doing well. I was shouting at people to get further back.' A curious moment then unfolded, which may have saved lives on board the train, Mr Aslam said. Williams looked towards an older woman, since identified as Christina Jones, who had been sitting near the front of the carriage with a purple wheeled walker in front of her table. 'She looked angry, like a mother fed up with her children. She had no fear on her face,' said Mr Aslam. 'This lady started to shout at him, pointing at him and telling him off. I can't remember the exact words because I was in shock. 'The guy was looking at her with a devoted look. He stopped with the knife in the air. 'I realised she couldn't scream very loud because she was an old lady. I started shouting: "People want to get off. I'm going to bleed to death. My children are never going to see me again. And the other people, you've killed them. You got what you wanted"'. Mr Aslam's children, from left, Maryam, 19, Aizah, eight, Abdur, 16, and Jibreel, 13 'His face changed. I saw a look of satisfaction and he put the knife down. It was like the calm after the storm. 'Sam ran towards me. I saw him from top to bottom and I'll never forget that moment. He was very seriously injured. From his head to his chest he was full of blood. He had cuts of every shape and size. 'I'd thought the knife had missed his head and arm and neck but it didn't. It hit him every time. 'I think he said to me "I don't think I'm going to make it."' A glance outside revealed a platform had suddenly appeared. 'I was confused. Then I realised the train had stopped. So then I realised the police are here and they're going to save everybody. Do you know how that felt?' added Mr Aslam. CCTV from Huntingdon railway station, where the train driver had made an unscheduled emergency stop, showed hundreds of people piling onto the platform and running away. Mr Zitouni, who had suffered numerous wounds, staggered off before falling between two carriages onto the tracks. Medics at the scene feared he was going to stop breathing as both his lungs were punctured. He was rushed to hospital and treated in intensive care, including four transfusions to counter his 'catastrophic blood loss'. Mr Aslam also received hospital treatment and had the nerve in his arm re-attached, but he still struggled to pick up anything heavier than a pack of crisps. 'I've been told I've got three years of healing to do and I'll be lucky to get 50-60 per cent of movement back,' he said. 'I've tried going back to work several times to get my mind off things but it didn't work. And I can't drive anymore because of my hand.' He is also taking medication to help him deal with the huge mental toll the experience had on him, leaving him with PTSD, depression and anxiety. Describing the emotional reunion with his family when he got home, he said: 'My wife and children came to my room crying. They were scared to hug me, thinking it would cause me more pain.' Despite the horrors of those few minutes on the train, Mr Aslam sounded a ray of hope, explaining: 'We're told of a broken British society but that [night] proved otherwise. 'Total strangers to one another, but risking their lives for each other, each person played an independent but critical role. All those roles put together just barely averted a massacre, against all the odds.' Maryam set up a crowdfunding page to help her father make ends meet – but with a modest target of just £5,500. She said: 'He has nightmares and flashbacks, and he hasn't been able to work since the attack. It's been hard on our family financially, but he's been trying to get through it without asking for anything. 'He was invited to meet the King for what he did that day, but because of the case and everything he was going through, he couldn't attend. He's never wanted recognition — he just did what he felt was right.' Williams was convicted of 14 counts of attempted murder following the five-week trial. He had previously admitted 12 counts of wounding with intent and two of attempting to wound with intent. The defendant also admitted a range of other offences, some in relation to other incidents, including theft of knives, possession of knives, affray and assaulting a police officer. He will be sentenced in February.
