Major incident declared at Manchester concert after 'tear gas' let off in venue

Article text

A major incident has been declared after a substance, believed to be tear gas, was "let off in the crowd". Thousands of concertgoers were rushed out of the Co-op Live Arena at around 10pm during a performance by US rapper Don Toliver. Videos on social media showed dozens of emergency vehicles surrounding the arena. Armed officers were also seen at the scene. Greater Manchester Police has now declared it a major incident. One person was taken to hospital after the incident, and several others were treated on site. In a statement, it said: "It is understood that a substance believed to be tear gas was let off in the crowd towards the end of a concert. "All attendees are currently being safely evacuated as a precaution. "Those showing signs of being impacted are being supported by emergency services, with thankfully no serious injuries reported. "Please stay away from the arena whilst emergency services help those that have been at the concert safely make their way to their transport home." Video filmed from within the venue shows the crowd suddenly dispersing as Mr Toliver performs. A separate video shows panicked concertgoers covering their faces as they head out of the venue. One is heard saying that he "can't breathe" as a result of a "smell". LATEST IN MANCHESTER: Neil Sayle, who was near where the tear gas was released, told the Manchester Evening News he heard a loud bang before "lots of smoke" was seen in the venue. And 22-year-old Molly told the newspaper that "everyone was running out, coughing" just 40 minutes into the performance. "Everyone was being handed out water and still in the arena so we left and saw all the police and ambulance and no one would tell us anything," she added. Greater Manchester Police urged anyone who is still suffering side effects or becomes ill to attend their local hospital. A venue official was also heard telling the crowd that "due to unforeseen circumstances, the show is now finished". "In your own time, please make your way to the nearest exit," the official instructed. "If you require medical assistance, please speak to a member of staff or visit the level zero concourse." Don Toliver, who rose to fame through collaborations with Travis Scott, has over 45 million listeners per month, according to Spotify. He is set to continue his NITROUS world tour with performances in London this week. Assistant Chief Constable Steph Parker, who is Gold Commander for the incident, said: "We understand the concern that this incident will have raised - particularly those with loved ones at the venue last night - and we'd like to thank everyone for their patience as we dealt with it. "There were over 12,000 people in attendance during this concert who were safely escorted from the arena so they could make their way home. "Our declaration of a major incident has allowed us to ensure emergency services and partner agencies can work together quickly and effectively to coordinate a safe response.
