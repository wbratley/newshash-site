A day after Brand was jailed for 10-and-a-half years for raping one woman and sexually assaulting three others, Walliams faced accusations of sexually inappropriate behaviour from women, including two who claimed they were “groomed” by the comedian when they were 15. Walliams has denied any wrongdoing and insisted he is “not a sexual predator”, adding he is “devastated” that the allegations, published by the Guardian and ITV News, have been brought against him. During ITV’s reporting, a photograph resurfaced showing Ross alongside Walliams at an event also attended by one of the women who later accused Walliams of “highly inappropriate” behaviour. Lawyers for Walliams have said he denies behaving inappropriately. In a statement on Tuesday night, Ross, who has appeared on TV shows with Walliams, said: “I am as horrified by the revelations about David Walliams and the crimes of Russell Brand as the rest of the nation. There was a time in my life when I was on friendly terms with both men, and during that period I was genuinely unaware of their conduct. “My thoughts are with the many, many women who have bravely come forward.” No10: Allegations against Walliams are 'deeply troubling' Downing Street described the allegations against David Walliams as “deeply troubling” after he was named in an investigation as the celebrity accused of grooming and sexually inappropriate behaviour towards multiple women. Asked about the investigation by Independent Television News and Guardian News and Media, publisher of The Guardian, the prime minister’s official spokesman said: “I don’t think we’ve seen the full extent of the reporting. “They’re clearly deeply troubling allegations, and the Government will doubtless respond to them during the course of the day, I’m sure.” Watch: Jonathan Ross jokes about David Walliams 'going home with 15-year-olds' at 2004 comedy awards Walliams issues emphatic denial in response to allegations David Walliams has insisted he is “not a sexual predator” and has said he is “devastated” that allegations have been brought against him. The Guardian published their joint investigation with ITV News, in which he is accused of sexually inappropriate behaviour by multiple women. In a statement, the comedian said: “Looking back, I realise that at times I behaved thoughtlessly towards other people I had casual sexual relationships with… I realise I did not always take people’s feelings properly into account. That is something I regret. “And whilst a power imbalance is inevitable when one party is famous, abusing that power is something else entirely and it is something that I never did… “I am not a sexual predator… I have not broken the law… I am devastated that some of the women I met ten to twenty years ago have now expressed hurt or anger towards me.” BBC explains why it is removing programmes featuring Walliams The BBC is in the process of removing some programmes, including Little Britain, while it carries out a review of “all material featuring and written by David Walliams on our platforms”, a spokesperson has said. “These are serious allegations,” the BBC said. “As such, the BBC is looking carefully at all material featuring and written by David Walliams on our platforms. “We are in the process of removing some programmes, including Little Britain, whilst this review is under way.” Who is David Walliams? The former Britain’s Got Talent judge, known for appearing in comedy shows including Come Fly With Me and Little Britain alongside Matt Lucas, has sold more than 60 million copies of his children’s books globally. They include The Boy In The Dress, Fing, and Gangsta Granny, the latter of which was turned into a one-hour BBC special in 2013, followed by a sequel starring Sheridan Smith in 2022. Further accolades won by Walliams include a Pride of Britain Award in 2006 for most influential public figure after raising £1.5 million for Sport Relief by swimming the English Channel. In 2017, he was appointed an OBE for services to charity and the arts. David Walliams: From controversial comedy to grooming allegations – his fall from grace has been a long time coming David Walliams, the comedian and television personality who became Britain’s best-selling children’s author, has been named as the “famous man” who sought a High Court injunction to prevent the publication of allegations against him. Now, with that attempt rejected, an investigation from The Guardian and ITV has laid out a series of claims going back over two decades. Walliams has been accused of grooming, sexual assault and a pattern of inappropriate behaviour towards women and girls as young as 15. He has denied any wrongdoing. Having seemed for a while to be on a trajectory towards “national treasure” status, Walliams’ reputation has plummeted in recent years. These new reports will likely finish off the remnants of his career and mark the start of the long process towards justice. Born in London in 1971, Walliams grew up in the suburbs and attended Reigate Grammar School, the alma mater of Sir Keir Starmer. While studying drama at the University of Bristol, Walliams enrolled in the National Youth Theatre, where he bonded with another young actor, Matt Lucas, over a shared appreciation for Vic Reeves Big Night Out. They had a mutual interest in comedy that verged on the grotesque, which led the duo to their first sketch show, Mash and Peas, directed by Edgar Wright, which appeared on the Paramount Comedy channel in 1996. Jonathan Ross says Walliams joke about a 15-year-old girl was 'tasteless' In his statement, Jonathan Ross also addressed his controversial joke about Walliams and said: “I recognise how deeply shocking it is to revisit footage of a joke delivered by me at the Comedy Awards in 2004, in which I make light of the notion of Walliams going home with a 15-year-old. Walliams is accused of grooming teenagers as young as 15. He denies all wrongdoing. Ross said: “[The] joke was submitted to me by the Comedy Awards script writing team and I believed that the punchline was a reference to the fact he had publicly dated younger women, exaggerated for comic effect. “It is also a tasteless joke that I would not dream of making today about anyone, but that is beside the point. “Had I had any reason to suspect that it might be referring to actual events, I would not only have refused to use it, but I would not have felt comfortable continuing a personal or professional relationship with him. “I also like to think that I would have acted upon that knowledge.” Jonathan Ross 'horrified' by allegations on Walliams and crimes of Brand Jonathan Ross has said he is “horrified” by the allegations about David Walliams and the crimes of Russell Brand. Walliams has been accused of sexual misconduct, including grooming teenage girls as young as 15 and abusing his position to pursue women. He denies all allegations against him. In Tuesday night’s ITV documentary David Walliams: Power and Silence, Ross was pictured alongside Walliams at an event. In a statement issued by Ross said: “I am as horrified by the revelations about David Walliams and the crimes of Russell Brand as the rest of the nation. “There was a time in my life when I was on friendly terms with both men, and during that period I was genuinely unaware of their conduct. “My thoughts are with the many, many women who have bravely come forward.” 'Women must feel safe in their jobs', says culture secretary Lisa Nandy Among those to react to the reporting of allegations against David Walliams tonight is culture secretary Lisa Nandy, who said it was time for industry bosses to “draw line in the sand”. The accusations against the Walliams come a day after Russell Brand was convicted of rape and sexual assault. Walliams has been alleged to have grooming teenagers as young as 15. He denies any wrongdoing. Ms Nandy said: “These allegations are the latest in a long line of reported abuses of power in an industry where we have known for so long that the power imbalances are huge.” The minister said she was bringing together senior members of the media and entertainment industries to “draw line in the sand”. She said: “We must finally take meaningful steps to end abuse and harassment in sectors where the power imbalances are huge and careers are built on contacts and reputation. “Women must feel safe in their jobs, whistleblowers must be protected, and those who abuse their position must no longer be able to hide behind it. To victims past and present, this government is not neutral when it comes to your safety."
