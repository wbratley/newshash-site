Inside the 007 casting, including the mistake that cleared the way for Robert Pattinson to land James Bond role - and the A-list legend in the running to play his nemesis

Inside the 007 casting, including the mistake that cleared the way for Robert Pattinson to land James Bond role - and the A-list legend in the running to play his nemesis - Get all the celebrity scoops and scandals straight from the heart of Hollywood in our new Spotlight US newsletter 'Was this really a secret audition, or did the other four guys never stand a chance?' It was a question posed by one suspicious fan on X after British heartthrob Robert Pattinson was crowned the new James Bond. And they're not alone in wondering. For the Batman star, 40, swooped in as a last-minute contender to beat out Nicholas Hoult, Callum Turner, Jack Lowden and Josh O'Connor. It's also a dramatic about-face for a franchise that famously favored lesser-known names under former Bond producer Barbara Broccoli, until Amazon MGM Studios took creative control in 2025 and tossed the old rulebook out the window. Theories have been flying ever since, with whispers that the original shortlist failed to wow in screen tests and that director Denis Villeneuve, Pattinson's Dune: Part Three boss, had always had his eye on his pal. Robert Pattinson, the new James Bond, may have Warner Bros. to thank for the role, as the studio's shaky DC future left him and Nicholas Hoult free to chase 007, an insider told the Daily Mail Nicholas Hoult, DC's Lex Luthor, was Pattinson's last rival for 007, with his future in the Superman franchise up in the air after a shake-up at DC Studios (pictured in 2025) But Pattinson's path to 007 may have been cleared by something far juicier than friendship or flopped screen tests – and it apparently all comes down to Warner Bros., which had both him and Hoult, who play Batman and Lex Luthor, respectively, under the DC Universe umbrella. 'Warner Bros. may be looking back at this situation and realizing they made a tremendous mistake,' one insider told the Daily Mail. 'They inadvertently created the perfect opening for both Robert and Nicholas to pursue something outside of their DC commitments.' So what was that opening? According to the source, it was the shaky future of the DC universe that left the pair up for grabs. 'The irony is that Warner Bros. had two actors in its stable who were perfectly positioned to become major franchise anchors for the studio,' they said. 'But the uncertainty surrounding DC left both men free to entertain one of the biggest opportunities in Hollywood.' And DC has had no shortage of uncertainty. After the DC Extended Universe fizzled out with The Flash, James Gunn and Peter Safran rebooted it with 2025's Superman. But just weeks after Paramount's $111 billion takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery closed in October, the newly renamed Skydance ousted the pair and named Marvel veteran Jeremy Latcham as DC Studios' new president, starting December 1. Warner Bros. had Pattinson, (pictured in The Batman), 'perfectly positioned' to anchor its franchise, but the 'uncertainty surrounding DC' left him free to chase Bond, an insider said Warner Bros. may have fumbled twice. Hoult, (pictured as Lex Luthor), was also left free to chase Bond, and his DC future is uncertain after James Gunn's exit as DC Studios boss That leaves both of the studio's would-be franchise anchors in limbo. The Batman: Part II has already been pushed to February 2028 after director Matt Reeves fell behind schedule and was temporarily replaced by Steven Soderbergh due to a personal issue. Puck's Matthew Belloni has suggested that without Reeves, Pattinson may hang up the cape. As for Hoult, 36, he is set to return as Lex Luthor in the Superman sequel Man of Tomorrow next July, but it's unclear whether the DC universe Gunn built will survive his exit – or whether Hoult will stick around if it does. With the Bat-signal fading and Lex Luthor's future up in the air, the gloves came off. 'Once that opportunity presented itself, it was really a matter of who could land the role,' our source said. And in that race, Pattinson was miles ahead of the pack. 'Robert Pattinson and Nicholas Hoult ultimately emerged as the final two contenders, but Robert was the clear choice when it came down to what Amazon wanted for the future of the franchise,' the source continued. Longtime Bond producer Barbara Broccoli reportedly liked Pattinson for the role as Daniel Craig's final outing, No Time to Die, was gearing up to shoot, but lost interest once he was cast as Batman (Bond producer Michael G Wilson, Daniel Craig, and Barbara Broccoli in 2019) 'He would have really had to screw up his audition to lose the job.' Amazon wanted a proven box-office name to launch a new era of 007, and Pattinson 'checked every one of those boxes.' 'He has already demonstrated that he can take over an established franchise and make an iconic character his own,' the source said, pointing to Twilight and The Batman."' The insider said Amazon, Villeneuve and casting director Nina Gold were all convinced Pattinson could deliver the biggest impact: 'In Hollywood, he's about as bulletproof as they come.' It doesn't hurt that the heartthrob is also a darling of the art-house crowd. 'What makes Robert particularly attractive is that he isn't simply a commercial star,' the source said, adding that he has won over everyone from mainstream audiences to directors like Christopher Nolan – 'a pretty rare combination.' As for Villeneuve, the source said: 'With Denis, he loves working with the same people and there is a friendship between the two so it was pretty much a no-brainer.' That cozy inner circle is exactly why fans have been side-eyeing the whole process. Our insider said Amazon, Villeneuve and casting director Nina Gold were all convinced Pattinson could deliver the biggest impact: 'In Hollywood, he's about as bulletproof as they come' The viral X post claimed that on Sunday morning, Villeneuve called Pattinson to make it official, while producer Amy Pascal 'had to call Nicholas Hoult, Callum Turner, Jack Lowden, and Josh O'Connor to break the bad news.' The fan also pointed out that Bond 26 co-producer David Heyman is 'the exact producer who cast a 19-year-old Pattinson in Harry Potter 21 years ago.' Villeneuve, at least, let the rejects down gently, saying: 'We were surrounded by extraordinary talent, but there can only be one 007,' while Pattinson called the role 'the honor of a lifetime.' And if the role was Pattinson's to lose, he certainly negotiated like it. According to Puck's Belloni, O'Connor and Lowden were required to sign on for four Bond films if chosen, while Pattinson's team got that down to three, plus a whole lot more money. Ironically, Pattinson could have been in the running years ago. Broccoli liked him for the role as Daniel Craig's final outing No Time to Die was gearing up to shoot, but quickly lost interest once he was cast as Batman, Puck reported. Still, not everyone believes she would have gone through with Pattinson. 'With a big budget and Denis preferring to work with familiar faces, Harrison Ford is being discussed for a Bond villain,' the Daily Mail source revealed 'This would never have happened under Barbara Broccoli,' Ian Fleming Foundation board member Matthew Field told the Daily Mail. 'She would not cast an actor who was already a movie star as James Bond.' But Broccoli's rules left with Broccoli. Amazon paid $8.5 billion for MGM with Bond as the crown jewel, only to find the Broccolis still held the creative keys, so it paid up again in February 2025 to wrest them away. 'Unlike previous installments, there is a feeling that the financial ceiling is virtually nonexistent with Amazon,' our insider said. 'They have the resources to attract some of the biggest names in Hollywood. The goal is to make this Bond feel enormous.' That could mean a legend as the villain. 'With a big budget and Denis preferring to work with familiar faces, Harrison Ford is being discussed for a Bond villain,' the source said. 'He is one of the last true movie stars, with a worldwide audience that spans generations. Pairing him with Robert would create a fascinating dynamic between two very different generations of leading men.' Ford, 84, starred in Villeneuve's Blade Runner 2049 in 2017 – and if the chatter pans out, the original Han Solo could soon be staring down the barrel of Pattinson's Walther.
