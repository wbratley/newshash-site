The King is at war with Princess Diana's brother, Earl Spencer, over his bombshell claim that Charles declared 'rest assured, we'll forget her soon enough' Prince Harry stands between Earl Spencer and Prince Charles outside Westminster Abbey during the funeral procession for Princess Diana on September 6, 1997 Charles and Diana on their last official trip together to South Korea in November 1992 In Swan Song: Diana, My Sister, the earl tells how in 1997 he clashed with Charles over the idea of Princes William and Harry walking in their mother's funeral procession. Earl Spencer, 62, says he has written the memoir to correct 'untruths' told about his older sister. The astonishing account of the King's alleged outburst, almost 30 years ago, is described by the earl over several pages. He says that one of Queen Elizabeth's most senior aides had told him, in the days before the funeral, that William and Harry would take part in the procession, which is when he responded that this was not what his sister would have wanted and was 'not going to happen'. Minutes later, he recalls, he received a phone call from Prince Charles who questioned the Spencer family's sense of duty and insisted that, 18 years earlier, he had walked in the funeral procession of Lord Mountbatten, his great-uncle. The memoir describes how Charles 'went nuts' after the earl pointed out that the prince had been 30 at the time of Lord Mountbatten's funeral in 1979. In one of the book's most extraordinary passages, Earl Spencer writes: 'Prince Charles continued his stream of anger, without me interrupting. Then he stopped. 'I assumed the pause signalled an effort to bring himself under control, but then he unleashed down the phone what I have always taken to be his pent-up contempt for Diana, and his horror that the world was so bowled over by her death. 'Rest assured,' he hissed, 'we'll forget her soon enough!'.' Earl Spencer said that he was 'so stunned' by the alleged remarks that he took a few seconds to reply. ''I can't believe you just said that,' I said flatly, before hanging up,' he writes. A source close to the King said the comment he is alleged to have made about Diana was 'just not in his nature and it certainly wasn't in his nature at that time, when he was a man absolutely grief-stricken'. They pointed out that the then Prince of Wales was the person who overruled Royal protocol to insist that Diana should have a full state-style ceremonial funeral. The rebuke was issued by the palace shortly after the Mail revealed claims made by Earl Spencer in his book. Julian Payne, the King's former communications secretary between 2016 and 2021, told BBC Radio 4's Today programme: 'I personally was working for him during the 20th anniversary of the late princess's death in 2017 and naturally we ended up discussing her several times during that summer because the media interest was so intense. 'What I remember was he was very clear that there was never to be any criticism, never to be any pushback on the narratives that were usually highly critical of him - but he was absolutely emphatic that we were to respect her memory and not undermine it in any way. 'So it seems to me very odd that in the moment itself with the grief of his family and his own shock that he would choose that moment to use those words. It's just not language that I recognise.' BBC royal correspondent Sean Coughlan said the Palace see the allegation against Charles as 'being entirely out of character for a man with a deeply compassionate nature, who at the time was dealing with his own grief and that of their two sons'. Royal expert Phil Dampier described the palace statement as 'extraordinary', adding: 'The days of never complain never explain have gone… not surprisingly.' Diana's death on August 31, 1997, aged 36, sparked a global outpouring of grief. On September 6, 1997, six days after Diana's death, Earl Spencer delivered a defiant eulogy at her funeral that was later named one of the greatest speeches of the 20th century. His raw oration in Westminster Abbey was delivered after he walked alongside William and Harry, as well as Prince Charles and Prince Philip, behind Diana's coffin in a funeral procession through central London. The new book includes a string of further extraordinary claims about Diana's failed marriage to Charles and the tumultuous aftermath of her tragic death in a car crash in Paris. The revelations are set to plunge Buckingham Palace into a fresh storm just weeks after the return of the Duke and Duchess of Sussex and their children to the UK from California. Police in the Netherlands are still investigating what publishing sources say is the 'extremely suspicious' overnight theft of four copies of the book from the back of a parked lorry earlier this week.
