Email tips@dailymail.com - See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source A visibly emotional Ed Sheeran teared up on stage tonight as he apologized for his handling of the Israel controversy that has engulfed his new tour. The singer told fans in Philadelphia 'I cannot hide how I feel ... anymore' as he made an awkward U-turn after previously stating that he was against using his platform for politics. Bowing to pressure as protestors swarmed outside the concert, Sheeran said he had made 'mistakes' and called the situation in Gaza 'catastrophic and unjustifiable.' The gig at the Lincoln Financial Field marked the first time the star, 35, returned to the stage since the rapper Macklemore was dropped as his opening act after taking a vocal stance in favor of a 'Free Palestine.' In a solemn speech at the start of the concert Sheeran said: 'I have tried in my career not to be a political commentator of any kind because I want my music and shows to be about unity and not division, about humanity and not politics. But this is a humanitarian issue and I cannot hide how I feel about it anymore.' 'What happened in Israel at Nova music festival on October 7 was horrific and compounded centuries of Jewish pain. 'What is happening in Gaza is catastrophic and unjustifiable and disproportionate,' Sheeran said in a video captured and shared online by a concertgoer. 'My heart has been broken by the scale of devastation and loss of civilians’ lives, of children’s lives, and the systemic injustice we’re seeing unfold in the West Bank cannot be overlooked,' he declared. Ed Sheeran has made his stance clear on the Israel-Palestine conflict after his Loop Tour fell apart amid Macklemore's 'Free Palestine' comments; pictured in October 2025 It comes as the concert was swarmed by pro-Palestine protestors before he took the stage Sheeran told the audience that he plans to 'continue my current tour' despite each of his openers dropping out in solidarity with Macklemore. It came after Macklemore said Sheeran had spoken to Robert Kraft, who owns Gillette Stadium in Massachusetts through his holding company, the Kraft Group. 'Ed told me that Kraft said I would not be allowed to perform in his stadium. Ed also told me that Kraft had rallied some of the other stadium owners and collectively they gave him an ultimatum: if Macklemore stays on the tour, you will not be allowed to play in our venues,' he wrote. On Tuesday, Sheeran released his own statement on Instagram, saying it wasn't his decision to remove Macklemore from the tour, but rather the promoter's. 'I would never let down the fans who had made plans, nor would I abandon the touring crew and the other support acts and musicians who rely on me for work and to make a living,' the British singer-songwriter added. Sheeran said in the lengthy statement that he has 'personal views on this devastating conflict,' but made the choice 'not to speak publicly.' He has faced criticism from other artists, including Pink Floyd co-founder Roger Waters, who bashed him on social media for not taking a stand against the decision. Tour openers including Aaron Rowe and the band Lukas Graham, along with Sheeran's long-time backing folk band Beoga, also withdrew from the endeavor. In his address to fans on Saturday, Sheeran stated, 'I’ve had to take and make difficult decisions, and I am making mistakes, and I’m so, so sorry.' He said, 'I don’t want to disappoint fans and I’ve never wanted to be an activist musician. But this has put me in the middle of an important and passionate argument about freedom of speech in the most complex political issue on the planet.' The pop star continued, 'Our concerts have always been a safe space for everyone, and that matters hugely to me. Honoring my commitment to my fans is also completely central to who I am and that is why I’m here tonight, on my own, and continuing my own tour.' He went on to say, 'I am deeply worried about the idea that venues’ pre-approved content performance. This happens in so many places that I’ve gone around the world, but it should never happen in the free world.'
