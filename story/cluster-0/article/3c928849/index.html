Tiger attack victim identified as William Chapman who was a former child actor - latest William Chapman, described as in his 20s, took harrowing footage of the 30st tiger after he entered the enclosure at Yorkshire Wildlife Park near Doncaster on Saturday The man who was horrifically mauled to death by a tiger at Yorkshire Wildlife Park near Doncaster was a former child actor, it has emerged tonight. William Chapman, described as in his 20s, jumped into the tiger enclosure at the zoo as terrified onlookers urged him to "play dead". The Amur tiger, named Altai, then viciously attacked Mr Chapman and he was later pronounced dead at the scene. He had filmed chilling footage of himself approaching the tiger breathing heavily in the moments before the mauling. Witnesses said Mr Chapman had seemed "so happy" before he climbed into the enclosure with police confirming he was not accompanied by anyone at the time of the attack . The park confirmed that the tiger will not be euthanised following the incident but the site remains closed for the fourth day in a row. A health and safety investigation is being conducted by City of Doncaster Council. Family pay tribute to 'bright warm and engaging' man who suffered 'significant mental illness' The family of William Chapman, who was fatally mauled by a tiger at Yorkshire Wildlife Park, have described the 23-year-old as a "bright, warm and engaging young man". Biologist at tiger's former home 'deeply saddened' by 'tragic incident' The tiger involved in Saturday's fatal attack, Altai, moved to Yorkshire Wildlife Park from Dyreparken in Norway in February 2025. The animal, now aged five, came to the zoo as part of the international EAZA European Endangered Species Programme for Amur tigers. He joined the park's resident tigress Tschuna. Dyreparken biologist Helene Axelsen said following last week's tragedy: We were deeply saddened to hear about the tragic incident at Yorkshire Wildlife Park over the weekend. Our thoughts are first and foremost with the victim's family, loved ones, and the entire team at Yorkshire Wildlife Park during this extremely difficult time. Victim was 'passionate about acting,' mum's blog reveals An old blog post written by William Chapman's mum has offered a glimpse into his life years before he was killed in a tiger attack. The post, published around 12 years ago, says William was "passionate about acting and drama" having attended a performing arts school in High Wycombe since the age of four. Describing his mood during an audition in London for the ITV show 'May Contain Nuts', William's mum said: William was only about 5 then, and unburdened by the nerves he can sometimes suffer from these days. He seemed to relax and thoroughly enjoy himself. The post also contains a short diary entry by William on his time as an extra in Pan, a prequel to the Peter Pan story which starred Hugh Jackman. He wrote: Once on set we were sheltering from the pirates under the ship's floorboards and we had to be terrified! Harrowing final photos of tiger death victim emerge after tragedy The victim of a tiger attack at a British wildlife park posted video footage of himself approaching the big cat before he was mauled to death, it emerged today. William Chapman was heard breathing heavily as he stalked the 30st (200kg) animal after scaling a perimeter fence and jumping into the enclosure at Yorkshire Wildlife Park. Members of the public cried out, "Oh my God" and "Seriously?" as he walked towards Altai the Amur tiger on an elevated platform. Moments later, Mr Chapman - believed to be in his early 20s - was pictured sat on the floor as Altai repeatedly lunged at him. He was pronounced dead shortly after 1.30pm on Saturday after being mauled by the big cat at the park near Doncaster, South Yorkshire. Full story here. Investigation launched 'in line with statutory duties' A health and safety investigation by City of Doncaster Council is underway following the man's death. Dan Swaine, the local authority's executive director of place, said the council was "deeply saddened" by the tragedy and sent its "deepest sympathies" to the victim's family and those who witnessed the attack He said: As the relevant licensing authority for the Yorkshire Wildlife Park, I can confirm that, in line with our statutory duties, the council has launched a health and safety investigation. We are also working with multiple agencies, including the police and health agencies, and are supporting the Yorkshire Wildlife Park in response to the incident. 'This is a very big animal with the power to bite and break through bones' Dr David Sands, a human-companion animal practitioner at the Animal Behavioural Clinic in Chorley, said tigers are "territorial" and it would be natural for them to attack. He told the BBC: This is a male tiger and the incredible weight and power, there's only one outcome because the tiger is in captivity - the enclosure is its territory and a male who can be scented becomes either competition or prey. We're so used to seeing them on the small screen in wildlife documentaries. This is a very big animal that's got the power to bite and break through bones. Visitors 'tried to distract tiger' Witnesses of the attack said some visitors had tried unsuccessfully to distract the tiger as it began attacking Chapman inside the enclosure. Altai the tiger is housed in the park's Land of the Tigers, which has a 300m-long raised walkway to allow visitors to view the animals from above. Chapman was reported to have entered the pen after climbing down from the walkway. 'Anyone entering an enclosure would be seen as a threat,' says expert A retired animal behaviourist says the blame should not be placed on the tiger following the tragedy. Dr David Sands told the BBC that anyone entering an enclosure would be seen as a threat and that the tiger was "just being the animal that one million years of evolution has produced". He explained: It's the top apex predator of its group of animals and I don't think any blame can be put on to the animal itself. Could you make the enclosure higher, bigger, more inner protections - well, where do you stop? Chapman 'shouted for help' as tiger grabbed hold of his neck, says witness A woman who filmed the tiger moments before the attack says Chapman had been "pacing around with his headphones in" before entering the enclosure. Ella Ford, 24, from Moreton, Wirral, was visiting the zoo with her boyfriend when she noticed Chapman jump into the pen and crawl in front of the tiger. She said the animal was "perched and just lay there". Ford said she then witnessed the tiger grab hold of the victim, and he began "shouting for help". Victim William Chapman was former child actor who appeared on BBC and ITV Tiger attack victim William Chapman was a former child actor who appeared on the BBC and ITV, it has emerged. Chapman starred in the ITV production May Contain Nuts alongside Shirley Henderson, best known for playing Moaning Myrtle in the Harry Potter films, reports the BBC. He also played Edward Jenner as a young boy in the BBC series True Stories. Man killed by world's largest tiger breed William Chapman was attacked by Altai, an Amur tiger weighing around 200kg (31 stone). The animal arrived at Yorkshire Wildlife Park from Norway last year and belongs to a subspecies regarded as the largest big cat in the world. Amur tigers, also known and Siberian tigers, are native to the Russian Far East and parts of north-east China. Adult males can weigh up to around 300kg (47 stone) and measure more than three metres from nose to tail. Everything we know about William Chapman - William Chapman was visiting Yorkshire Wildlife Park in Doncaster on Saturday afternoon. He was on his own and appeared to be in a good mood, with witnesses recalling he looked "so happy" in the minutes before climbed into the enclosure and was killed. - Mr Chapman was "pacing around with his headphones in" and asked a fellow visitor to "grab a shot" of him with the tiger. Could the victim have survived the attack? Witnesses who watched William Chapman enter the tiger enclosure at Yorkshire Wildlife Park have reportedly told him to "play dead", hopeful that the animal would ignore the man who invaded his space. But Reece Oliver, an expert animal keeper, said Mr Chapman "stood no chance" of survival. "You can't say the tiger was being malice," Reece Oliver told Sky News. "But what we have to think is, the tiger is 200 kilos. If it really wanted to kill him, it could've probably killed him in ten seconds. "I don't think it knew what it was going to do, because the only thing that goes into that enclosure normally, would be enrichment toys or food. "So I think he was probably just playing, using his predatory instinct, seeing what it was - and unfortunately, it ended up being a fatal attack." Tiger was 'just playing' before fatal attack An animal expert has said that Altai, the 30st tiger was probably "just playing" when it began pawing at William Chapman before fatally injuring him. "If it really wanted to kill him, it would have probably killed him within 10 seconds," animal keeper Reece Oliver told Sky News. "I don't think it knew what he was going to do, because the only thing that goes into that enclosure normally would be enrichment toys or food. "So, I think he was probably just playing, using his predatory instinct, seeing what it was, what he was, and unfortunately it ended up being a fatal attack." What happened to the tiger? Yorkshire Wildlife Park confirmed that Altai, the 30st Amur tiger, will not be euthanised as a result of Saturday's incident. The statement read: "We would like to thank everyone who has expressed their concern following yesterday's tragic incident. "Our thoughts and heartfelt sympathies remain with the family and loved ones of the man who sadly lost his life. [...] "Many, many people have contacted us about Altai, the tiger. "We can reassure everyone that he is safe and well and will not be euthanised as a result of this incident." Yorkshire Wildlife Park remains closed. What did the police say? The latest statement from South Yorkshire Police regarding the incident on Saturday was released the evening after the incident. It reads: "At 1.35pm today (Saturday 10 October) we received reports of a man entering a tiger enclosure at Yorkshire Wildlife Park. "The tiger subsequently attacked the man, resulting in serious injury. "Emergency services attended and sadly, despite the best efforts of medical personnel, the man was pronounced dead at the scene. "The man's family is aware and is being supported by specialist officers. Our thoughts are with them at this distressing time." Man told tiger to 'stop being naughty' before attack William Chapman reportedly told the tiger to "stop being naughty" after the animal started pawing him inside the enclosure. Witnesses are said to have told him to "play dead" before the animal killed him but he did not understand and instead told the tiger to "stop being naughty", Sky News reports. Other visitors described how the man had asked them for a photo in front of the tiger before climbing into the enclosure. Victim was 'pacing around with headphones in' before jumping into enclosure Ella Ford, 24, from Moreton, Wirral, was visiting the park with her boyfriend and filmed the tiger just moments before the attack. Speaking to the BBC, she described seeing a man by the tiger enclosure "pacing around with his headphones in". A few minutes later, she saw the same man had "climbed up onto the barriers". Woman tried to pull man back before entering tiger enclosure According to a witness, a woman had tried to pull Mr Chapman back onto the viewing bridge above the tiger enclosure as he was climbing in. She "wasn't strong enough", however, and Mr Chapman "pulled away from her, climbed down, and jumped about 12ft" into the pen, the visitor said. He then "knelt in front of the tiger," who didn't immediately attack and was initially just circling the man. The witness added: "But then the young man started yelling at the tiger and waving his finger at him, yelling 'no,' and the tiger grabbed him by the neck. "It all happened very fast. The tiger staff were in there quickly, and fired two shots to the ground to scare the tiger away from the man."
