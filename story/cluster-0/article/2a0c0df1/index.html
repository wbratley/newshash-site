← Nvidia Reinstates CEO Jensen Huang After Resignation and Trump Intervention
i Paper centre

Trump has parachuted his pal Huang back into Nvidia, with dollar signs in his eyes

Article text

As the US markets opened on Monday, Nvidia – which is perhaps the most strategically important company in the world, as it makes most of the chips needed by the AI industry – had a bombshell announcement ready. Jensen Huang, the founder and CEO who had resigned just days earlier after allegations he had several affairs, including with a woman alleged to work with Chinese intelligence, would be reinstated as chief executive. Like OpenAI CEO Sam Altman before him, Huang would return from an ousting, perhaps stronger than ever. Markets certainly seemed delighted by the news: Nvidia shares rose 33 per cent in the first few minutes of trading alone. To judge solely by its share price, the company’s board had made a mistake in asking for Huang’s resignation, which they have solved with this U-turn. Problem solved. The reality, though, is far messier. Big tech is delivering the kind of boardroom drama usually reserved for daytime soap operas – and Donald Trump and his White House operation are in it up to their necks. Just hours before the announcement, Trump had publicly called for Huang’s reinstatement on his Truth Social platform. “I hope the Board on Nvidia rehires Jensen Huang,” the President wrote. “It would be the best thing for the Company, and also, for the U.S.A.” A screen showing the reinstatement of Huang as CEO of Nvidia on the trading floor of the New York Stock Exchange on Monday, as their shares rocketed by 33 per cent (Photo: Angela Weiss/Getty Images) Behind the scenes, Trump’s cabinet officials had reportedly been lobbying for Huang’s return, too – amid media reports that the Securities and Exchange Commission was considering a probe into Nvidia’s financial affairs following the ouster. All this has raised speculation that Huang’s reinstatement is a direct result of Trump’s intervention, possibly accompanied by pressure from the administration. The implications of that are enormous. To understand the significance of them, we need to remember the importance of Nvidia to the USA and to Trump personally. Nvidia designs the chips upon which every other AI company relies, to train models and to deploy them to customers. It has played a central role in the AI race between the US and China, especially after the White House restricted exports of the most advanced chips to Beijing. Trump, whose investment accounts have made tens of thousands of trades in his second term, has logged up to $97.9m (£75m) in Nvidia transactions since returning to office. The AI boom has been fuelling the US economy for the entirety of Trump’s second term: without it, the US would have barely any economic growth to show, and its stock market would be stagnant at best. Nvidia’s role has been even larger than supplying the chips. Through an elaborate web of financial deals, it has effectively served as the banker of the sector, too, providing financing for other companies to use to buy even more of its chips. Trump, whose investment accounts have made tens of thousands of trades in his second term, has logged up to $97.9m in Nvidia transactions since returning to office. All this means that if Nvidia stumbles, the US economy could be severely damaged. No wonder Trump was worried. Huang was the driving force of Nvidia for the full 33 years of its existence, and there was no succession plan after he left. But the circumstances of his exit matter even more than the fact of it. Huang was accused of having affairs not only with other Nvidia executives – often enough to end a CEO’s career in corporate America – but also with Alice Huang (no relation), the CEO of one of the company’s customers. According to several media reports, US intelligence had been tracking Alice Huang for more than a year, believing her to be in regular contact with Chinese intelligence, and alleging her company was serving as a front to channel banned chips to China. She denies all wrongdoing. At the time of his resignation, Jensen Huang was facing questions as to how much he knew, and when. Trump was reportedly warned at least twice about Jensen Huang’s relationship with an alleged asset of Chinese intelligence – a move that presented a massive risk to the USA’s strategic interests, even if Huang was unaware of his paramour’s alleged ties. Most presidents would have taken decisive action against an executive in such a compromised position. Instead, Trump has gone out to bat for his friend. “The NVIDIA Board of Directors has made the correct and proper decision to return Jensen Huang to the position of CEO,” Trump said at the beginning of a subsequent, longer post. “The Country needs Jensen’s leadership at NVIDIA in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy.” The ramifications of Huang’s reinstatement will ripple out for months, if not years. His return shows the dominance of Trump over big tech, and the closeness of the ties between the administration and tech’s top execs – even as Democrats return to Congress. It signals that tech execs cannot be reined in by their boards, suggesting powerful founders are free of restrictions. Beyond that, it signals that old geopolitical certainties simply do not exist under the Trump presidency. Trump spent the weekend sharing content describing Vladimir Putin as “my friend”. Now, he has personally helped reinstate a tech exec accused of wittingly or unwittingly backchannelling chips to China. Trump is his own, stubbornly independent man. Conspiracies suggesting he is a Russian or a Chinese asset misunderstand his character entirely. The question at this point is a stranger one still: could Russia or China be doing any better at this point if Trump actually was on their payroll?
