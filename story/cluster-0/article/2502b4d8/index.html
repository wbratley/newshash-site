Huntingdon train attacker guilty of attempting to murder 14 people

Log in A knifeman has been found guilty of attempting to murder 14 people in a series of stabbing attacks, including 10 whom he stabbed on a London-bound train that made an unscheduled stop in Huntingdon. Anthony Williams, 33, carried out attacks on a LNER train between Peterborough and Huntingdon, where it made an unscheduled stop on 1 November last year. A jury at Cambridge Crown Court on Thursday found him guilty of seven counts of attempted murder after 15 hours 53 minutes of deliberations, before later finding him guilty of seven more counts. These were on-board staff member Samir Zitouni, 48, and six passengers: Jonathan Gjoshe, 22, Sachin Balakrishnan, 29, David Presland, 73, Scott Bletcher, 50, Scott Green, 52, and 61-year-old Stephen Crean. The jury later reached guilty verdicts on seven further counts of attempted murder, including the attempted murder of three more people on the train: Kevin Deely, 58, Raza Aslam, 44, and 34-year-old Michael Paffett. Williams was also found guilty by majority verdicts of the attempted murders of three people in Peterborough on 31 October last year, at a footbridge near Henry Penn Walk and in the car park of the Railworld attraction. They were a 14-year-old boy who he stabbed, who cannot be identified for legal reasons, and two men who he attempted to stab, Dawid Taborski, 26, and 22-year-old William Ogelby. He was also found guilty by majority verdict of the attempted murder of Isaiah Idris, then aged 17 and now aged 18, who he stabbed on a DLR train that was approaching Pontoon Dock station in London in the early hours of 1 November. His trial was told that Williams stabbed a boy and attempted to stab two men in Peterborough on the evening of Halloween last year, before travelling to London by train. Once in the capital, he stabbed a boy on a DLR train in the early hours of 1 November, before returning to Peterborough and spending much of the day at home. On the evening of 1 November he walked from his home to Peterborough station, boarded a London-bound LNER service and stabbed nine passengers and a member of on-board staff, after the high-speed train had departed. Some heard Williams talking about the devil, making comments including “the devil won’t win”, and he had a bottle of fortified tonic wine with him that he drank from. The trial heard how on-board staff member Samir Zitouni tried to fend off Williams with a frying pan, and one passenger used a toaster, while others tried to block his way with trolleys and luggage. Some sheltered in toilets, or in whatever safe areas they could find. The driver stopped the train at Huntingdon with the help of a signaller, ahead of its next scheduled stop at Stevenage, and Williams was tasered and arrested in a car park at the station. Jurors were told that Mr Zitouni was taken to intensive care in a life-threatening condition, having received stab wounds including three to the back. He suffered “catastrophic blood loss” and required four blood transfusions. The tip of the blade of Williams’s knife was close to the heart of passenger Sachin Balakrishnan at the time of the stabbing, jurors were told in agreed facts, and a wound to passenger Stephen Crean’s scalp was within 0.79cm of his brain. Jurors were told Williams had asked Mr Crean: “Do you want to die?” All survived their injuries. Williams had admitted carrying out the attacks but denied that he intended to kill, and at his trial it was said that at the time he was suffering from an acute psychosis as a result of his schizophrenia. But the defendant was found guilty of seven counts of attempted murder. Williams did not give evidence at his trial and he appeared via video-link from Rampton Hospital, a high-security psychiatric hospital in Nottinghamshire, rather than in the dock at the court. Prosecutor Christopher Paxton KC earlier said that train passengers fled “in sheer terror” during the five-minute attack on the LNER train on the evening of 1 November. He said Williams had “posed a dangerous threat, marauding through towns and cities, on streets and trains alike, armed with a knife”. He added: “He stabbed many, seeking to end their lives with deadly intention, striking out at them when they were defenceless and in fear.” Going through psychiatric evidence, defence counsel Gillian Jones KC referred to the “delusional premise that his (Williams’s) life was at risk from a wide-ranging conspiracy by the devil”, and she suggested he did not intend to kill. The judge had told jurors in his legal directions that a “person who is mentally ill, acting psychotically under a delusion, can still form an intent to kill”. At a pre-trial hearing, Williams had pleaded guilty to wounding 12 of his victims with intent to cause really serious harm, and to the attempted wounding of Mr Taborski and Mr Ogelby. Williams had also pleaded guilty, at the pre-trial hearing at Cambridge Crown Court, to three counts of possessing a knife in a public place. He pleaded guilty to an affray at Ritzy barber shop in Peterborough, where he brandished a knife on 31 October, and to the theft of knives from an Asda store in Stevenage the same day. Williams pleaded guilty to the common assault of a man, Jordan Morley, whom he spat at on a train approaching Biggleswade on 1 November, and to the assault occasioning actual bodily harm of Pc James Farrell, whom he punched in the nose while Williams was in police custody on 2 November.
