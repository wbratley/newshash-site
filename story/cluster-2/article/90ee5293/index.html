Days on from the initial disaster, rescuers are still scouring the mud and debris left by the flood along the Himalayan border between Nepal and China-controlled Tibet. Police in Nepal said on Friday morning that the country's death toll had risen to 469 from 390 a day earlier. China had not updated its report of three deaths in Tibet. More than 1,500 people are missing overall. Among them are ex-banker and beauty pageant winner Surabhi Sahu, 44, her businessman husband Santosh, 46, their son Spandan, 18, and daughter Syna, 13, who were on an adventure trek across the Nepal-China border. Mr and Mrs Sahu ran a successful technology company in London together before he set up a thriving pharmaceutical business. The family live in a £1.3million six-bedroom house in Cobham, Surrey. They travelled to India last week and then on to the Nepalese-Tibetan trek. They were due to return in ten days before the start of the new school year. A poignant final photograph of them with their group – mostly Britons – on the Alpine Eco Tours trip shows them happy and laughing as they pose by their bus amid the stunning peaks of the Himalayas. The group was last heard from as they crossed the frontier from Nepal into Tibet – at the exact moment on Wednesday that a horrifying 600ft torrent of ice, mud and rocks engulfed the Giyrong border crossing, with CCTV footage showing people running for their lives. The organisers of the tour group say they have been frantically trying to reach them ever since. There are hopes the tourists are alive and waiting to be rescued, but phone lines are down and military are struggling to reach the mountainous area. The Sahu family were well-travelled and on a once-in-a-lifetime pilgrimage to Mount Kailash, a religious site for Hindus and Buddhists in Tibet.
