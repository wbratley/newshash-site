Terrorists could use AI to target UK with drones and bioweapons, MI5 chief warns

Log in Terrorists could exploit artificial intelligence to target Britain with drones or biological weapons, the head of MI5 has warned. Speaking after his annual speech at MI5 headquarters in London, Sir Ken McCallum said the UK had entered a “new era of national security” and the security service was directing more resources to keeping Britain safe now than in the Cold War, warning that essential services such as banking, flights, and healthcare were at risk of being disrupted by adversaries, including Iran and Russia. He warned about the growing threat of AI provoking real fear that terrorists could use the technology for warfare, saying “we’ve caught terrorists seeking to exploit AI to improve their reconnaissance and attack methodologies”. He said the possibility of AI-enabled drones added “a new twist” for bad actors and warned the greatest concern is “AI’s potential to change the stakes on existing or even new bioweapons”. “These risks are real,” he said. “The UK intelligence community – MI5, GCHQ, MI6 – is working closely with the UK’s globally respected AI Security Institute to take them on. Part of that means retaining the capability edge against those who mean the UK harm. As they seek to harness AI, we must stay ahead, so these powerful technologies bolster rather than undermine our shared security.” Putting the public on guard, Sir Ken warned: “We have again entered a new era of national security.” He called for a “whole-of-society effort” to face threats to national infrastructure, warning that hacks could disable banking services, drones could disrupt flights, and cyber attacks on hospitals could have an adverse impact on health treatment. The MI5 director general also said that the suspected terror plot against RAF Fairford in Gloucestershire, which prime minister Andy Burnham said was “almost certainly” directed by Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard, was “not unique in the UK”, with Irans seeking to expand its hybrid campaign against Britain this year. In the past twelve months, MI5 investigations have led to more than 50 arrests in Iran-linked operations in the UK, and its state threats caseload has quadrupled in the past decade, he said. Sir Ken added: “You might be surprised to know that in 2026, MI5 is devoting more resource to countering state threats than during the Cold War.” He said that there have been dozens of arrests under the National Security Act in the three years since it came into force, with cases linked to Russia, Iran and China. “Anyone tempted by apparently easy cash from shady overseas taskmasters might want to bear this in mind,” Sir Ken warned. He said that Russia remains “aggressor number one”, with Russian military and intelligence services “once again escalating their attempts at hybrid warfare”, citing a cyber-attack on Polish energy infrastructure and an attempt to use a drone to blow up a cargo plane in Germany. Sir Ken also talked of Iranian use of “criminal proxies to try and intimidate oppositionists, Iranian media organisations and Israeli sites”. His warning comes after he revealed in an interview with The Independent last month that Britain faces greater and more complex terror threats now than on 9/11. Addressing journalists after his speech on Tuesday, Sir Ken said: “The Iranians in the UK are principally using a modus operandi of hiring criminals to do their biding. Their level of capability is really a function of the sophistication level of those criminals”. But he warned that the UK should be readying itself for more aggressive plots from the Iranian security services. Speaking about the ongoing threats from terrorism, Sir Ken said MI5 was still having to respond to attempts by radicalised Islamists to inflict mass-casualty attacks on the UK. And an increase in extreme right-wing terrorism, with a growing number of cases where “extremist versions of anti-immigration and anti-Muslim grievance are tipping into terrorist intent”. He cited a case where, a few months ago, MI5 was investigating a radicalised extreme right-wing 13-year-old in the UK. This investigation “generated insight that enabled one of our international partners to prevent a lethal attack on their soil”, Sir Ken said. He also announced that MI5 has launched an AI Threat Assessment Centre (AITAC) this year, which will produce assessments of the risks posed by the new technology. “In the case of bioweapons… one concern would be that comparatively low sophistication actors may have a capability uplift in the AI era, if the guard rails don’t operate or do not function as intended. “It has long been the case that many terrorists would dearly love to have some kind of capability around chemical or biological weaponry, but that hasn’t been easy for them to achieve. One risk, and as I said in my speech this is not yet manifest, but one risk is that AI could enable knowledge to get into the hands of people we would not want to have that knowledge. “A second additional risk, much discussed… is the possibility that AI could generate novel pathogens not previously seen, so these are among the risks that we are seeking to address.” When asked about potential Russian interference in the Brexit referendum, Sir Ken said: “There is a really important difference between interference attempted and outcome swayed.” He warned that it was “up to us to decide how far we allow our various adversaries to divide us”, adding: “I am a little bit worried that if we all stay in this debate for another few days, Russian trolls who didn’t appear to do all that much at various points over the years, they’ll be delighted because they are front page news once again.” Asked whether Britain would be able to get back to a less fractious time, Sir Ken said: “Our planning assumption is not that this is a temporary blip and we will all be back to normal by next Christmas. “We are planning on the basis that, in effect, the period of comparative geopolitical stability following the end of the Cold War has sadly ended and we are back in a more contested era now. We are planning on the basis that these risks will endure.” Yesterday, the defence secretary, Wes Streeting, told MPs on the Commons defence committee: “Fundamentally, I think this country and our allies in Nato face a threat of a kind we haven't seen since the end of the Cold War. We don’t seek conflict with Russia. We don’t seek escalation. We don't seek to provoke Russia, but we know that every single day Russia is testing us, testing our allies.” He also said the term hybrid threats was a “sanitised description of what are acts of sabotage, targeted assassinations, effectively state-backed terrorism.
