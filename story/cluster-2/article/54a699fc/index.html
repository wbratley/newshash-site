Erin Patterson is locked up for life: Mushroom monster loses appeal and will die in jail as judges increase her sentence Erin Patterson will die behind bars alone after her desperate attempt to appeal her conviction failed and her non-parole period was overturned. On Friday, Supreme Court of Victoria appeal judges Justices Stephen McLeish, Lesley Taylor and Peter Kidd dismissed the cold-blooded killer's appeal. Supreme Court of Victoria Justice Christopher Beale had sentenced Patterson to life in prison on September 8, 2025, after she was found guilty of the murders of Don and Gail Patterson and Gail's sister, Heather Wilkinson, after feeding them a poisonous beef Wellington. Justice Beale had set a non-parole period of 33 years. However, the prosecution succeeded in its bid to have Patterson's sentence increased, and the panel of judges increased her jail term to life without parole. Patterson, who appeared in court via video link wearing a blue prison-issued jumper, long brown hair in a pony tail and glasses, showed no emotion as her fate was sealed. Her lawyers have indicated she will appeal to the High Court, the last available legal avenue for Patterson to fight her conviction and sentence. Patterson's victims died after consuming death cap mushrooms served in beef Wellingtons during lunch at her Leongatha home on July 29, 2023. Pastor Ian Wilkinson, who did not attend Friday's hearing, survived after being critically ill, with Patterson also convicted of his attempted murder. Erin Patterson will likely die behind bars alone after her desperate attempt to appeal her conviction failed Patterson appealed her murder convictions on seven grounds, arguing that multiple errors caused a substantial miscarriage of justice and that her convictions should be quashed. Her appeal focused largely on her claims a 'fundamental irregularity' occurred when the jury was sequestered in the same hotel as police and prosecutors during deliberations, potentially contaminating the verdicts – a ground the judges found had 'no basis'. The courtroom was packed with true crime buffs, reporters and Simon Patterson's brother Matthew. The short hearing was livestreamed on the court's internet site, with a television crew also allowed inside the court to capture the final chapter in Patterson's legal journey. The sentence means Patterson is only the second woman in Australian history to be sentenced to life without parole. NSW woman Katherine Knight became the first in 2001 after she stabbed her partner John Price to death, skinned his body and cooked his head with the intention of feeding it to his children. Reading out the judgment, Justice McLeish said it was 'inappropriate' for Justice Beale to fix a non-parole period when sentencing Patterson last year. Patterson had also argued certain evidence should not have been admitted, or was wrongly excluded, including mobile phone tower data linked to death cap mushroom sightings, Facebook messages and the evidence of ‘friends’ and photos and videos of mushrooms found on an SD card that could have supported her foraging claims. Her failure will now see her transported back to the Dame Phyllis Frost Centre, where she is expected to spend decades among the nation’s worst female villains. So loathed is Patterson within those walls that she is held in near-solitary confinement in the restrictive Gordon Unit. There she occupies herself for up to 23 hours a day with crocheting, watching TV, using a computer and reading books or magazines. Trapped in a small cell with a tiny attached courtyard, she receives meals and medication through a door slot, has almost no contact with other inmates and requires two officers to escort her for any limited movement within the prison. They are conditions imposed for her own protection due to the notoriety of her crimes. The end of the drawn-out legal saga might now provide some semblance of peace for the surviving victims. Detective Sen-Constable Laura Colley, who was the officer who slapped the cuffs on Patterson, beamed as the decision was handed down. Lone survivor Ian Wilkinson had attended practically every day of Patterson's ten-week trial in Morwell last year and was there during her appeal hearing in August. During a pre-sentence hearing, Wilkinson condemned Patterson for the 'callous and calculated disregard' for the lives of people who had only good intentions toward her, and questioned the 'foolishness' of believing murder could solve problems. 'I am no longer Erin Patterson’s victim. She has become the victim of my kindness,' he said. Little has been seen recently of Patterson’s former husband Simon, who believed she had repeatedly tried to murder him in the years before she killed his family. Mr Patterson alleged his wife tried to poison him on multiple occasions in 2021-2022 with meals she prepared, including a Tupperware of penne bolognese, a chicken korma curry during a camping trip, a beef stew while he was recovering at her home and a curry wrap on another outing. Each time he became seriously ill, requiring hospitalisation; one episode left him in a medically induced coma for more than two weeks and required emergency bowel surgery.
