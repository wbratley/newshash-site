Reform UK conference LIVE: Nigel Farage says ‘campaign of attacks’ won't distract Reform as speech descends into chaos Nigel Farage has finally addressed allegations the party knowingly accepted foreign donations to pay for polls that weren't declared, before speaking at Reform UK's conference Nigel Farage says Reform won’t be distracted by ‘campaign of attacks’ in his speech at the Reform UK conference tonight. Mr Farage took to the main stage at the National Exhibition Centre in Birmingham to give his speech this evening, during which he was interrupted numerous times by protesters and hecklers. The party leader said: “We have been the subject of a highly co-ordinated campaign of attacks.” He added: “But I promise you, we will not be distracted by any of this. We are absolutely determined in what we’re doing.” The annual gathering has gone off the rails after explosive allegations over foreign donations saw two high-profile aides forced to step down. Comms chief Dan Jukes and head of policy James Orr were recorded allegedly plotting for a foreign donor to pay £32,500 for off-the-books polling on behalf of the party, in a potential breach of electoral law. Meanwhile, Mr Farage has insisted Reform UK has “broken no laws” and had not “taken any dodgy money” amid allegations the party breached political donation rules. Foreign companies are not a permissible source of funding for parties, including for payments for studies and research, and accepting donations from them could constitute a criminal offence. The Reform party conference is due to continue tomorrow, but here's everything you need to know so far... Key Events As yesterday's conference came to an end Zia Yusuf told BBC NewsNight: "Obviously the documentary, there’s no two ways about it, it was disappointing to see that - it's not comfortable to hear some of the things that were said. But, the party is taking it seriously and there's an investigation there going on and I don't want to prejudice that investigation so I'm going to allow that to take its course." Other senior Reform figures will also address the conference on Saturday, which has been centred around its plans for the first 100 days of a Reform government. The party’s Treasury spokesman Robert Jenrick will speak about cutting benefit spending, and taxes for both working people and pensioners. Education spokeswoman Suella Braverman will announce plans to cut student loans being issued for “Mickey Mouse degrees”, and to lift the cap on medical school places. Reform was plunged into turmoil on Friday night as Scotland Yard said it was assessing reports that senior figures sought to evade electoral law over donations. Two top aides to Nigel Farage were forced to step down following an undercover sting, which appeared to show the pair discussing how to disguise donations from foreign donors. Long-standing aide Dan Jukes and policy chief James Orr left their posts, pending an internal probe, as the donations scandal threatened to engulf the party. They all deny wrongdoing. Labour and the Liberal Democrats reported Reform to the police over a report, by Channel 4 News and undercover group Verbatim, which aired on the eve of the party's annual conference. But Mr Farage sought to downplay the secretly-recorded comments as "loose pub talk" as he addressed delegates in a chaotic speech beset by hecklers and delays. If there was one thing that was the same between Nigel Farage's conference speech this year and last year, it was that viewers aren't walking away talking about the policies, plans or promises of Reform UK. I was sitting near the front among rows of Reform members who booed hecklers, chanted "out, out, out" and cheered as protestors were dragged out of the conference hall. While Farage attempted to brush off the interruptions, the temperature shot up in the room and everyone was on the edge of their seats, with heads shooting around looking for a sign another heckler was about to start shouting. The conference has already been overshadowed by an undercover sting alleging Reform may have breached rules on political donations, with two senior Reform figures stepping down this morning. Reform figures often try to dismiss the "noise" and criticism that comes from outside their fanbase... but nobody could ignore Farage being drowned out by shouts of corruption during his big conference moment today. The Liberal Democrats have asked Parliament's standards watchdog to investigate allegations Nigel Farage's aides sought to conceal impermissible donations to Reform UK. Lisa Smart, the Lib Dems' Cabinet Office spokeswoman, said in a letter to Parliamentary Standards Commissioner Daniel Greenberg that a report by Channel 4 News "demonstrated the lengths that close aides of Nigel Farage went to in order to facilitate an impermissible donation and to conceal it". She said: "Nigel Farage met personally with the undercover reporters, whom he understood to be prospective American donors, and was subsequently recorded praising the effectiveness of private polling that this funding had paid for. "The activities, shown on camera, represent clear breaches of the law – specifically section 56 and 61 of PPERA (Political Parties, Elections and Referendums Act)." Pollster JL Partners has admitted to a "lapse of judgment" for not "proactively" telling media outlets who was behind polling reportedly commissioned by Reform UK and paid for by a company set up by undercover journalists. In a lengthy statement following the report, JL Partners said IL Office LLC paid for three pieces of opinion research between January and July, 2026, after the company – which it understood to be the family office of a UK-based philanthropist – was introduced to the pollster by Reform figures. The statement said: "As an opinion research firm that conducts extensive polling on current affairs, we maintain good relations with various political parties, both in Britain and abroad and, in that context, sometimes people with a party alignment make suggestions about potential clients who may be interested in commissioning and paying for polling. "Some polls are commissioned in-house while others are commissioned by clients. Our usual practice is to inform a media partner when a poll has been commissioned by a particular client. "In the case of the polls that IL Office LLC had paid for, we did not offer that information proactively. "While there is no legal requirement to do so, it was a lapse of judgment that was not consistent with the approved procedures of JL Partners or with rule 2.1 of guidelines of the British Polling Council, of which JL Partners is a member." JL Partners said it contacted the BPC itself after concluding it had breached its rules, and is reviewing its own procedures.
