Online predator jailed for over 14 years after blackmailing vulnerable girls for explicit content

Log in An online predator who blackmailed vulnerable young girls into providing explicit photographs and coerced them into self-harming "for his own pleasure" has received a prison sentence of more than 14 years. Beau-Jack Mensforth, 20, assumed a central position within online exploitation networks that coerced underage girls to generate "content for the community". An extensive police inquiry identified nine victims across the UK and the United States, all under the age of 18, who were groomed by Mensforth and forced into sending progressively severe and explicit material. At Newcastle Crown Court on Friday, he received a sentence of 14 years and six months in custody, alongside an extended licence period of six months. Victims and families voiced their anguish during the proceedings. One victim branded Mensforth a "psychopath" who "fed off the pain of smaller beings because he needed to feel like the bigger man”, while a mother described him as a "monster" who had "invaded the safety of our home". The court was informed that several victims were forced into carving Mensforth’s internet handle into their skin, and he attempted to convince one teenager to "sacrifice" a family pet. Prosecutor Charlotte Hole detailed how Mensforth, who resides in Sunderland, was 17 when authorities linked him to the online harm communities. These groups deliberately targeted young girls—frequently those struggling with mental health issues—who were searching social media for support regarding self-harm. Ms Hole explained that the young girls believed they were entering genuine romantic relationships. Instead, they were manipulated into transmitting indecent photographs of themselves, which were subsequently used as leverage to blackmail them into forwarding more explicit content and footage of self-harm. This material was then shared among members of the online forums, which functioned as an organised criminal enterprise. Group members kept victims in line by threatening to publish their explicit images, post their private details online, or submit false reports to emergency services to prompt armed police responses at their residences. The court heard that when a 14-year-old victim asked Mensforth why he compelled individuals to hurt animals, he answered: "For content." A victim impact statement provided via video link from the United States revealed how a girl was drawn into the network at age 16 through a page on X, formerly Twitter, that posed as a support group. She described being forced to carve two names into her skin before the images were uploaded to a platform where thousands viewed them. In the statement read by Ms Hole, the victim said: "I begged them to take it down but the more I cried about it, the more amusement it seemed to bring them." Sitting next to her, the girl's mother directed her own statement "to the monster who hurt my daughter". She admitted to having been "naive to this type of predatory behaviour" and unaware "that cowardly monsters like yourself were hiding behind a screen tormenting our baby girl". The mother added: "Not only had you invaded the safety of our home, you invaded the safety of our daughter’s mind, filling her with fear and shame." A UK-based victim also watched via video link and requested Ms Hole read her statement. She recounted being manipulated into sexually explicit online exchanges with Mensforth at age 13, during which she sent indecent images. Having since changed her name, she stated that Mensforth had taken "not only my innocence but my education, my dignity and a part of my soul". She told him directly: "You are what I can only describe as a psychopath – you feed off the pain of smaller beings because you need to feel like the bigger man." Another victim noted: "You manipulated and coerced me as a 12-year-old child to believe it was okay to self-harm for your pleasure." Police searched Mensforth’s address three times between October 2023 and June 2024, discovering a different iPhone on each visit. This demonstrated that he "enthusiastically" persisted with the online groups following his initial arrest, telling officers he acted out of being "bored". In chat logs, he referred to a victim staying in a psychiatric facility, writing: "I’m hoping my girl gets out of the ward soon… she’s a really good cutter." Defence barrister Robin Patton said Mensforth had entered the groups as an isolated 14-year-old following his father’s death, explaining: "As often happens with online offending, his began with gaming and starts moving into online forums." Mr Patton, noting Mensforth's autism spectrum disorder diagnosis, said he had to "prove himself" to join, describing him as "a victim who has become a recruiter". Mensforth admitted guilt to 17 charges, including blackmailing victims for self-harm material, sexual communication with children, creating and sharing indecent images of minors, and possessing extreme animal pornography. Passing sentence, Judge Edward Bindloss told Mensforth: "This disturbing case proves that you are a calculating predator who groomed and blackmailed victims by making threats." CPS Head of Special Crime Division Malcolm McHaffie stated: "Beau-Jack Mensforth played a leading role in an online network that targeted vulnerable young girls for exploitation, humiliation and abuse." He added: "Using social media and encrypted messaging platforms, members of this group manipulated children into sharing indecent images before subjecting them to threats, blackmail and coercion." He concluded: "The offending in this case caused significant harm to vulnerable victims." DCI Steve Brown from Northumbria Police urged parents to monitor digital activity, stating: "We do encourage families to be aware of this out in the community, and we encourage people to support their children, support the young people in their lives, by looking at their devices, monitoring and checking what they are online doing." He advised: "Check the groups that they are in. Check the people that they’re having conversations with, understand the platforms that they are using." Reflecting on the case, he added: "Since this investigation has taken place, I do that to my kids."
