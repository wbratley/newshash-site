Doctor behind Christa Pike's botched execution is seen for first time since Satanist killer survived lethal injections...as his other bungled case is revealed The doctor at the center of the botched execution of murderer and Satanist Christa Pike is exclusively pictured by the Daily Mail at his isolated family home – less than 24 hours after the horror. Dr Mark Fowler, 69, came to the door of the house just outside Union City, Tennessee and declined to discuss Pike surviving two syringes of the purportedly lethal injection drug pentobarbital. ’Thank you for coming. I have nothing to say,’ he said from behind the screen door of the four-bedroom property set on 1.5 acres. Fowler lives 160 miles northwest of the Riverbend Maximum Security Institution in Nashville where 50-year-old Pike was injected with seven needles on Wednesday night, according to one of her attorneys, Randy Spivey. She remains alive and is in critical condition at an ‘off-site’ medical facility, which local TV station WSMV has named as Vanderbilt University Medical Center. Pike's case marks the second botched execution in Tennessee this year - both of which were overseen by Fowler. Fowler was supervising triple-killer Tony Carruthers' execution in May when the death row inmate survived 90 minutes of failed attempts by the medical team to establish an IV connection for the lethal dose. A complaint against Fowler, filed by Carruthers’ sister, Dr Tonya Hervey, with the Tennessee Department of Health, claimed that 40 minutes into the execution attempt, the doctor took over the search for vein access and announced he was going to set a central line – a large flexible tube inserted into a chest vein. Dr Mark Fowler, 69, who oversaw the botched execution of murderer and accused satanist Christa Pike in Tennessee was spotted for the first time at his home on Thursday Christa Gail Pike, now 50, is alive and in critical condition in the hospital after surviving the botched execution The tube’s needle was pushed into Carruthers’ chest while he was ‘wide awake and groaned in agony,’ the complaint claims. Dr Hervey also alleges Fowler was unqualified to place the line because he previously admitted in a deposition that he had not performed the procedure for more than a decade. According to the claim, Carruthers suffered a stroke as a result of the botched execution, leaving him partially paralyzed. Tennessee Governor Bill Lee later called off the execution and gave a one-year reprieve to Carruthers, 58, who was convicted of the 1984 kidnapping and murders of Frederick Tucker, Marcellos Anderson and Anderson’s mother, Delois. Fowler could face formal disciplinary charges if the health department finds evidence of a violation in its review. He has previously refused to comment on the complaint on the accusations. But he told NBC, 'Every attempt was made to minimize the defendant’s discomfort in the case at hand.' According to his LinkedIn profile, Fowler gained his law degree from the University of Tennessee, Knoxville in 1984 and spent three years as a public defender before retraining as a physician in 1996. Fowler has worked as an ER physician and a family doctor and is reported to hold several medical directorships.
