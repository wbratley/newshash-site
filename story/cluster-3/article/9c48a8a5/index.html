Michael Carrick LIVE: Man Utd chiefs set for crunch Monaco meeting as Mauricio Pochettino tops shortlist Michael Carrick is set to be relieved of his duties as Manchester United manager after a disastrous start to the season left them in the Premier League relegation zone Michael Carrick is set to be sacked as Manchester United manager in a crunch meeting held in Monaco tomorrow. The Old Trafford hierarchy are set to finally wield the axe following a disastrous start to the season. United have won just once in the Premier League, leaving them marooned in the relegation zone. Carrick was given three games to save his job before the international break, but suffered defeat by Chelsea before a stirring comeback saw United beat Roma 3-2. The Red Devils then drew 4-4 with Aston Villa on Sunday, twice recovering from a two-goal deficit, but it wasn't enough to save Carrick, with United to relieve him of his duties today. Club chiefs Omar Berrada and Jason Wilcox are expected to meet with Sir Jim Ratcliffe on Wednesday, where they are expected to decide to make a change. He will then be told in person at Carrington on Thursday. Mauricio Pochettino is the favourite to succeed Carrick and is reportedly open to the job, while Antonio Conte and Eddie Howe are also in the frame. Stay right here for all the latest updates throughout the day. Key Events That's all from us Well it has been a day. But tomorrow is when the judgement will come down - with Michael Carrick getting an answer one way or another. We'll be back tomorrow with all the latest. Man Utd against interim If Man Utd do decide to Michael Carrick from his position, they want to avoid having an interim head coach take over. Carrick himself was an interim and the club have gone down this route several times previously, including with Ralf Rangnick back in 2021/22. Man Utd consider coach alongside Carrick Man United could yet keep Michael Carrick on as boss - but with additional coaching support as the club's senior figures weigh up their next move. The likes of Omar Berrada and Jason Wilcox will sit down at a summit with minority owner and Ineos chief Sir Jim Ratcliffe on Wednesday, where the expectation is that Carrick will be removed from his role. But if Carrick is to remain in charge beyond the international break then there is the option to bring in more coaching quality around him. Bayern Munich coach Aaron Danks, who worked with Carrick at Middlesbrough and was seen as a potential appointment in the summer, is one option, reports the BBC. Win and draw not enough on their own So, since the damaging loss against Bournemouth Man Utd have somewhat rallied, beating Roma and drawing with Aston Villa. In both those games they were two goals down. And the BBC reports that the chaotic nature of the games and the inability to stop opposition from scoring so frequently has led Berrada and Wilcox to conclude that Carrick does need to be moved on. Man Utd's manager sack u-turn Man Utd's trajectory could look very different had they opted to sack Erik ten Hag in 2024, with two men lined up to replace the Dutchman. Ten Hag would ultimately retain his job despite leading the Red Devils to an eighth place finish in the Premier League - at that point their lowest ever finish. A victory in the Fa Cup final at Wembley proved crucial in him remaining in his post. Sir Jim Ratcliffe and Ineos were newly on the scene and had scoured the market for a new boss with the decision to sack Ten Hag all but taken, only to change their minds late on. Prior to that FA Cup success over Manchester City United had looked elsewhere and settled on Thomas Tuchel and Roberto de Zerbi as their two main candidates - with both men out of a job at that point. 'Man Utd's commercial priority is part of the problem' Our own Andy Dunn wrote today... "It has been pointed out by many that if a manager of, let’s say, Real Madrid or Bayern Munich had won just one of his opening ten league games, there would be no chance of them remaining in the job. "Perhaps not. But that does not mean United cannot act with class. Carrick did a fantastic job when he took charge last season. He over-achieved to a considerable extent. That has been part of his problem this season. Complacency has been a serious issue with post-Sir Alex Ferguson United. "And when one of the owners boasts that United is a club whose commercial power is not greatly dependent on its on-field success, you can understand why. "Some players think they have made it when they land at Old Trafford or when they sign a new contract, regardless of subsequent results. That is what Carrick and his predecessors after Fergie have had to deal with. This does not look like a squad full of players who go away and do extra graft." Is the Poch move too late? Say they do go for Mauricio Pochettino - has the ship almost sailed? When he was first linked with the role he was outperforming his resources at Spurs, and you'd argue his stock has never been higher. Ole Gunnar Solskjaer beat him to the job then and since Pochettino has beek sacked by Spurs, PSG and Chelsea. Now he's in charge of the USA. The excitement around a Pochettino appointment in 2018 would've been massive, he was very much the next best thing. Now, it feels like others have leapt ahead of him in that regard. So is he really what Man Utd should be going after? Merson - transfer business to blame He told Sky Sports: "It doesn't matter if you have Arsene Wenger, Sir Alex Ferguson, Pep Guardiola or Jose Mourinho sitting on the bench. The mistakes they are making are down to the players. "If they weren't running around for the manager, they'd have been ripped to shreds by Roma and Aston Villa and they didn't. They came back in both of those games. I'd be quite surprised if they let Carrick go at the moment "They got it the wrong way around with their transfer policy. The first window they went for buying forwards. The next window, they started bringing in midfield players and now at the end of the season, they're probably going to go and buy defenders." What was the darkest moment? Pfft, there's been a few. Losing at home is never good. The 3-0 humbling at the hands of Bournemouth felt like the afternoon things turned toxis. The 4-1 loss to Como, who were someway down the Italian football pyramid when United were in their heyday, showed just how quickly the Red Devils had fallen. Equally, surrendering a 2-0 lead at home to Brighton, eventually losing 3-2, was a dark day. Patience has been afforded Yes the Man Utd set-up doesn't seem to be lending itself to any manager being successful. But it must be said, given the run of results, Carrick can't say he's not been given time to turn it around. All of his predecessors were axed when they'd enjoyed better results than Carrick, and yet the club have stuck by their former player in the hope he can find the solution. But it seems they can only wait so long.. Nothing off the table... surely?! For all those arguing about what Man Utd should and shouldn't do regarding their next boss. The club have tried everything and its not worked... The proven heavyweight - LvG and Mourinho. The club legends - Solskjaer and Carrick. The up and coming - Ten Hag and Amorim. None have worked, but that's not to say they can't work. Should Carrick have ever been given the job? Well, we're going to be asking that question over the coming days aren't we. His interim stint was so good it made him hard to ignore, and certainly gave United bosses a decision to make. The romantics saw it as a perfect fit, even if they were warned about the parallels with Ole Gunnar Solskjaer, who ultimately won nothing in his near three year spell. The cynics always said that United having no other competitions gave Carrick the ideal scenario. He could also lean on the new manager bounce. Perhaps a really strong football department would've said thanks and sent Carrick on his way, who knows where he and the club would be if they'd done that. Michael Carrick has been 'let down' Michael Carrick has been 'let down' in the transfer window, according to former Man United man Louis Saha. Speaking to FootballPredictions.com, he said: "It’s a hard question, because you can say from the start that he has been let down in the transfer market. There is definitely a big, big question mark in midfield, and I don’t think that has been answered. A question mark in defence – I don’t think it’s been answered. A question mark in attack – I don’t think it’s been answered. Maybe the goalkeeper – even with the two mistakes, or a few mistakes, that he has made, he’s still a valuable goalkeeper. I do think he needs to improve, as everyone knows, but I think he’s okay in his position. "The defence is changing every week. The midfield is missing the kind of power or dominance that you need, physically first and tactically. So regardless of who can attack and join the attack going forward, it’s inconsistent. You have a bit of a threat, but you don’t have the player that every defence is scared of. "They are good players, definitely good players that you have to manage, like [any player at the] standard of the Premier League, but you don’t have Harry Kane or Haaland, those types of players where you know you’re going to struggle to keep them out of any game – you know they will get opportunities. That’s not the case at the moment. They sometimes do a bit of magic, but that’s not enough to win games." Omar Berrada and Jason Wilcox decision Manchester United chiefs Omar Berrada and Jason Wilcox are set to remain at the clubs, despite calls for there to be change at the top. The Telegraph reports that the pair maintain the trust of club co-owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe, who has faith in them to guide the club through the current situation. The pair have been the subject of chants by angry United fans calling for them to go and for the club to be sold. The Glazers' Mauricio Pochettino stance Mauricio Pochettino is the top pick to be the next Manchester United manager by the club's majority shareholders, the Glazer family, according to The i Paper. Sources in the US, tell the paper that the Glazers are determined not to make the same mistake again in overlooking Pochettino. The Argentine was interviewed for the United job twice in 2018 and 2022 but missed out to Ole Gunnar Solskjaer and Erik ten Hag. The Glazers are said to have close ties to Ken Griggin, who helped finance Pochettino's new contract with the USA back in the summer. Where does Michael Carrick rank Michael Carrick was the eighth permanent manager at Manchester United since the retirement of Sir Alex Ferguson. But where does he rank in terms of win percentage? According to Opta, Carrick ranks sixth out of the eight permanent managers. He has a win ratio of 47%, better than Ruben Amorim and Ralf Rangnick, who both had 38%. The best of the eight managers since Ferguson was Jose Mourinho with 58%, followed by Erik ten Hag (55%). Three reasons Carrick could stay Despite the widespread reports that Carrick is set to be sacked, Sky Sports reports that no final decision has been made and outlines three reasons why United are taking their time. Club chiefs are said to be in agreement over the three factors that could make them decide to stick with Carrick: - Crowd support - Despite showing their discontent towards the club, the crowd have not yet turned on Carrick - Dressing room support - The comebacks against both Roma and Aston Villa show that the players are still fighting for their manager - The difference a change will make - United bosses are said to be unconvinced that a managerial change will turn the season around and solve the team's problems. Mauricio Pochettino comments are encouraging Mauricio Pochettino has already hinted that he has his eyes on a return to the Premier League. The former Spurs boss is a leading candidate to take over from Michael Carrick. In an interview with L'Equipe earlier in the year, Pochettino spoke of his love of the Premier League... “I miss the football scene in England. I love the country, its culture, the football culture. For anyone with a competitive spirit who wants to measure themselves against others and test their limits, it's the ideal place. Here, you have to give your best constantly.” Decision made tomorrow A final decision on Michael Carrick's future will be made tomorrow, with a meeting planned between Omar Barrard, Jason Wilcox and co-owner Sir Jim Ratcliff. There is a growing expectation that the recommendation will be to part ways with the manager. Berrarda and Wilcox are said to be ready to present their recommendation on Wednesday, but a firm decision may not come until Thursday.
