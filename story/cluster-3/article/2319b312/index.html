Andy Burnham under fire for letting influencers film TikTok videos inside Downing Street – including offensive clip

Article text

ANDY Burnham was last night facing impropriety questions over his embrace of influencers – as he was forced to disown a TikTok star making “disgusting” videos on the steps of No10. The Tories have written to Cabinet Secretary Dame Antonia Romeo demanding answers over whether public money was used to boost the PM’s personal image. Downing Street threw a bash for social media creators this month and encouraged them to make content from the event. Guests were treated to personalised letters from the PM and professional portraits outside the famous black door. Influencers were also given the Hollywood treatment with a bespoke No10-branded GlamBOT of the kind used at the Oscars. In the letter seen by The Sun, Tory Mike Wood said: “It is unclear what this event and subsequent digital content were for, if not to promote the Prime Minister’s image. READ MORE ON ANDY BURNHAM “In addition, there are clear rules around the vetting of influencers before they are featured in Government Communications. “Considering some of the content that has materialised in the past 24 hours on social media, it would appear proper vetting did not take place.” Whitehall rules explicitly ban the use of public resources to build ministers’ personal brands, saying that is a job for their political party instead. The November 9 event had already landed Mr Burnham in hot water after influencer Max Balegde filmed himself outside the famous No10 black door miming along to a viral song featuring the lyrics “bomboclaat pussywagon chicken nuggets”. Most read in The Sun Bomboclaat is a Jamaican profanity referring to a menstrual cloth while pussy is a slang term for female genitals. Following a national outcry, Downing Street branded the now-deleted clip “clearly not acceptable”, despite the official No10 account “liking” the post initially. Tory leader Kemi Badenoch branded the antics a “disgrace” and demanded the PM disclose every penny spent on courting influencers. She said: “I find it extraordinary that this government is raising taxes to pay people to tell them what a great job they’re doing.” Mr Wood is demanding answers over whether any of the influencers have been paid by the Government and whether taxpayer-funded equipment was used to produce their videos. He also questioned whether No10 was aware of the potential misuse of official government branding in clips posted on influencers’ personal accounts. He warned: “The Government pledged to cut wasteful spending on communications. “But spending on influencers is soaring. “Taxpayers have a right to know whether their money is being spent wisely, and whether it is being co-opted for the promotion of political causes. Analysis by The Sun found Mr Burnham has filmed content with at least 30 influencers since becoming Prime Minister in July.
