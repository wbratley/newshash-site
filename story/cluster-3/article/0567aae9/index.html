UK welcome to rejoin EU ‘anytime’, French finance minister says as Burnham visits Berlin

Log in France’s finance minister said the UK would be welcome to rejoin the EU “anytime”, as Andy Burnham visited Berlin after suggesting a return to the bloc could be possible. Alongside his first face-to-face talks with German chancellor Friedrich Merz, the leaders have established a joint UK-Germany security framework aimed at countering cyber attacks, sabotage and other hybrid threats from hostile foreign actors, particularly Russia. The strategic agreement will see both countries share intelligence and coordinate action to monitor, deter and disrupt potential dangers, while building greater resilience around critical national infrastructure. It comes as Mr Burnham looks to rebuild Britain’s ties to the EU 10 years after the nation voted to leave the bloc. The prime minister last week acknowledged he could go “all the way” and propose rejoining the EU as he considers the UK’s future relationship with Brussels. He also suggested it is “possible” Labour’s next election manifesto could include an in-out referendum. Speaking to the BBC, French finance minister Roland Lescure said it was up to the British public whether to return to the EU, but said they could “come back, anytime”. He said: “British people decided, and that’s obviously their 100 per cent right, and if they’re willing to come back, they will have to decide. “But what I can tell them is: come back, anytime.” The Independent has led the campaign for Britain to rebuild its relationship with Brussels on the basis of clear evidence that it has damaged the economy and failed to deliver promised benefits on a wide range of issues including immigration. Mr Burnham and Mr Merz met on Thursday and agreed on the need for a “strong, collective European response to Russia’s hybrid threats” on the back of an attempted drone attack at Leipzig airport. Mr Burnham said: “We’ve had an excellent discussion today, and we will look to see if we can turn the summit into a good outcome for the EU, but also for the UK as well.” A Downing Street spokesperson added: “They agreed there was opportunity to go further, and to work together to counter the malicious misinformation that threatens the resilience of Europe’s democracies.” As well as a collective defence in relation to a threat to Europe, the leaders “reaffirmed their unwavering support for Ukraine ... and agreed on the need to keep up the pressure on Russia this winter”. In addition to defence, they discussed migration and the need for “close cooperation” to tackle illegal migration and the criminal gangs behind it. In relation to the UK developing a closer relationship with the EU, Mr Burnham “committed to building a stronger, more ambitious partnership, focused on delivering practical benefits for citizens and businesses both at home and across the rest of Europe”. Mr Burnham and Mr Merz also discussed responses to Middle East instability, and the disruption of key global shipping lanes such as the Strait of Hormuz. They convened a UK-Germany Regional Growth Forum featuring British and German business leaders, aimed at driving investment, deepening industrial ties and ensuring economic benefits reach communities in both nations. And they formally ratified the Kensington Treaty, signed in July last year, committing both nations to significantly deeper cooperation across science, technology, defence, security and growth. In Berlin, Mr Burnham also said he was “open” to Germany joining a project to build the next generation of fighter jet. Speaking alongside Chancellor Merz after ratifying the Kensington Treaty between Britain and Germany, the PM said: “We are talking today about defence collaboration, and I’ve indicated to the chancellor, in our discussion prior to this ratification, our openness to Germany joining Gcap, the next generation of fighter aircraft.” Foreign Office minister Stephen Doughty said on Thursday the prime minister will be “looking at the future” of the UK’s economy and security relationships on the visits. Asked about Mr Burnham’s recent comments on the EU, the minister said: “What he has been very honest about is that if we're looking at the future of our economy, the future of our security relationships, as indeed he’s doing today in Germany.” He added: “We need to have an honest conversation about the future for this country, the future of the relationship with our nearest geographic trading and security partner. That’s the heart of what he’s discussing today with Chancellor Mertz, particularly in relation to the German relationship.” The prime minister’s trip comes amid an intelligence scandal that has rocked Germany, where its former spy chief has been arrested on suspicion of espionage. August Hanning, who headed Germany’s Federal Intelligence Service (BND) from 1998 to 2005, is “strongly suspected of treasonous espionage, spying out state secrets, attempted treason, and espionage”, the federal prosecutor’s office said in a statement. Mr Hanning, 80, is accused of using classified intelligence to “draft an analysis” for an employee of one foreign intelligence service, and of “passing on information” to an employee of another foreign spying service. A former aide, identified as Manfred D, was also arrested and has been charged with aiding and abetting attempted treason and treasonous espionage. Mr Burnham’s visit comes as concerns about sabotage and hybrid threats rise in both the UK and Germany. Last month, the British government summoned a senior Russian diplomat to protest over an “egregious and reckless attack” at Germany’s Leipzig airport, a major international freight hub used for providing support to Ukraine. Moscow denied involvement, and Russian president Vladimir Putin accused Germany of planting evidence to blame Moscow.
