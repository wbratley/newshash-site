Kemi Badenoch blasts Labour despite net migration drop: 'We're not taking any lectures!'

Article text

Tory leader Kemi Badenoch has blasted Labour shortly after it was revealed that net migration had dropped to the tens of thousands. Mrs Badenoch, who served in ministerial positions under both Boris Johnson and Rishi Sunak, claimed the 77,000 provisional figure recorded in the year to June 2026 was primarily caused by changes introduced under the Tories. "I'm pleased to see the net migration figures fall," the Leader of the Opposition said. "Net migration was too high. The Government will, of course, take credit for bringing them down. "The truth is the Conservatives made changes after that huge surge, and that is why we're seeing the numbers fall. "But what those numbers hide is that a lot of good people are leaving this country, highly skilled, talented Brits, because they think the Government is killing businesses, making it impossible for them to succeed, and they're going elsewhere." The Office for National Statistics revealed its 77,000 figure in the year to June 2026 is the lowest seen in the UK since 1997, excluding the coronavirus pandemic. However, it also found 245,000 Britons had left the country, with just 109,000 returning to UK shores. Net migration peaked under the Tories at 944,000 in the year to March 2023, with the party earning its "Boriswave" nickname. Mishandling the migration crisis proved fatal to the Conservative Party's electoral chances, with Reform UK hoovering up more than four million votes at the 2024 General Election. Ex-Prime Minister Rishi Sunak has already admitted his migration crackdown should have been introduced sooner, including measures to restrict overseas students bringing dependants, increase the minimum salary threshold for foreign workers and prevent overseas care workers from bringing family members to Britain. However, pressure mounted on the Tories following consecutive manifesto commitments to curb net migration, including David Cameron's pledge to bring the numbers down to the tens of thousands. Mrs Badenoch added: "If the Labour Government had actually done anything, we would have congratulated them, but they just sat there and said, 'Look, net migration is falling; give us some credit'. LATEST DEVELOPMENTS "We should have done more earlier, and that is something I have acknowledged, but the Conservative Party is under new management now, and I have said we are going to keep migration numbers down. "Not just on net migration, but also to deal with the small boats crisis. We're the only ones with a plan to do so. We had the Rwanda plan; Labour came in and scrapped it. "Given Labour scrapped the plan that other European countries are using, I don't think we are going to be taking any lectures from them, congratulating themselves for just sitting and doing nothing." However, Home Office Minister Anna Turley argued the Tories had lost control of both legal and illegal migration. She insisted Labour's decision to overhaul visas, raise skills levels and increase the salary cap has had a direct impact on net migration numbers. Speaking to GB News, Ms Turley said: "The Tories can try and claim credit all they like. "I'm old enough to remember every single Tory manifesto that said they'd bring the numbers down to the tens of thousands and did no such thing. "We've got control of the chaos they've left behind." Meanwhile, Reform UK's home affairs spokesman Zia Yusuf claimed there was "no cause for celebration” after the ONS released its provisional figure. Mr Yusuf added: “The only way to improve quality of life for British people is to deport all those who came illegally and reverse the Boriswave to deliver net negative migration.” There are also concerns about Shabana Mahmood's decision to water down reforms to indefinite leave to remain. The Home Secretary scrapped plans to make care workers wait 15 years for settlement, exempted some lower-paid public service workers from the 10-year qualifying period, and allowed eligible care workers earning at least £25,000 to retain the five-year route to indefinite leave to remain.
