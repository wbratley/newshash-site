Andy Taylor, guitarist in Duran Duran, dies aged 65

Article text

Andy Taylor, the guitarist with 80s pop megastars Duran Duran, has died aged 65. He had been diagnosed with stage four metastatic prostate cancer in 2018, which he disclosed publicly in 2022 when Duran Duran were about to be inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame. “To the world, Andy was a brilliant guitarist, but to us, he was the most wonderful husband, a loving dad and the proudest grandfather,” read a statement shared with Variety. “He was the rock of our family. For all the incredible experiences music brought him, family always came first. Nothing made him happier than having us all together, and no matter how far music took him, his heart was always at home.” Taylor’s wife of more than 40 years, Tracey Wilson said his last few years were faced with “so much courage and determination” and that he “never los[t] his love of life”. A statement from Duran Duran added: “Andy was there at the very beginning of our story. From the moment he arrived in Birmingham from the North East with a guitar and an unshakeable belief in what we could become, he brought an indelible fire and swagger to our music. His playing drove the songs that took us around the world, and with a dry wit that was matched only by his wonderfully unsentimental, no-nonsense outlook on life he kept us grounded and gave the band its edge through the extraordinary years that followed.” Musicians paid tribute once the news broke. Rod Stewart remembered Taylor as his “dear pal”. “We made some good music together and at the same time had some outrageous fun,” Stewart wrote on Instagram. Singer Belinda Carlisle called Taylor a “genius”. “He played the guitar solo on Mad About You, appears in the video and he made special appearances onstage for some of my first solo shows. What a talent,” she captioned a video of the pair performing together. “Thank you Andy for the music and all of the joy you brought to the world which will live on forever. I hope you are feeling the love… Godspeed.” Taylor was born in 1961 and grew up in Cullercoats, Northumberland, in a family who worked in the local fishing industry. He began playing guitar from the age of 11 and was tutored by Dave Black of the David Bowie backing band the Spiders from Mars. He left school early to start playing in bands on the working men’s club scene. In 1980 he joined the nascent Duran Duran, who had formed in 1978 but whose lineup was far from settled – they advertised for a guitarist in Melody Maker, and Taylor travelled to Birmingham to audition. “A couple of days later they rang me and said out of 40 or so I was the only one who knew how to tune up, let alone play,” he later recalled. The following month Simon Le Bon joined as frontman, by February 1981 they had released their debut single Planet Earth, and with the July release of Girls on Film – a song propelled by Taylor’s ultra-tight and rhythmic playing – they became major stars in the UK and US. Rio, their second album released in 1982, cemented their international playboy image and went Top 10 in both countries, and follow-up Seven and the Ragged Tiger topped the UK chart. Speaking to Smash Hits in 1983, Taylor described his new-found career as “a hobby gone mad”. That year Taylor also opened a wine bar called Rio, in Whitley Bay in his native Tyneside. Following their third album Duran Duran went on a brief hiatus, during which time Taylor and bassist John Taylor (no relation) formed a new funk-rock group, the Power Station, with singer Robert Palmer and former Chic drummer Tony Thompson. Two singles, the original track Some Like It Hot and a cover of T Rex’s Get It On, reached the US Top 10 in 1985. Later that year Taylor rejoined Duran Duran to record A View to a Kill, the US chart-topping James Bond film theme, and perform with them at Live Aid in Philadelphia. But amid drug use, relations had become strained: drummer Roger Taylor left due to exhaustion, and Andy Taylor himself left to pursue the rockier direction he began with the Power Station – though his Duran Duran bandmates initially believed he would be continuing with the band. “I’d come close to a nervous breakdown from the stresses of our crazy life and my own cocaine problem,” Taylor later said. “Combine that with all the shouting matches and the icy silences – I just knew I had to get out. So I did. The day after Live Aid, I stopped drinking and taking drugs. And I quietly started getting legal advice on extricating myself from Duran Duran.” Collaborating with Sex Pistols’ Steve Jones, he released a solo LP, Thunder, in 1986, and the single Take It Easy was a minor US hit. As well as recording a covers album, Dangerous, he spent a number of years as a writer and producer for hire, with his song Lost in You becoming a transatlantic hit for Rod Stewart in 1988. The Power Station briefly reunited in 1996, and then in 2000, the classic lineup of Duran Duran reformed and released a new album together, Astronaut, in 2004. He also toured with the band and worked on another (later scrapped) album Reportage, but was hit hard by the death of his father to cancer, and left the band again in 2006; he was diagnosed with depression the following year. He spent the next decade away from music, first publishing his memoir Wild Boy: My Life in Duran Duran in 2008. He released a new solo single in 2019 and joined British rock band Reef as a tour guitarist, also producing their 2022 album Shoot Me Your Ace. Despite the at-times fractious relationship with Duran Duran over the years, Taylor said in his statement accompanying the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame induction that there was no “bad blood … just pure love and respect for everything we wrote, recorded and achieved together”. He married Wilson in 1982, and they had four children together: Andrew, Georgina, Bethany and Isabella. Following his cancer diagnosis, he credited his successful music career with keeping him upbeat. “I’ve been very fortunate, I’ve had so much, in terms of living the dream, but because I do music, I think that’s probably really helped me to live with pessimism of an incurable disease,” he said.
