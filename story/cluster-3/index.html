UK bond yields hit 2008 high as Burnham faces first PMQs, budget concerns grow

Unbiased summary

On Wednesday, UK 10-year government bond yields reached 5.29%, the highest since 2008, amid a global sell-off of government bonds driven by inflation fears, geopolitical tensions (notably the Iran war and Strait of Hormuz shipping risks), and concerns over deficits. Prime Minister Andy Burnham used his first PMQs to reassure markets, pledging fiscal responsibility while declining to rule out future tax rises or increased borrowing. Chancellor John Healey faces reduced spending headroom as the Office for Budget Responsibility will base its budget forecast on current market rates. Burnham blamed the previous Conservative government for leaving the UK vulnerable, pointing to 14 years of stagnant growth and rising debt. The opposition, led by Kemi Badenoch, accused him of being a 'spendthrift.'
