US and Denmark announce Greenland security deal; Trump claims permanent US control

Unbiased summary

The US and Denmark have announced a security agreement for Greenland, resolving a diplomatic dispute triggered by President Trump's threats to seize the territory. Trump claimed the deal grants the US 'permanent control over security and all other needs' in Greenland, with no cost to Washington, and bars US adversaries from having military bases or sensitive investments there without US approval. Nato welcomed the agreement, saying it advances regional stability. Denmark's Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen and Greenland's Prime Minister Jens-Frederik Nielsen said the deal strengthens Arctic security while respecting Greenland's sovereignty and self-determination. The agreement is expected to be signed at the UN General Assembly and requires parliamentary approvals. No text of the agreement has been released.
