Andy Burnham ally urges PM to reform 'nonsensical' council tax and stamp duty

Article text

Andy Burnham ally urges PM to reform 'nonsensical' council tax and stamp duty Lord Jim O’Neill, an economist who advised Andy Burnham as he prepared for Downing Street, said it was time for 'bold action' to fix property taxes once and for all A former economic adviser to Andy Burnham has urged the Prime Minister to reform “utterly nonsensical” property taxes like council tax and stamp duty. Lord Jim O’Neill, who advised the PM as he prepared for Downing Street, said it was time for “bold action” to fix the system, as costly stamp duty payments can put people off moving house, or prevent older homeowners from downsizing. He also argued that the council tax system can mean owners of expensive properties pay less than due to rising house prices since 1991, when the bands were set. The out-of-date measure can mean people in the North and the Midlands pay higher rates than people in the South where property prices have risen more quickly. His calls were echoed by Compass director Neal Lawson, a long-time Burnham ally, who said the inequality in the system must be tackled. Mr Burnham has previously argued that land is undertaxed and branded council tax "highly regressive". But he ruled out immediate changes to council tax and stamp duty soon after becoming Prime Minister. Pressure is now mounting on the PM to take action in his 10-year plan in December, with a debate in Parliament on Monday after 100,000 people signed a petition demanding action. Lord O'Neill, a former Goldman Sachs economist who turned down a job in Mr Burnham’s Government, said: “There is widespread agreement that we have an utterly nonsensical approach to the taxation of property. “The owners of higher-value homes can pay far less Council Tax as a proportion of their property’s value than people at the other end of the scale, while Stamp Duty discourages people from moving and holds back economic activity. Now is the time for bold action to fix the property tax system once and for all.” Mr Lawson said: “The Prime Minister has committed to taking on the big challenges where other politicians have failed, and the inequality at the heart of our property tax system should be absolutely central to that. "We need an independent review to consider the options and deliver for the working people that put their trust in Labour to deliver real change.” The pair joined a coalition of MPs, think tanks, campaign groups, charities and economists who are pushing for reform to be included in the Prime Minister’s 10-year plan next month. Jonathan Hinder, Labour MP for Pendle and Clitheroe, said: “Council tax is the most unfair, regressive tax in Britain, and a considered independent review of comprehensive property tax reform is long overdue." Andrew Dixon, Founder of Fairer Share, said: “We have a property tax system that penalises people living in modest homes, discourages people from moving and places growing pressure on households and councils. No government designing a tax system today would start here." The Liberal Democrats have also backed a separate Commons motion calling for a property tax overhaul. The party's Treasury spokeswoman Daisy Cooper said: “This government has promised to grasp the nettle tackling problems ignored by Westminster for too long, and Andy Burnham has previously criticised our property taxes. "With momentum growing for an overhaul, his administration must now walk the walk.” A Government spokesperson said: “The 10 Year Plan will set out the Government’s long-term vision for Britain and how we tackle the deep-rooted challenges facing the country over the decade ahead. We won’t pre-empt the detail ahead of its publication. “As has always been the case, decisions on tax are a matter for the Chancellor to set out at fiscal events.”
