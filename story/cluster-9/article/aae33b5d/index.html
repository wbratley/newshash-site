Chicken Shop Date fans left in tears as Amelia Dimoldenberg's final episode takes an unexpected emotional turn Chicken Shop Date fans were left in tears at the unexpected emotional turn during Amelia Dimoldenberg’s final ever episode. The long-running YouTube series usually sees the comedian, 32, interview a celebrity guest, but for the last outing, Amelia was joined by Belgian relationship therapist Esther Perel. And Esther's appearance took the show's light-hearted tone into a somewhat different direction, with the episode turning into a therapy session for Amelia as she opened up on her dating life and her real reasons for wanting to end the show. With the deadpan jokes and awkward banter out of the window and replaced by a sentimental Amelia laying bare her feelings on wanting to find a romantic partner, viewers were caught off guard. Taking to the comments, fans shared that they were left in tears at the raw and heartfelt final episode, while praising Amelia for her honesty. They penned: 'Jokes on you I was planning to cry today,' 'I legit cried. Ugh, this was perfect. So much love.' The TV star's series began as a magazine column when she was just 17-years-old before transitioning into an online series in 2014. The premise of her programme was interviewing celebrities in local chicken shops on a 'date' setting as she quizzed them on their life with a deadpan expression. The final episode saw Amelia admit that she wanted to find a partner, as she said: 'I think I have to end the show to meet somebody. 'I definitely would love to meet someone that wasn’t a celebrity, if I’m being honest. It seems very intense to go out with a celebrity.' Esther also gave Amelia advice on her decision to draw the show – which started in 2014 – to a close, with her words bringing the host to tears as she said her final goodbye. Earlier this week, Amelia revealed she quit her show Chicken Shop Date because it was hampering her chances of finding love in real life. The comedian, who said it left her 'feeling isolated', had her first panic attack and then her second within a week before deciding it may be time to end the show. Amelia explained how she wanted to end it 'before I start resenting it' ahead of the final episode which was released on YouTube on Friday. But despite the show's huge successes and countless 'dates' with stars, Amelia has now explained that the series was not helping her to find a partner. She wrote in The Cut magazine: 'When you are a child who desires fame, you are really a child who yearns for connection. The presenter announced the news on her Instagram page with a note scribbled on a napkin that said: 'All good dates must come to an end' Explaining her decision, Amelia wrote: 'When I was 17 I had a fantastic idea that I might meet the love of my life on a date in a chicken shop' 'I thought the more famous I became, the more likely it was [that] someone would fall in love with me. If anything, playing a decade-long role of the serial dater has made my real dating life challenging. 'People often meet you with a preconceived idea of who you are, their curiosity misplaced. I am so proud of my success, but it has isolated me in some ways.' Amelia, who began the series by interviewing artists from the rap and grime scene, said she 'delusionally' believed she may fall in love with someone on-camera. She said she mused 'why wouldn't a celebrity want to be with me?' and admitted she has had crushes on guests who appeared on the show. Amelia's most famous Chicken Shop dates included Hollywood actors and global pop stars to some of the biggest names in music. Over the years she has spoken to stars including Jennifer Lawrence, Billie Eilish, Cher, Paul McCartney, Ryan Reynolds and Hugh Jackman. Amelia announced the end of her YouTube show, Chicken Shop Date, in September after 12 years as she shared her plans for a 'new adventure'. She revealed her professional dating career is over and would wrap up her show with 'a few final dates', the last of which aired this week. Amelia's upcoming projects include starring in an Amazon MGM's Orion Pictures romcom and writing a Skins-style drama with the BBC, which has yet to be green lit.
