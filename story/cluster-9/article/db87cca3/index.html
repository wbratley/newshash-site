The pair shared a friendship spanning more than five decades after becoming part of the same generation of British comedy stars who rose to prominence during the 1970s. Following news of Mr O'Sullivan's death on Friday, Mr Askwith, 75, took to social media to share his heartbreak alongside a photograph of the late actor laughing. He wrote: "Anyone who knows anything about me knows this hurts. "He was a rascal, and I loved him… Richard O'Sullivan RIP." Mr Askwith ended his message with a reference likely to hold special meaning for the pair, adding: "I'll just say...sausage rolls...!" (sic) The tribute comes shortly after Mr Askwith had celebrated his friend's 82nd birthday, sharing another warm moment with Mr O'Sullivan during a FaceTime call. Robin Askwith said Richard O'Sullivan was a 'rascal' as he shared an emotional tribute to the late actor |ROBIN ASKWITH/X Their friendship stretched back more than half a century, with both actors becoming familiar faces during the golden age of British television comedy. Mr O'Sullivan became a household name through his roles in classic sitcoms including Man About the House and its spin-off Robin's Nest, while Mr Askwith found fame playing Timmy Lea in the Confessions film series. Their careers also shared a remarkable connection, with Mr O'Sullivan allegedly having been offered the lead role in Confessions of a Window Cleaner before turning it down. The role ultimately went to Mr Askwith and helped propel him to stardom. Mr O'Sullivan largely stepped away from public life after suffering a stroke in 2003 and subsequently lived at Brinsworth House, the Royal Variety Charity's care home for members of the entertainment industry. Mr Askwith remained a close friend, regularly keeping in touch and sharing affectionate updates about the sitcom star with fans. The Royal Variety Charity confirmed Mr O'Sullivan's death on Friday, revealing he had been surrounded by close family at Brinsworth House.
