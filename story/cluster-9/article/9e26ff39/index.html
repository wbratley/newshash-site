‘Genuinely emotional’: the world-conquering Chicken Shop Date’s final, eye-opening episode

Article text

The decision to screen the full final series of Chicken Shop Date in cinemas before the finale airs on YouTube is, it could be argued, overkill. The cinema is a place where you sit rapt among like-minded people, bombarded with cutting-edge sound design and world-class visuals. On the other hand, the fun interview show that is Chicken Shop Date is the thing I watch alone on my computer while I pick my toenails. It isn’t a natural fit, is what I’m trying to say. And yet, goddamn it if the whole thing didn’t leave me slightly choked up. After 12 years of flirting and awkwardness, Amelia Dimoldenberg’s final guest is Esther Perel, the Belgian psychotherapist and relationship guru beloved by Oprah Winfrey. It is by no means a traditional episode, almost completely dismissing the tone of everything that came before it. Dimoldenberg’s well-honed persona, alternately dismissive and needy, is nowhere to be seen. Instead, Dimoldenberg does something that Perel must be deadeningly familiar with when meeting new people: she uses the episode as a free therapy session. If you’ve been following Chicken Shop Date for any length of time, the conversation is quite revealing. Dimoldenberg explains that she has essentially outgrown the show that made her famous, and continuing to do it is sad and exhausting. Again, longtime viewers may have suspected this for a while. The scrappiness of the early days, when Dimoldenberg would almost exclusively interview musicians she fancied, has long gone. Now, Chicken Shop Date is just another stop on the promotional celebrity parlour game circuit, squeezed between chugging hot sauce and ranking scotch eggs. Dimoldenberg has had to work harder and harder to manufacture any sort of chemistry with these people. Add to this the fact that, as outlined in a piece she wrote this week for the Cut, Dimoldenberg started experiencing panic attacks towards the end of the show, and the writing was on the wall. After a tiny – possibly ironic – bit of pushback after she notices that nobody has actually flirted with her, Perel tells Dimoldenberg to imagine she is having a conversation with Chicken Shop Date itself, and that it is time to say goodbye. What follows is a genuinely emotional monologue where the host talks about everything she has gained from Chicken Shop Date, and everything she would lose if she continued to do it. As she thanks the show and bids it farewell, she tears up. This is the part that got me. Now, I will admit that circumstances might have heightened my response a little. I saw the finale at the premiere, in a small screening room full of people who worked on the show, with Dimoldenberg herself sitting in the row behind me. Everyone in the room was sad to see it go. There is a possibility that, without all the stifled sobbing around me, I wouldn’t have been so moved. But who knows. Amelia Dimoldenberg has been doing this for 12 years. Chicken Shop Date is something she dreamed up at a youth club, with no idea what would come from it. This hasn’t been a case of overnight fame for her. Everything about the show has been incremental; the rise in viewers, the viral moments with Louis Theroux and Andrew Garfield, the gig at the Oscars. When Chicken Shop Date started, online video was seen as a silly little sideshow. As it ends, the form has displaced television as the pre-eminent way of consuming entertainment. The show was one of the drivers of this change. It has been bigger than anyone’s wildest dreams. Of course, this isn’t to say that showing the whole thing in cinemas is necessarily the best way to watch Chicken Shop Date, because the film version consists of the last six episodes, five of which have been available on YouTube for weeks. Inevitably, thanks to pure familiarity, some of it does drag a bit. But what do I know? Some of the screenings this week sold out. Maybe there is demand for recent YouTube videos to be given the cinematic experience after all. And let’s allow Dimoldenberg her lap of honour. Chicken Shop Date has been such a wild success that she could walk into any hosting gig on the planet at the moment, so it’s impressive that she has set her sights slightly higher. She’s writing a romcom, and seems focused on becoming a movie director. The woman is already used to realising her dreams, so that should be a cinch. If any of it lands with even a fraction of the impact of Chicken Shop Date, we’ll be hearing a lot more from Amelia Dimoldenberg for years to come.
