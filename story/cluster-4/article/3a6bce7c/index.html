New Orleans cruise crash live: All passengers accounted for after tanker crashes into cruise ship All passengers have been accounted for after a tanker crashed into the side of the American Cruise Lines' American Heritage at the Port of New Orleans - a rescue mission has been launched on the Mississippi River A cruise ship capsized after colliding with an oil tanker, sparking a huge rescue operation - will all passengers pulled to safety with no reported injuries, the U.S. Coastguard says. The Atlantic Asphalt tanker crashed into the side of the American Cruise Lines' American Heritage at around 12:30pm local time. The river cruiser was docked on the Mississippi River at the Port of New Orleans when it was hit. Footage caught the moment passengers screamed as the ship began falling to its side and sinking rapidly. In other videos circulating on social media, several people are seen in the water wearing life jackets as helicopters fly overhead. The latest statement from the U.S. Coastguard reads: "Working closely with our partners, the USCG has confirmed all passengers are accounted for, with no reported casualties following the collision on the MS River. Our focus now shifts to safely reopening the river and coordinating salvage operations." New Orleans Mayor Helena Moreno called it "a miracle on the Mississippi." "All passengers are accounted for, with no reported casualties" Donald Trump thanks first responders President Donald Trump, in a post on his Truth Social account, praised the work of first responders, law enforcement and the U.S. Coast Guard in responding to Friday’s collision. “Thank you to the United States Coast Guard for doing such an incredible job of rescue, and other work, having to do with the crash of the two ships in Louisiana. Likewise, all of Law Enforcement and First Responders were fantastic!” Trump said in the post. “Incredibly, nobody was killed by this major collision. 32 people are in the hospital.” Investigation is underway, Landry says Louisiana governor Jeff Landry says an investigation to establish the cause of the crash is underway. Captain Melanie Burnham said during the news conference that there appeared to be "some type of a loss of steering," She added: "The exact reason why - we don't know that." 87 people rescued while ship was afloat Rescue workers were able to get 87 people off the ship while it was still afloat. Tug boats, a police boat and a firefighting vessel were sent to help those who had entered the river, Montroll said. It took around 30 to 45 minutes to get all passengers safely out the water. Most passengers are believed to be tourists, police say Police told the news conference that most of the passengers on board the stricken ship appeared to be tourists, adding there were no children on board. Those who were rescued were given blankets when they were brought back to land, police chief Melanie Montroll said. Donald Trump called to offer 'whatever support was needed' amid rescue effort Donald Trump called to offer whatever support was needed during the rescue effort, Louisiana Governor Jeff Landry said during a news conference. Landry praised all the authorities who worked together to get passengers out of the water. He said: It is a literal miracle on the Mississippi. When you think about the population that was on that cruise ship, and the ability for us to get them off, get them out of the water. The Mississippi River is not some quiet pond, right? I mean, there were challenges out there. Two cops who fell in water in 'good condition,' says harbour police chief Two harbour police officers who fell into the water while attempting to rescue passengers from the capsizing ship are in a "good condition," harbour police chief Melanie Montroll said. Both of the officers are in hospital, she added. 'It was just chaos,' says nearby worker A man who was working close to the crash site says workers began rushing down the dock before he got out of his truck and noticed the American Heritage on its side. Devon Flanagan also saw passengers in the water as other ships tried to help out. He said: It was just chaos. You had all types of ships, like other barges and other companies down there trying to help out, which also was a little convoluted because you had people still in the water. At least 180 passengers and crew rescued At least 180 passengers and crew members were rescued following the collision. The much larger Atlantic Asphalt tanker "lost control" before hitting the cruise boat while it was docked, New Orleans Mayor Helena Moreno said. Helena Moreno said 32 people are in hospital, but that none of those patients have suffered critical injuries. 'Truly a miracle on the Mississippi' New Orleans mayor Helena Moreno said the fact that all passengers and crew have been accounted for was "truly a miracle on the Mississippi." The ship narrowly missed hitting a bridge carrying around 160,000 vehicles daily, she added. Police entered water to rescue people Harbor Police Department Chief Melanie Montroll says police arrived at the scene immediately and began trying to help people off the ship. She adds: Unfortunately, as we were in the process of evacuating those passengers, the boat did begin to take on water, and it subsequently would capsize. Montroll says officers were in the water trying to rescue people. Crash leaves 32 people in hospital - all passengers are accounted for The crash has left 32 people in hospital, mayor Helena Moreno says. None of those patients have suffered critical injuries, she added. Moreno adds that zero people are unaccounted for and thanked emergency services for their quick response. Tanker ship 'lost control' before colliding into cruise ship, says mayor Mayor of New Orleans Helena Moreno said the tanker lost control before crashing into the cruise ship, which then began to sink. She added that passengers began disembarking the ship as it started capsizing. Moreno said: The passengers began disembarking from the ship after that collision, but while that was happening, the cruise ship began to sink, and then eventually the cruise ship toppled over and flipped. Now, when that occurred, we started to have people in the water. Press conference begins A press conference on the New Orleans cruise ship crash has begun with a statement from Mayor of New Orleans Helena Moreno. We'll be sharing the latest updates here. American Cruise Lines says it is 'deeply concerned' for everyone on board American Cruise Lines has issued a statement saying its river boat, American Heritage, was involved in a collision while docked in New Orleans. The company said: We are in close coordination with the US Coast Guard and local authorities. Guests and crew were evacuated, and we are deeply concerned for everyone involved. We will share more information as it becomes available. Louisiana Senator John Kennedy 'praying for everyone involved' Louisiana Senator John Kennedy says he and his wife Becky "are praying for everyone involved in the terrible ship collision in New Orleans". The message, posted to X, adds: "We're grateful for the brave first responders working to rescue those in danger and praying everyone makes it home safely." 'It looked like something off of Titanic' Witness Jamie Holford said passengers were rushed off the cruise ship as it began slowly sinking, with many of those on board appearing to be elderly. "It looked like something off of Titanic," he said. People were "hollering, crying, screaming" as the vessel began to roll over, leaving what appeared to be only crew members on top of the ship, he told CBS. Mr Holford described the sound of the collision as "like a war movie" likening it to "tons of steel crunching". Witness describes 'surreal' scenes as ship 'rolled over' within minutes A witness has described the collision as "surreal," adding that the ship had rolled over within just 15 minutes of being hit. Jamie Holford told WDSU-TV: Surreal - that's what that was. Fifteen minutes from impact to where it rolled over Mississippi River closed to all vessel traffic near crash site - US coast guard The US coast guard says the Mississippi River "is closed to all vessel traffic between mile markers 93 and 96," near the crash site. The closure is in place to help responders as they "assess the situation and conduct response operations," the coast guard said. Expert believes crash came after tanker experienced steerage or engine failure A maritime expert has offered his theory on why the tanker may have collided with the docked cruise ship. Martin J Davies, professor at Tulane University, told Fox 8 that he suspected the crash was caused by either steerage failure or engine failure. He explained: This ship is headed upriver and for it to have lost the ability to avoid the moored vessel, there must have been some fairly substantial failure, with the steering gear or the propulsion of the ship to prevent them from avoiding the vessel on shore. Given that the pressure of the water going down river is sufficiently strong that the upbound ship would have been going fairly slowly. Images show passengers being pulled from water in dramatic rescue operation The rescue operation is continuing following the collision, with passengers seen being helped out of the water after scrambling to stay afloat using inflatable rings and life jackets. Emergency crews, including firefighters and police, have gathered as the scene is cordoned off. Other images show passengers and rescuers climbing out of the stricken vessel, which is lying almost entirely on its side. Louisiana governor travels to New Orleans after ship crash The governor of Louisiana, Jeff Landry, said he was aware of the incident and in close contact with local officials and law enforcement as he made his way to New Orleans. Everything we know so far The American Heritage cruise ship was carrying almost 150 people when it was hit by an oil tanker at around 12:30pm local time. The ship was docked on the Mississippi River at the Port of New Orleans. It is said to have arrived two hours earlier to begin disembarkation, reports local TV station WGNO. The collision has sparked an urgent rescue effort, with multiple people having already been pulled from the Mississippi River. There were around 129 passengers, in addition to crew members, on board the ship when the collision occurred, Port NOLA said. Rescue efforts are onoing, with police, the US Coast Guard and fire crews all at the scene. Multiple people pulled from water but rescue efforts ongoing, mayor says New Orleans mayor Helena Moreno's communication team said multiple people had been pulled from the water, but that rescue efforts remain ongoing, a Fox News affiliate reports. Dramatic pictures show crash aftermath Photos from the aftermath of the collision show the American Heritage lying entirely on its side in the Mississippi River. Ferry service suspended Ferry service across the Mississippi River has been temporarily suspended as "a precaution" following a "collision involving two vessels", government agency Nola Ready says. Tanker 'may have lost power' before hitting ship, says emergency response source According to an emergency response source quoted by The Guardian, rescue efforts were underway to pull people from the water. The source also suggested the tanker may have lost power before colliding with the American Heritage.
