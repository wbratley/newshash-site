Two trains collide in Egypt, killing at least seven and injuring more than 100

Unbiased summary

Two passenger trains collided on Sunday in al-Ayyat district, Giza province, about 50km south of Cairo. A train traveling from Giza to Fayoum crashed into the rear of a stationary train heading from Cairo to Aswan. At least seven people were killed and over 100 injured, according to health officials. The driver of one train was among the dead. Emergency services transported the injured to nearby hospitals, and cranes were deployed to clear the wreckage. President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi ordered an investigation and accountability for any negligence. The incident occurred near the site of Egypt's deadliest train disaster in 2002. Train crashes are common in Egypt due to poor infrastructure and mismanagement.
