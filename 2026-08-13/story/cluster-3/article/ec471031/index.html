'The system is broken': Residents of tiny village soon to be outnumbered by 1,250 male migrants hit out at Andy Burnham's plans to make middle-class areas house asylum seekers - See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source Locals living in a village that is soon to be outnumbered by migrants have criticised Andy Burnham's plans to make middle-class areas house asylum seekers. The Prime Minister said this week that he would make sure arrivals were not only placed in the 'poorest communities' and 'all parts of the country need to play their part'. But locals living in Piddington are infuriated by proposals to house 1,250 males at a migrant camp near the tiny Oxfordshire village at Bicester barracks. Such is the ire of villagers, where just 350 people live, they have voted to stage a symbolic referendum on leaving the UK as part of their protests. The vote was backed by the parish council, with its chairman Tim McNally saying people are fed up with not being listened to. They have cried foul that the area is 'completely inappropriate', impractical, and unnecessary, and say it has taken months to get any sort of response from Labour. The Daily Mail visited the quaint village this week where white signs with red block capital letters reading 'No to Bicester MoD asylum centre here' greet visitors. Mr McNally, 60, chair of Piddington Parish Council, said he was concerned by the amount of energy it had taken for Mr Burnham 'to even respond to us'. 'The whole system is broken, and the politics of this country is broken as no one is representing us. No one is saying we can stop this. That is why we are very despondent about the whole thing. 'We are paying our money, we are paying taxes, but we aren't being represented. We are now pushing back. The independence referendum is an example of this. 'And to now say this is on you, it is coming to your middle-class areas, that is ridiculousness because the cost of living is the cost of living. 'Just because houses here might be more expensive than other places, the money is earned, people work hard here and they have chosen not to live in a city. We work hard in this village, we are not spoon-fed.' He said locals are worried about how the government has tried to claim that every individual is security vetted. 'So we don't know what is coming,' he said. 'The men will be allowed out during the day. We are near HMP Bullingdon but that is locked up. We have absolutely no problem with that whatsoever. Unfortunately, the asylum site is not locked up. What are 1,000 men going to do during the day?' Melise Witkin, 59, resident of Piddington, said: 'This is not about being rich or poor, this is about safety. People have worked really hard throughout their lives to live in this village. It is not such a rich or poor village. I just don't think that is a conversation to be had. This is about safety. 'We are 356 people in the village with 46 children and 1,250 men turning up is a recipe for disaster. People walk their dogs, children play, I just think it is madness. An analysis by the Daily Mail has revealed that there are 17 local authorities with no taxpayer-supported asylum seekers. Asylum seekers who are waiting for a decision on their asylum claim are often eligible for accommodation and money to cover their living expenses. This is provided by the Home Office, and the migrants themselves are dispersed across the country. Home Office figures showing the number of supported asylum seekers per local authority at the end of March reveal the areas that currently house no migrants are overwhelmingly rural beauty spots. These include the Cotswolds, Derbyshire Dales, Malvern Hills, the New Forest and the Scottish Highlands. Others include Mid Devon, East Hertfordshire, Ceredigion (a scenic county in west Wales) and Hart, a 118-square-mile local authority taking in major towns, rural parishes and villages in Hampshire. The Gloucestershire market town of Tewkesbury is also on the list, as are the Isle of Wight, Isles of Scilly, Orkney Islands, Shetland Islands, Mid and East Antrim and Mid Ulster, and Na h-Eileanan Siar – an island chain off the west coast of Scotland.
