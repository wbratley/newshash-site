Insiders reveal painful real reason behind Karoline Leavitt's decision to QUIT 'thankless' White House... and Trump's pleading messages The shock announcement Wednesday that Karoline Leavitt will be leaving the White House at the end of this month dropped like a bomb in Washington. After all, Leavitt, 28, had rushed back to her press secretary podium last month just weeks after giving birth to her second child and declaring: 'Let's get back to work.' But now, multiple insiders tell the Daily Mail that Leavitt's decision to quit has actually been months in the making - and deeply pained. 'Being a mother and welcoming a new baby while working in one of the most demanding jobs in the world has been the most rewarding yet challenging season of my life,' Leavitt wrote in a statement Wednesday afternoon. 'The truth is since returning to the White House after the birth of my daughter, I have felt in my heart that I cannot be the best mom my two young children deserve while devoting the constant time, energy, and attention required of the White House Press Secretary.' The mother-of-two had returned from maternity leave on July 16 after giving birth to her daughter in May. She also has a two-year-old son with her husband Nicholas Riccio, 60. One source close to Leavitt - the youngest ever White House press secretary - reveals she had been strongly weighing whether to quit during maternity leave, as the president would contact her often and repeatedly ask when she was coming back. 'No doubt one of the hardest decisions of her life so far,' the source said. Karoline Leavitt speaks to President Trump at the White House Correspondents' Dinner in Washington last month Leavitt, 28, made the shock announcement Wednesday that she would be quitting as White House Press Secretary at the end of this month Leavitt (pictured carrying her son Niko in April) has spent months mulling the decision, say insiders Another source detailed an extreme 'burnout' in the few weeks since Leavitt had returned to work. 'It was a very stressful job to do with her children. She never got any sleep, and she didn't want her performance to slip either, so she decided to stay as long as she could and do the job well,' the source said. 'The president did everything he could to keep her.' 'It's a thankless job, and these are some big shoes to fill,' another insider said. 'I do know that the replacement isn't chosen just yet. They are still weighing their options.' Another source says that, after returning from maternity leave, Leavitt very quickly realized she definitely needed out. 'First week back she realized she wasn't feeling it anymore,' the source said. 'Trump really didn't want her to leave as he actually trusts her and doesn't like having to deal with new people in his circle when he wants to focus on getting stuff done in his time left in office.' Two sources also explain that Leavitt has been confiding to those close to her over the past two months that she is ready to step down. 'She was just trying to figure out how to politely step down with his [Trump's] blessing,' one of those sources explained. On Wednesday, the president posted on Truth Social that it was 'a decision I totally understand and respect,' calling Leavitt 'one of the best White House press secretaries in the history of the office.' Trump added that Leavitt would remain a 'top outside adviser' and an 'influential voice' in the Republican Party as the White House gears up for crunch midterm elections this November. Rarely has a press secretary wielded the level of access Leavitt has commanded, from sitting in on classified Situation Room meetings to steering crisis strategy around the Jeffrey Epstein scandal. With no clear substitute appointed even during her maternity leave, the question of Leavitt's successor remains completely up in the air. First entering Trump's orbit in 2018, Leavitt initially served as a presidential writer before taking on the role of assistant White House press secretary in the summer of 2020. She then served as press secretary for Trump's 2024 campaign and, after his reelection, took up the role of White House Press Secretary at the age of just 27. 'I know that she has enjoyed every moment about being a mom and relished the time being at home with her son and daughter,' Katie Miller, wife of Trump aide Stephen Miller, told the Daily Mail. 'Babies have a way of changing your perspective and shifting your priorities that is truly unexplainable. You only have one chance when your children are little. 'Karoline is incredibly talented. I know she is going to have other major opportunities in her future.' The Daily Mail has contacted Leavitt for comment.
