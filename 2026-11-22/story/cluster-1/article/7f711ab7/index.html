Russian cargo ship 'carrying military supplies' is hit by drones in Gulf of Finland as it drifts towards Nato waters

Article text

Russian cargo ship 'carrying military supplies' is hit by drones in Gulf of Finland as it drifts towards Nato waters A Russian cargo ship carrying military supplies caught fire after it was struck by drones in international waters in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday. The vessel was attacked on the Baltic sea, west of Russia's Gogland island, before it drifted into Finnish territorial waters, Finland's Defence Forces said, confirming an earlier account by Russian officials. Leningrad region Governor Aleksandr Drozdenko said the ship, named Sparta II, had been attacked by two drones in international waters about three nautical miles from Russia's border. The crew was quickly evacuated and the extent of the damage was being assessed by the relevant authorities, he added. Finland's defence ministry said the military had reintroduced temporary aviation and maritime restrictions in the eastern Gulf of Finland as a precaution to protect civilians and enable authorities to operate if drones needed to be intercepted. Following the attack, the Scandinavian nation's border guard said that there were no further threats to other vessels or people, rescue units were at the scene and that it was coordinating operations with neighbouring countries. In a statement on X, Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo said: 'The war is spilling over into our neighbouring areas. 'Finnish authorities are handling the situation in the Gulf of Finland, where a drone strike has hit a Russian merchant vessel. A Finnish Border Guard's handout photo of smoke rising from the Russian cargo ship Sparta II Another photo published by the border guard showing the damaged Sparta II cargo ship on fire in the Gulf of Finland Mr Orpo added that 'Ukraine has the right to fight against the aggressor'. New photographs released by the Finnish Border Guard show the Russian tanker in flames and billowing thick clouds of grey smoke from the ship's bridge. The Sparta II is subject to sanctions and linked to the Russian shipping company Oboronlogistika, according to the US Treasury Department. Oboronlogistika provides logistics services for the Russian defence ministry, the company's website said. 'At the moment, there is no confirmed information about what cargo is on board, but it cannot be ruled out that there is military materiel on board,' Finnish Border Guard Commander Ilja Iljin told Reuters. The vessel is the second Russian ship to be hit in the Gulf of Finland this month, after a warship was targeted by Ukraine on November 11. Ukraine's SBU security service earlier said it attacked the ship Naro-Fominsk as part of a broader drone operation against Russia's Ust-Luga oil export depot on the Baltic coast. In a separate incident a Russian helicopter was suspected of violating Finnish airspace on Saturday, possibly in connection with rescue or monitoring operations involving the damaged vessel, according to Finnish authorities. Estonia's Police and Border Guard Board said in a statement on Saturday it received reports that a fire had broken out aboard the Russian cargo vessel Sparta II and that preliminary information indicated there had been explosions on board. 'The situation poses no danger to other maritime traffic... Preparations for potential rescue operations are being made in cooperation with other authorities in Finland and internationally,' the Finnish Border Guard previously said in a statement. Mr Drozdenko also said 80 drones had been shot down over the Leningrad region and lifted a drone threat warning. Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson said on X his country was monitoring the situation and had offered help to its neighbour Finland, without giving details. Ukraine's foreign ministry did not immediately respond to a request for comment. Kaliningrad Governor Alexei Besprozvannykh said the incident posed no threat to supplies to the region. Gogland lies in the eastern Gulf of Finland, about 40km (25 miles) from Finland's coast. Tensions between Russia and the EU flared this week after Russian missiles and drones struck Poland on Friday, and close to where hundreds of British troops are carrying out drills. Nato warplanes were scrambled after two Kh-101 cruise missiles and a jet-powered suicide drone breached Polish airspace. One rocket slammed into a field and exploded about 47 miles from where 300 UK soldiers were operating with the so-called 'Coalition of the Willing'. A second strike hit Rudnik na Sadem, just 22 miles from the base, while the third struck an area in Molodycz, around 50 miles away. He said on Saturday: 'It's no good Putin turning up to international summits, walking up the red carpet and talking the language of peace, if in the days that follow we see heavy bombardment, particularly of civilian infrastructure. 'So I think in Europe we know exactly who we're dealing with in Putin … he is a cold-blooded killer.' Mr Streeting described the attack in Poland as an 'outrageous provocation', adding: 'It seems more than a coincidence that those airspace violations took place at the same time as the multinational force for Ukraine exercise.' He then urged there to be 'a response'. The incident came in the same week Moscow was accused on the same day of being behind an assassination attempt on a Ukrainian-American rapper in Denmark.
