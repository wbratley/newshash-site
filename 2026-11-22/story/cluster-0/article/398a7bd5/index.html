Barnham votes to leave UK: 94 per cent call to quit country in protest over plan to turn ex-RAF base into asylum seeker camp

Article text

Barnham votes to leave UK: 94 per cent call to quit country in protest over plan to turn ex-RAF base into asylum seeker camp Villagers in Suffolk have voted overwhelmingly to leave the UK in protest over plans to house almost 1,000 male asylum seekers on their doorstep. The 'Piddington-style' referendum was held after locals claimed the Government was ignoring their opposition to the plans for a giant migrant camp at RAF Barnham, Suffolk. Residents who cast their votes claimed the presence of so many men would swamp the village and endanger women and children. The result was announced just before 4.30pm with 257 villagers - 94 per cent - having voted in favour of becoming a Crown Dependency, with just 16 against the move and one spoiled ballot paper. Following the results, the Home Office said it would be 'listening carefully to the concerns being raised'. The referendum was organised by the Barnham Residents Association which was formed just four weeks ago to fight the proposed camp. It followed the poll held in September when villagers in Piddington, Oxfordshire, voted for full independence and self-determination to oppose plans to house 1,250 asylum seekers at nearby military barracks. But despite opposition from the community, Housing Secretary Angela Rayner has given the plans the green light. The referendum in Barnham differs slightly as it only asked residents if they wanted to become a Crown dependency, like Guernsey, Jersey or the isle of Man. The ballot paper suggested such a move might place its 'community outside Home Office planning control', meaning that the Government would not be able to give itself consent to open the giant camp. Helen Beck, the chairwoman of the Barnham Residents Association, admitted that the demand was 'only symbolic' and did not mean anything, other than raising the profile of the village's battle against the camp. Retired NHS executive Ms Beck, 67, said volunteers had visited more than 200 homes and issued around 380 voting slips in advance to adult residents aged over 18. Villagers were invited to cast their votes in the village hall between 10am and 4pm, but also had the option to leave their slips in sealed envelopes in advance if they could not attend in person today. Ms Beck added: 'We wanted to give people a chance to have their say, and to publicise what is proposed here.' She said the huge local opposition to the migrant camp centred around concerns about plans to house up to 931 men there, despite the village only having around 600 residents. Ms Beck said: 'There is real concern that this is a small community with an incredibly low crime rate. We don't have a police presence and can't deal with an influx of so many men, especially with so many being undocumented. Ms Beck said her association was also concerned about the effect on the local environment with the camp being in a protection area for ground-nesting birds such as stone curlews, nightjars and woodlarks. She added: 'It is also wrong that asylum seekers should be placed in former military accommodation. It is potentially traumatising for those who are genuinely fleeing war and conflict.' The association has set up a website, publicising its opposition to the camp plans, with the domain name 'barnhamnotasecret.org', which hints at the village's history of 'keeping Government secrets'. Ms Beck said: 'We were the place where troops were taught to operate the first tanks in World War One before the world knew they existed. 'The camp here also had a mustard gas store and was one of only two nuclear weapons stores in the country during the Cold War. 'The villagers always knew what was going on, but kept things secret because it was in the national interest. But these plans to house migrants here are very different and are totally opposed.' Reform UK's Home Affairs spokesperson Zia Yusuf was in the village hall to hear the referendum result announced. He said the plans for the camp had 'de-humanised local people and turned their lives upside down.' He added: 'Government after government has repeatedly failed to listen to people who don't want to be outnumbered by migrant men… The fact that Andy Burnham is doing this to the people of Barnham is outrageous.' Alison Keast, 71, who voted in support of the Crown Dependency move, said: 'The site was only ever big enough for 321 RAF personnel, so it is totally unsuitable for so many asylum seekers. 'The plans are for young single men to be housed there. They will be illegal immigrants who think the UK is a soft touch. They should be seeking asylum in the first country they get to rather than here. 'I am not anti-immigration, but it has got to be handled in the right way. My daughter sis autistic and very worried and stressed about the whole thing.' Mother-of-two Stehanie Donaldson, 31, who helps run a family car sales and dog day care business, said: 'My main concern is for safety. There has been no consultation which has caused frustration for residents. 'We just don't have the infrastructure here to deal with so many people flooding the area. This referendum is symbolic because it shows our unity, and hopefully it will make the Government think again.' Last month a £3.4million contract with construction giant Kier was signed, which could see 930 single male migrants housed at a former RAF base just three miles away in Thetford, Norfolk. The former nuclear weapons base was earmarked by the Home Office in June and it intends to make use of Urgent Crown Development, a fast-track planning route which bypasses the local council. The RAF base is one of three ex-military locations where the Home Office is proposing to house a total of 3,750 asylum seekers. Residents believe their small village, which has one primary school, no pub and no shop, is a totally unsuitable location for the facility. An encampment has been set up outside the gates with rotating protesters and placards read: 'Thetford says no!' and 'Don't trust Home Office – it's all lies'. Kim, who did not want to give her surname, has lived in Barnham for 13 years after moving from London. She said: 'There's a lot of fear and anxiety about what could happen. We moved here from north London and took a pay cut to live here, start a family and give our kids a better childhood. 'But now I feel like I've made it worse for my children as their quality of life is going to be impacted by this. 'I'm happy to leave our children playing out the front of the house as we all look after each other. 'But there could be thousands of unvetted, undocumented men at a camp down the road from us with only one way in or out. 'They have security to protect them but there won't be any security to protect us.' Charles Day, from Thetford, said: 'It's a sad time for people. I feel for the people of Barnham and Thetford. 'Why can't the Prime Minister just say no?' No planning application has been formally lodged for Barnham which is home to less than 600 people and has no shop or pub. The Home Office intends to use the 179-hectare site for at least ten years. The move comes after Prime Minister Andy Burnham said that people in more affluent areas of the UK must 'play their part' in housing asylum seekers. A Home Office spokesperson said: 'We recognise the strength of feeling in local communities and are listening carefully to the concerns being raised. 'The Government is working on a fairer distribution of asylum accommodation across the country, with over £1 billion saved in costs since the election. 'This includes closing all asylum hotels and moving to alternative accommodation, including former military sites. These are designed to be self-contained, with 24/7 security and CCTV, to reduce the impact on local communities.'
