Female chemistry teacher who went on the run will be sentenced on Monday for having sex with 14-year-old pupil up to 30 times

Female chemistry teacher who went on the run will be sentenced on Monday for having sex with 14-year-old pupil up to 30 times A female chemistry teacher who went on the run will be sentenced on Monday for having sexual relationships with two pupils at an all-boys school. Sally-Anne Bowen, 65, lured the two boys into sexual relationships when they were students at Christ's College, Finchley, in north London in the 1980s. She had sex with one of the boys 20 to 30 times in five months in the spring and summer of 1988 in a room she rented near the school. Bowen also asked the second pupil to fondle her breasts while kissing him on different occasions. Her trial heard she wore short, tight skirts and flirted in class, with one former pupil comparing her behaviour to 'a Sabrina Carpenter video'. Bowen, who was on bail, did not turn up to Harrow Crown Court on Friday morning, and a warrant was issued for her arrest. Judge Karim Ezzat was informed by prosecution counsel Neelan Gomersall that Bowen was not at court at roughly 10.30am. Then just after 11am Judge Ezzat said in open court: 'I will issue a warrant not backed for bail. I would ask that if she is arrested today she is brought to court.' When police officers arrived at Bowen's home in Hemel Hempstead, she was not there but her partner Darren Scotchbrook was at the address. Sally-Anne Bowen is facing jail for indecently assaulting two boys in the 1980s Bowen, pictured when at a younger age, lured the boys in 'like the pied piper' Her partner Darren Scotchbrook said he had sex with Bowen for seconds due to her condition A Met spokesman confirmed Bowen was tracked down in Hampshire, around 70 miles away. He said: 'A warrant was issued for her arrest and officers promptly launched enquiries to locate her. 'At around 3pm, officers from a neighbouring force found the woman at a location in Hampshire and arrested her on suspicion of failing to appear at court.' Bowen will now be held in custody until she is brought back to court for sentencing on Monday. At the time of her offending Bowen was aged 25. During her trial prosecution witnesses said she wore tight tops with short skirts and discussed sex with the boys – aged 14 to 15 – to lure them in like the 'Pied Piper'. Bowen denied the allegations and claimed that she had a medical condition which meant she could only have sex for a short time. She called Mr Scotchbrook to give evidence at her trial earlier this year in an effort to convince the jury she was telling the truth about her condition. But jurors convicted her at Harrow Crown Court of seven counts of indecent assault by a majority of ten to two after six hours and 29 minutes of deliberation. Bowen was banned from teaching by the Teaching Regulation Authority in December 2023 in relation to the abuse. At the trial, jurors were informed of the disciplinary process but were not told of the decision to bar Bowen from the classroom out of concern it would prejudice them against her. The teaching profession's disciplinary board described her as 'calculated' and motivated 'by the desire to seek her own sexual gratification'. One victim told jurors that he had sex with her 'about 20 or 30 times' over several months, regularly visited her home and considered her his girlfriend. He had fallen in love but kept their relationship secret because Bowen had “drummed it” into him that he must not tell anyone, the court heard. The victim said Bowen was able to teach for years 'because I remained silent'. In a police interview played to jurors, the victim said Bowen dressed in a 'sexually provocative' way. He said his 'introduction to the world was completely governed by Sally-Anne Bowen”. The man said he enjoyed the sex, 'games' and 'secrets' at the time, but it led to 'the destruction of my education'. He added: 'I had no respect for authority, no self-respect. My mother never forgave me.' The second victim described spending lunchtimes with Bowen in a cafe, where he was “goaded” into kissing her by other boys and recalled touching her breasts. Former pupil Tobias Oliver, who was not targeted by Bowen but remembered her flirting with other boys, was called to give evidence during the trial. He said she once sat on a desk and slowly opened her legs, revealing her underwear. Mr Oliver also recalled her discussing sexual positions from the Kama Sutra during a lesson. He said: 'She would often chew on the end of her hair or cardigan. She would brush her hand over her breasts. 'It was more like what you would see in a Sabrina Carpenter video than what you would see in a classroom. 'The topics of conversation in class often strayed away from chemistry and became quite sexual.' Bowen denied the allegations saying she couldn't have sex for long periods of time Prosecutor Madeleine Wolfe said during the trial that while the boys may have appeared willing, Bowen had 'exploited' her position as a teacher 'for her own sexual gratification'. The court heard that the first victim’s mother became suspicious and raised concerns with the school. She was later told Bowen had left, without an explanation, Ms Wolfe said. Bowen told the boy she was moving to a kibbutz in Israel, leaving him 'completely lost' and 'heartbroken', the prosecutor added. Former head of science Richard Luker recalled to the court the mother’s concern that her son planned to travel abroad with Bowen. The victim reported the allegations to police in 2014. Bowen denied the allegations as 'total fabrication' and claimed a medical condition made sex difficult and painful. She denied flirting, saying: 'I didn’t flirt with students, I was just friendly.' Defending her clothes, she said she 'wasn’t going to dress like Margaret Thatcher'. The court heard prior to yesterday's hearing that there had been communication between Bowen and the police which was 'suggestive of refusal rather than inability' to attend. Bowen’s neighbours said on Friday that they last saw the former teacher walking her black Labrador in the days prior to Friday's hearing, which she failed to attend. Defence barrister Mark Kimsey said she had been in contact with solicitors and had engaged with a pre-sentence report, adding there had been 'concerns as to her welfare due to the content of the messages' from her. After being convicted Bowen's bail conditions did not require her to hand over her travel documents to the police. One neighbour, whose children attended The Cavendish School, a secondary comprehensive in nearby Hemel Hempstead while Bowen taught there as a supply teacher for a year or two, said: ‘When I saw her walking she seemed nervous. There was something about the way she walked. ‘She walked with her head down and was quite quiet, but she was very nice to speak to when we did chat.’ Bowen lived in a secluded row of houses about a ten-minute drive outside of the small town in Hertfordshire. Over the two decades she lived there she tended to her front garden, which was immaculately kept with a low sitting bench and a flowerbed edged around a small grass lawn. ‘There was always something a bit strange about her, but in recent years she became more reserved,’ another neighbour added. ‘I used to see her walk her black Labrador twice a day. She would walk past my front window while I was watching TV. ‘We both had our hips fixed at roughly the same time so I knew her enough to say hello to, and she would ask me how I am getting on. But there was always something about her.' Neighbours confirmed seeing a police car with two uniformed police officers arriving at her house at roughly 11.30am after a warrant was issued for her arrest. ‘I read about this story online, but I did not connect the woman pictured going into court looking so glamorous with the woman at the end of the street,’ one of her neighbours added. 'She always used to walk around wrapped up because of the weather and for want of a better word she looked "homely". ‘It is strange to think that she lived so close to me. When I spoke to her, she was very reserved, but I had no idea about the case. I thought that was just her personality.'
