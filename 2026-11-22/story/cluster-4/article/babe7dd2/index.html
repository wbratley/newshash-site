Spitting Image co-creator Peter Fluck dies aged 85

Article text

Spitting Image co-creator Peter Fluck dies aged 85 - Published Peter Fluck, co-creator of award-winning satirical puppet show Spitting Image, has died aged 85. His family said he died in the early hours of Saturday at home in Cornwall, following a recent illness. Spitting Image was immensely popular and sometimes controversial, running from 1984-1996 across 18 seasons, and featured puppet caricatures of celebrities, politicians and royals, including former Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher and Queen Elizabeth the Queen Mother. "The spirit that Spitting Image brought to the country was epoch defining," a family statement said. "With Roger Law ('Luck and Flaw'), he brought a unique cutting edge to caricaturing that later evolved into a production company that created the TV series Spitting Image," it added. "Peter's creative life continued after the series for many years from his studio in Cornwall. He was a private man who eschewed fame and attention." The show won several awards, including one Bafta TV award and two Emmys and at its peak drew an audience of 15 million. Fluck was born in Cambridge in 1941, and created the show alongside childhood friend Roger Law, who he met while studying at Cambridge School of Art. Following school, both Fluck and Law moved to London and set up as freelance illustrators, and in 1976 set up their partnership 'Luck and Flaw' back in Cambridge. "You could say we took the traditional newspaper editorial cartoon and put it on the screen," Fluck told ArtCornwall in an interview, external in 2006. He described the show as being shown "at the difficult time of 10 o'clock on a Sunday night when all politicians should have been in bed". Many of the characters included parodies of Thatcher's cabinet, with Douglas Hurd depicted with "Mr Whippy ice cream" hair, and her successor John Major caricatured as a dull, grey puppet with a penchant for peas. "We did get quite a lot of approaches from politicians for their puppet to be made, because if you didn't appear as a puppet on Spitting Image you hadn't made it as a politician," Fluck said in an interview in 2011. , external It was eventually axed by ITV because of declining audience numbers. In a statement, the broadcaster said Fluck's "creative contribution to TV satire is simply unrivalled... his cutting edge puppetry and caricature skills, alongside the show's sharp and always shrewdly observed humour, became a defining part of the cultural zeitgeist. "Our thoughts and deepest sympathies are with his family and friends at this very sad time." When he stepped away from the show, Fluck moved to Cornwall and focused his art on ceramics, printmaking and mobiles. In 2018, Law donated his entire archive of Spitting Images to Cambridge University.
