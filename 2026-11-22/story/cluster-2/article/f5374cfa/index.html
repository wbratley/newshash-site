BBC forced to apologise as 'police called' to Strictly Come Dancing ballroom

Article text

BBC forced to apologise as 'police called' to Strictly Come Dancing ballroom The police are investigating after an incident during the show which left host Emma Willis saying 'sorry for any offence caused' The police were reportedly called to the Strictly Come Dancing ballroom to investigate after chaotic scenes during Saturday's live show from the historic Blackpool Tower. Viewers complained of hearing offensive 'sex noises' during the broadcast of the annual TV special from the tower ballroom, as unusual noises interrupted the programme. The noises came as the judges gave feedback to the first couple of eight to dance on Saturday night, singer Delta Goodrem and her partner Kai Widdrington. Infamous online prankster Daniel Jarvis, also known as Jarvo69, was livestreaming during the show and suggested during his broadcast that he knew something unexpected was going to happen. Jarvis appeared to think judge Craig Revel Horwood was going to be the target of a “naughty noises” prank. When Revel Horwood appeared on screen, Jarvis pointed at the judge and said: “This is for all the people you keep bullying. “Don’t bully people.” Jarvis then appeared to make a phone call which he expected to trigger a “sex noise” in the ballroom. The prank, however, did not go as planned, with noise playing out while judge Motsi Mabuse gave feedback. Mabuse stopped, looked around red-faced, and said: “It’s not me.” During his livestream, Jarvis complained about a delay and told his viewers: “Sorry, I was trying to get the other one.” He tried to ring the phone again during the episode, but the call did not go through. It is understood that police officers were onsite at the Strictly ballroom and the source of the disruption was quickly identified. At the end of the episode, presenter Emma Willis issued an on-air apology. She said: “Before we see the judges’ leaderboard, we’d like to apologise if you heard any audio disruptions earlier in tonight’s show and for any offence caused.” An edit will be made to the programme available on BBC iPlayer. A BBC spokesperson said: “We apologise to any viewers offended during the live broadcast of Strictly this evening. “We are investigating how this happened.” A spokesperson for Lancashire Police said: “We are aware of an incident that took place during the filming of Strictly Come Dancing in Blackpool. “We have been in contact with the event organisers, and a formal complaint has been made to us. “A mobile phone has been seized and we are now looking into the matter to see if any criminal offences have taken place.” Taking to X, dozens of people claimed they heard the noises. Another said on the social media platform: "Someone's playing a clip of sex noises in the Strictly Blackpool Tower #Strictly" This week marked the return of pro dancer Kai.
