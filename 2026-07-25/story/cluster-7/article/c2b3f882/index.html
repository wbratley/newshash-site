'Once again the system bends over backwards for a child killer': Fury of victims' families as Southport killer 'manipulates' his way out of prison and into Broadmoor

Article text

'Once again the system bends over backwards for a child killer': Fury of victims' families as Southport killer 'manipulates' his way out of prison and into Broadmoor - See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source The families of three girls murdered by Axel Rudakubana have accused the Southport killer of trying to 'manipulate the system' after he was transferred to Broadmoor. The 19-year-old was moved to the secure psychiatric hospital from Belmarsh prison earlier this week after it was decided that he could not be managed safely in a prison environment. Lawyer Chris Walker, of Bond Turner, who is representing the Southport families, said: 'The three families we represent have been made aware of this development in the last 48 hours and it is safe to say that whilst this news would devastate them at any time, the fact that this has occurred as we approach the second anniversary in our view is another move by AR who would appear to be attempting, once again, to manipulate the system. 'We have been given the barest of details and whilst we are supporting the families through this we are urgently seeking a meeting with the appropriate authorities to understand the situation before commenting further.' Despite the transfer, the Ministry of Justice (MOJ) insisted the 19-year-old will remain behind locked doors for the rest of his custodial sentence and is 'unlikely to ever be released'. Rudakubana, from Lancashire, was jailed for life in January 2025 and ordered to serve a minimum of 52 years after admitting the murders of Elsie Dot Elsie, seven, Bebe King, six, and Alice da Silva Aguiar, nine. The teenager also attempted to murder eight other children, dance instructor Leanne Lucas and business leader John Hayes, who tried to disarm him during the Taylor Swift-themed dance class in Southport on July 29, 2024. In addition to the murders and attempted murders, he was convicted of producing the biological toxin ricin and possessing an al-Qaida training manual, an offence under the Terrorism Act. Axel Rudakubana was jailed for life and ordered to serve a minimum of 52 years, at Liverpool Crown Court in January last year (L-R) Bebe King, six, Elsie Dot Stancombe, seven, and Alice da Silva Aguiar, nine, were all murdered in the atrocity on July 29, 2024 Mark Fairhurst, chair of the Prison Officers' Association, said Rudakubana had 'played the system' by securing a move to Broadmoor. 'This vile offender has played the system and will now enjoy the freedoms that are afforded mentally unwell patients in secure psychiatric hospitals, including benefits that law abiding tax payers will fund,' he said. 'My members will be placed at risk without the protections afforded to staff in prisons because he is now classed as a patient. He should spend his entire sentence in a prison in solitary confinement. 'My thoughts are with the families of the victims and all those affected by his heinous crimes.' Emma Webber, whose son Barnaby was one of three people killed by mentally ill Valdo Calocane in the 2023 Nottingham stabbings, told the Mail: 'Once again the system bends over backwards for a child-killer who has already shown he will attack prison staff. 'Axel Rudakubana has manipulated his way into a secure hospital, and the statements from the victims’ families and the Prison Officers Association leave no room for doubt: this is an insult to the dead and a betrayal of the living.’ Mr Webber was just 19 when he was stabbed to death as he walked home with his friend, fellow first-year student Grace O’Malley-Kumar, in Nottingham three years ago. Calocane then ambushed and killed school caretaker Ian Coates, and used his van to drive into pedestrians. Valdo Calocane, who carried out the Nottingham attacks in 2023, is currently serving a hospital order Calocane had been sectioned multiple times and was in fact wanted for assaulting a police officer at the time he struck but was repeatedly freed into the community. It was announced this week that police are investigating Nottinghamshire Healthcare NHS Foundation Trust for potential corporate manslaughter over their handling of the case. Calocane was handed an indefinite hospital order having pleaded guilty to manslaughter on the grounds of diminished responsibility – a decision which infuriated his victims’ families, who wanted prosecutors to push for a murder conviction. An inquiry this year heard how he repeatedly duped medical staff overseeing his care. Mrs Webber said today: 'My son Barney was slaughtered by a man the authorities already knew was dangerous, the allowed fear of stigma and incompetence to overrule public safety. 'Now we watch the same soft, broken system reward another monster with better conditions. Enough. Lock them up, keep them locked up, and stop treating public safety as optional. 'I am absolutely disgusted.' Many will question why Rudakubana is being sent to Broadmoor despite having been deemed fit to stand trial in January 2025. Ian Coates, Barnaby Webber and Grace O'Malley-Kumar were killed by Valdo Calocane in June 2023 The move will also significantly increase the cost to taxpayers. A place in a high-security psychiatric hospital costs more than £300,000 a year, compared with around £60,000 to keep an inmate in prison. Sally Jameson, Parliamentary Secretary in the Cabinet Office, insisted Rudakubana would be returned back to prison 'as soon as possible'. 'It's not right, and I sincerely hope that he won't be qualifying for benefits,' she told Nick Ferrari on LBC. 'But what I think is important to note is he will not get time off his sentence, and he will be returned to jail as soon as possible. And also, the high security hospitals, they're not like ordinary hospitals. 'They are a prison hospital. He will be kept under very stringent conditions befitting actually to his crime, but also the level of security that needs to be put around him. 'And I am absolutely confident that he will be returned to prison as soon as possible and serve out his sentence, and I agree that should be under the most punitive and solitary conditions that is possible.' People held in secure psychiatric hospitals are treated as patients rather than prisoners, although they remain detained under strict security measures. However, the MOJ stressed that Broadmoor should not be viewed as a softer option. A spokesman said: 'The families of those murdered and injured in Southport will be in this country's heart forever and our thoughts remain with them. 'The offender who carried out these vile crimes will rightly spend the entirety of his 52-year custodial sentence locked up, and is unlikely to ever be released. 'High secure hospitals are not places of freedom. He will remain under constant supervision in a high-security psychiatric unit, and be returned to prison as soon as possible. 'The transfer of an offender follows a robust independent clinical assessment.' The Department for Work and Pensions would not comment on the specific case but said: “People moved from prison to a hospital setting are not entitled to claim benefits.” Separate legislation is being finalised to remove benefit entitlements from convicted killers and other serious offenders sentenced to hospital instead of prison. Rudakubana attacked a prison officer at HMP Belmarsh in May 2025, triggering an internal investigation. His supervision was reportedly downgraded just before he used the kettle in his cell to boil water which he then launched over the officer. Details disclosed after the killings revealed Rudakubana had an extensive history of contact with mental health services. Alder Hey Children's NHS Foundation Trust confirmed he was under its care between 2019 and 2023 before he 'stopped engaging'. He had first been referred after taking a knife to school and later admitting he had done so 'to use it'. Rudakubana was also referred to the Government's Prevent counter-extremism programme after researching school massacres on a computer while attending his next school, The Acorns. Two further referrals followed, although none resulted in intervention because investigators concluded there was no clear ideological motivation behind his fixation with extreme violence. The inquiry into the Southport murders also heard there had been a 'wholesale and general failure' to stop Rudakubana in the lead-up to his attack on a Taylor Swift-themed dance club. Inquiry chair Sir Adrian Fulford cited factors including Rudakubana's ability to access violent material and buy knives online at a young age, as well as his capacity to leave home unsupervised to commit the massacre. In his remarks, Sir Adrian said that far from being 'an unforeseeable catastrophic event', Rudakubana had a 'known predilection for knife crime' and posed a 'very serious and significant risk of violent harm'. He was referred to Prevent on three occasions, on December 5, 2019, February 1, 2021 and April 26, 2021, after being spotted researching school shootings, the Libyan military dictator Colonel Gaddafi and the London Bridge terror attack online.
