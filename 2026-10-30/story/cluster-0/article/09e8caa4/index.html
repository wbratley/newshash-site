He performed in playhouses across England before making his London theatrical debut in 1966 and touring with the Old Vic company, one of the most selective drama schools in the world. He then joined the prestigious Royal Shakespeare Company in 1966, where he made his first Broadway appearance as Snout in Shakespeare’s A Midsummer Night’s Dream in 1971 and began his 27-year association with the establishment. Stewart’s love for Shakespeare was born after he was given The Merchant of Venice by his English teacher Cecil Dormand. He told The Guardian in 2021: ‘I barely understood a word, but I loved the feel of the words and sounds in my mouth. A 400-year-old writer reached out a hand in invitation to me [and] I felt a sense of an internal, private me being released and connecting with something mysterious, alien and exciting. I was hooked.’ Stewart was extremely grateful to Dormand for ‘saving’ him from his ‘dull, uncomfortable and sometimes scary home life, living with an abusive father,’ by putting Shakespeare into his hands and giving him hope for a better future. Stewart remained thankful to Dormand throughout his life, often wondering what his life would have been like if he had not crossed paths with the English teacher. Stewart continued as a stage actor for the first 25 years of his career, despite landing a few small roles in British and American films from 1973. He once said: ‘All I know is that I have to act. It’s a compulsion. I’m driven to it. I wouldn’t say that I would die if it were taken away from me, but a large part of me would shrivel up.’ International recognition came in 1987 when he secured the role of Captain Jean-Luc Picard in the science-fiction series Star Trek: The Next Generation (1987). He was so convinced that he was going to be fired that he did not unpack his bags for six weeks, saying he didn’t think he would ‘survive beyond the pilot’. He said: ‘I thought the producers would come to their senses and realize they’d made a grave error in casting me. Eventually it became clear to me that not only wasn’t I going to go away, the series wasn’t going to go away. I stayed and have relished every moment.’ The role brought Stewart an exceptional level of fame with his refined voice and natural gravitas making him a household name and winning him a place at the heart of The Next Generation series. He went on to reprise his role as Capt Jean-Luc Picard and starred in a string of successful Star Trek films: Star Trek: Generations (1994), Star Trek: First Contact (1996), Star Trek: Insurrection (1998) and Star Trek: Nemesis (2002). In 2008, he took on the role of Claudius in Hamlet (Above) and received his third Laurence Olivier Award Stewart was knighted in 2010 by Queen Elizabeth II for services to drama A proud Yorkshireman and lifelong supporter of Huddersfield Town Football Club, Stewart was born in Mirfield, West Yorkshire, on July 13, 1940, to textile worker Gladys Barrowclough and army personnel Alfred Stewart. Stewart struggled at school and said he spent much of his childhood being ‘afraid’, with a father who was often violent and abusive. Stewart appeared in other films and stage productions including Masterminds, an episode of The Simpsons, and A Christmas Carol, as well as being considered for a role in Doctor Who: The Movie, and the part of Willy Wonka in Charlie and the Chocolate Factory (2005), which went to Johnny Depp. Stewart also used his perfect British diction to comedic effect in cartoon TV shows and lent his voice to several animated movies – Gnomeo and Juliet (2011), Ice Age: Continental Drift (2012), Legends of Oz: Dorothy’s Return (2013) and The Emoji Movie (2017). Despite Stewart’s many on-screen successes, he never stopped performing Shakespeare on stage. His masterful performance of the title role in Shakespeare’s Macbeth got him a Tony Award nomination. In 2008, he took on the role of Claudius in Hamlet and received his third Laurence Olivier Award for his performance, and in 2010 he was awarded a knighthood. On receiving his knighthood, Stewart called it an ‘honor that embraces those actors, directors and creative teams who have in these recent years helped fill my life with inspiration, companionship and sheer fun’.
