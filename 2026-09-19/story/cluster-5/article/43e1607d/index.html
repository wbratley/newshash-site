The rapper was due to open for Sheeran at eight of the remaining ten shows on his tour in America, which continues on Friday in Philadelphia, but on Tuesday it was announced that he had been dropped. The American rapper had faced a boycott petition to remove him after he delivered a politically motivated speech in support of Palestinians in Gaza and the West Bank during his performances at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey on September 4 and 5. Sheeran has now lost his remaining support acts – Billie Eilish's brother and music partner Finneas, his close friend Myles Smith, Aaron Rowe, Lukas Graham and Beoga – who have all put out separate statements announcing that they will not continue with their dates on the Loop Tour. Responding with an Instagram statement after the news he had been dropped, Macklemore claimed that Sheeran told him billionaire Robert Kraft, whose company owns the Gillette Stadium in Massachusetts where Sheeran is set to play later this month, orchestrated the move after 'rallying stadium owners and issuing an ultimatum.' Later that day, Sheeran, 35, addressed the 43-year-old rapper being removed from the tour with his own statement, which said that Macklemore's speech had upset the owners of venues they were set to play while reminding fans he will never personally take a publicly political stance. Finneas wrote: ‘Artists must not be silenced when they speak up for the oppressed. I have decided to withdraw from my upcoming tour dates with Ed Sheeran. I stand with Palestine and its people.' And Irish singer Aaron Rowe took a strong stance as he wrote: 'Ed has been a friend to me and has changed my life. I could never thank him enough for this. ‘But as Irish people, we know all too well about genocide, forced famine and violent occupation.’ The Hey Ma hitmaker continued by saying he 'cannot stand by and allow billionaires to use their position of power to silence the rightful voices of those who speak up.' The third support act who pulled out was the group Lukas Graham who wrote: 'We should be able to speak about war, about civilians being killed, about children who deserve to grow up. Wherever they live. Whatever flag flies over them.' It remains uncertain how the Loop Tour will proceed with zero openers scheduled and 18 dates still set through the end of the year. Sheeran assured his followers that Macklemore's removal would not affect his tour, as he told them 'I would never let down the fans who had made plans'. In his reaction, which came two days after Macklemore's own statement, he said: 'I am appalled by the conflict between Israel and Palestine. 'The suffering and pain caused on both sides is a human tragedy. There are too many wars across the world causing immeasurable pain, none of which should be tolerated. Kraft has defended his decision to block Macklemore from performing, saying the rapper 'crossed a line.' 'Gillette Stadium has a longstanding commitment to providing a welcoming environment for all guests and ensuring that events held at our venue do not provide a platform for hate speech,' Kraft said in a statement. 'Based on Macklemore’s recent actions, material shared from the stage during Ed Sheeran’s shows in New Jersey, and a broader history of antisemitic rhetoric and imagery that we believe has been deeply offensive and hurtful to the Jewish community, we determined that his participation in the September 25 and 26 concerts at Gillette Stadium would cross that line.' The promoter for the American side of Sheeran’s Loop Tour, Messina Touring Group, said in a statement that they were told by venues of future dates on the tour that 'they will not allow a concert to take place with Macklemore on the lineup.' 'Which would result in the cancellation of the tour and impact hundreds of thousands of fans,” a spokesperson continued to Rolling Stone. 'After discussions with stakeholders, Macklemore will not be performing on the remaining support dates.' As seen in fan-taken footage from his September 5 performance in New Jersey, Macklemore wore a keffiyeh at one point during his set and also performed his 2024 song Hind's Hall with footage of Gaza on the massive screen behind him. In the lyrics for Hind's Hall, the rapper criticises actions in Gaza. Proceeds from the song go toward humanitarian efforts from the United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East. 'One of the reasons why I wanted to come on this tour was because I wanted to stand up here on stages in stadiums across America and say two words that are very near and dear to my heart: Free Palestine. I said, Free Palestine!' he told the attendees. 'I want those words to be loud and clear so that people from Gaza, all the way to the occupied West Bank know that we have not forgotten them.' He then introduced Hind's Hall, stating that it 'was inspired by the bravery of Columbia University students who led an encampment movement that mobilized the entire world. Opened up hearts, myself included.' 'So thank you to those students that risked their diplomas, that risked their tuition, that risked their scholarships, that stood up and said, "No." CUAD students, Students for Justice in Palestine, and Jewish Voice for Peace. Thank you guys.' He addressed the Jewish community, stating: 'To all of my Jewish brothers and sisters: Criticism of Israel, criticism of apartheid, being against genocide in no way is a criticism of you.' 'My message is for peace. Love. For all human beings to be treated with dignity, respect, and equality. So I say free Palestine. I say free Lebanon. I say free Cuba. I say free Congo, free Sudan, free all people in America living in fear over this terrorist organization ICE. None of us will be free until we are all free.' Macklemore went on to post a video of his speech on social media with the caption: 'Wanting all humans to be treated equal should never be controversial.' In 2014, Macklemore issued an apology after wearing a disguise on stage in Seattle that many condemned as an anti-Semitic Jewish caricature. In his apology after the Jewish caricature controversy, he said: 'There is no worse feeling than being misunderstood, especially when people are hurt or offended... I respect all cultures and all people. 'I would never intentionally put down anybody for the fabric that makes them who they are. I love human beings, love originality, and... happen to love a weird outfit from time to time.' Macklemore added that he never thought he looked Jewish. 'Some people there thought I looked like Ringo, some Abe Lincoln. If anything, I thought I looked like Humpty Hump with a bowl cut,' he said.
