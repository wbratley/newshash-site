UK Salmonella Outbreak Linked to Imported Eggs Kills One, Sickens 200

Unbiased summary

The UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) has declared a national salmonella outbreak after 207 people across the UK were infected, with one death and 34 hospitalisations. The outbreak has been linked to imported eggs, particularly those used in cafes and restaurants. The Food Standards Agency (FSA) has advised vulnerable groups—children under five, adults over 65, pregnant women, and those with health conditions—to avoid runny or undercooked eggs when eating out, as well as products containing raw eggs like mayonnaise and hollandaise sauce. FSA officials emphasised that well-cooked eggs are safe, and British-produced eggs from supermarkets are not linked to the outbreak. Early investigations suggest the source is imported eggs, with genetic testing indicating a common origin. The FSA has described the outbreak as 'unusually large and rapidly increasing.'
