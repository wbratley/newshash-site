Kitchen porter who murdered Microsoft executive near private members' club on the Thames while serving suspended sentence for attacking another woman with a wine bottle is jailed for 22 years A kitchen porter who murdered a Microsoft executive near a private members' club on the River Thames has been jailed for more than 22 years. Nelio Gouveia, 45, appeared at Oxford Crown Court on Friday afternoon where he was sentenced to life imprisonment for the murder of Jennifer Symonds, 44, in Henley-on-Thames, Oxfordshire, on March 6. Portuguese-born Gouveia murdered Ms Symonds who was described as a 'selfless member of the local community' while serving a suspended prison sentence for attacking another woman with a wine bottle, the court heard. Her body was discovered at 7.50am in the grounds of the Phyllis Court private members' club located at the finish line of the Henley Royal Regatta, where Gouveia worked as a kitchen porter. Thames Valley Police launched a murder investigation after officers were called to Phyllis Court Drive, next to the club, later discovering that the two knew each other. The court heard that a fellow employee at the club reported that Gouveia had previously said: 'If girls are annoying and aren't behaving, you just hit them.' Gouveia and Ms Symonds had agreed to meet in Henley on March 5 following a conversation about 'smoking cannabis and drinking alcohol'. Together they visited pubs and consumed alcohol, cannabis and cocaine during the evening. Nelio Gouveia, 45, appeared at Oxford Crown Court on Friday where he was sentenced to 22 years and six months years for murder Jennifer Symonds, 44, was found dead in the water in Henley-on-Thames on Friday, March 6 They then went to Gouveia's room at the private member's club where a member heard a man shouting 'I love you, I love you, I love you' at around 1.30am. The member said he also heard a woman shouting and screaming who 'sounded like she was deeply upset about something', the court heard. After leaving Ms Symonds at the club pavilion, Gouveia visited a pornographic website before retrieving a metal weights bar from his room and tucking it into his trousers. Upon his return he approached Ms Symonds who was waiting for him atop a flight of stairs on the riverside and hit her over the head three or four times with the metal bar. During the attack, the pair fell against a moored boat on the riverside before falling into the water. Prosecutor Neil Moore said Gouveia made an effort to 'deliberately drown her' and used his hands to submerge her head. At one point the killer had his hands on the river bank while 'using his feet to keep her down' during the 12 minutes in water. Ms Symonds's body was subsequently found in the River Thames by police with no clothing on her top half. Police searching the River Thames in Henley-on-Thames, south Oxfordshire in March this year Ms Symonds was drowned by Gouveia in the River Thames after he attacked her with a metal weights bar Gouveia, who frequently searched for escorts and pornography on the internet, was arrested on the day Ms Symonds's body was found. He pleaded guilty to murder at Oxford Crown Court on October 29 where he was remanded into custody. The court heard the murder was caught on CCTV footage, which was not shown, as Mr Justice Jay described it as 'deeply disturbing and upsetting'. The prosecutor said similarly to Gouveia's previous conviction, the murder involved a 'completely unannounced attack from behind to the head on a woman with a weapon'. The prosecutor said: 'Jennifer Symonds fought desperately to cling on to her life. The more she fought, the more determined the defendant became to ensure she was kept under the water. 'He was not going to leave that jetty until he was sure she was dead.' Sentencing Gouveia, Mr Justice Jay said: 'My conclusion is your actions stemmed from your propensity in certain circumstances to commit acts of violence towards women fuelled on this occasion by your excessive use of drugs and alcohol.' He added that Gouveia was fuelled by his 'appetite for violent pornography and some of the websites you visited that night'. The judge said the murder was an 'exercise of power and desire to dominate' and Gouveia's removal of half of Ms Symonds's clothing was an additional act of 'humiliation and degradation'. Prosecutors said his motivation was likely Ms Symonds having rejected a sexual advance from him, with the judge saying this theory 'may be right but is not established to the criminal standard'. The judge said Ms Symonds was a 'much-loved daughter, sister and aunt with close friendships and close ties to the community in which she lived', adding her family described her 'warmth, generosity and determination'. Ms Symonds, who was born in Zambia where her parents were working and moved to the UK aged three, studied at the University of Gloucestershire and worked for Microsoft before she was made redundant last year. After murdering Ms Symonds, Gouveia took a taxi to Heathrow Airport with the intention of fleeing to Latvia and was seen wandering around the airport's Terminal 5 before attempting to stay in a hotel. But instead of leaving the country, Gouveia returned to Henley and went to a police station, where he told officers: 'I think I hurt someone. I think I may have killed someone and I need to speak to someone quickly.' Mr Moore said this was a 'reluctant and necessary return to Henley because he couldn't fly out of the country' and 'when he got there he tried to pretend he couldn't remember most of it'. The court heard Gouveia told police 'I tried to hold her, I pushed her up', with Mr Moore saying he continued to maintain he was trying to help Ms Symonds in the water while mentioning his consumption of cocaine and alcohol. Mr Moore added: 'The defendant was trying to minimise his culpability that through drink and drugs he couldn't really remember what happened – an obvious and serious lie.' Ms Symonds' brother, Andrew Symonds, told the court she was 'deeply missed every day', adding she was a 'kind, caring person who touched the lives of so many people'. Her father Peter Symonds said in a victim impact statement that the trauma was 'devastating' and he was 'haunted by what she must have gone through in her final moments of life'. During the statement, he also asked the judge to 'please protect other women for as long as possible'. Wearing a grey prison-issue tracksuit and holding rosary beads, Gouveia's head was bowed as he was sentenced to life imprisonment with a minimum term of 22 years and six months. Detective Inspector Rob Underhill, of Thames Valley Police said: 'Following our investigation, Nelio Gouveia has been jailed for murdering Jennifer earlier this year. 'Our thoughts remain with the family and friends during this incredibly difficult time; we have offered them our support but please respect their privacy.'
