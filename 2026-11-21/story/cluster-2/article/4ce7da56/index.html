Anti-migrant activist Danny Tommo sobs as he's jailed for 15 months for slashing small boat in Channel - as judge brands him a 'publicity seeker'

Article text

Anti-migrant activist Danny Tommo sobs as he's jailed for 15 months for slashing small boat in Channel - as judge brands him a 'publicity seeker' Anti-migrant campaigner Danny Tommo broke down in tears as he was jailed for slashing an inflatable boat in the middle of the Channel. The privately educated activist, whose real name is Daniel Thomas, filmed himself taking a boat out towards migrants in a dinghy being assisted by the RNLI, Border Force and the French Coastguard in September. Thomas filmed the crime so it could be 'celebrated' by his audience, and shouted 'this is England' as he damaged the boat which had a French rescue worker on at the time. He admitted criminal damage at a previous hearing and was jailed for 15 months today. Thomas held his head in his hands as Judge James Newton-Price KC described him as 'publicity seeker'. The judge said the offence 'amounted to flagrant interference and a deliberate disruption of the legitimate activities of the Border Force, Coastguard and RNLI volunteers'. He said: 'All of those organisations perform essential roles. They do necessary, highly sensitive and difficult jobs, and which should not be disrupted by a publicity-seeker slashing with a knife a boat they were attempting to recover.' Outlining the case at Portsmouth Crown Court this afternoon, prosecutor Joel Smith KC described 37-year-old Thomas as a founder and leader of 'Patriot Platform', an anti-migrant movement which publicly promotes 'taking our country back' and 'reclaiming our borders'. Daniel Thomas is seen using a knife to slash a migrant dinghy in the English Channel Thomas has been jailed for 15 months after pleading guilty to criminal damage It is a movement which is heavily reliant on social media, he said, with its members often wearing matching outfits. The prosecutor said: 'At the time of this offending, the defendant was on bail awaiting trial in Kent for offences of aggravated trespass, public order offences and maritime offences committed in Dover. 'In March of this year, the defendant is alleged to have entered a migrant processing centre by scaling a perimeter fence and, as he was escorted from the premises, he became aggressive. 'A month later he was found on a disused pier filming the same processing centre and as officers approached, he threatened to jump into the sea, resulting in a two-hour stand-off. 'He also repeatedly shouted comments concerning migrants in the centre, likening them to terrorism, and shouting at life boat volunteers. 'Later in the same month, it is alleged that the defendant entered the western docks in Dover, climbing a fence and interfering with the activities of Border Force and security personnel, this taking place after he had previously been personally directed by the harbourmaster not to return to the port.' Describing the criminal damage offence on September 22, Mr Smith said: 'The defendant and others were in the English Channel on a boat, a rigid hulled inflatable boat, or RHIB. 'The defendant was filming and live streaming another RHIB carrying migrants across the channel. Thomas wipes his eyes during a hearing at Basingstoke magistrates' court in September 'The occupants of the boat were being assisted by the RNLI, coastguard, the Border Force and French lifeboat. 'On his livestream, the defendant can be heard to broadcast the postcode of the place to which the migrants were to be taken, seemingly to encourage protest. 'There is no suggestion that he was encouraging violence, indeed he seems to be discouraging this. 'The migrants who had been on the RHIB were brought on to RNLI lifeboats. 'This having been done, the defendant asked another person on his boat to approach the RHIB, saying 'let's get rid of that boat'. 'The defendant was shouting about migration and said, amongst other things, 'This is what needs to happen to every one of these boats' and 'You're not using these boats, no more migrants in this country.' 'The defendant thought that he was in English waters. 'As the defendant's boat approached the RHIB, a French rescue worker stepped onto the RHIB. 'The defendant told him that he was in English waters. He then slashed the RHIB with a knife, causing a hole to the side of the RHIB and a hissing sound. 'He then shouted 'Get off the boat, you've got time to get off the boat' and 'This is not France, take them back, no more of this, we pay you too much money, don't take that back to France, traitors.'' The court heard Thomas uploaded the footage online. A seven-minute clip played to court showed Thomas in a boat on video following a migrant dinghy, with two rescue boats beside it. In it, Thomas told his viewers to 'please share this everywhere' and 'like, share it'. Prosecutor Mr Smith said Thomas had a raft of previous convictions. He said: 'On February 2, 2006 he was sentenced to a conditional discharge for using threatening, abusive or insulting words and behaviour, with intent to cause fear or provocation of violence. 'On August 2, 2007 he was sentenced to a conditional discharge for using disorderly behaviour, or threatening, abusive or insulting words and behaviour, likely to cause harassment, alarm or distress. 'On May 16, 2011 he was fined for being carried in a stolen vehicle. 'On October 7, 2013 he was conditionally discharged for a fraud matter. 'On June 17, 2016 he was sentenced to two years' imprisonment for kidnapping.' Defence barrister Simon Pentol KC described what happened as 'spur of the moment'. He said: 'He wished to cause no harm to any person. 'This was nothing more than performative activism on matters he feels very strongly about.' Thomas previously worked as a bodyguard for high-profile anti-migrant figure Tommy Robinson. He said he had become a Christian and began attending church every week to 'pray for forgiveness'. His biography on X says he is 'dedicated to defending my family and homeland', and 'Trusting in Jesus Christ, Our Saviour'. Thomas has previously organised a Ukip rally and a 'Brexit Betrayal' march, as well as becoming a key part of the Raise the Colours anti-migrant group based in Birmingham, which played a role in England and Union flags being put up across the country, known as 'Operation Overlord'. After parting company with Raise the Colours, Thomas continued to use the phrase 'Operation Overlord' for his 'stop the boats' protests.
