White supremacist who wanted to 'kill as many migrants as possible' found guilty of plotting to burn down hotel housing asylum seekers

Article text

White supremacist who wanted to 'kill as many migrants as possible' found guilty of plotting to burn down hotel housing asylum seekers A neo-Nazi has been found guilty of plotting an attack on a hotel housing asylum seekers to ‘kill as many migrants as possible.’ White supremacist Mikka Lee Anderson, 24, stockpiled petrol bombs and researched how to make napalm ahead of the planned assault on the historic Royal Hotel in Hull city centre, where asylum seekers were housed by the Home Office as their claims were processed. His deadly plan was only foiled when he tried to recruit an accomplice who he believed shared his ‘far-Right’ ideology. But the man, who cannot be named for legal reasons, was so appalled by Anderson’s murderous plan that he went to the police. Anderson, who was said to have hidden his extremist ideology ‘behind a veil of normality’, was arrested as he arrived for work at a Wren Kitchens factory in February. Following a trial at Newcastle Crown Court, he was found guilty today of preparing acts of terrorism. He also convicted of possession of knives and other weapons for the purposes of terrorism. The trial heard that Anderson admired Adolf Hitler, believed Muslims were going to ‘destroy the white population’ and non-white people were a ‘societal pest which needed to be dealt with.’ He and two other ‘white nationalists’ purchased land in Devon to act as a training ground for a race war against ‘invaders’ – however he later claimed they planned to use it for ‘glamping.’ Mikka Anderson, 24, was found guilty of preparing acts of terrorism Anderson had a hatred of Muslims and regularly discussed using violence against those from ethnic minorities At the flat he shared with his fiancée and young child near Hull city centre, he stockpiled at least 20 knives, crossbows, camouflage clothing and a gas mask. He also had a copy of the Anarchist’s Cookbook which helped him make Molotov cocktails, napalm and smoke bombs, jurors were told. Images recovered by police following the raid on his property showed him making Nazi salutes while his phone also contained videos he had filmed when conducting ‘reconnaissance’ on the Royal Hotel in October and November last year. As he looked through the windows and filmed asylum seekers he could be heard shouting: ‘Dirty little f****** illegal immigrants.’ Giving evidence, Anderson denied ever being a racist and claimed he was amassing so many weapons because he was a ‘survivalist’ and was ‘doomsday prepping.’ He also claimed some of the weapons were used to go hunting. But jurors were read messages from his phone where he repeatedly used appalling slurs to describe those from ethnic minorities and messaged one friend saying: ‘I am stocking up and preparing for violence… Muslims aren’t here to assimilate, they’re here to take over. ‘They’re a literal bioweapon.’ He and two other 'white nationalists' bought land in Devon which they planned to use as a 'training ground,' the court heard The court heard that on February 16, he approached a man on Facebook who had joined a group called ‘Hull Patriot Protestors’ and attended anti-immigration protests. The next evening the two men met at the back of the St Stephens Shopping Centre in Hull city centre, just before 6pm, and Anderson told him about his plan to throw petrol bombs through the windows of the hotel in order to set it alight and showed him a knife he was carrying. He discussed using a crossbow and talked about the best places in the hotel to target, where potential weak spots would be and how to set up a diversion to draw away the emergency services. However two days later, the man went to a police station in Hull and reported what Anderson had told him. Giving evidence, the man said: ‘This is not just talk. ‘This is not just a guy p***** off with the community. He wants to kill someone or as many people as possible. ‘I wasn’t able to serve my country [in the armed forces] because I got into trouble but I want to serve my country and killing innocent people is not the way to do it. ‘I thought it was my duty to save innocent lives.’
