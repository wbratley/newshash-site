Log in A 65-year-old former teacher who skipped her sentencing hearing has been arrested in Hampshire, the Metropolitan Police said. Sally-Anne Bowen, who was convicted earlier this year of seven counts of indecent assault, had been due to be sentenced at Harrow Crown Court on Friday. The convictions relate to sexual relationships Bowen, then in her 20s, had with two pupils at an all-boys school in the late 1980s. Her trial heard she was in her mid-20s when she taught at Christ’s College in Finchley, north London. She had sex with one pupil up to 30 times and kissed a second boy when they were aged about 14 to 15. A Met spokesperson said: “On Friday November 20, officers were made aware that a 65-year-old woman had failed to appear at Harrow Crown Court for sentencing. “A warrant was issued for her arrest and officers promptly launched inquiries to locate her. At around 3pm, officers from a neighbouring force found the woman at a location in Hampshire and arrested her on suspicion of failing to appear at court. “She has been taken into police custody. A new sentencing date will be set by the court in due course.” Bowen, of Hemel Hempstead, Hertfordshire, did not appear in the morning, and Judge Karim Ezzat issued a warrant for her arrest, “not backed by bail”. Metropolitan Police officers had gone to her home on Friday morning but could not find her, the court heard. Neelam Gomersall, prosecuting, said Bowen’s partner of 36 years, Darren Scotchbrook, was at the address when officers turned up, but Bowen was not. Ms Gomersall had earlier told the court there was “no evidence” that Bowen could not attend the sentencing, and that information from her partner was “suggestive of refusal rather than inability” to attend. Defence barrister Mark Kimsey said: “Our instructing solicitor has tried to contact her and the phone rings but doesn’t answer.” He had earlier said that Bowen was in contact with solicitors on Thursday and had engaged with a pre-sentence report, adding there had been “concerns as to her welfare due to the content of the messages” from her. At her trial, one victim told jurors that he had sex with Bowen “about 20 or 30 times” over several months, regularly visited her home and considered her his girlfriend. He had fallen in love but kept their relationship secret because Bowen had “drummed it” into him that he must not tell anyone, the court heard. The victim said Bowen was able to teach for years “because I remained silent”. In a police interview played to jurors, the victim said Bowen dressed in a “sexually provocative” way. He said his “introduction to the world was completely governed by Sally-Anne Bowen”. The man said he enjoyed the sex, “games” and “secrets” at the time, but it led to “the destruction of my education”. He added: “I had no respect for authority, no self-respect. My mother never forgave me.” The second victim described spending lunchtimes with Bowen in a cafe, where he was “goaded” into kissing her by other boys and recalled touching her breasts. Both victims cannot be identified for legal reasons. The trial also heard evidence about Bowen’s conduct in the classroom, with one former pupil comparing her behaviour to “a Sabrina Carpenter video”. Former pupil Tobias Oliver told jurors Bowen flirted and openly discussed sex in class. He said she once sat on a desk and slowly opened her legs, revealing her underwear. Mr Oliver also recalled her discussing sexual positions from the Kama Sutra during a lesson. “The topics of conversation in class often strayed away from chemistry and became quite sexual,” he said. Prosecutor Madeleine Wolfe said during the trial that while the boys may have appeared willing, Bowen had “exploited” her position as a teacher “for her own sexual gratification”. The court heard the first victim’s mother became suspicious and raised concerns with the school. She was later told Bowen had left, without an explanation, Ms Wolfe said. Bowen told the boy she was moving to a kibbutz in Israel, leaving him “completely lost” and “heartbroken”, the prosecutor added. Former head of science, Richard Luker, recalled to the court the mother’s concern that her son planned to travel abroad with Bowen. The victim reported the allegations to police in 2014. Bowen denied the allegations as “total fabrication” and claimed a medical condition made sex difficult and painful. She denied flirting, saying: “I didn’t flirt with students, I was just friendly.” Defending her clothes, she said she “wasn’t going to dress like Margaret Thatcher”.
