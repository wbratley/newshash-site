Harry Kane faces 'months out' after suffering devastating ankle ligament tear

Article text

Harry Kane has been ruled out for the coming months after sustaining a torn syndesmotic ligament in his left ankle during Bayern Munich's 1-1 draw with FC Cologne on Friday evening. The Bavarian club confirmed the news after an examination by its medical department, stating that the England captain "will therefore be sidelined for the next few months". Kane, who only last month became the first Englishman in a quarter of a century to claim the Ballon d'Or, went down in agony inside the opening 20 minutes at the Allianz Arena after a collision with Cologne defender Joel Schmied. The injury is a major blow for both Bayern and England, with the 33-year-old recently putting pen to paper on a new contract with the Bundesliga champions running until June 2029, reportedly worth more than £400,000 per week. The incident occurred when Schmied lunged to intercept a low cross from Michael Olise near the back post, made contact with the ball, then followed through heavily into Kane's left leg inside the penalty area. Kane immediately grabbed his lower leg and was left writhing on the ground in visible distress before eventually hauling himself upright. The striker attempted to carry on but was little more than a passenger for the next few minutes, barely able to move freely. He soon signalled to the Bayern bench that he could not continue, dropping to the turf to receive treatment from medical staff before two physiotherapists helped him off the pitch. German midfielder Tom Bischof was brought on as his replacement with just 20 minutes on the clock. Cologne had taken the lead through a 12th-minute strike from Mikey Moore before Nathaniel Brown drew Bayern level in the 37th minute. The injury comes at a particularly cruel juncture in Kane's career, with the forward having enjoyed one of the most celebrated periods of his footballing life. His Ballon d'Or coronation took place at a ceremony in London on October 26, making him the first Englishman to receive the prestigious award in 25 years. LATEST DEVELOPMENTS During the recent international break, Kane visited the Tottenham academy at Hotspur Way, where he posed for photographs with current youth players and staff, including Bradley Allen, who coached him during his own time in the Spurs development setup. His goalscoring record since departing north London for Bavaria three years ago has been nothing short of extraordinary, netting 156 times in just 163 outings across all competitions and helping the club secure a league and cup double last term. His new deal makes him Europe's highest-paid footballer before bonuses. Kane entered the Cologne fixture having failed to find the net in his previous four Bayern outings, though he rediscovered his sharpest form during the international window, scoring twice for England in their Nations League victories over Spain and Croatia, including a penalty in the memorable 3-2 triumph in Madrid. His absence now raises serious questions about whether he can recover in time for England's Nations League quarter-final clash with Norway in March. Bayern are already contending with a mounting injury crisis among their attacking options. Olise himself was forced off during the same match after a challenge with Cologne's Tom Krauß, leaving the stadium on crutches, though the club confirmed the Frenchman had suffered only forefoot bruising and is expected to return next week. Ismael Saibari, Serge Gnabry, Luis Diaz and Alphonso Davies are also currently sidelined through injury. The game against Cologne marked Bayern boss Vincent Kompany’s return to the dugout after missing their last five matches following a collapse last month and a diagnosis of meningeal irritation. Michael Olise also limped off with a bruised foot as Bayern moved a point clear at the top of the table, although they could be overtaken on Saturday if Borussia Dortmund beat Borussia Monchengladbach. Kompany said: "I think this result was fair and Cologne deserved the point. We have other worries now, I have to honestly say.
