Crime DOES pay! Grand Theft Auto VI makes £2.89 billion after just ONE day of being on sale - making it the biggest entertainment launch of all time

Article text

Grand Theft Auto VI has become the world's fastest-selling game, after it raked in a staggering £2.89billion on its first day of release. The hotly-anticipated game, whose last version came out 13 years ago, has now broken records by becoming both the best-reviewed game ever and the most successful entertainment property in history. Take-Two Interactive Software Inc. said that GTA VI, which was released in the UK at midnight on Thursday, had generated a revenue of $3.79billion. The game costs around £70 ($79.99 in the US) for the Standard Edition and £90 ($99.99 in the US) for the Ultimate Edition, which includes a bonus vehicle, weapons, outfits and other in-game products. Gamers can play it on a PlayStation 5 or an Xbox Series X|S, although an online version of the game is expected to be released at a later date - as is a PC edition. It had been an eagerly awaited release for gamers - many of whom booked the day off work for the occasion. GTA VI has broken records and become the world's fastest-selling game, raking in £2.89billion on its first day of release. Pictured: The game being sold in a shop in New York The new edition of the game now rewards 'honourable' behaviour, while needless violence can also hamper a player's progress through the game And it did not disappoint - certainly not for the company, which said today that it had sold 32.5million units in presales alone, Bloomberg reported. Despite its controversies, it is also a huge British - and Scottish - success story, led primarily by a team at Rockstar North in Edinburgh, with support from other Rockstar Studios teams across the UK and all over the world. Almost 5,000 people are credited with working on the game, which entered pre-production in 2014 and full development in 2018. Its 2013 precursor, GTA V, remains one of the best-selling video games of all time. It generated sales of $800million in its first 24 hours of release, hitting $1billion in three days. GTA VI returns to the neon-soaked streets of Vice City, Rockstar's fictional version of Miami, which has featured in previous instalments of the series. The game follows two protagonists, Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, who find themselves caught up in a criminal conspiracy in the fictional state of Leonida, inspired by Florida. The pair become embroiled in a Bonnie and Clyde-style crime story after 'an easy score goes wrong', leaving them 'forced to rely on each other more than ever if they want to make it out alive'. The world appeared to stop as it was released this week, with eager players quickly sharing their opinions on social media. The game has had overwhelmingly positive reviews, being lauded for its graphics and gameplay. It debuted with a score of 99 on review aggregator website Metacritic – equalling 1998's The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time as the highest-rated video game ever. US rapper Travis Scott - whose music appears on the in-game radio stations - was among those hailing its release, tweeting: 'Congrats to all the developers who put the heart, blood and sweat into this game. F***ing love you.' It has received universally positive reviews from critics, with The Guardian praising creator Rockstar Games for creating 'the most extraordinarily, indulgently detailed virtual world that we have yet seen'. The latest instalment in developer Rockstar's blockbuster series was unlocked for UK users at midnight on Thursday - ending a 13-year wait since the franchise's previous instalment Vice also gave a glowing review, praising the highly detailed nature of GTA VI's open world and 'groundbreaking gameplay innovations', while Forbes highlighted the 'fantastic lead characters'. HR firm BrightHR said the game was mentioned in 4.6 per cent of time-off requests by UK employees on its launch day yesterday, and the bill for GTA annual leave was expected to reach £3.14million. BrightHR added that sickness absence was up 56 per cent on Thursday compared to the previous Thursday. Average lateness today was 29 minutes compared to 24 minutes last Thursday. Of those who were late, 9 per cent said they overslept compared to 4 per cent last Thursday. In the UK, buyers are estimated to spend £6.4billion on GTA VI, budgeting £308 each to purchase the game plus any console or accessories they need - more than four times the £69.99 cover price. At a standard VAT rate of 20 per cent, this would raise £1.3billion for the Treasury. There were some technological concerns in the hours leading up to the game's release, with the official PlayStation Network status website on Wednesday acknowledging some players were unable to access online features. Meanwhile, schools have urged pupils not to use the release of the game – which carries an age rating of 18 – as an excuse to skip lessons. Some are even entering pupils who do turn up into prize draws for vouchers, in a bid to boost attendance. Sales of gaming consoles and accessories surged in the run-up to launch day.
