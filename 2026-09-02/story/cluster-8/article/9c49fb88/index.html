Keir Starmer to stand down as MP triggering by-election and first political test for Burnham

Article text

Keir Starmer to stand down as MP triggering by-election and first political test for Burnham The former prime minister said he would be turning his concentration to international affairs - Bookmark - CommentsGo to comments Sir Keir Starmer has announced he will step down as the MP,triggering a by-election in his London seat and prompting the first major political test for his successor Andy Burnham. Sir Keir resigned as prime minister in July to make way for Mr Burnham after he lost the support of his party in the wake of dire local election results, the scandal over appointing Peter Mandelson as ambassador to the US and a failure to deliver on key policies including welfare reform. He told the Camden New Journal that he felt now was the “right time” to leave the House of Commons to focus on new work around international affairs. The move will be a relief for the new top team around Mr Burnham with concerns that supporters of Sir Keir could form a new faction in the party to undermine the new prime minister. Sir Keir’s Holborn and St Pancras seat was previously seen as a winnable target by the Green Party and the by-election will be a test of how much support Mr Burnham has won back from its leader Zack Polanski, who has not ruled out running in the race. Announcing that he was stepping down, Sir Keir said: “It has been a huge honour and privilege to represent the best constituency in the country and to have taken over from Frank Dobson, who was such a great constituency MP and a member of the last Labour government. “I will now focus on other things and that will be international affairs including defence, security, trade and technology in a fast-changing world. I will also continue, as I have done for a long time now with others, work on combating violence against women and girls; that is an issue that matters to me hugely. “It’s work I started before I became an MP and it’s work I’m keen to continue after I’m an MP. So international affairs on the one hand, and continuing my work on violence against women and girls on the other.” Sir Keir has informed local Labour constituency party staff of his decision to exit parliament after 11 years representing the north London seat. In his statement, he said his legacy as an MP and as a prime minister will benefit his constituency going forwards, which has included free school meals and lifting the two-child benefit cap. “I want to thank all of those I was privileged to work with in the constituency on issues that matter hugely: affordable housing, first-class education and the work we did on knife crime through the Camden youth support, which was really, really important,” he said. Sir Keir became prime minister in July 2024 following a landslide victory at the general election, but oversaw a massive decline in popularity for the government as discontent with his leadership grew. Labour suffered back-to-back record defeats at the local ballot box, with Sir Keir’s party losing 1,496 councillors across England and losing control of 38 councils in local elections. The victory was seen as a vindication of his attempts to detoxify the Labour brand after the Jeremy Corbyn years had led to allegations of a culture of antisemitism. He won the election on the back of a collapse in support for the Tories following scandals with Boris Johnson over parties in Downing Street during the Covid lockdown and Liz Truss’s disastrous mini-Budget, which crashed the markets. But as PM, Sir Keir found himself tied in knots over a lack of money available for his government’s priorities, a failure to grow the economy and pledges he made in the manifesto election on not increasing the major taxes while not revisiting Brexit, explicitly ruling out rejoining the EU, Single Market and Customs Union. Mr Burnham’s victory in the Makerfield by-election effectively fired the starting gun for a leadership challenge against Sir Keir, and was the final trigger for his long-awaited resignation. Now, as Mr Burnham prepares to make his first speech as PM to the Commons his predecessor is going. In a statement on X, Sir Keir said it was the right time to “hand the baton over to a successor”. He said: “Over the summer, I have had time to reflect and consider my future. I have decided that now is the right time to step down as MP for Holborn and St Pancras and hand the baton over to a successor.” Reflecting on his time as prime minister, Sir Keir again pointed to Labour’s 2024 election victory, and said: “Whoever is lucky enough to become the Labour candidate here will have my full support and backing and I will continue to give my full support to the prime minister and this Labour government.” The news also comes two months after two men were jailed for conspiring to carry out arson attacks targeting a property and a car connected to Sir Keir, which were believed to have been ordered on behalf of Russia. The Tories said Sir Keir’s decision to step down marked “another U-turn”. Conservative Party chairman Kevin Hollinrake said: “It is fitting that his final act as a member of parliament is yet another U-turn having pledged to stay on. “The decision to stand down will mean a costly by-election for taxpayers at a time when Andy Burnham and John Healey are plotting tax rises on hardworking families across the country. “The Conservatives look forward to giving the people of Holborn and St Pancras a chance to vote for a stronger economy and a stronger country in the weeks ahead.” Greens leader Mr Polanksi is currently a member of the London Assembly and has not stood in any by-elections for a chance at securing a seat in Westminster. A Green Party spokesperson said: “Zack has always made it clear he would like to be a London MP.
