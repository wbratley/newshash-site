Healey delivers first Budget with energy support, tax rises on banks, savers and landlords

Unbiased summary

Chancellor John Healey presented the 2026 Autumn Budget, the first under Prime Minister Andy Burnham. Key measures include a £100 one-off payment to around 20 million households in council tax bands A-D in England to help with energy bills, the abolition of gas standing charges from 2027, an extension of the VAT cut on electricity to July, and a freeze on fuel duty until July. Taxes will rise: a temporary bank surcharge increase from 3% to 6% for 15 months, and a 2% rise in income tax rates on savings and rental income from April. The minimum wage and state pension will both rise by 3.9%. The Budget also included a new First Time Buyer ISA, a £40 billion council house-building programme, and commitments to increase defence spending and cut welfare spending. The Office for Budget Responsibility downgraded growth forecasts and warned of a squeeze on living standards.
