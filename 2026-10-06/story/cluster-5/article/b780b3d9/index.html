British Airways BA295 RECAP: Heathrow flight emergency as plane plunges 28,000ft British Airways flight BA295 from London Heathrow to Chicago made a U-turn over Ireland after declaring an emergency on Monday, dropping rapidly to just 9,000ft A British Airways flight eventually returned to Heathrow on Monday afternoon following an emergency just one hour into its journey to Chicago. Flight BA295 declared a mid-air emergency following a sudden and steep descent over Ireland. Oxygen masks were deployed for the passengers and crew. The Boeing 777-200ER plane departed from London Heathrow Airport's Terminal 5 at 11.50am, with a scheduled flight time of eight hours and 40 minutes to Chicago O'Hare International Airport (ORD). The aircraft had climbed to its cruising altitude of 37,000ft when it appeared to encounter an issue and declared an emergency. Flight-tracking data shows the aircraft descended rapidly from cruising altitude to around 9,000ft. The plane burned fuel off the coast of Wales before touching down at Heathrow again at around 5pm. Plane burning fuel in holding pattern In order to burn more fuel and reduce weight, the crew will lower the landing gear while flying in the holding pattern. This is because the aircraft was designed to land after a long flight during which it would have burned most of its fuel. Planes take off much heavier than they can land, and airliners must burn or dump fuel before an emergency landing to reduce the aircraft's total weight to the safe maximum landing weight. Landing while carrying too much weight runs the risk of extreme physical stress on the landing gear, wings and fuselage. Plane is oldest in British Airways fleet British Airways Flight BA295 is operated by a 30 year-old Boeing 777-200ER registered as G-VIIA. According to simpleflying.com, G-VIIA is the oldest aircraft in the British Airways fleet, having been delivered to the airline in July 1997. It was also the first ever 777-200ER to roll off the production line. The carrier operates 43 Boeing 777-200ERs delivered between February 1997 and July 2009, with an average age of around 26 years.
