Russell Brand's 'proud' wife says she will 'honour' the rules after his controversial prison message sparked outrage

Article text

Russell Brand's wife Laura has said she will 'honour' the rules after his controversial prison message sparked outrage. In a new video, Laura Gallacher read from Brand's bible but insisted she will not relay any more messages from her spouse. The 39-year-old made the vow during her 'Sunday prayer service' show posted on the jailed comedian's social media accounts. But she said she would continue 'to be proud to be a wife, and a mother, and a woman defending her family'. Last Sunday, she read out a message Brand had composed behind bars in which he claimed to be innocent. In the statement, he said: 'As you know, I'm not guilty, I am not guilty for the offences I have been jailed for, so something strange is happening. 'The good news is that Christ is with us when we choose him, and I do.' The Ministry of Justice called the message 'completely unacceptable' and launched an 'urgent investigation'. Laura Gallacher almost broke down in tears today as she said she will not read out messages from her rapist husband Russell Brand anymore Laura pictured with Brand arriving at Southwark Crown Court in October Laura today acknowledged that 'last week's episode drew a considerable amount of attention and, now that I understand the parameters set by the brilliant and professional staff at HMP Littlehey, I will absolutely honour that'. She said she would continue to post the Sunday Service YouTube show she started with her convicted rapist husband 'because I really enjoy it'. 'It continues to be a great education for me and I enjoy reading the supportive comments and I have been so heartened, even in the past week, by many of the messages that I have got,' Laura said. 'It's also a place where I will continue to be proud to be a wife, and a mother, and a woman defending her family at a time when the media's image of what kind of a woman I am is under so much scrutiny.' She added how her friend had convinced her to carry on with the show last week, saying it 'gave her faith and it gave her strength and so, even if I were to only do it for that, that would be worth it to me'. 'I was thinking a lot today about the times when I wake up in the night and it feels, in many ways - and anyone who has been through grief, or absence, or a highly stressful situation will know that when you feel that desolation, or loneliness, or fear that it can feel like you are out at sea, it's pitch black and you don't know how to navigate the waters,' Laura said. 'Truly, I think that feeling of the unknown and that the path ahead will have twists and turns and you can't predict, that brings you to a place, truly, where you have to put all your faith and hope in God.' Brand's wife acknowledged that Sunday's podcast would likely draw in more viewers than usual, remarking that she had been 'heartened' by messages she had received in recent days. Throughout, she discussed her 'grief and sadness' and described how she had been awake at night feeling 'loneliness' and uncertainty over her next moves. After discussing researchers' latest investigation of Turkey's boat-shaped Durupınar Formation near Mount Ararat amid claims it could be remains of Noah's Ark, Laura gave a reading from the story of Jonah and the whale. She then entered into a monologue about the 'depths of despair' before reading from The Practice of the Presence of God by Brother Lawrence which she labelled 'one of Russell's favourite books'. Brand's wife also read a series of supportive comments from viewers, before adding in a final prayer: 'Please God, look after my husband, care for him, and bring him comfort. Please look after my children and bring them courage and joy.' Laura visited her jailed husband at HMS Littlehey earlier this week where she spent around two hours with Brand. After meeting the convicted rapist, 51, she spoke to prison staff for 20 minutes before getting into the car and leaving. Prison sources said Brand had 'won the lottery' when he was transferred from HMP Wandsworth – one of Britain's toughest jails – to Littlehey, which has a far more relaxed regime and offers activities including yoga, gardening and football. Brand's wife was seen arriving at the visitors' centre at HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire on Wednesday, where she spent around two hours During last week's Sunday Service YouTube show, the convicted rapist maintained his innocence in a message relayed by his wife Officials this week were establishing how Brand's statement was communicated from inside prison and investigating whether it was simply a declaration of innocence or an attempt to influence his accusers amid a police investigation into further allegations. Prison governors have the power to restrict the ability of inmates to communicate with people on the outside if it is deemed necessary to protect victims, public safety or prison security. Inmates are also warned they cannot ask anyone else to share messages on their behalf that they wouldn't be allowed to do themselves. Justice Secretary Alex Norris said: 'Prisoners are rightly banned from making statements that cause distress to victims. 'This is being urgently investigated and I expect action to be taken if he is found to have broken the rules.' Downing Street officials and campaigners reacted with fury to Brand's statement being shared on YouTube. The Government called on Google to take down Brand's YouTube account after his wife used their channel to read the message from prison. A justice minister met with representatives from the company to make the request, after Meta disabled the convicted rapist's profiles on Instagram and Facebook.
