Andy Burnham 'sells out Britain' as hidden £1.5billion cost of Brexit surrender revealed

Article text

Andy Burnham has been accused of "selling out Britain" after a hidden cost of his Brexit "reset" deal was revealed. According to fresh research from Reform UK, students from the European Union could leave Britain grappling with a £1.5billion bill in unpaid university fees. On Friday, the Prime Minister agreed a new deal with Brussels on food checks and a youth mobility scheme. However, the EU has threatened to walk away from the agreement entirely unless the Government agrees to slash university tuition fees to the same rate paid by British students. Brussels has handed the Prime Minister a six-month deadline to approve the measure. Higher education bosses have warned the agreement could leave UK institutions facing a £500million funding black hole. Some 172,000 students from the EU currently owe British universities around £6.2billion, according to Reform UK. Data from the Student Loans Company suggests 43,700 of these graduates - one in four - have failed to make a single payment over the past year, costing £360million. The analysis from Nigel Farage's party suggests if these students do not pay back their loans, UK universities will face unpaid debts to the tune of £1.5billion. Reacting to the figures, Reform UK’s education spokesman Suella Braverman said: “It is disgraceful that Andy Burnham is willing to spend hundreds of millions cutting student fees for foreign students while allowing British youngsters to be ripped off with mountains of debt. “I will not hesitate to reverse his sell-out and put our own students first.” The party have put forward plans to prohibit foreign students who fail to pay back their tuition fees from setting foot on British soil. LATEST DEVELOPMENTS The Reform UK analysis comes amid warnings over the financial impact of Mr Burnham's Brexit deal on already struggling institutions. The University of Manchester’s vice-chancellor, Professor Duncan Ivison, said: “If nothing was done to compensate or provide additional funding, it would take £500million we estimate, out of the higher education sector at a time where there’s a lot of pressure on the sector.” St Andrews vice-chancellor Professor Dame Sally Mapstone, meanwhile, warned an agreement to slash tuition fees could cost the university up to £12million per year and have “knock-on effects”. Despite welcoming “closer ties with Europe”, she told The Times that “unless we sort out the important financial practicalities of this, the unintended consequences for higher education in the UK could be considerable”. Should Labour cave in to Brussels' demand, students from EU member states will see the cost of their fees cut from up to £38,000 per year to just £9,790, in line with what British youngsters pay. Vivienne Stern, the chief executive of Universities UK, told The Sun: “You can’t go on like this. We could not maintain the reputation we have got for an excellent university system if we continue to chip away at the income that universities get for teaching. “We agree it would be a good thing to get a solid basis for EU students to study here and UK students to study in Europe. “But it has to be financially sustainable for UK universities too.” A Government spokesman said: “The food and drink, emissions trading and Youth Experience deals agreed at the summit stand on their own, and dates for implementation are set out in the joint statement agreed by the UK and the EU. “Both sides are now taking them through their respective legal processes, while tuition fees are a separate negotiation.” Our Standards: The GB News Editorial Charter Follow related topics:
