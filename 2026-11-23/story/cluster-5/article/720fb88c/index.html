Huge Kremlin warship 'destroyed' in explosion at Libyan port in suspected drone attack - after Russian cargo ship attacked in Baltic Sea

Article text

A huge Russian warship has been destroyed in a massive explosion while docked in a port in Libya in a humiliating blow to Vladimir Putin. It came hours after a Russian cargo ship came under fire from drones in the Baltic Sea, in an attack attributed to Ukraine by NATO member Finland. Ukraine has not spoken out about or taken credit for either incident, but Libyan military officials said several Ukrainian sea drones were launched at the ship from a base run by Ukrainian forces in western Libya, according to an initial investigation into the attack. At least four drones were intercepted and destroyed, while two hit the warship, they said. The ship, believed to be the £150million missile corvette Merkury, was engulfed in flames after a blast rocked the Mediterranean port of Benghazi early on Sunday. Video footage showed the anchored vessel exploding in a flash of light before a giant mushroom cloud erupted from the deck after a suspected drone attack. Libya has been divided since a NATO-backed uprising toppled longtime dictator Muammar Gaddafi, who was later killed, in 2011. A UN-supported government is based in Tripoli, while a rival administration controls the east. The oil-rich nation remains a focal point for a long-running rivalry between Russia and the West. Video footage showed the anchored vessel erupting in a giant mushroom cloud following a suspected drone attack Satellite images showed a huge cloud of smoke billowing from the Russian warship Merkury in Libya The vessel exploded in a flash of light after it was hit by a huge blast while anchored in a Libyan port The fire has now been extinguished, eastern Libyan government officials said, as they announced a joint investigation alongside Russia to 'uncover the circumstances of the attack, its source, and the party behind it.' The destruction of Merkury presents a major loss to Putin's navy, which has suffered repeated attacks since the invasion of Ukraine. The vessel, a Project 20380 Steregushchiy-class corvette, had been in port for several days. It was in the Mediterranean escorting a convoy of Putin regime cargo ships used to transport military supplies. Naval expert H I Sutton reported that the warship was almost certainly destroyed, but there was no immediate confirmation whether it had sunk at its berth or been burnt out amid reports of secondary explosions. Sutton, writing for the specialist publication Naval News, reported that satellite images and other sources showed the vessel ablaze. Libyan authorities confirmed a major explosion at the Benghazi naval base, but suggested it was an ammunition depot rather than the warship. The cause of the explosion remains unknown. Local witnesses described hearing three loud blasts near the port, followed by flames and flying debris, according to Libyan outlet Ean Libya. Residents also reported sightings of aircraft and apparent anti-aircraft fire. Firefighters rushed to tackle the inferno as residents were warned to stay away. Tawfiq al-Shamekh, a spokesman for eastern Libya's emergency authorities, said the flames had been extinguished. Italian news agency Agenzia Nova reported that a Russian military vessel or its cargo may have been targeted in an air attack. The vessel had stopped at Benghazi port to refuel and resupply on its way back to Russia, under maritime co-operation agreements between the two countries. Two medical sources said no deaths had been reported, but there were injuries. The number and nationalities of those injured were not immediately known. The vessel is normally assigned to Russia's Black Sea Fleet but has operated in other waters, including from Arctic naval bases. It was one of the fleet's newer vessels and only went into service in 2023. The eastern-based government said port and naval base facilities, including warehouses, were safe and that normal operations would resume once the fire was brought under control and the site secured. It condemned the suspected attack as a violation of Libya's sovereignty and a threat to maritime safety, saying it would take legal and diplomatic measures against anyone found responsible. A Western Libyan government spokesperson said it 'will not allow Libyan ports, territory or territorial waters to be turned into an arena for settling scores or exporting international conflicts,' in an apparent reference to Russia's war in Ukraine. The latest explosion also follows previous mysterious disasters involving Russian-linked shipping in the Mediterranean. In December 2024, Russian cargo ship Ursa Major sank after explosions in the Mediterranean. And in March this year, the Russian LNG tanker Arctic Metagaz was devastated by explosions off Libya. Yesterday, the smouldering wreck of Sparta II drifted into Finnish waters and weighed anchor there as the 16 Russian crewmembers were evacuated onto a nearby Russian ship. The vessel was attacked by two drones in international waters about three nautical miles from Russia's border. Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo said the attack risked the war spilling over into its neighbouring areas. Finland's defence ministry said the military had reintroduced temporary aviation and maritime restrictions in the eastern Gulf of Finland as a precaution to protect civilians and enable authorities to operate if drones needed to be intercepted. Following the attack, the Scandinavian nation's border guard said that there were no further threats to other vessels or people, rescue units were at the scene and that it was coordinating operations with neighbouring countries. The vessel was the second Russian ship to be hit in the Gulf of Finland this month, after a warship was targeted by Ukraine on November 11. Meanwhile, air raid sirens were sounded in Russia's nearby European enclave of Kaliningrad early this morning, over fears of a drone strike on the heavily militarised region. There was no proof of an attack from Ukraine, while Lithuania 'categorically' denied being involved in a plot, insisting it has never authorised the use of its airspace for an attack on Russia. Videos and photographs spread online last night, appearing to show strikes on Kaliningrad, with claims they were a Ukrainian strike from nearby NATO territory. But the images were from the separate conflict in Iran. Russian President Vladimir Putin last month threatened to use nuclear weapons in the event of any Western threat to Kaliningrad. Tensions between Russia and the EU have also flared this week after Russian missiles and drones struck Poland on Friday, close to where hundreds of British troops are carrying out drills. Poland said two Russian missiles hit its territory, with the NATO country accusing Moscow of an 'intentional provocation'. Hours earlier, Donald Trump had appeared to make excuses for Vladimir Putin, telling Fox News the Russian president 'has been landing drones in Poland, and a couple of little missiles, by mistake'. One rocket slammed into a field and exploded about 47 miles from where 300 UK soldiers were operating with the so-called 'Coalition of the Willing'. NATO warplanes were scrambled after two Kh-101 cruise missiles and a jet-powered suicide drone breached Polish airspace on Friday morning. A second strike hit Rudnik na Sadem, just 22 miles from the base, while the third struck an area in Molodycz, around 50 miles away. Mr Streeting described the attack in Poland as an 'outrageous provocation', adding: 'It seems more than a coincidence that those airspace violations took place at the same time as the multinational force for Ukraine exercise.' He then urged there to be 'a response'. The incident came in the same week Moscow was accused on the same day of being behind an assassination attempt on a Ukrainian-American rapper in Denmark.
