COP31 climate talks end without agreement after fossil fuel dispute

Unbiased summary

The COP31 UN climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, ended on Sunday without a consensus agreement after negotiations extended beyond the scheduled Friday finish, becoming the longest climate conference in history. A draft deal proposed by the Turkish and Australian presidencies, known as the Antalya pact, did not include a direct reference to transitioning away from fossil fuels, though it invited countries to electrify economies and called for finance for developing nations. The EU, UK, and over 130 countries rejected the draft, demanding explicit mention of fossil fuels. Saudi Arabia and other oil states reportedly opposed such language. The conference passed decisions on related matters, including a just transition, but no cover decision was adopted.
