Alcaraz outplays Zverev for first ATP Finals triumph

Article text

Alcaraz outplays Zverev for first ATP Finals triumph - Published Carlos Alcaraz outplayed world number one Alexander Zverev to end his wait for a first ATP Finals title and complete a remarkable post-injury return to the sport. A season which began with Alcaraz becoming the youngest man to complete the career Grand Slam, before he was ruled out for more than four months with a wrist issue, ended with the Spaniard holding aloft another of the sport's biggest trophies. Alcaraz, still wearing heavy strapping around his upper-left leg following his injury scare earlier in the week, collapsed to the ground in celebration after sealing a 6-2 7-6 (9-7) win in a dramatic second-set tie-break. It was a statement victory over Zverev, who took advantage of Alcaraz and Jannik Sinner's injury absences to rise to the top of the rankings and claim his first two major titles. It was a 14th successive win and a third-straight title triumph for seven-time major winner Alcaraz, who demonstrated beyond any doubt that he is back to his devastating best. "Lifting this trophy is something I have been chasing for a really long time, it means the world to me," the 23-year-old said. "I have been through really difficult moments with myself, with my team. For me it's been an amazing week. "I couldn't finish the week in a better way." Alcaraz ends long wait for ATP Finals glory It is just 84 days since Alcaraz made his return at the US Open in August after a lengthy spell on the sidelines. Since that tournament, the current world number three has won 21 of his 22 matches and swept titles at four of the five tournaments he has entered. With his latest victory, Alcaraz has become the youngest man to win all four Grand Slams and the season-ending Finals - and only the fourth in history. Zverev, who ends 2026 with an ATP Tour-leading 69 match victories, had not dropped serve in four straight-set matches to reach the final. But for much of the two-hour title decider, his greatest weapon was dismantled by a brilliant Alcaraz. Alcaraz handled the occasion far better as he emerged from a tense start with a 4-1 double-break lead on his way to the first set. The contrasting emotions of the two players were evident when Alcaraz wore a big grin after pulling off a between-the-legs lob that Zverev smashed into the net. At the other end of the court, the German threw away his racquet in disgust. While Alcaraz danced to music during a change of ends, Zverev's frustrations only grew as, after winning back one of the two breaks of serve, he received a second time violation on his way to losing serve for a third successive game. The top seed left the court after unravelling in the first set, having produced just three winners to 19 unforced errors in an inconsistent start. Alcaraz's march to the title appeared inevitable as he took control of the second set with a fourth break of serve, but Zverev restored parity in the eighth game and kept his hopes alive by forcing a tie-break. Overturning a 3-0 deficit in the tie-break, Zverev then won six straight points to arrive at three set points. However, he failed to take all of them, including one on his own serve, before Alcaraz drove in a backhand winner on his second match point. After picking himself off the floor, the Spaniard celebrated with his team in the stands after finally landing the title he had been chasing for four years. In the doubles, French debutants Theo Arribage and Albano Olivetti lifted the trophy after a comeback victory against Marcelo Arevalo and Mate Pavic.
