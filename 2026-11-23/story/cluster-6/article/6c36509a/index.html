Alcaraz puts Zverev in shade as world’s best beats No 1 for first ATP Finals title

Article text

The titles of ATP world No 1 and the best male tennis player in the world are carried by two different names. This has always been clear throughout this messy, injury-ravaged men’s season but it was underlined in the grand finale as Carlos Alcaraz ended his campaign with a statement victory, defeating the top-ranked player, Alexander Zverev, 6-2, 7-6 (7) to win the ATP Finals for the first time. So much of Alcaraz’s fortunes this season were defined by the wrist injury that forced him out for four and a half months during the spring and summer. No matter, he still managed to tick the two most important items off his to-do list this year. After starting 2026 by becoming the youngest man in history to earn the career grand slam, he is now, at 23, the fourth man to win all four grand slams and the ATP Finals. Despite his long absence, he has missed only two of the four majors and two of the nine Masters 1000 events: the Canadian Open and Shanghai Masters. Faster indoor courts were previously viewed as the conditions that make Alcaraz most vulnerable. It is fair to say that this is no longer true. Alcaraz ends his year on a 14-match winning streak after triumphing on two of the fastest courts, Paris and Turin. This victory is all the more impressive considering he picked up a thigh injury during his match on Wednesday against Daniil Medvedev. Although he kept on winning, the Spaniard has been more cautious with his peerless movement and his leg has been heavily taped. He has learned that he does not always need to throw himself around the court at 100% intensity, that he can actually conserve energy, play more efficiently and still defeat the best players in the world. That might just be the most worrying development for the rest of the field. Until the final, Zverev’s tournament had served as another demonstration of his enormous yet freakishly consistent serve, with the German not dropping serve at all en oute to the final. However, the opening game immediately underscored how much better Alcaraz is than the rest of the top 10. The Spaniard immediately generated three break points on Zverev’s serve. In Zverev’s second service game, Alcaraz landed ample returns, forced Zverev into baseline exchanges and broke to love. For much of the match, Alcaraz completely outplayed Zverev from the baseline. His concerns surrounding his thigh problems have forced him to play even more offensively as usual, and he has struck the ball so sweetly for most of the tournament, but he was also incredibly smart. He peppered Zverev with ample slices and slower balls, drawing short balls from the German by arresting his rhythm, before unleashing in full. Alcaraz’s forehand, the best in the world, usually commands all the attention, but he struck his backhand incredibly well all match. However, even for a player who has won so much at such a young age, he rarely makes things easy for himself. From 6-2, 4-2, Alcaraz began to spray unforced errors as he fell to 4-5, 0-30 on his serve in set two. Then, after recovering to lead 3-0 in the tie‑break, Alcaraz suddenly trailed by triple set point at 3-6. During this stretch, Zverev served well, he forced the Spaniard to earn every point through his consistent defence and came up with improvisational solutions to Alcaraz’s drop shots and variation on some decisive moments. The Spaniard, however, was extremely poor. Things may not often be straightforward for Alcaraz: his ability to play his best tennis when it most matters remains an astounding quality. To the surprise of nobody at all, he locked in, saved the three set points, earned a match point with a sickly sweet point-ending drop shot and then, without hesitation, he pounded a backhand down-the-line winner to close out the victory. After ample discourse surrounding his credibility as the new No 1, which Zverev complained about during the Paris Masters, he did respond in the right way on the court here, reaching the final with wins against four top 10 opponents. But there are levels to this game and the gap between Alcaraz and the rest of the field in Turin this year is vast. He ends his individual season by providing an unforgettable reminder of the excellence the sport had to go without for so long.
