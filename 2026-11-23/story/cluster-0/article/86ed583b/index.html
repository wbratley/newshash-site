Don Toliver fans scream 'I can't breathe' and 'it burns' after substance released at Manchester gig

Article text

Don Toliver fans scream 'I can't breathe' and 'it burns' after substance released at Manchester gig Witnesses said the standing area of the venue was cleared at around 10pm this evening as Don Toliver was performing on his NITROUS tour, amid claims a smoke canister was thrown This is the horrifying moment fans screamed "I can't breathe" and "it burns" after an unknown substance was released at a concert in Manchester. Fans were evacuated from the UK's largest indoor music venue on Sunday night after what was thought to be tear gas was let off. Video shows police and other emergency services outside Co-op Live in Manchester. Witnesses said the standing area was evacuated at 10pm on Sunday (November 22) as Don Toliver performed on the venue's B stage, reports the Manchester Evening News. Police have confirmed the arena was evacuated after tear gas was released in the crowd during the performance. A video taken from the floor standing area showed the moment fans started coughing and using their t-shirts to cover their face and nose as they make a quick exit when the gas was let off. The person filming is heard saying: "What the f*** was that smell bro? I can't breathe bro. Bro what was that?!" Someone in the background can be heard adding: "It burns." Another clip, taken from a balcony area, shows a gap in the crowd open up as gig-goers began moving away from the tear gas while Don Toliver continues to perform. The show was stopped shortly afterwards. Jessica Gannon was at the event. She took to X to say she was "devastated" by the incident. She posted: "[I] was at Don Toliver tonight in Manchester and I’m devastated that someone ruined the show. At least we are safe but genuinely annoyed at the security at Co-op Live as I got challenged for a portable charge yet you let someone in with tear gas. I want a refund." There have not been any reported injuries at this stage, a Greater Manchester Police spokesperson said: "Emergency services are currently in attendance following an incident at Co-op Live this evening. It is understood that a substance believed to be tear gas was let off in the crowd towards the end of a concert. "All attendees are currently being safely evacuated as a precaution. Those showing signs of being impacted are being supported by emergency services, with thankfully no serious injuries reported. "Please stay away from the arena whilst emergency services help those that have been at the concert safely make their way to their transport home. A major incident has been declared to ensure emergency services and partner agencies can work together quickly and effectively to coordinate a safe response." They also show more than a dozen emergency service vehicles, including police and ambulance, parked outside the entrance of the arena, and more emergency service vehicles arriving at the scene of the arena as fans stream out of the venue. Another showed the moment fans were told the show was over. A man, dressed in black, can been seen on-stage holding a microphone while the house lights are on. He tells thousands of fans: "Due to unforeseen circumstances, the show is now finished. In your own time, please make your way to the nearest exit. If you require medical assistance, please speak to a member of staff or visit the level zero concourse." The 23,500-seat venue is Britain's largest music venue, and has hosted acts like Paul McCartney, Hans Zimmer, and Charli XCX. It has also hosted the Brit Awards, and is due to stage Britain's first NBA basketball game in the coming years. The Greater Manchester Mayor's office has been contacted for comment.
