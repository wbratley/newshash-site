US rapper's Manchester concert is halted in 'major incident': Revellers seen coughing and rubbing their eyes as thousands evacuated from Co-op arena after 'tear gas' was let off during show

Article text

- Were YOU at the concert? Email Sam.Lawley@dailymail.co.uk Police cars and ambulances raced to the scene after US rapper Don Toliver's concert in Manchester was halted after 'tear gas' was let off during the gig. The musician, 32, was halfway through a show in the the Co-op arena on Sunday evening when fans started rushing out of the standing area, coughing and rubbing their eyes. Videos posted to social media show crowds being ushered out of the venue. Police believe that tear gas was released during the gig, seeing the masses evacuated. No one is believed to be seriously injured and police said that those 'impacted' are being supported by emergency services. Greater Manchester Police (GMP) have now urged concertgoers who notices side effects or starts feeling unwell to go to their local hospital. Social media posts circulating on Sunday evening seemed to show the incident happened while Don Toliver, real name Caleb Zackery Toliver, was performing. Clips showed a swarm of police cars and vans circling the arena, with sirens blaring and officers heading to the scene. In one video, thousands of concertgoers can be seen standing confused in the middle of the venue's dancefloor with the lights switched fully on. Several posts on social media quickly reported that 'tear gas' had been released during the show. Later, a clip showed an organiser addressing a large crowd of concertgoers and confirming the gig had been called off for the evening. The incident comes just three months after the rapper had a concert stopped halfway through. TMZ reported at the time that a man had 'attempted to smuggle a gun into the arena' in Houston, Texas. A statement from GMP said on Sunday night: 'Emergency services are currently in attendance following an incident at Co-op Live this evening. 'It is understood that a substance believed to be tear gas was let off in the crowd towards the end of a concert. 'All attendees are currently being safely evacuated as a precaution. Those showing signs of being impacted are being supported by emergency services, with thankfully no serious injuries reported. 'Please stay away from the arena whilst emergency services help those that have been at the concert safely make their way to their transport home. According to a promotional website, fans were able to enter the arena from 6.30pm, with the warm-up act set to start soon after. Mr Toliver is midway through a lengthy tour which will see him head to the O2 in London on Monday - less than 24 hours after tonight's alleged incident. He will go on to the OVO Arena Wembley the following night before continuing his world tour. The force said in the early hours of Monday: 'Emergency services have safely evacuated concertgoers at last night's (Sunday 22 November) Don Toliver show at Co-op Live after reports of people in the crowd suffering effects from an unknown substance. 'Several people remain to be medically assessed on site for side effects, as a precaution. 'Officers first were alerted by venue staff shortly after 10pm and attended alongside other emergency services on a priority response. 'Members of the public began to be evacuated when safe to do so, to ensure those that were suffering the effects of this incident could be assessed by medical professionals. 'Anyone at last night's event who has since noticed side-effects or become ill should attend their local hospital. 'Enquiries are underway into what the substance is and what caused the incident.' Assistant Chief Constable Steph Parker, who is Gold Commander for the incident, said: 'Our priority was to ensure the safety of every person that was attending the concert. 'Several people were treated on site, and one has been taken to hospital at this time. Anyone who has left the event and since become unwell is advised to attend their local hospital. 'We understand the concern that this incident will have raised - particularly those with loved ones at the venue last night - and we'd like to thank everyone for their patience as we dealt with it. 'There were over 12,000 people in attendance during this concert who were safely escorted from the arena so they could make their way home. 'Our declaration of a major incident has allowed us to ensure emergency services and partner agencies can work together quickly and effectively to coordinate a safe response. 'We will now begin to fully understand what this substance was and how this incident came about.'
