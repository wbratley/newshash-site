British Museum announces changes in bid to lower Bayeux Tapestry queues

Article text

Log in The British Museum is extending its opening hours and introducing new measures in an effort to clear severe backlogs and overcrowding at its Bayeux Tapestry exhibition. Confirming the operational overhaul, a spokesperson for the museum told The Independent: "The response to the Bayeux Tapestry has been extraordinary, and we are delighted by the excitement and interest it has generated. "We also know that some visitors have faced longer queues than we would have wanted, so we are introducing a series of measures - including an audio guide, clearer signage, additional seating and longer opening hours - to make the experience smoother and give more people the time they need to enjoy the Tapestry." The swift changes follow widespread visitor frustration over two-hour delays and an incident last week where hundreds of ticket holders were turned away when the venue closed early for a private event. The museum has since apologised to affected visitors, confirming that anyone turned away will receive a full refund alongside a priority slot to rebook for a rearranged time. Starting Monday, an audio guide will offer visitors extra historical detail. Clearer signage will explain the introductory displays and videos setting the scene before visitors reach the main showcase, while additional seating will allow for breaks during the wait. Details on the extended opening hours are set to be announced shortly. The measures build on earlier interventions inside the gallery, where staff banned photography and introduced recorded messages prompting crowds to keep moving steadily along the one-way route. Ticket demand for the display has been unprecedented since going on sale in July, with a record 100,000 passes sold on the first day alone. Unsuccessful fans will have two further chances to secure entry when additional ticket batches are released in October and January. On loan from Normandy, where it has been exhibited since 1983, the masterpiece marks the first time the historic work has returned to England since its creation over 900 years ago. The exhibition opened on 10 September and is scheduled to run until July 2027. Standard adult entry costs £27 off-peak and £33 during peak hours – compared to the usual €12 (£10) fee at its home in France– while children under 16 enter free of charge. The 70-metre-long masterpiece is an embroidery of dyed wool thread on linen cloth rather than a woven tapestry, as its name suggests. Across 58 narrative scenes, it uses Latin inscriptions alongside detailed needlework to depict the extraordinary events surrounding the 1066 Norman Conquest, culminating in the Battle of Hastings and the end of Anglo-Saxon rule in Britain. The historic work features roughly 626 human figures, 200 horses, 500 other animals, 37 ships, and depictions of key historical phenomena, including an early record of Halley's Comet.
