Would Labour win an early election? Here’s what the polls say

Log in A sustained polling boost for the new government is fuelling speculation that Andy Burnham is considering an early general election, as a fall in popularity for Reform UK continues. Labour has remained consistently above Nigel Farage’s party for approval since Mr Burnham’s coronation in July, when Labour overtook Reform for the first time since March 2025. Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch has claimed that the prime minister could call an election as soon as next May, although Mr Burnham has recently said he wants to “knock dead” the possibility. The latest poll from YouGov shows a resurgence for Reform, at 25 points to Labour’s 24, although an average of results from other top pollsters continues to indicate declining support for the party. Andy Burnham’s leadership has also affected the ratings of Zack Polanski’s Green Party, which has seen a drop in support over the past few months. The government has said it does not intend to call an early election. However, Labour sources recently told The Independent that the by-election to replace Sir Keir Starmer in Holborn and St Pancras is being seen as a test run for the possibility of a nationwide vote. Sir Keir announced that he would be leaving parliament earlier this month, after resigning as prime minister and Labour leader to make way for Mr Burnham. Mr Polanski has confirmed he will stand as the Green candidate for the seat next month. Opposition parties also appear to be preparing for the country to go to the polls early, with Liberal Democrat leader Sir Ed Davey revealing a manifesto working group has already been formed. “We don’t normally do it this early,” he told The Guardian. Meanwhile, Reform sources have also suggested Mr Burnham could be convinced to call an early election depending on the outcome of the London by-election. They told The i that if Labour’s candidate beats Mr Polanski “convincingly”, he could be dismissed as a “dud” and convince Labour that the progressive vote was uniting behind them once again. A Tory source also told the paper: “[Burnham’s] whole thing is to consolidate the left. If he continues to constrict the Green vote then it will tempt him into an early election whilst the right remains split.” Under Sir Keir’s leadership, polling showed Labour began to lose support from left-leaning voters over issues such as Gaza and welfare spending. This coincided with a boost to the Greens, who recorded a high of around 14 per cent approval in April this year, based on an average of top pollsters, compared to a Labour low of 19 per cent in the same month. Pollsters have broadly forecast that Labour will win the Holborn and St Pancras by-election, having taken nearly 50 per cent of the vote in the seat in 2024. However, the picture has changed since then. The Greens took three London councils from Labour at the local elections earlier this year, and increased its share of seats in Camden – home to Holborn and St Pancras – from one to 11 at the contest, while Labour held overall control. Will Labour call an early election? There are clear upsides for Mr Burnham in calling an early election, particularly if Labour holds the seat of Holborn and St Pancras. The new prime minister has pledged to make sweeping changes. However, after he secured the role with just 24,000 votes from the people of Makerfield – and ran uncontested to be Labour leader – his opponents have argued that he does not hold a mandate from the nation to pursue his agenda. A general election victory would inarguably grant this, plus an extra two years to deliver the policies he has started to set out. Under present rules, the latest the next general election can take place is in 2029. Polling suggests it would also be an inopportune time for two of Labour’s key political opponents – Reform and the Greens – given falling support for both parties. Reform’s approval now sits at its lowest in 20 months, at an average of 23 points, and the Greens at 10 points, down from 14 in April. The Conservatives, meanwhile, have begun to plateau at around 20 points, and the Lib Dems at around 11. However, polling expert Sir John Curtice has cast doubt on Labour optimism in the face of these trends, telling The Independent ahead of the party’s conference that there is still a chance for Reform UK to become the biggest party at a nationwide contest. “The numbers are just not there,” he said. Labour's disastrous local election results – where it lost 38 councils – saw it drop support in places where it was previously the strongest party, Sir John explained. This pattern means Reform could “still do perfectly well in terms of seats”.
