Vain rapist Russell Brand begged prison guards to let him use special hair loss shampoo as he fears going bald

Article text

SHAMELESS Russell Brand begged prison guards to let him use specialist shampoos and hair loss pills over fears his trademark long locks will fall out behind bars. The rapist former comedian, 51, had his £60-a-month anti-baldness aids confiscated after he was convicted of rape earlier this month, The Sun understands. Sources said Brand, caged for ten and a half years last week, pleaded with warders to let him have Alpecin caffeine shampoo and Propecia tablets, a prescription drug used to treat male pattern baldness. But despite his pleas he had the drug, which is only available on a private prescription, swiped before he was locked up in HMP Wandsworth in South West London. One insider said: “He was kicking off because he’s worried he’s going to lose his hair. “He takes Propecia pills for his hair, but he had them confiscated inside. Read More on Showbiz “He isn’t allowed to take them because they’re not essential. They’re considered cosmetic. “He asked to have his special Alpecin caffeine shampoo and his Propecia pills.” Prisoners arriving for their first night behind bars undergo healthcare assessments, including a review of prescribed medication. Any pills have to be prescribed from the prison chemist and cannot be taken in from lags on the outside. Most read in Showbiz Finasteride, the drug in Propecia, is not available on the NHS for hair loss because baldness is classed as a cosmetic problem instead of a medical one. Healthcare at HMP Littlehey, in Cambridgeshire, is run by Northamptonshire Healthcare NHS Foundation Trust, with Brand unable to access any private medical care. Experts say those who stop taking the daily pill can expect hair loss problems to return within six to 12 months. A month’s supply of Propecia costs up to £60, while Alpecin’s popular caffeine shampoo, which the German brand claims helps halt hereditary hair loss, is £6.49 a bottle. Brand’s backcombed mane has been his trademark look since he shot to fame on MTV and Big Brother’s Big Mouth in the 2000s. In 2010, he was pictured with a box of Propecia 1mg tablets – the standard, once-a-day dose – poking out of the inside pocket of a sheepskin coat as he stopped to sign an autograph for a fan. On Sunday we revealed Brand had been whisked in a secret operation to Littlehey. He was driven 90 miles from Wandsworth in South West London, where he had been held alone in a segregation unit to stop fellow lags attacking him. Wearing a grey prison tracksuit, he was marched by ten guards to a single cell on E Wing, where new arrivals spend their early weeks, and is said to have asked for extra blankets on his first night. Another source said: “Brand looked like he was s****ing himself as he was led to his cell. “He’s got his own cell and he’s not interacted with any of the other inmates yet.” It is unclear if Brand will be handed back his supply after his move on Friday to HMP Littlehey, a far softer Category C prison. Inmates at Littlehey can play football on an astroturf pitch, do yoga, sing in a choir and take origami classes. Retired prison governor Vanessa Frake said she was “absolutely flabbergasted” he had been sent to a Category C jail so quickly. She added: “He has no previous convictions, but he has got a substantial sentence, and there’s possibly more in the pipeline. “I’d have thought Category B would have been more appropriate.” Brand had been assessed for a move to HMP Wakefield, dubbed “Monster Mansion”, where Lostprophets paedo Ian Watkins was killed last year. But it is understood that safety fears led prison bosses to opt for the softer jail. He was convicted on November 5 of raping one woman and sexually assaulting three others between 2004 and 2009. Sentencing him last Monday, Mr Justice Joel Bennathan told him he had become a “sexual predator” and said he would serve two-thirds of his sentence, seven years, before being eligible for release on licence. Brand’s wife Laura, 39, can visit him once a week but faces a 180-mile round trip from their Oxfordshire home. The Ministry of Justice declined to comment. The Department of Health and Social Care was contacted for comment.
