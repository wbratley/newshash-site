Met Office warns UK households ‘act now’ before ‘deep snow’ on Tuesday

Article text

Met Office warns UK households ‘act now’ before ‘deep snow’ on Tuesday Households have been urged that conditions can change very quickly and you should make sure you are stocked up on essentials as temperatures are set to plummet Brits have been urged to 'act now' and be prepared as the country braces for a -6C chill, with snow warnings in place in numerous areas across the country on Tuesday. The warning is in place from 12am to 12pm on Tuesday and stretches from the West Midlands to southern Scotland. According to reports, depths of up to around 10 inches of snow are forecast in certain locations. According to the Met Office, some roads and railways likely to be affected with longer journey times by road, bus and train services - there is also a small chance of power outages or degradation of mobile phone service. The Met Office has warned that households could potentially be without food, heat or light if they are cut off by snow and can't access services and amenities for a number of days. While the snow may not be this severe, households are being told to "act now and be prepared" and make sure they have the essentials ready. You should be stocked up on torches and batteries, candles and matches or lighters and ensure you have plenty of blankets and warm clothing. If you need to drive somewhere, you should check ahead, plan your route and make sure you leave in plenty of time to account for potentially dicey conditions on the roads. The Met Office states you should always make sure your phone is fully charged, also. Met Office meteorologist Aidan McGivern said in an evening weather update that there will be an “unsettled picture” on Tuesday. He said: “It’s during Monday evening that we’re likely to see some freezing rain or snow over upland parts of mid to North Wales, rain at lower levels, and then as that system moves into the colder air across Northern England and southern Scotland during the early hours of Tuesday, we’re likely again to see some significant and disruptive snow above 300 metres for northern England, above about 150-200 metres for southern Scotland. “That, accompanied by increasingly strong winds, we’re talking about 50-60 mph wind gusts, means that it is going to be hazardous for upland parts of northern England and southern Scotland, with the risk of drifting snow, ice accretion, and blizzards at lower levels.” As there is a chance of power outages on Tuesday, forecasters are asking UK households to make sure they have candles and matches or lighters, torches and batteries and a mobile phone power pack. The Met Office has stressed that weather warnings can change very quickly, and that Brits should be prepared for conditions to worsen. Where is snow forecast on Tuesday?
