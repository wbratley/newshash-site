Alan Milburn review to recommend national insurance cuts for young workers and welfare changes

Unbiased summary

Alan Milburn's independent review into youth unemployment, due to be published on Tuesday, will recommend gradually reducing employer National Insurance contributions for workers aged 21 to 24 over five years, starting with retail and hospitality, and doubling the threshold at which employers begin paying NI for this age group from £5,000 to £10,000 when fiscal conditions allow. The review will also propose cutting the health element of Universal Credit and Personal Independence Payment for under-25s, replacing it with a youth allowance, and providing a legal guarantee of a job, training, or employment support, with benefits sanctions for those refusing help. Work and Pensions Secretary Pat McFadden confirmed some young people will receive less financial support in future. The number of young people not in education, employment, or training is nearly one million, and Milburn has said tackling the crisis will take ten years and billions of pounds.
