Prince Harry makes surprise trip to Ukraine as he defiantly says: 'You're going to win the war!'

Article text

Prince Harry made a surprise appearance in the Ukrainian city of Lviv as he defiantly told troops, "You're going to win the war!" It marks the fourth time the Duke of Sussex has visited the country since Vladimir Putin's invasion began. Prince Harry visited the Superhumans Centre, a specialised medical, prosthetics, and rehabilitation facility in the city He also took to the stage at the first international War Trauma Conference at the All-Ukrainian War Trauma Center. He told the cheering crowd: "'Every single time I come back to Ukraine, I feel welcome, I feel warmth. "You're going to win the war and then you're going to be set up for success." The 42-year-old wore a dark, hooded puffer jacket, dark blue trousers and brown desert boots as he attended a series of events and spoke with veterans. In a school sports hall, he watched wheelchair rugby players as they vied for a place on Ukraine’s team at next year’s Invictus Games, scheduled for Birmingham. Ukraine is planning to send 60 competitors – its largest-ever team – to the 2027 edition of the event. The country has about 1.4 million combat veterans, more than 10 per cent of whom have disabilities. Later on Saturday, Harry told wounded veterans joining the Invictus movement: “When the war is over and when Ukraine wins, we will be ready to support you in holding an Invictus Games.” At the rugby game, he hugged a player he recognised, Valery Odainyk, a former contestant at the Invictus Games in Canada in 2025, who has an amputated leg. Mr Odainyk said afterwards that he told Prince Harry his involvement in the sport “all started and continues with the Invictus Games”. The Duke also spoke with a pickleball player who has both legs amputated below the knee and recently had two prosthetics fitted. Kneeling to be at the same level, Prince Harry asked whether he had played sport before his injury and said, "Sport is good for muscles," tapping him lightly on the chest. The 36-year-old veteran, Radik Fateyev, said of the meeting with Harry: "It was cool; we chatted, and he asked me how I was playing. I said I had already won twice. He asked me to come to England for the pickleball tournament." The player, from Zaporizhzhia in southern Ukraine, added: "I think I will go; it’s very interesting." Prince Harry, who also posed for selfies with players, said: "You can see by the reaction how much sport and how much this community means to them so I’m just really proud. You can see it on their faces, you can see it in their confidence, you can see it in their joy. “The key thing is to keep that going, not only for Invictus trials but all the way to the games and then beyond as well.” The Duke then joined in a game himself, taking off his jacket to show a grey flannel shirt.
