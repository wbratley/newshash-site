Prince Harry hails Ukraine’s wounded veterans as ‘role models’ during surprise visit

Article text

Prince Harry hails Ukraine’s wounded veterans as ‘role models’ during surprise visit The Duke of Sussex, Prince Harry, watched players in the western city of Lviv compete in the black and yellow colours of the Invictus Games, which he founded to help wounded military and veterans Prince Harry on Sunday on the second day of a surprise visit to Ukraine hailed the country's wounded veterans as "role models" with "so much to give to society". The Duke of Sussex watched players in the western city of Lviv compete in the black and yellow colours of the Invictus Games, which he founded to help wounded military and veterans. Ukraine was holding heats to select players in disciplines including the new sport of pickleball to compete at next year's international Invictus Games in Birmingham in England. Harry has been to Ukraine three times before, more any other British royal. As the final winners were named at a closing ceremony, the audience broke out into a spontaneous chant of "Garry, Garry" as his name is pronounced in Ukrainian. Ukraine will field a record team of 60 players in Birmingham as the war leads to ever-increasing numbers of ex-soldiers with amputated limbs and other disabilities. "That room is full of role models for society," Harry told journalists after watching pickleball players, some playing in wheelchairs, others with amputated arms and prosthetic legs. The prince founded the Invictus Games in 2014 inspired by his experience serving in the military. Referring to the some three million veterans in Ukraine, Harry said: "There's a huge amount of work to do... Those three million individuals have so much to give to society. Through Invictus we're going to play the best role we can in that process." Calling for support for Ukraine's military to extend to veterans, he said: "We've invested in them before they put the uniform on, we must invest with them once the uniform comes off." Ukrainians have "learned to live with war", said the prince, on his fourth visit since Russia's invasion. He continued: "Every single person in Ukraine is playing their part in this war. The rest of the world will continue to support you. In my mind you've already won, I hope the war is going to come to an end." Wearing black jeans and a black padded hooded jacket with the Invictus Games logo and brown boots, Harry spent time talking to one pickleball player with both legs amputated below the knee who recently had two prosthetic legs fitted. The prince asked him if he had done sport before his injury and said "sport is good for muscles," knocking him lightly on the chest. The veteran, Radik Fateyev, 36, said: "It was cool, we chatted, he asked me how I was playing. I said I had already won twice. He asked me to come to England for the pickleball tournament." The player, from Zaporizzhia in southern Ukraine who recently had prosthetic legs and a bionic knee fitted, added: "I think I will go (to the Invictus Games), it's very interesting." The prince told reporters: "You can see by the reaction how much sport and how much this community means to them so I'm just really proud. You can see it on their faces, you can see it in their confidence, you can see it in their joy." "The key thing is to keep that going, not only for Invictus trials but all the way to the games and then beyond as well." Harry posed for selfies with players and then joined in a game himself, taking off his jacket to show a grey flannel shirt. He played against two men who each had an amputated leg, teaming up with a man with one arm amputated. He smashed in some shots to applause. Oleksandr, a veteran from Odesa with an amputated leg, said: "I asked him to play and he didn't refuse. "It's good, he is an open person, he talks to people, he isn't like a royal personage. He loves ordinary people. He is completely different." The prince said organisers of the heats "haven't been able to do it all around the country because of security concerns." But he said to "bring so many veterans and service personnel together is absolutely amazing." Volodymyr Fatkiv, 31, who has a paralysed arm said: "It's the first time that I play pickleball, but I like it." From Lviv, he served in a unit made up of police. In 2024, in the Donetsk region in Torets, he came under fire from Russians. He recalled: "A bullet hit me near my heart and then came out in my back, and I don't know how I stayed alive.... Sport is very helpful for rehabilitation and now I help other veterans," adding that he dreams of going to the Invictus Games in Birmingham.
