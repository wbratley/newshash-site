England banish Argentina demons with gritty win over Spain after Elliot Anderson self destructs

Article text

England banish Argentina demons with gritty win over Spain after Elliot Anderson self destructs SPAIN 2-3 ENGLAND: Elliot Anderson's red card made it harder for The Three Lions, but their backs-to-the-wall defending saw them over the line in Madrid This should have gone down as one of England’s greatest nights. Instead, it became torture as well as being unforgettable as England’s heroic 10 men overhauled the world champions with a sensational performance. Harry Kane, Ezri Konsa and Ben White put them 3-0 up and in sight of one of their best and most convincing victories on foreign soil. Then Elliot Anderson pressed the self-destruct button by getting himself sent off after some reckless defending by Jarell Quansah and England made us all suffer once more. Blimey, can’t they ever make life easy? Mikel Oyarzabal scored the penalty, Nico Williams got a second and suddenly England were manning the barricades as if they were back at the World Cup on that famous night in Mexico. But, unlike that woeful semi-final surrender to Argentina, this time Thomas Tuchel’s braves somehow held out for a famous, gritty and heroic victory. JOIN US ON FACEBOOK! Latest news, analysis and much more on Mirror Football's Facebook page England keeper James Trafford made a string of heroic saves as England saw it out to see off Spain and top their Nations League Group with a famous win in the Metropolitano Stadium. Just to remind England of the task in front of them, Spain paraded the World Cup in front of the Metropolitano Stadium before kick-off. The reigning world and European champions went into this game on the back of a 43 game unbeaten run and you could understand why Thomas Tuchel described it as “the biggest test in world football.” Tuchel’s men just took the game to Spain from the start with a high defensive line, a high press which showed no respect to the world champions. They put them to the sword. England were fast out of the blocks, Spain looked slightly bewildered and were caught on the back foot. Tuchel’s men were on a mission. This night will live long in the memory. Within the first two minutes, Jude Bellingham was denied by an incredible save from Spain keeper Simon. Declan Rice sent over a corner, the ball carried to Bellingham whose shot looked destined for the net until Simon’s sensational reflex stop. It was all England from then on. It was surely one of the best first halves of football England have produced - and showed just what they are capable of. Lewis Hall typified England’s dominance by owning the left flank as he put Lamine Yamal in his back pocket. Rice was booked for a foul on Yamal and the Barcelona teenager turned to the crowd to try and whip up the home support because they had been left painfully quiet. And suddenly the 3,515 travelling fans were making all the noise. Elliot Anderson won the ball, Marc Cucurella screamed foul but Kane led a charge, cleverly waited for the challenge and was brought down by Pau Cubarsi. It was a clear penalty. But there was huge pressure on Kane’s shoulders and deafening whistles but none of that stopped England’s Captain Marvel dispatching the spot kick into the far corner in the 44th minute. Spain are not used to losing - and took it badly. Cucurella was still protesting and it took giant Dan Burn to intervene and stop a bust-up with Ben White and Spanish players as they headed down the tunnel at half time. England’s major problem at the World Cup was not seeing the job through. But clearly all the disappointment from that Argentina collapse did not haunt them as much as inspire them this time. Rice sent over another corner and there was Ezri Konsa racing into the box and headed powerfully into the net. It was an England goal straight from an Arsenal set-piece. Then, five minutes later, England were 3-0 up. Kane held the ball up, Bellingham threaded the ball through to Ben White and the Arsenal defender fired the ball through the legs of the so-called best keeper in the world. But then England became their own worst enemies. And the nightmare of the World Cup came back to haunt them. Jarell Quansah was caught in possession, Elliot Anderson pulled back Yamal as he got himself sent off and up stepped Mikel Oyarzabal to convert. Suddenly England were down to ten men, there were shades of Mexico in the summer and a touch of Argentina as they sunk deeper with a man less. It soon became torture. Nico Williams set up a grandstand finish as he made it 2-3 against England’s 10 men but they somehow got the job done.
