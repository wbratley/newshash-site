Uefa agrees to boycott Fifa competitions if World Cup sell-off plans proceed

Article text

European countries have agreed to boycott all Fifa competitions if Gianni Infantino pushes forward with his proposals to sell chunks of the World Cup to US-based private investment. In an extraordinary development all 55 Uefa members resolved not to participate in future World Cups or other Fifa-run events, saying in a statement that they “unanimously and unequivocally reject” the proposals. An emergency meeting that lasted just short of two hours ended with a united front despite earlier fears that smaller countries, attracted by the money on offer through Infantino’s scheme, would be swayed in favour. European FAs are expected to write to Fifa this week with a formal notification of their threat to boycott future tournaments unless the planned World Cup sell-off is withdrawn. It means Infantino faces an uphill battle to push his plan forward. Countries had been given until 19 September to sign up but there will now be significant pressure to drastically revise his scheme or withdraw it completely. While the majority of European FAs had submitted letters of support in favour of his re-election next March, such a decisive line in the sand from Uefa weakens his position dramatically. Uefa’s statement confirmed the decision. “As a result of today’s discussion, no Uefa national teams will participate in any Fifa competition for so long as these proposals remain alive, unless this proposal has been abandoned in its entirety and binding assurances have been given that Fifa will never again open its governance or competitions to private ownership,” it read. Thursday’s meeting was introduced by the Uefa president, Aleksander Ceferin, with executive committee members and Uefa’s Fifa council representatives also addressing delegates. Those early speeches are understood to have set the tone, with around 20 further FA heads taking the floor and multiple sources describing an atmosphere of “unity”. Among those to offer their view was Debbie Hewitt, the president of the FA, who one attendee said gave a “very tough” exposition in favour of a boycott. That was backed up in a statement by an FA spokesperson that read: “We stand shoulder to shoulder with our European colleagues and fully support the collective view. We oppose Fifa’s plans – the Fifa World Cup belongs to football and always will.” Along the lines spelled out by Uefa, at senior level the proposed boycott would begin with next year’s Women’s World Cup, while the first age-group Fifa tournament to be boycotted is likely to be the Under-20 Women’s World Cup. It is due to take place in Poland from 5 September with six of the 24 competitors, including England, due to represent Uefa countries. England’s women’s team also have a two-legged World Cup qualifying playoff tie against Greece in October, which would be a major test of the FA’s resolve if Fifa refuse to back down. The European governing body invited other confederations to follow their lead. Uefa are understood to be confident that a number of Asian countries, in particular, are similarly minded. Infantino’s strongholds traditionally lie outside Europe and a collapse in support elsewhere could have severe consequences for the Fifa president. “National associations around the world are now presented with an ultimatum: accept the irreversible capture of football’s greatest competitions or bear the consequences,” Uefa said. “This is not a ‘democratic decision’, but governance by intimidation – an act of coercion unworthy of an institution entrusted with the stewardship of the global game.” Sources within European football believe Uefa’s decision increases the chances of a Europe-backed candidate running against Infantino in next year’s Fifa election, which he had been expected to sail through without opposition. The North, Central American and Caribbean confederation, Concacaf, also unanimously rejected Fifa’s proposal after an emergency meeting chaired by the president, Victor Montagliani, who is seen as a potential successor to Infantino, but stopped short of following Uefa’s lead by announcing a boycott. Despite this, it is understood that the majority of Concacaf’s 41 members are losing confidence in Infantino’s leadership of Fifa, raising the possibility that his own position could be in serious doubt for the first time since he took office in 2016. A number of European national associations are understood to have held talks with their respective governments over the matter, many of whom have backed a boycott in private. The new UK prime minister, Andy Burnham, was the first politician to criticise Fifa’s plan on Tuesday evening, writing on X that the World Cup “was never anyone’s to sell”. “This is a principled decision that we strongly support. Football belongs to the fans, not billionaire investors. Enough is enough. It’s time to take a stand to protect our game.”
