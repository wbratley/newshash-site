Heartbroken family of British woman found dead in suitcase pay tribute to 'loving' daughter

Article text

Heartbroken family of British woman found dead in suitcase pay tribute to 'loving' daughter Greek detectives believe Elisabeth Jane Ross's killer used her phone to send deceptive messages to loved ones after her death Don't Miss Most Read The heartbroken family of a British woman whose body was found inside a suitcase in Athens have paid tribute to their "beautiful, loving and kind" daughter. Elisabeth Jane Ross, 38, from Scotland, was discovered inside luggage in the Kypseli district of the Greek capital on July 18 after a homeless man spotted what he believed to be "a human limb" protruding from the suitcase and alerted police. Investigators believe Ms Ross had been dead for between five and seven days before her body was discovered. Her aunt, Anne Bangham, said the family were struggling to come to terms with the tragedy. "It is horrific. We just can't believe it. She was such a beautiful, loving and kind young lady," she wrote. "I wish I could get my hands on whoever did it." Ms Bangham said her niece had only recently arrived in Greece for work, adding that the reality of what had happened would "take a while to sink in". "She had not long arrived there to work. We can't believe this has happened," she wrote. Elisabeth Jane Ross, 38, from Scotland, was discovered inside luggage in the Kypseli district of the Greek capital |Ms Ross travelled from the US to Athens via Edinburgh on June 29, where she had planned to stay with friends. Earlier reports suggesting she had travelled through Cyprus were later corrected. She initially stayed with friends in Keratsini, a suburb of Athens, before reportedly meeting American friends in Kypseli on July 15, just days before her body was found. Greek police believe her killer used Ms Ross's mobile phone after her death to send deceptive messages in an apparent attempt to delay the discovery of the crime. One message, sent to a Greek male friend she had known "for years", read: "I need some time - I'm going to a Greek island." Her father, an Edinburgh-based lawyer, told investigators he received messages from her phone between July 15 and July 29. The device has not been recovered, but investigators said it remained active in the Athens area until Ms Ross was formally identified. They believe it was switched off shortly after international media began reporting her death. Toxicology tests are under way to establish Ms Ross's cause of death as detectives continue to investigate the circumstances surrounding her killing.
