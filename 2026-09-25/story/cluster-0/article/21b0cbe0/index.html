Far-right activist Daniel Thomas charged over alleged dinghy slashing in Channel

Article text

The far-right agitator and anti-migrant activist Daniel Thomas has been charged with criminal damage and failing to disclose his phone pin to police following an incident in the Channel on Tuesday, where he allegedly slashed a dinghy while a French rescuer was on board. Thomas, 37, of no fixed abode, has been charged with one count of criminal damage along with an offence under section 53 of the Regulation of Investigatory Powers Act 2000 (Ripa), for allegedly failing to comply with a notice to disclose a phone pin to police. He has been remanded in custody to appear via videolink at Basingstoke magistrates court on Friday. The charge came as the Home Office recorded 781 arrivals in 10 boats on Wednesday, the highest number for a single day so far this year. Responding to the figures, the government said it would “push further and harder” to crack down on the people organising the crossings. Chris Derrick, of the Crown Prosecution Service, said: “The CPS has decided to prosecute Daniel Thomas following an investigation by Hampshire constabulary into an incident on Tuesday 22 September. “Our prosecutors have worked to establish that there is sufficient evidence to bring the case to court and that it is in the public interest to pursue criminal proceedings. “We remind all concerned that proceedings against the defendant are active and he has the right to a fair trial.” Hundreds of people gathered outside Basingstoke investigation centre, where Thomas was believed to be held, on Thursday evening. They included scores of mainly men dressed all in black, though the vast majority were not covering their faces. One person said he had come from Manchester to take part in the protest. Some chanted: “Free DT” and “Oh, Danny, Danny”. One person had brought a sign reading: “Get Danny out.” Extra police were drafted in from forces as far away as Kent, and a police drone hovered above the protest. Thomas’s livestreamed footage appeared to show him approaching one of the dinghies, which was empty, shouting: “Let’s get rid of that boat.” After the rescue worker got onboard, Thomas shouted: “Get off the boat. You’ve got time to get off the boat.” His footage shows his boat speeding away before the rescue worker climbed back on to a larger boat. Thomas, who goes by the name Danny Tommo, had spent the afternoon in a boat, filming himself as he tracked two dinghies carrying asylum seekers from France to the UK. The livestreamed footage was watched by more than 200,000 people. A longtime associate of fellow far-right activist Stephen Yaxley-Lennon, AKA Tommy Robinson, Thomas this summer gathered dozens of nationalist protest groups under the umbrella anti-immigration movement Patriot Platform. The organisation was behind marches this month in Dover and Portsmouth, when masked men in black gathered and protested over immigration and asylum policy. Thomas repeatedly told his followers they should march silently, without violence, and avoid doing anything that could lead to arrests. Thomas apparently managed to film and upload footage of his own arrest before police were able to stop him. On Wednesday night a 56-second clip was uploaded to his YouTube platform showing a police officer telling him he was being arrested on suspicion of criminal damage and “recklessly endangering someone’s life”. Thomas said: “I understand. I knew this was coming.” The latest Home Office figures put renewed focus on the government’s handling of small boat arrivals in the UK. A government spokesperson said: “Our progress this year in tackling small boats is indisputable, with crossings down by 42% and the lowest recorded over the summer months since 2020. “But this week’s events show that while the peaks we are seeing are far below those of previous years, we need to push further and harder in order to eradicate this vile trade.” Wednesday’s arrivals take the provisional total for Channel crossings for the year so far to 18,565. Home Office data indicates this is down 42% on this time last year (32,188) and 26% below the figure at this stage in 2024 (25,165). This week ministers have also been concerned by two uncontrolled landings from the Channel in two days. Most small boats carrying asylum seekers to the UK are escorted to shore by rescue services or Border Force vessels, and no unescorted small boats had been recorded since 6 September 2025. The education secretary, Lucy Powell, said: “You can’t stop everything all of the time,” after 21 people were detained when a boat evaded Border Force to reach the beach of Samphire Hoe in Dover on Wednesday. Another dinghy made an uncontrolled landing in Folkestone on Monday morning. The home secretary, Shabana Mahmood, has ordered an urgent review by Border Security Command, describing the failure to intercept the two boats as “not acceptable”. Speaking on BBC Radio 4’s Today programme, Powell said: “Of course, it’s a concern. That’s why the home secretary has demanded and ordered this urgent review, because this is not what we want to see. This is not acceptable.” On Thursday a 42-year-old man was jailed after throwing a plastic bottle at a police line and swearing at officers during an anti-migrant protest in Portsmouth. Del Somerville, of Paulsgrove, was sentenced at Portsmouth crown court to two years in prison, having previously pleaded guilty to the charge of violent disorder. He is the first to be sentenced over violence that erupted after the arrival of a small boat in the Hampshire city on 6 September. Judge Daniel Sawyer told him his sentence would act as a deterrent to others after subsequent protests in the area. Describing Somerville as a “thug”, he told the defendant: “Police officers dedicate their lives to the protection of the public regardless of their own views, the very least they can expect is that those who engage in protests treat them with respect and the courts punish those who assault them.”
