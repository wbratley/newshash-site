Moment migrant abandons child before scrambling onto dinghy - as record 781 cross Channel on small boats in a single day

Article text

- See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source Video footage captured the moment a man abandoned a child before scrambling onto a dinghy in France – as it was confirmed nearly 800 small-boat migrants reached Britain in a single day. Harrowing and chaotic footage recorded off a beach in France shows the man clutching a toddler as he attempts to board the small boat. In chest-deep water, and amid heavy swell, he then lets go of the child who is left floating in the sea for a few seconds, face up. Members of the French lifeboat organisation, the SNSM, then swim up and pluck the child from the water. The youngster, who appears to be aged three or four, is then taken back to shore on a rescue board and the man resumes his attempt to clamber aboard the dinghy. It is not known whether there is any relationship between the man and the child, who was dressed in a black, hooded anorak and an orange lifejacket. The video clip was filmed off Berck beach, about 30 miles south of Boulogne, on Monday afternoon. The chilling scenes came as the Home Office confirmed 781 migrants arrived on Wednesday, beating the previous high for this year of 752 on July 29. It means 1,085 migrants reached Britain across the Channel in the first three days of this week. The figures are embarrassing for Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood, who claimed only three weeks ago that Labour's tactics in the Channel had begun to work. On September 2 she said: 'We are boosting officer numbers to record highs to crack down on small-boat launches and the gangs behind them. This work is bearing fruit.' The 781 arrivals were more than five times the average number seen on a 'red day' – when weather conditions and other factors make Channel crossings more likely – in the first eight months of this year. Just two weeks ago the Home Office published statistics trumpeting the fall in numbers on red days. It said that from January 1 to the end of August the average number of crossings on a red day had fallen 'significantly' to 144. Yet in the wake of the latest figures the Government insisted its methods are working. A spokesman said: 'Our progress this year in tackling small boats is indisputable – with crossings down by 42 per cent and the lowest recorded over the summer months since 2020. 'But this week's events show that while the peaks we are seeing are far below those of previous years, we need to push further and harder in order to eradicate this vile trade.' But Shadow Home Secretary Chris Philp said the number of small-boat migrants to have entered Britain under Labour - at 83,279 - has now topped the number of full-time soldiers in the British Army. 'Labour has let an entire Army-sized population cross the Channel illegally,' Mr Philp said. 'More people have arrived in small boats than we have soldiers defending Britain. 'That is completely bonkers. 'Labour has lost control of our borders and seems utterly incapable of getting it back. 'The Conservative plan is to end hotels and benefits for illegal immigrants and instead deport them.' On Wednesday ten small boats reached British shores, picked up at the mid-point of the Channel and ferried to Dover by UK Border Force. The surge in crossings may indicate that people-trafficking gangs are no longer experiencing problems obtaining dinghies and other maritime equipment such as outboard motors after a law enforcement crackdown restricted supply routes. This week has also witnessed two 'uncontrolled landings' in which migrant boats reached the UK without being intercepted by the authorities. In a chaotic 48 hours on the Channel, up to 30 migrants reached a Kent beach by dinghy on Tuesday before police arrived on the scene. It came just a day after up to seven migrants reached another location in Kent without being intercepted. Ms Mahmood has ordered an urgent review of how the boats made it across the Channel unchallenged. In further chaotic scenes this week a dinghy launched from near Bayeux, in Normandy, made a record 38-hour voyage. They launched just before 10am on Monday and, on Tuesday afternoon were picked up by RNLI lifeboats and later transferred to a Border Force cutter. The 51 migrants aboard were eventually brought ashore to Dover in the early hours of Wednesday. Education Secretary Lucy Powell insisted the Government was powerless to stop every boat. '[You] can’t stop everything all of the time,' she said on BBC Radio 4’s Today programme. 'Of course, it’s a concern. 'That’s why the Home Secretary has demanded and ordered this urgent review, because this is not what we want to see. This is not acceptable. 'But the overall picture is an improving picture. 'It is hard, difficult work that requires constant, constant effort by our border forces, our security services, police, our partnerships with France and other countries. 'This is a mammoth and huge task, which is now starting to reap some dividends. 'But of course, you know where there are still issues arising, we must absolutely tackle them.' Labour scrapped the Conservatives' Rwanda asylum deal as one of its first acts in office in 2024. Instead, the Government has insisted it can tackle the Channel crisis by increasing law enforcement activities both here and in Europe. Ms Mahmood signed a new £662million, three-year deal with the French government in April which was designed to improve French beach patrols and prevent crossings. But critics have said the deal does not provide the British taxpayer with value for money and French gendarmes have been seen failing to intervene as dinghies launch towards Britain. Wednesday's 781 arrivals was the highest daily total since December 20 last year when 803 reached the UK. The all-time record arrivals day was 1,305 on September 3, 2022.
