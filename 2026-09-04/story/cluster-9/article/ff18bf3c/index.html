The feminist icon and women's rights activist died at the age of 92 on Wednesday. After the news broke on Thursday morning, a string of famous names remembered Steinem and her influence and legacy. Witherspoon posted a throwback photo of herself and Steinem posing with their arms around each other in the Oval Office of the White House. 'Gloria Steinem changed how women saw themselves and what was possible…I was one of them,' the actress and producer shared. 'When I was deciding whether to do Legally Blonde, I watched a documentary where Gloria spoke about the importance of seeing an underestimated woman become the hero of her own story,' she continued. 'Her words changed how I saw Elle Woods and helped me say yes.' Steinem died peacefully at her home in New York City surrounded by 'some of the many that loved her,' according to her and her foundation's social media pages. In 1963, she rose to fame after working undercover as a Playboy bunny for 11 days. Her two-part groundbreaking expose, A Bunny's Tale, detailed the poor pay and sexist treatment that Playboy bunnies, originally waitstaff, endured. Much of her life as an advocate was spent on the road, speaking at college campuses and community centers, at rallies and marches, with her trademark streaked hair parted down the middle and large aviator glasses adding to her recognizable presence.
