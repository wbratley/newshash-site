Earl Spencer's memoir alleges Charles's behaviour after Diana's death; Palace responds

Unbiased summary

Earl Spencer's forthcoming memoir 'Swan Song: Diana, My Sister' includes claims about King Charles's conduct after Princess Diana's 1997 death, including an alleged remark that 'we'll forget her soon enough' and a 'giddy' tone. Buckingham Palace issued a rare statement questioning Spencer's account, attributing possible distortion to 'fraternal grief.' Spencer defended his claims in interviews with the BBC and CBS, saying he is an 'eyewitness to history' and not resentful, while also stating he knows more about the King that he omitted from the book. He said the claims would not surprise Princes William and Harry. Queen Camilla made a separate speech about togetherness, widely seen as a subtle response. The book is due for release this week.
