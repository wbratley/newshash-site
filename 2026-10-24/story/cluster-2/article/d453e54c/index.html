Prince William proves he's king of the sofa promoting his ecology awards on Graham Norton - as viewers hail 'down to earth' heir for being 'up for a laugh'

Article text

Prince William revealed he once dreamed of becoming a police officer and secretly posts comments on Aston Villa fan forums as he made a surprise appearance on The Graham Norton Show. The Prince of Wales, 44, swapped royal engagements for the famous red sofa on Friday night, delighting the studio audience as he showed off his more relaxed side alongside Hollywood stars Cate Blanchett, Hannah Waddingham and Robert Irwin. The future King, who was greeted by gasps when Norton announced his arrival, opened up about his unlikely childhood career ambitions, his love of football and the television programmes that have his children hooked. And in a revelation likely to delight Aston Villa supporters, William admitted he secretly joins discussions on online fan forums, allowing him to share his passion for the Premier League club away from the spotlight. The Prince also confessed that before accepting his royal destiny, he had considered a career in the police. He later also developed ambitions of becoming a DJ, although his attempts to establish himself behind the decks were apparently less than successful. 'Then I went through a phase of wanting to be a DJ, which didn't go very well. I was called DJ Xara, with an X,' he revealed. But his confession failed to impress Ted Lasso star Hannah Waddingham, who jokingly responded: 'Oh that's rubbish.' Charlotte and Louis are obsessed with it. It's really good telly,' he said. His laid-back performance was welcomed by royal fans, with viewers praising the future monarch for appearing approachable and willing to poke fun at himself. One social media user described his appearance as 'a bit of fresh air', adding that he 'brought positive energy and did well for someone who's not used to the TV show format'. The show, which also featured a performance from singer Jorja Smith, went out on BBC One at 10.40pm on Friday Another praised the 'feeling of normalcy' surrounding William, while one admirer described him as 'the fun and cool and unproblematic royal'. William's appearance marks a departure from the more traditional royal interviews and engagements usually associated with heirs to the throne. He is only the second future British monarch to take a seat on a television chat show sofa, following in the footsteps of his father, King Charles. The Prince has increasingly embraced less formal appearances in recent years, including interviews on sports podcasts and BBC Radio 1, as he attempts to connect with audiences beyond the usual royal circles. But his latest television outing has also prompted speculation about the timing, coming amid renewed publicity surrounding his estranged brother Prince Harry and sister-in-law Meghan Markle. PR expert Mark Borkowski suggested the appearance could be a 'riposte' to the Sussexes' recent domination of royal headlines following their return to Britain. However, sources close to William have insisted his decision to appear was motivated by his determination to promote the Earthshot Prize rather than any desire to compete with his younger brother. There was no mention of the brothers' estrangement or the possibility of reconciliation during the interview. Instead, the Prince concentrated on the environmental initiative he founded in 2020, which awards £1million each to five winners every year to help develop solutions to major environmental problems. Explaining why he established the awards, William said: 'For many years I was hearing stories about environment issues and one thing that struck me was the negativity and doom which got me down. 'I thought we could do something more powerful and more optimistic and the idea of an awards ceremony came about and we could talk about urgency and optimism together. It's all about getting excited.' He added: 'There are some amazing people out there doing things to protect the world.' His fellow guests all have longstanding connections to the project. Oscar-winning actress Cate Blanchett is a member of the Earthshot Prize council, Australian conservationist Robert Irwin serves as a global ambassador, while Waddingham hosted the 2023 ceremony in Singapore. Singer Jorja Smith also appeared on the programme, performing her single Dancing. This year's Earthshot Prize ceremony will be held in Mumbai next month, with William expected to meet Bollywood stars, learn more about India's film industry and spend time with some of the country's cricketing greats. The event will see £5million awarded to five environmental innovators, while children across India have also been encouraged to devise their own solutions to environmental challenges through the 'I Can Earthshot Challenge'. Previous ceremonies have been staged in London, Boston, Singapore, Cape Town and Rio de Janeiro. And despite the continuing tensions with Harry, William appeared determined to keep the focus on his own family, interests and charitable work. For one evening at least, the future King seemed perfectly happy to trade royal formality for a few jokes on Graham Norton's sofa.
