UK weather live: Britain braced for Storm Boelo as 'danger to life' warning issued

Article text

UK weather live: Britain braced for Storm Boelo as 'danger to life' warning issued The Met Office has issued 'danger to life' amber weather warnings for Storm Boelo, which arrives just after Storm Austen caused serious damage in the South West Storm Boelo is set to bring 80mph winds and 'widespread travel disruption' to the UK this weekend, with forecasters warning of potential flooding, power cuts and problems on the roads. It comes after Storm Austen led to airports being closed, flights cancelled and even Brighton's iconic West Pier partially collapsing. Meanwhile, Prime Minister Andy Burnham warned on Friday that the UK could face “a winter of greater flooding” and said we are living in a world where “unexpected events” are “more and more common”. An amber weather warning is in place across Scotland on Saturday, along with the north of England down to Carlisle and the north-east of Northern Ireland. It comes just a day after Storm Boelo battered the South West of England, with schools closed and 31,000 homes at one point left without power. Tom Morgan, a Met Office meteorologist, said: “Yesterday’s Storm Austen affected the far south of the UK and northern France and the Channel Islands, whereas today’s Storm Boelo will be affecting the north of the UK." Key Events PM warns of 'winter of great flooding' The Prime Minister warned on Friday that the UK could face “a winter of greater flooding” and said we are living in a world where “unexpected events” are “more and more common”. Andy Burnham denied the suggestion that the Government is playing catch-up with civil preparedness as he attended a summit organised to learn from the summer’s record wildfires. He said: “We are having that conversation about resilience. That’s why we’re holding this summit today, it’s why we’re putting the guidance out to the public. “We potentially are facing a winter of greater flooding, so it’s about recognising all of those risks and taking action.” RNLI issues urgent warning ahead of Storm Boelo RNLI regional water safety lead, Michael Avril, said: “Storm Austen and Storm Boelo are set to bring potentially dangerous conditions so we’re asking the public to stay vigilant and not take unnecessary risks by travelling to coastal locations. “If you are near to the coast or a waterside location, we urge you stay a safe distance from the water as conditions could knock you off your feet or wash you into the sea. “If you find yourself in trouble in the water, remember to Float to Live: Lie back in the water with your head so far back that your ears are wet, extend your arms and legs and try to relax as best as you can until you get control of your breathing before then calling for help or swimming to safety. “If you see someone else in danger – do not enter the water yourself as you could also end up in serious difficulty. “Instead, remember phone, float, throw – phone 999 or 112 and ask for the Coastguard; tell the struggling person to float; throw them something buoyant, like a life ring.” More rail disruption expected across Scotland Rail lines in western and northern Scotland are set to close later as the country braces itself for Storm Boelo, which is expected to sweep in with gusts of up to 80mph. Forecasters have issued an amber warning for much of Scotland on Saturday evening into Sunday as the storm hits, the day after Storm Austen which brought heavy rain to many areas on Friday. Planes cancelled and historic pier collapses as Storm Austen batters South West Those living in the South West of England will be hoping Storm Boelo will not cause as much damage and disruption as Storm Austen. At one stage, 31,000 homes were without power, planes had been cancelled and part of the historic West Pier in Brighton collapsed into the sea. Storm Boelo to batter UK's north Tom Morgan, a Met Office meteorologist, said: “Yesterday’s Storm Austen affected the far south of the UK and northern France and the Channel Islands, whereas today’s Storm Boelo will be affecting the north of the UK. “We have an amber wind warning in place across Scotland later today and through tonight and into the end of Sunday. “With the strong winds, potentially gusts exceeding 80 miles per hour locally, there will be some widespread travel disruption across Scotland, possibly northern parts of Northern Ireland and the far north of England as well through the next 24 to 36 hours. “I’m expecting delays to ferry services, if not cancellations, perhaps some quite widespread impacts to travel on the railways and on the roads as well.” Beach fight breaks out after family 'ignores red flag warnings' A passer-by allegedly attempted to alert a young family about the perils of entering the water at a 'red-flagged' beach in Cornwall as Storm Austen continues to wreak havoc across the UK. A couple accompanied by their two small children were shockingly recorded playing on Polzeath beach wearing wetsuits and carrying bodyboards. You can read more here. Storm Austen begins to ease as Storm Boelo moves in next Storm Austen is beginning to come to an end across the UK after bringing strong winds, travel disruption and power cuts through Friday. Some warnings remain in place into the night, but attention is now turning to Storm Boelo, which is set to bring another spell of wet and windy weather over the weekend. The first Boelo warning comes into force across Northern Ireland at 10am on Saturday, with further yellow and amber wind warnings covering Scotland and parts of northern England later in the day. The Met Office says gusts could reach 70-80mph in exposed coastal areas, with travel disruption, power cuts and dangerous conditions possible. Storm Boelo is expected to remain a threat through Sunday, with warnings currently stretching into the evening. What does an amber weather warning actually mean? The Met Office says an amber warning means there is an increased likelihood of severe weather causing disruption. Possible impacts can include travel delays, road and rail closures, power cuts and a risk to life and property. Forecasters say people should consider changing plans that could be affected by the weather and take steps to protect themselves and their property. The warning colour is based on both the expected impact of the weather and how likely those impacts are to happen. Passengers are facing further disruption as strong winds continue to affect operations. 31,000 homes without power as Storm Austen hits South West More than 31,000 homes were without power across the South West as of 3pm as Storm Austen continued to cause widespread disruption. Strong winds have also brought down trees, disrupted rail services and forced flight cancellations, while parts of Brighton’s historic West Pier collapsed into the sea. Can schools, attractions and events close because of a yellow warning? A yellow weather warning does not automatically mean schools, attractions or events must close. The Met Office says yellow warnings can still cause disruption, while the Department for Education says schools decide locally whether it is safe to open during severe weather. Event organisers and attractions can also cancel or close if conditions such as strong winds, flooding or unsafe travel create a safety risk. What needs to happen for flights to be cancelled during a storm? Flights are not automatically cancelled when winds reach a certain speed, with airlines and airports taking a number of factors into account before deciding whether it is safe to operate. The Met Office says strong crosswinds can make flying conditions more difficult, while severe weather can also force air traffic controllers to increase the distance between aircraft. This reduces the number of flights an airport can handle at one time. It adds that when particularly severe weather is forecast, airports and airlines may agree to cancel some flights in advance to reduce congestion and help the remaining operation run more smoothly. Meanwhile, the Civil Aviation Authority says passengers whose flights are cancelled because of extreme weather are still entitled to care and assistance from their airline. However, weather-related cancellations may count as “extraordinary circumstances”, meaning passengers may not be entitled to fixed compensation. Visitors have been advised to keep an eye on the festival’s social channels for further updates over the weekend.
