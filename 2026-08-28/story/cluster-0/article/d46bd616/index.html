Nepal-Tibet floods live: King Charles 'heartbroken' with 1000s missing including Brit kids A 13-year-old girl and a 14-year-old boy are among 33 British tourists missing in Nepal and Tibet after an avalanche led to major flooding along the Bhotekoshi River King Charles said he and the Queen are "heartbroken" by the "devastating" flash floods in a Nepal border area, where dozens of British nationals are among the hundreds missing and feared dead. At least 389 people have died in Nepal and nearly 1,000 peope are missing, while 558 people are missing in Tibet, where three people have been confirmed dead. On both sides of the border, hundreds of foreign nationals are among those missing, including 33 British citizens. A 13-year-old girl and a 14-year-old boy are believed to be the youngest UK citizens unaccounted for. The Kathmandu-based International Centre for Integrated Mountain Development said river levels rose by up to nine metres in just 30 minutes at the height of the flash floods on Wednesday. According to the Red Cross, "entire villages and market areas" have been wiped out by the torrent of water and mud, which has flattened all in its path along the Bhotekoshi River. Key Events Nepal's National Disaster authority has shared a database that tracks live rescue updates as search and operations continue after Wednesday's horror floods. The catastrophic deluge razed entire villages, swept away massive suspension bridges, and flattened entire power plants. As for the human toll, 469 bodies have been recovered so far. 977 people remain missing with 517 of those foreign nationals. More than 3,000 people have been found alive, the NDRRMA data confirms. More than 30 Brits remain among the hundreds of people missing and feared dead as rescue efforts continue following flooding in the Rasuwa district, which borders China’s autonomous region of Tibet. At least 469 people have died in Nepal and hundreds more are missing after Wednesday's flash flooding in the Himalayan border region, authorities have revealed. Officials are warning that there is a risk of possible further flooding at the China-Nepal border, days after Wednesday's catastophic deluge razed entire villages wiithout warning, as rescuers continue to search for the nearly 1,000 people still missing. China has warned that a barrier lake formed at the confluence of the Tsogyal and Purupqiangzangbu rivers is overflowing and could burst. Nepal's National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority added. Chinese satellite data suggests the river was “blocked” and a lake had formed near the site of the floods. The latest figures out of Nepal have been shared by the mountainous nation's National Disaster authority. As per the latest report, chronicling the disaster reports in the wake of Wednesday's catastrophic flash floods along the Bhotekoshi and Trishuli river banks, the death toll stands at 389 dead and 977 missing. Of the missing, 517 are foreign nationals. Here is a full breakdown of the latest figures from Nepali authorities regarding the Himalayan nation's response to Wednesday's unparralelled emergency. The flooding appears to have been triggered by a glacial collapse and debris flow near the Nepal-China border, according to the United States Geological Survey. A seismic event recorded at 8.37am local time on Wednesday was initially thought to be an earthquake, but the USGS later said further analysis showed “no earthquake had occurred”. It concluded the event was a glacial collapse which sent debris downstream, causing what it described as “subsequent catastrophic impacts”.
