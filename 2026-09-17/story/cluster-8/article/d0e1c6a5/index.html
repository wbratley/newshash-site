Revealed: How a father-of-two became key figure in Britain's anti-migrant protests after asylum seekers were moved onto military base near his home

Article text

- See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source A protester has revealed his transformation into a prominent figure in Britain's anti-migrant movement after becoming angry about asylum seekers being housed locally. Liam Tomsett started organising rallies in Crowborough after hearing that a former military training camp would be used as accommodation for up to 540 men. The father-of-two, who lives in the Sussex commuter town, set up 'Crowborough Says No' with a friend as they tried to stop contractors from working at the centre. But the confrontations soon became violent, leading to Mr Tomsett's arrest – and the demonstrators began facing counter-protests as others condemned their actions. Plans to use the camp for housing migrants were first announced last October by the Government, before the move was confirmed in January - sparking huge protests. Mr Tomsett was followed by filmmaker Jamie Roberts for nine months for a new Channel 4 documentary called 'England 'Til I Die' which airs at 10pm tonight. Speaking about his life before starting the group, he said: 'Last summer, I was just changing jobs, moving house, newly single and going to the odd protest.' Liam Tomsett leads a 'Crowborough Says No' protest against the opening of an asylum camp Liam Tomsett embraces his partner Sharna at a Tommy Robinson rally in London The activists tried to stop contractors working at the centre, in protests which turned violent The utilities manager decided to set up the group after hearing on the radio about plans for the use of the camp, saying: 'I thought something's got to be done about it.' On the blockade, he added: 'We're trying to make people feel uncomfortable about being there. That if you are working there, you are part of the problem.' He was arrested and charged with common assault and possession of an offensive weapon, although the charges were later dropped. On his involvement with the protests, Mr Tomsett also said: 'It's taken over. I've got two grown-up children. I've not been there enough for them. I've lost my father. I don't think I've had time to grieve.' He added: 'Six weeks ago life was very easy. I bought a new place. Got a nice job. Had a few quid in the bank. Don't take anything for granted as things can change very, very quickly.' Mr Tomsett was also filmed interrogating three men who came from the asylum centre to go to church for a Sunday service. He said: 'I wouldn't say I was intimidating them. I was engaging them in conversation. We weren't chasing them. We were walking with them. 'If they were scared, how do you think the women and children feel walking home at night? They are not wanted. End of. How do we know they haven't committed a crime?' Later in the documentary, Mr Tomsett was arrested again for confronting a business owner trying to provide training to asylum seekers, before being released by police with no further action. Mr Tomsett's new partner Sharna also appears in the documentary, saying that he 'opened her eyes fully'. Liam Tomsett addresses demonstrators at an anti-immigration rally in Braintree, Essex Liam Tomsett and others hold a banner in front of the statue of Winston Churchill in London She added: 'It's always in your algorithm. We are dwindling. Which is scary. White British people being taken over by Muslims very, very rapidly.' Mr Tomsett said: 'There is an agenda to replace us. By powers bigger than Government. By globalists.' Asked whether he was prepared to lose his job over his beliefs, he said: 'My beliefs? It's not about beliefs. It is about keeping your community safe. I don't think it is a belief, it is a duty.' It comes as police forces have stepped up planning and intelligence-gathering after Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood warned that anti-migrant protesters are trying to intimidate communities. Crowds of masked men blocked road access to the Port of Dover on September 5, followed by official vehicles being damaged and a handful of police officers being injured in a stand-off with protesters the following night in Portsmouth. In Oxfordshire today, a village which could house more than 1,000 asylum seekers voted overwhelmingly to become an independent state in a symbolic 'referendum'. The people of Piddington voted to secede from the UK after the Government announced plans to house 1,256 male asylum seekers at a nearby former military base. Mr Roberts, who is behind the new Channel 4 documentary, was also a co-director of the controversial BBC documentary Gaza: How to Survive a Warzone. Ofcom sanctioned the BBC for breaching the Broadcasting Code in that documentary after the corporation failed to disclose the 13-year-old narrator was a Hamas official's son. 'England 'Til I Die' is on Channel 4 tonight at 10pm
