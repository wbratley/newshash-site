Vladimir Putin issues chilling warning to Europe just moments after 'world peace' talks with Donald Trump

Article text

Russian President Vladimir Putin has declared that Russia will retaliate with a "more painful response" against Ukraine after three separate strikes on the Crimean bridge. The Kremlin leader made the threat after holding historical trilateral talks with Xi Jinping and Donald Trump at the APEC summit in Shenzhen. Putin said the bridge was used exclusively for civilian purposes and not to transport military equipment, as Kyiv has alleged, or petroleum products. Speaking in China after the talks, Putin made clear: "After the strikes on the Crimean Bridge, we will not negotiate. "We will respond to these strikes. I believe that our response, as I just said, will be much more painful than what Kyiv did to the bridge." A third strike by the Ukrainians forced a suspension of all traffic and train services across the bridge, Russian authorities said. Sergei Aksyonov, Crimea's Russian-installed governor, confirmed the attack earlier today. In a statement, Russia's transport ministry said no one had been hurt in the attack and that specialists were assessing damage to the bridge. The ministry said in a statement on Telegram: "As a result of a nighttime drone attack, train services on the railway section of the Crimean Bridge have been temporarily suspended." On Tuesday, Ukrainian drones and missiles destroyed the central road section of the bridge, forcing its main road arch to crumble into the sea and leaving a gap. Ukraine's first attack on the bridge on Monday caused more minor damage, but gave Ukrainian forces a chance to see how Russian air defences would react. The warning came shortly after Putin met with US President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping in an unprecedented three-way summit, where the three world leaders adopted a joint "peace declaration". LATEST DEVELOPMENTS Speaking after the talks, which lasted approximately 90 minutes, Mr Trump declared that they are "really making history". Putin described the encounter as a new "page in the history of diplomacy," whilst Xi said the three nations "share a common perspective on safeguarding world peace". The declaration, published by Chinese state media and the Kremlin but not yet released by the White House, comprises a preamble and four substantive paragraphs addressing international security, UN reform, energy cooperation and nuclear risk reduction. On UN reform, the three permanent Security Council members pledged to strengthen the organisation's capacity to address global challenges and deliver on its core purpose of preserving international peace and security. The energy provisions commit the three countries to expanding the production and supply of refined petroleum products, with the stated aim of benefiting nations worldwide. The leaders reaffirmed "the importance of the stable flow of energy." Regarding diplomatic protections, the declaration stressed the principle of state consent and the respect owed to the immunities of government officials. It stated that "the International Criminal Court and other similar institutions must not abuse their jurisdiction." On nuclear weapons, the three powers reiterated that "a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought."
