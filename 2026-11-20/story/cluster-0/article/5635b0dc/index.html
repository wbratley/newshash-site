Trump ‘demands Europe stop backing Ukraine during angry call with Nato allies’ after meeting ‘friend’ Putin & Xi

Article text

DONALD Trump reportedly demanded European leaders stop bankrolling Ukraine in a furious phone call just hours after cosying up to his “friend” Vladimir Putin and China’s Xi Jinping. The US president allegedly tore into Nato allies over plans to send billions more to Kyiv, urging them to focus instead on ending the bloody war. Trump reportedly made the explosive intervention from Air Force One as he dialled into a crunch meeting of European leaders in Berlin following his historic summit in China. Two sources described his outburst as a “tirade”, claiming he demanded allies abandon discussions over emergency financial aid and concentrate on his controversial peace plan, according to the Telegraph. The row centred on proposals to plug Ukraine’s staggering €70billion (£62billion) funding gap for 2027. But a third source said Trump was simply arguing that European nations could save billions by securing a peace deal rather than continuing to fund the war. READ MORE IN WORLD NEWS The White House denied the claim, insisting Trump was highlighting the war’s devastating human and financial toll. They said: “The President emphasised that the war is extremely costly in terms of human life and financing, and everyone should all be striving for peace because it will save lives and money that can be invested in our countries’ own defenses and prosperity.” The explosive call came shortly after Trump held unprecedented three-way talks with Putin and Xi in Shenzhen, China. The trio unveiled a four-point peace plan at the historic summit, drawing comparisons to the 1945 Yalta Conference that shaped the post-war world. Most read in The Sun Trump had warmly welcomed the Russian tyrant before the talks, declaring: “He’s my friend. I look forward to our meeting.” He added: “We’ve had a great relationship for many years. We’ve known each other for a long time, and I think a lot of wonderful things can come out of this.” The three leaders later pledged to prevent nuclear war, reform the United Nations and increase global energy supplies. They also agreed that international criminal courts should not “abuse” their powers, despite Putin being wanted by the International Criminal Court over alleged war crimes in Ukraine. But the grand declaration offered no breakthrough on ending Russia’s bloody invasion, sparking fresh alarm among European leaders. Trump nevertheless hailed the summit as a triumph, rating the talks “a 12” out of 10. Putin was equally jubilant, claiming his private meeting with the US president had gone “even better than expected”. The Kremlin described the Russian despot’s mood as “excellent” following their discussions. He said at the summit it was the duty of the “victors of World War Two” to “preserve and strengthen international law“. The dictator added that the three countries will “uphold world peace and stability”. He also claimed that the three have “numerous shared interests in the settlement of regional conflicts, including the conflict in Ukraine”. Xi had started the summit – held at the Qilin Shanzhuang residence – by saying that China, the US and Russia are working together to “meet global challenges”. Trump finished off the opening remarks by saying that he, Xi, and Putin are “really making history” by holding the talks. He also highlighted the importance of nuclear disarmament, saying: “Everybody in this room knows and hopefully to create a better future for all of us. “The world’s three largest nuclear powers by far, the United States, Russia and China, are uniquely positioned to discuss how to reduce the number of nuclear weapons on Earth.” Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen warned that the summit risked emboldening the Russian tyrant. She said: “I think Putin will be even more emboldened after today’s meeting. “He looks happy. Unfortunately, that is not a good sign for world peace. That is not a good sign for Ukraine. “And therefore I think that what will come out of this is that he will hit us Europeans even harder, unfortunately.” Trump’s controversial intervention in Berlin came through the phone of his son-in-law Jared Kushner, who attended the meeting alongside fellow US peace envoy Steve Witkoff. British Prime Minister Andy Burnham and his European counterparts pushed back against Moscow’s demands, insisting their support for Ukraine remained unwavering. Burnham slammed Putin’s sweeping security proposals, which include demands for Nato troops to withdraw from Eastern Europe. He said: “It is not for the aggressor in this conflict to set out wide-ranging and unrealistic security demands.” The PM insisted the coalition was “more united” than ever despite Trump’s reported outburst. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz dismissed the China summit as little more than “a photo opportunity”. French President Emmanuel Macron also rejected suggestions that the talks marked a dramatic shift in the global order. He said: “We must reopen the question of Ukraine’s membership to Nato.” The diplomatic row erupted as Putin threatened a brutal retaliation against Ukraine over fresh strikes on his prized Crimea Bridge. The furious Kremlin leader warned Kyiv would face a response “far more painful” than the damage inflicted on the strategic crossing. He said: “After the strikes on the Crimean Bridge, there will be no agreements. We will respond to these strikes. “And I want everyone to know this: I believe our response will be far more painful than what the Kyiv regime did to the bridge.” Ukraine’s military confirmed it had struck the vital road and rail link for the third time this week. The £3billion crossing, built after Russia’s illegal annexation of Crimea in 2014, has become a prized target for Ukrainian forces. Putin also warned that Moscow would not tolerate European troops being deployed to Ukraine as part of any future peace settlement. His threats came as Russian strikes on Kyiv damaged European embassies in the Ukrainian capital shortly after the China summit.
