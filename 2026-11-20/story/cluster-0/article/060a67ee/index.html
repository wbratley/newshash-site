Russia is intent on attacking us too, Andy Burnham says

Article text

Russia is intent on attacking us too, Andy Burnham says Britain continues to face 'disruption and hybrid attacks' from Russia, Andy Burnham warned last night, as he said Kremlin aggression 'isn't just about Ukraine'. After attending a Coalition of the Willing summit in Berlin, the Prime Minister said he believed all parties could soon 'move forward into meaningful negotiations'. But he warned the UK would have to 'step up to keep the peace' if an agreement is reached to pause the war. He said: 'Let's not forget here, this is about Ukraine, but it's about wider security across the continent and Britain's security too. 'We continue to face disruption, hybrid attacks from Russia. So this isn't just about Ukraine – it's about all of us.' But the PM said there was also 'a degree of alignment that we've not seen in recent times', with US peace envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff 'in the room' in Berlin. Prime Minister Andy Burnham, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz pictured at a joint press conference in Berlin last night US President Trump shook hands with Vladimir Putin last night, after attending a summit with the Russian leader and China's Xi Jinping in Shenzen Donald Trump also dialled into the meeting after his own summit with Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping in China which had alarmed the US's Western allies. Mr Burnham said the US President 'was very clear that the war is a lose-lose for everybody'. He said: 'Nobody is winning this war, including Russia, and he pointed to the very significant casualties on the Russian side.' German Chancellor Friedrich Merz convened the meeting of over 30 allied nations in a show of unity with Ukraine in response to the summit in Shenzhen. He said European powers 'are prepared to hold talks with Russia' and that 'these talks are now set to take place soon.' He added that 'we are looking for locations on neutral ground' and named the United Arab Emirates as a possible location. When asked about the Shenzhen summit, Mr Merz dismissed the meeting as 'above all a photo op'. Burnham, pictured at the Coalition of the Willing summit on Thursday night, warned that Kremlin aggression 'wasn't just about Ukraine' The PM's comments came as President Trump met with Russia and China's leaders, in what the German Chancellor Merz described as 'above all a photo op' Last night Mr Putin invited European leaders to Moscow but did not commit to allowing them a formal role in negotiations. Speaking in Shenzhen, he said Russia had 'suggested through diplomatic channels that the Europeans come to Moscow to define their position... after which we will decide in which format we will continue the dialogue on Ukraine'. But in a further sign of his defiance, he said 'possible military co-operation' between Ukraine and EU countries 'is a problem to which we must pay particular attention'. He insisted Russia would not stand for any foreign troop deployments there after the war and that full normalisation of relations with Europe would not be possible until 'the political elite changes in certain countries'. The Kremlin declined to say which European leaders it had invited to visit Moscow. In another blow, there were reports Mr Trump unleashed a tirade against European leaders when he dialled in. Officials said he was angry at plans by EU and other backers to help cover Ukraine's £60 billion financing gap for next year. He apparently emphasised how much money countries could save if the war ended and they no longer had to provide financial assistance. Volodymyr Zelensky reminded those gathered in Berlin that for all Putin's talk of peace, for the past year 'the Russians have rejected every single proposal for de-escalation'. He said: 'Various partners have discussed this with them, and Putin has spurned every proposal: de-escalation regarding energy infrastructure, de-escalation in the Black Sea, other ceasefire arrangements.' The Ukrainian president was seen embracing Mr Kushner before the meeting, and said he and Mr Witkoff had confirmed Europeans would be present for future negotiations with Russia. 'Europe will be represented by five countries plus the EU and Nato, and America fully understands why strong and co-ordinated European participation in the negotiations is necessary,' Mr Zelensky said. Reiterating his blueprint for a peace deal, Mr Kushner yesterday said Mr Trump was open to an Article 5-style security guarantee for Ukraine. The clause states that an armed attack against one Nato member is an attack against all. Mr Kushner said: 'I do think a security guarantee has the potential to be a cornerstone, and the goal is to make it as strong, as durable as possible.' He added that Russia is 'comfortable' with Nato-like security guarantees. Meanwhile, in a further show of support, Britain committed to delivering 1,000 long-range drones to Ukraine as part of a £10 billion package of support.
