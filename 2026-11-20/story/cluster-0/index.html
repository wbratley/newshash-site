Trump, Xi and Putin hold first trilateral talks; Trump criticised European Ukraine funding in call with allies

Unbiased summary

US President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping held their first-ever trilateral meeting on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen, China, lasting about 90 minutes. They discussed nuclear arms control, the war in Ukraine and US criticism of the International Criminal Court, and issued a joint declaration on peace. Afterwards, Trump dialled into a meeting of the Coalition of the Willing in Berlin from Air Force One. Sources described his remarks as a 'tirade' in which he criticised European plans to fund Ukraine's €70bn financing gap for 2027 and urged focus on a peace deal. The White House denied he demanded an end to aid, saying he emphasised the war's human and financial costs. European leaders said they were ready for talks with Russia, while Putin rejected any European role in US-led peace talks.
