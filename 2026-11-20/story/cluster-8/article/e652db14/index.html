Unseeded Sykes stuns Humphries in Grand Slam thriller

Article text

Unseeded Sykes stuns Humphries in Grand Slam thriller - Published Luke Humphries followed Luke Littler in exiting the Grand Slam of Darts after losing 10-9 to unseeded Tom Sykes in a second-round thriller. Having trailed 4-0 and 9-7, the second seed battled back to force a deciding leg and had the advantage of throwing first. But his fellow Englishman Sykes was unfazed, quickly took control of the leg and when Humphries missed double 10 for a 128 checkout to snatch victory, the world number 81 nervelessly hit double 18 to secure a place in his first major quarter-final. "It's unbelievable," Sykes, 34, told Sky Sports. "Luke let me off a few times in the first bit but I set off really well. "When he got it back to 5-5, I thought 'here we go' and he started turning it on in the last 10 legs, but I'm just buzzing to get over the line. "I was a bit nervous [going for double 18 for the match]. I thought the game was over when I saw Luke hit the the first treble 18, then the second. "But I was so cold on that last one, I thought 'just hit it first dart, no messing about' and I did. I'm riding a wave at the minute and I'll keep trying to ride it." Sykes' victory was all the more impressive given Humphries did not play badly at all, averaging 102.02 and hitting 37% on the doubles. But the Castleford thrower made a flying start, averaging just shy of 108 in the first mini-session, to earn a sizeable lead and maintained his composure as the former world champion fought back. He will face three-time Grand Slam champion Michael van Gerwen in the last eight after the Dutchman beat seventh seed James Wade 10-8. In a high-class contest that saw six 100-plus finishes, Wade earned a 7-5 lead but Van Gerwen, who finished with an average of 103.83, took out 121 to level at 7-7. He broke again to go 9-7 up and although Wade hit a 130 checkout to break back, 10th seed Van Gerwen made it three breaks in a row to avoid a one-leg shootout. With Humphries and Littler gone, third seed Gian van Veen is the top-ranked player remaining in the draw in Wolverhampton. The Dutchman beat Germany's Martin Schindler 10-7 and was completely dominant before a slight wobble as he tried to close out the win. Van Veen raced into a 9-3 lead but then proceeded to lose four legs on the spin, missing three match darts, before eventually finishing the job. England's Nathan Aspinall awaits last year's World Championship finalist in the quarters after a 10-7 win over German Gabriel Clemens. 'The Asp' averaged 100.72 and posted an impressive 53% checkout rate as he came out on top in a back-and-forth match.
