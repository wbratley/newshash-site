Injured Luke Humphries winces in pain as he loses to rank outsider in Grand Slam of Darts

Article text

Injured Luke Humphries winces in pain as he loses to rank outsider in Grand Slam of Darts Luke Humphries has followed Luke Littler in crashing out of the tournament An injured Luke Humphries crashed out of the Grand Slam of Darts after losing 10-9 to rank outsider Tom Sykes. The 2024 Grand Slam champion went into the match with a neck injury and fell 4-0 down, but rallied to force a decider. Humphries had a dart to win it as he almost took out 128 before the world number 72 held his nerve to secure the biggest win of his career. It means the world numbers one and two have gone out on consecutive nights after world champion Luke Littler lost to Jermaine Wattimena on Wednesday. Humphries was hampered throughout by his injury, struggling to bend down at one point after dropping one of his arrows. Wayne Mardle said on Sky Sports commentary: "This afternoon, and as you can see, the way he bent down there, Luke Humphries had a humongous heat pad on his neck. "He was telling me that he had a trapped nerve and it's been released now by a specialist, but he's still struggling. So I don't know how he's going to cope, but when he dropped that dart, you see the way he bent down? There's limited movement." Despite that, Cool Hand battled on and hauled himself back into the match, taking a 6-5 lead. Sykes then won the next three legs to go 8-5 up. At 9-7 down, Humphries won two in a row to force a decider, fitting for a match of such quality and drama. Humphries carved out a chance to win it but missed it, and Sykes coolly mopped it up to set up a quarter-final against another big name - James Wade or Michael van Gerwen. Luke let me off a few times in the first bit. I set off really well, and I got into a 4-0 lead. When he got it back to 5-5, I thought, 'Here we go.' He started turning it on. But, yeah, I'm just buzzing to get it over the line in the end." On needing double 18 to win it, he added: "I was a bit nervous, but obviously I thought the game was over when I turned round and saw Luke hit the first triple 18 there, and then he hit the second one, and then he wired it. "I was so cold on that last one. I just thought, 'Just hit it first dart, no messing about and get into it,' and I did. "It is mental, honestly, 15 years of hard work, and now I'm playing the best guys in the world every week. I'm riding a wave at the minute and I'll keep trying to ride it." Humphries referenced his injury on social media after the match. He wrote on X: Well done @TomSykes180, absolutely amazing performance!! "So proud of the way I played tonight with what I've had to go through with injury the last 48 hours. Gave it my absolute all there. Wish everyone all the best for the rest of the tournament." Sykes is in his first year as a PDC Tour player, making his win over Humphries an even bigger shock than Wattimena's triumph over Littler 24 hours earlier. The 19-year-old was eyeing a third successive title in Wolverhampton, but succumbed to a 10-8 loss to the Dutchman. After winning the first five major titles of the year, Littler looked unbeatable, but he has now lost his cloak of invincibility on the big stage. The Nuke is not due to play again until the opening night of the World Championship on December 11, having failed to qualify for the Players Championships Finals in Minehead at the end of November.
