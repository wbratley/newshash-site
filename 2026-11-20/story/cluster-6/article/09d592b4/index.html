Anthropic shares explode on Wall Street debut as AI giant's total valuation rockets past $2trillion

Article text

Anthropic shares explode on Wall Street debut as AI giant's total valuation rockets past $2trillion Anthropic opened for trading this morning on the Nasdaq stock exchange, completing the largest initial public offering in history and marking the stunning market debut of the world's leading artificial intelligence company. The creator of the Claude AI platform raised an unprecedented $81 billion in its IPO, selling 980 million shares at $83 a piece on Wednesday. CEO Dario Amodei rang the Nasdaq opening bell at 9:30am to mark the occasion, as his company began trading under the ticker ANT. In the background, tech outages and software blackouts have sewn chaos throughout the US and around the world over recent days - as many commentators have sought to blame AI platforms as a prime suspect, without much evidence. But today is Anthropic’s day - as the company's shares opened at $111.50, 34 percent above the $83 IPO pricing level, stunning Wall Street and giving the company a total market cap of more than $2 trillion. 'In many respects, this IPO may mark a turning point in the AI space,' Gavin Ezekowitz, CIO and co-founder of BFA Global Investors, told the Daily Mail. 'We may be now moving to a phase where the true value is measured in the outcomes from all that compute.' The IPO leaves many questioning what happened to OpenAI, creator of ChatGPT, which has struggled to get its own market debut in order over recent months. 'Whatever you think about the IPO today, OpenAI's loss to Anthropic will be studied - and Anthropic is dominating in nearly every financial metric,' Nic Puckrin, markets expert and former Goldman Sachs analyst, told the Daily Mail. In addition to the Amodei siblings, the other five co-founders are chief compute officer Tom Brown, research director Jack Clark, chief science officer Jared Kaplan, chief architect Sam McCandlish and Chris Olah, who leads interpretability research efforts. The seven co-founders each sold 3.4 million shares in the IPO, according to the company's filings, leaving them owning from 237 to 241 million shares in the company - worth a total of around $26 billion each. Approximately 5,300 Anthropic employees own equity grants that will be worth tens of billions of dollars once their lockup periods expire. Around 1,000 early hires own stock grants worth $100 million or more a piece, while thousands of newer hires hold smaller packages averaging around $20 million each. All in all, the company’s employees hold around $216 billion in stock at the IPO price, with another $160 billion reserved for future hires. Ashton Kutcher’s venture capital firm Sound Ventures invested $435 million in Anthropic back in 2023, when the company was valued around $5 billion - and after the IPO the firm’s position is worth around $7 billion, firmly establishing Kutcher as a billionaire. In addition, as a public benefit corporation Anthropic allocated 5 percent of its shares to a dedicated fund aimed at supporting the responsible development of AI. The company believes it could rapidly accelerate scientific innovation, greatly increase economic growth rates, bring cures for major diseases within reach and solve problems that have resisted generations of human effort. But the same document offers a stark warning: 'Sufficiently advanced AI systems could ... pose catastrophic or existential risks to humanity.' And in a video posted earlier this week, one Anthropic researcher warned that AI would likely leave 'pretty much all humans on Earth' permanently unemployable and dependent on government welfare. Tao Lin, who works on safeguards for Anthropic’s Claude models, said in an interview with Palisade Research that he expects 'broadly superhuman AI' in almost every field by around the end of 2027.
