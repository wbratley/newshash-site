British investors in scramble for Anthropic shares as value smashes past $2trillion

Article text

British investors in scramble for Anthropic shares as value smashes past $2trillion Anthropic smashed past the $2 trillion mark as shares soared nearly 40 per cent on its record-breaking stock market debut. In what was the biggest initial public offering (IPO) ever, the shares were priced at $83 to give the Claude AI chatbot developer a value of $1.5 trillion. But they raced as much as 39 per cent higher to $115 as trading got under way in New York, sending its value past $2 trillion. The blockbuster IPO saw a scramble for shares from UK investors – but two-in-five missed out on the full allocation they applied for. The rally faded, however, with Anthropic ending its first day as a publicly listed company at $96.86, while the tech-heavy Nasdaq 100 gave up all of its early gains to close down 2.2 per cent. The Dow Jones Industrial Average and the S&P 500 also fell, while the FTSE 100 dipped 0.9 per cent in London. Anthropic boss Dario Amodei, centre, and fellow co-founders Daniela Amodei, Jared Kaplan and Jack Clark cheer the IPO With Nvidia continuing its decline in the wake of the departure of philandering boss Jensen Huang, hopes that Anthropic would breathe life into the wider stock market were dashed. Joe Maher, an economist at Capital Economics, said Anthropic 'won't save the AI trade', adding: 'Anthropic's blockbuster IPO has failed to lift sentiment in the stock market and history offers a note of caution: major IPOs and surging equity issuance often coincide closely with stock market peaks.' The listing did see the value of the shares held by each of the seven co-founders – including Brighton-born Jack Clark – hit $26billion. Hundreds of Anthropic staff are also sitting on stakes worth £75million or more. Anthropic sold 980m shares at $83 – raising $81billion – as institutional and retail investors scrambled for shares. That was far more than the 825m shares it was expecting to sell, and above the $70-to-$78 price range targeted. Alongside its listing, Anthropic raised $26billion in a private placement from partners including Nvidia, Microsoft and Google. The total fundraising of $107billion exceeds the $86billion raised by Space X in June, making it the biggest IPO of all time. Ben Barringer, head of technology research at Quilter Cheviot, said: 'Given all the tech-related market volatility we have seen in November, the broader market needed the Anthropic IPO to be a success. It seems this has ticked that box. 'However, as with many IPOs, the stock is likely to be volatile in the days and weeks afterwards as that initial exuberance wears off. We saw that with Space X, and we are likely to see it again with Anthropic.' The listing follows weeks of turmoil on markets with tech stocks hit amid concern over high levels of debt-fuelled spending on AI and a crackdown on the roll-out of data centres following Democrat victories in the recent US midterm elections. The crisis at Nvidia, where Huang quit last week after a string of extramarital affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese spy, has fuelled concerns that the AI boom is over. The Nasdaq is down around 15 per cent from last month's peak while Nvidia has fallen nearly 40 per cent – wiping about $2.2trillion off its value. Analysts warned of a rocky road ahead. Susannah Streeter, at Wealth Club, said: 'Even though shares have popped on the first day, the weeks and months ahead are still likely to be laden with volatility.' Neil Wilson, at Saxo, said: 'It feels like Anthropic has picked up the baton from Nvidia as the point man for the AI trade. 'Its success or failure will have huge ramifications for the wider AI ecosystem.' He added: 'Is it worth it? Can Anthropic justify a $1.5trillion valuation, or $2trillion? I don't think anyone really knows.' Many Brits miss out in mad dash for shares More than 100,000 British armchair investors applied for shares in Anthropic ahead of the float – but two in five did not receive as many as they wanted. RetailBook, which oversaw the offer in the UK, said 110,000 retail investors signed up, the most since Royal Mail listed in London in 2013 and Saga in 2014. It also eclipsed the 100,000 who took part in the float of Elon Musk's Space X in June. But just 63 per cent of investors received their full allocation. And UK investors splashed out less on Anthropic shares than they did on Space X. Anthropic sold just over 3.6m shares in Britain for around $300million – below the $364million Space X raised from UK investors. Only those who applied for up to $2,822 of Anthropic shares were allocated in full, according to Hargreaves Lansdown, handing them 34 shares. Applications above that level were cut back, with a maximum of 1,700 shares worth $141,100 allocated to any single UK investor. RetailBook said many applicants submitted the minimum investment of £250. Founder admits AI carries 'very real risks' Anthropic boss Dario Amodei said AI risks can be avoided Anthropic boss Dario Amodei yesterday admitted the artificial intelligence (AI) revolution being spearheaded by his company carries 'very real risks'. As it made its record-breaking stock market debut, Amodei addressed growing fears that AI could have a cataclysmic effect on the future of the world. Anthropic's initial public offering came just days after a member of the board at rival Open AI, the firm behind ChatGPT, said the risk of a tech takeover in which interfering humans are swept aside was 'exceptionally high'. Some have gone even further, with a top Anthropic researcher recently claiming there was a more than one in ten chance that AI could 'kill all humans' within the next decade. But speaking ahead of the bell-ringing in New York to mark the start of trading in shares, Amodei sought to allay such fears. 'We've always been honest that this technology has very real risks,' Amodei said. But these 'are avoidable and we can avoid them', he added. Amodei said he and his co-founders, including sister Daniela Amodei, Anthropic's president, wanted to create technology that could help cure diseases and make scientific breakthroughs. He said: 'We believe that the most powerful technology of our time should be built for the benefit of humankind.' The brother and sister lost their father to a disease that 'just a few years later, became curable', he said, adding that their vision was for AI to help provide intelligence that could, among other uses, speed up medical advances. 'Sufficiently powerful AI could compress a century of scientific and medical progress into a decade,' Amodei said. He told broadcaster CNBC that Anthropic was 'pacing' development of the technology. He said: 'We could drive at 200 miles an hour. Maybe let's only drive at 90, spend time figuring out how to do that safely. 'In the long run, that will work out pretty well for investors as well, because the company that builds the safest, that has trust of the customers and the enterprises of the world, I think that is the company that's going to be best for society and… best for the performance of the company.' It came as International Monetary Fund chief Kristalina Georgieva highlighted the huge repercussions AI may have on everything from inflation and interest rates to jobs and the risk of cyber attacks. She warned that higher inflation caused by the AI boom in spending will mean rate rises by central banks around the world. And in a blunt message to countries to prepare for a future where 'AI is forever', she added: 'You snooze, you lose.'
