'We made it': Daughter of Joanne Young - whose husband drugged and raped her then invited five men to sexually assault her - speaks out on how proud she is of her 'incredibly strong' mother

Article text

The daughter of a woman who was drugged and advertised online for sex by her ex-Tory councillor husband has spoken of how proud she is of her 'incredibly strong' mother. Joanne Young, 49, was violated in her own bed in Swindon, Wiltshire, by multiple strangers husband Philip Young had 'plucked from the internet' as he made sick videos of the attacks. On Thursday, four of the men - Richard Wilkins, 62, Norman Macksoni, 48, Mohammed Hassan, 38, and Connor Sanderson-Doyle, 32 - were convicted of assaulting Ms Young alongside her 50-year-old husband. They were found guilty at Winchester Crown Court of offences ranging from sexual touching to rape between 2016 and 2023 on separate occasions. Now, Joanne and Philip's daughter, Alex, has detailed what her family has gone through across the last two and a half years. In a LinkedIn message poste, the 21-year-old student said: 'Over the last two and a half years, I have watched my family come together to overcome this. I have seen mental health become a struggle for all of us. But despite that, we made it this far. 'The journey isn't over yet, there's a long way to go… Through all this, Joanne Young, my mum, has been incredibly strong. I am so proud of her, and every decision she has had to make to get here.' Ms Young, who appeared beside her mother at Winchester Crown Court on Thursday, said 'the verdicts we have had are what we hoped for and more'. In a case heavily paralleled to the Gisèle Pelicot trial in France, depraved business consultant Young invited men into his family home where they abused his wife as she lay heavily drugged. Speaking outside court earlier on Thursday, Ms Young said the ordeal 'cost me my life as I knew it', adding: 'The person I trusted most in the world betrayed me in the most horrific way'. She described the guilty verdicts as a 'relief' but also urged the Government to look at the law of consent 'as a priority' so that 'women are properly protected in the future', adding her abuse case raised 'serious questions'. Daughter Alex has also pleaded for change, saying: 'I am calling for the Sexual Offences Act 2003 to be looked at and updated. 'We live in a world where there is debate over whether third-party consent is enough to have sex with someone. 'We live in a world where someone's partner can consent for them, and that is enough to use it as an argument in a court of law. 'This is simply unacceptable.' The four men found guilty today were all denied bail. Young admitted to almost 50 offences against his ex-wife earlier this year while the four strangers he invited into their home denied assaulting her. A fifth man Dean Hamilton, 47, of Butleigh, Somerset, previously pleaded guilty to raping, sexually assaulting and sexually touching Ms Young. Another suspect wanted in connection with alleged sex offences against the mother-of-three in 2022 is yet to be identified despite several police appeals. At trial, the men accepted Ms Young had not consented to the encounters but claimed they had been deceived by Young at the time into believing she had. For years, Ms Young had no say in the horrors being inflicted on her as she slept in a drugged stupor in her own bed. But standing on the steps of the court after a series of guilty verdicts, the brave mother-of-three finally had her voice heard. She spoke of the devastating betrayal she suffered at the hands of her husband, the 'man she had trusted most'. She said: 'The guilty result today is a relief and it's a relief not just for me and my family but a relief for what this means for other women in the future. 'It means that those who find themselves facing what I have are more likely to come forward and more likely to have faith in the criminal justice system. 'More broadly, for women and girls who have been abused by men, it sends the message that you can be believed and understood.' Young had ushered men he had summoned from the darkest corners of the internet into their home in Swindon to abuse his unsuspecting wife, at times while their young children slept nearby. Ms Young, who had been robbed of any knowledge of what was being done to her, and by whom, bravely attended court throughout most of the harrowing trial. During the case, she remained resolute as she took to the stand to give evidence and as the jury was shown sordid videos of her abuse by strangers alongside her husband who filmed and relished in the attacks. 'Knowing what has happened to me without my knowledge or consent has been incredibly difficult to process and this abuse has cost me my life as I knew it,' she said. 'The person I trusted most in the world betrayed me in the most horrific way. This has resulted in a devastating impact on the lives of myself and my children. 'What happened to me was not the act of one man alone. Others were invited to take part, and they chose to. That choice makes them complicit in what was done to me, and today's verdicts recognise that.' She continued: 'This case also raises serious questions about how the law deals with consent. Consent has to come from the person themselves. 'A number of sections that previously attracted concerns from law enforcement agencies have been removed or changed over time, and offenders seeking to commit crimes often migrate between different websites, forums and encrypted platforms. 'This means that tackling offending of this nature is not simply about monitoring a single website. 'Effective prevention requires a combined approach involving technology providers, law enforcement, regulators and the public. Intelligence sharing, reporting mechanisms, safeguarding measures and robust investigations all play a vital role. 'Where evidence is identified that online platforms are being used to facilitate criminal activity, police will work with domestic and international partners to identify those responsible and take appropriate action.'
