'It feels like Christmas': The Grand Theft Auto VI mega fans who booked four days off work and spent more than £1,000 to play game on release day

Article text

'It feels like Christmas': The Grand Theft Auto VI mega fans who booked four days off work and spent more than £1,000 to play game on release day Grand Theft Auto (GTA) VI mega fans across Britain have skived off work and spent thousands of pounds to play the most anticipated video game of the decade. The latest instalment in developer Rockstar's blockbuster series was unlocked for UK users at midnight, ending a 13-year wait since the franchise's previous instalment. Players from around the country took annual leave, called in sick and said goodbye to their loved ones to spend time in the game's fictionalised version of Miami. Sam Elward, from St Albans, is among those who have been preparing accordingly for one of the entertainment events of the decade, booking four days of annual leave and spending around £1,400 on a new gaming set-up, including a customised GTA VI controller. While Mr Elward struggled to sleep last night because of the excitement, he confessed he was 'unable to stay up' for the release of GTA VI but instead set his alarm for 7am with plans to 'lock myself away' and focus on the game. He said: 'It feels a bit like Christmas. 'Even trying to go to bed yesterday … Even for the past week, knowing it's been pre-downloaded and sat on the console. 'I've just been opening it and just looking at the clock counting down, it's crazy.' 'I got a new TV for it. I was really locked in and ready.' 'I've wanted to upgrade the TV, but I guess having a deadline like this has got me to sort it out quicker,' he added. 'I probably ended up spending in total maybe about £1,300 or £1,400 – including the TV and everything. 'There's no point of playing it and not having the best capabilities to be able to see it and everything. 'From the time we've played so far it is, in my opinion, definitely worth it.' Mr Elward, who works as a general manager for a beer shop in Hitchin, said he is responsible for his workplace rota, which made it 'a little bit easier' to take four days off dedicated to gaming. 'I've been waiting a very long time for this,' he said. 'With how detailed the game is itself, I thought I need a bit of time to actually digest it and go through what the actual story of it is. 'Unlike a movie, it's not a couple of hours. This is going to be a long playthrough, and I wanted to make sure I could get a few hours in. You need to keep up pace with everybody else.' He added: 'I've been really excited. I've been playing the series since GTA III. 'The wait and the delays for it coming out – there was a lot of hype and build-up, so I was very much looking forward to it.' As he and his partner are both fans, he said they are taking it in turns to play and are alternating 'back and forth every so often'. 'It's been maybe me hogging it so far this morning,' he joked. Eager players were quick to share their opinions on social media, with the game overwhelmingly lauded for its graphics and gameplay. It debuted with a score of 99 on review aggregator website Metacritic – equalling 1998's The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time as the highest-rated video game ever. Almost three million Britons are expected to call in sick or take time off to play Rockstar's new mega hit. A wave of annual leave requests has been dubbed 'Grand Theft Auto Flu' and some schools have even emailed parents to encourage their children to attend. Professional gamer Pavan Singh, 27, took a day off today to virtually step into the world of GTA VI. Pavan from Greenwich, London, said he logged in at midnight to play the game on his Playstation 5 alongside his friends who were also skipping work. The admin worker said it had been a long time coming for the much-anticipated release and he couldn't miss a second. Pavan said: 'I'll be up at midnight when it releases and then I'll see how long I last. 'You don't want anything spoilt. 'It's like when a popular TV show or film comes out, and you want to watch it straight away. 'This game has taken 13 years to make and it should be perfection.' Pavan said he didn't put 'GTA' down as the reason for his day off but said he was sure people would guess the reason. He said: 'A friend of mine works in a team of 20 and there's five guys who have taken the day off. Pavan said the hype was worth it. He explained: 'Me and all my mates were in school and when it dropped, it brought people from different groups together and it built friendships. 'Every day, after school, we'd hop on to play races or heists. 'We were 13, 14, back then and now we're all adults - and I'm still friends with most of them now so we'll be playing again together.' He added: 'GTA taught us a lot as kids - everyone wanted the best apartment or car. 'It taught you how to save money and work towards an objective. That stays with you as you get older. 'I'm most looking forward to the music. GTA V put me on Kendrick Lamar as a few of his songs were on the game radio. 'I'm looking forward to driving around and listening to the new tracks. 'This is a cultural event.' HR firm BrightHR said the game was mentioned in 4.6 per cent of time-off requests by UK employees on launch day, and today's bill for GTA annual leave is expected to reach £3.14million. BrightHR added that sickness absence was up 56 per cent today compared to last Thursday. Average lateness today was 29 minutes compared to 24 minutes last Thursday. Of those who were late, 9 per cent today said they overslept compared to 4 per cent last Thursday. Throughout November and December, an estimated 83,000 days have been booked for GTA at a projected cost of £12.4million to UK employers. British internet providers experienced a surge in demand in the weeks leading up to the release of GTA VI, while its launch broke broadband records as fans rushed to download it on Wednesday, with many pulling all-nighters to play. Despite millions set to log in today, Andy Burnham has said he would not be one of the people playing. A spokesman for No10 said that while 'Rockstar Games is a British success story' the Prime Minister's 'diary is very full. He won't be playing it.' Social media users raved online about the release with some calling it 'mindblowing' while another wrote: 'I’ve never enjoyed a game this much, it deserves every bit of praise it gets.' Meanwhile, a school in Birmingham sent a minibus to collect kids trying to bunk off school to play the new blockbuster. Another, Ninestiles secondary school, is offering its pupils prizes if they attend all their lessons this week in an attempt to stop them from staying home. It wasn't all praise for the new video game. Hours after the global release of GTA VI, hardcore fans were already rebelling against what they saw as its post-MeToo and 'feminist' makeover. Players, who have been used to doing whatever they please in past editions, have complained that they are no longer able to sleep with prostitutes, have casual sex or even cheat on their in-game partner. One gamer accused GTA VI of 'betraying its roots', while another branded it 'a humiliation ritual for all male gamers'. A third wrote: 'Gone are the days of edgy, based masculine GTA games... right now it's looking like it will be a woke fest.' It centres on Bonnie-and-Clyde-style couple Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos. Lucia is the franchise's first major, fully voiced female star. Academics who study video games have suggested the shift away from sex workers and infidelity towards consensual, 'gentle' sex may be aimed partly at avoiding the alienation of the growing number of female gamers around the world. Research suggests women will account for around four in ten GTA VI players in the US, compared with roughly one in eight players of GTA V. GTA's British-founded developer Rockstar Games, now based and owned in the US, said the changes were made to keep players focused on the romantic relationship between the two protagonists.
