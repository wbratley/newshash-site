Staggering 1m Brits throw sickies to play Grand Theft Auto VI costing economy £400m as epic game brings UK to standstill

Article text

BRITAIN was gripped by gaming fever yesterday — with nearly a million people bunking off work and school to play Grand Theft Auto VI. An astonishing 2.8million Brits planned to binge the most hotly anticipated video game of all time. The economy is estimated to have lost up to £400million in productivity on the launch day of the £70 action blockbuster — 13 years after Grand Theft Auto V. Some schools offered pupils rewards just for coming in. But the huge success of the game could actually end up making almost £1.3billion for the UK Treasury in game and console sales, accessories, merchandise and a standard VAT rate of 20 per cent. The game follows criminal couple Jason and Lucia as they beg, borrow and steal to get rich in the fictional US state of Leonida. READ MORE ON GTA HR firm BrightHR found GTA was mentioned in 4.6 per cent of time-off requests, with an estimated 83,000 days booked off specifically to play on the game across November and December. The company said sickness absence had rocketed 56 per cent compared with last Thursday, while staff who turned up late were, on average, 30 minutes overdue. Of those who were late, one in ten said they overslept, compared with one in 20 last Thursday. And it was not just the adult workforce making the most of the game’s release. Most read in Tech Some schools picked up pupils who had been “busted” — the term used in the game when your character is arrested — for truanting, while others offered incentives for them to come in to lessons. Belfast Boys Model School offered the chance for students to win a £70 PlayStation or Xbox gift card if they attended as normal. Rashel Rahman, the headteacher at Shenley Academy in Birmingham, wrote to families after students told staff that they were considering taking the day off. He described GTA VI as “the enemy” and said that it was “imperative” they only play the game “outside of school hours”. Jon Pead, headteacher of Sandon School in Chelmsford, Essex, raffled Amazon vouchers, free Christmas dinners and lunchtime queue-jump passes to keep pupils in class. He said: We’re not expecting half-empty classrooms on Thursday and Friday, we’re just aware of the excitement around the release and we wanted to take that proactive stance and approach.” In a letter to parents, the school warned that skipping lessons to “play, purchase, or engage with the game” would be recorded as an unauthorised absence. The Ramsey Academy in Halstead, Essex, is handing out “sweet treats” and extra house points to pupils for simply “walking through the doors”. And Darwen Vale High School in Lancashire warned parents that teachers would be “conducting home visits at random for students we suspect are absent for the purpose of playing the new game”. The school told parents: “Gaming, regardless of how anticipated a release may be, is not a valid reason to miss school.” Other schools, including Farlingaye High School in Woodbridge, Suffolk, warned parents they could face £80 fines if pupils were off due to “tiredness from staying up late”. School management software provider Arbor said its provisional attendance figures showed about 35,000 more secondary school pupils were absent on Thursday, compared with Monday to Wednesday this week. The figures from morning registers taken in more than 1,400 secondary schools also showed Thursday had the lowest attendance of any school day in the past two weeks. PM Andy Burnham’s spokesman said: “Children should be in school because every day in school matters.” Asked if the PM himself would play the game, the spokesman added: “He will be at work. And very busy tomorrow.” Elsewhere, dad Jason Waller, 56, from Bedford, booked two days of annual leave so he and his son could play the 18-rated game for an hour or two after midnight. He said the time would not be “wasted” — the term used when your character in the game dies — because he is going to play all day today and through the weekend. And one unidentified fan who booked a hotel for an entire week so he can play the game undisturbed said his wife suspected he was actually having an affair. The game was released in the early hours yesterday, with broadband networks struggling under the strain of the 200gb-plus download. BT and EE branded it an “unprecedented gaming download event”. GRAND THEFT AUTOCRATS WORLD leaders have also been playing Grand Theft Auto VI – in AI-generated memes. Donald Trump, who has previously posted GTA-style video montages of himself, was mocked up in a dressing gown and slippers as he gamed with his feet up at the White House. In reality, he was at an historic trilateral meeting with Russia’s Vladimir Putin and China’s XI Jinping. But even they did not escape the GTA VI meme craze, as the three leaders were shown as pals playing the game together. Asked if Andy Burnham will be playing, a spokesman said he was too busy. Sony said it was the most pre-ordered game in PlayStation Store history, while analysts Sensor Tower estimated it had raked in more than £760million before a single person had played it. John Lewis reported a 62 per cent jump in console and accessory sales and Currys a 149 per cent rise in PS5 console sales. The game is projected to achieve £2.5billion in global sales during launch week — more than the entire revenue generated by Taylor Swift’s Eras tour. Its predecessor GTA V made £760million in three days in 2013 and has sold more than 230million copies. In Greenwich, South East London, fans held a launch party to play the new game, created by Scottish studio Rockstar North. Host Sumit Singh said: “The graphics, attention to detail, storyline and soundtrack are impressive. “I have already played for three hours straight since midnight. Can’t wait to see what’s to come.” Fans praised the game’s minute details across 91 main missions and five chapters, including jet-skiing dogs and the fact that there are 62 different types of bird to spot.
