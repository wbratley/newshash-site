Beloved Cincinnati Zoo hippo Fiona dies after swallowing a knitted hat

Article text

Fiona, a beloved nine-year-old Nile hippopotamus who captured hearts worldwide after her premature birth, died after she ingested a knitted hat. Cincinnati Zoo confirmed the devastating news in a statement posted on Instagram, saying that extensive efforts by veterinary staff and keepers ultimately failed to save her. A spokesman from the zoo said: "It is with extreme sadness that we share with you the devastating news that Fiona did not make it after multiple attempts from our amazing vets and keeper team to help her through what ended up being a medical emergency." The hat accidentally ended up in Fiona's habitat about ten days before her death after a child dropped it, and it made its way into her enclosure. Fiona swallowed part of the garment, triggering a crisis her body could not overcome. "Despite multiple attempts to move the hat out of her system, it was ultimately too much to overcome," the zoo added. Zoo spokeswoman Michelle Curley explained the bizarre sequence of events that led to the hat landing in Fiona's enclosure. A visitor threw the hat into the air, where a ceiling fan caught it and propelled it through a small gap into the habitat. She said, "We probably couldn't make [it] happen again if we tried." Fiona initially appeared unaffected, but her appetite began to wane several days later. Staff tried to coax her into eating, hoping food would help push the hat through her digestive system. Over the following days, the zoo documented round-the-clock care, including medications, a colonoscopy, and even a vibration plate borrowed from a nearby horse farm to encourage the garment to pass through her body. An ultrasound showed the hat moving through Fiona's small intestine, but surgery wasn't feasible for a fully grown hippo. LATEST DEVELOPMENTS Fiona entered the world six weeks early in January 2017, weighing just 13 kg, roughly half the lowest previously recorded birth weight for her species. She was unable to stand, and her chances of survival were slim. The zoo shared daily updates charting her feeding, growth and milestones, turning her into a viral sensation followed by legions of fans across the globe. Her remarkable story of resilience spawned a children's book series, an ice cream flavour and even a character in Cincinnati's ballet. Hippos in captivity typically live up to 50 years, making Fiona's death at nine tragically premature. Thane Maynard, executive chair of the zoo's board, said: "Fiona was such a gift. I have never known an animal so beloved in all my life. From the day she was born, she was like lightning in a bottle. She will be remembered and beloved many years from now." Cincinnati Mayor Aftab Pureval described Fiona as "a symbol of perseverance and joy" for the city. He said: "She brought smiles to so many faces, from kids to parents to visitors from around the world. Our city is heartbroken, but we couldn't be more grateful that she was part of our lives and our community as a whole." Zoo Director Dave Jenike said: "We are in shock at the moment. "We did not expect this outcome. She fought until she couldn't anymore. Our animal care team was heroic in their efforts to save her. They did everything possible, but her body couldn't fight anymore." Peta's managing director of captive wildlife, Debbie Metzler, called Fiona's death "a tragic reminder that everyone should behave carefully and respectfully around animals."
