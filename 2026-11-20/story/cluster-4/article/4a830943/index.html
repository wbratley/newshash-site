Fiona the hippo dies after swallowing a hat at Cincinnati Zoo

Article text

Fiona the hippo dies after swallowing a hat at Cincinnati Zoo - Published The Cincinnati Zoo in Ohio says its beloved hippopotamus Fiona has died just days after swallowing a knitted hat that slipped into her enclosure. "It is with extreme sadness that we share with you the devastating news that Fiona did not make it after multiple attempts from our amazing vets and keeper team to help her through what ended up being a medical emergency," the zoo said on Thursday afternoon. Fiona, who became a social media star after her premature birth in 2017, swallowed the hat about 10 days ago and required emergency care. The vet staff "tried everything they could to help her" but "it was ultimately too much to overcome", the zoo said. "We are in shock at the moment," Cincinnati Zoo director Dave Jenike said in a statement. "We did not expect this outcome. She fought until she couldn't anymore." Michelle Curley, a spokesperson for the zoo, had said that the hat got into Fiona's enclosure by accident, after a visitor tossed it in the air and it caught on a ceiling fan that flung it through a small opening. "We probably couldn't make [it] happen again if we tried," Curley said. While she seemed fine at first, her appetite started to decrease a few days later, and the zoo staff tried to encourage Fiona to eat to help push the hat through her digestive system. An ultrasound before her death showed the hat passing through Fiona's small intestine. A zookeeper said surgery was not an option for full-grown hippos and "our best chance for success with Fiona would be if she could pass this on her own". The zoo also tried a different approach to attempt to get the hat to pass by encouraging Fiona to stand on a vibration plate platform borrowed from a local horse farm. "Our animal care team was heroic in their efforts to save her. They did everything possible, but her body couldn't fight anymore," Jenike said. Fiona was born premature at the Cincinnati Zoo in 2017 and at first struggled to survive. At birth, she weighed 13 kg (29 lbs), which the zoo said was about half the previous lowest recorded birth weight for her species. Her death is also very premature, with hippos normally living up to 50 years in human care, according to the zoo. "Although this was not an intentional incident, of which, regrettably, there are many, Fiona's painful death is a tragic reminder that everyone should behave carefully and respectfully around animals," said Debbie Metzler, the managing director of captive wildlife for the animal rights group People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals (Peta). "Peta expects that the zoo will review its protocols to help prevent anything like this from happening again, and we urge every visitor to secure their belongings and never toss objects near an animal's enclosure." As well as being famous online, Fiona's story inspired an ice cream flavour and a character in Cincinnati's ballet. Cincinnati Mayor Aftab Pureval said Fiona "was a symbol of perseverance and joy" in the city. "She brought smiles to so many faces, from kids to parents to visitors from around the world," he wrote on X. "Our city is heartbroken, but we couldn't be more grateful that she was part of our lives and our community as a whole." Related topics - Published9 hours ago
