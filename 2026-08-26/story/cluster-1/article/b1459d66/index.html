The 'gang attack' that unleashed carnage and led to five youths plus two police officers being killed in wrong-way A66 crash

Article text

- See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source A stolen car which ploughed head-on into a police patrol vehicle and killed seven had been chasing a rival gang as part of a violent feud, it was claimed today. The crash on the A66 killed two police officers as well as all five occupants of a stolen VW Passat, including 17-year-old Cole Worthy. Now the Daily Mail can reveal that the chase was allegedly sparked by a rival gang, who used a Volvo XC60 to ram into the home of Cole's father at 3am on Saturday – just 40 minutes before the devastating smash. It is understood that the house belonging to Gye Worthy was rammed twice by the Volvo, which deliberately reversed into the front window, causing extensive damage. Within minutes, the VW Passat with his son Cole and four friends inside was racing around the streets of Middlesbrough in pursuit of the Volvo. The Passat was later spotted by a police patrol, resulting in the horrific crash that killed PC Tom Clough, 38, and PC Matthew Blades, 37. The five occupants of the stolen car – Worthy, Makai Saddington, 18, Jakub Matusiak, 23, Theo Rae, 17, and Michael Cahill, 23 – were also killed. Reports of the crash have until now described the youths involved as 'joyriders', but it now appears they were out for revenge rather than thrill-seeking. The Mail understands that the occupants of the Volvo and the Passat belonged to rival gangs operating as part of an organised criminal network across Teesside. There is no suggestion that Cole's father Gye, 39, was involved in any criminal activity. The attack on his home, however, appears to be the flashpoint that led to the fatal crash. The chase was allegedly sparked by a rival gang using a Volvo XC60 to ram into the home of Cole Worthy's father at 3am on Saturday. Pictured: The extent of the damage to the property Gye Worthy with his son Cole, who died in the crash on the A66. A neighbour said Mr Worthy led a 'normal life' and there is no suggestion he was involved in criminal activity Scores of people held orange flares aloft in a field in South Bank, Middlesbrough, to mark the deaths of the five youths yesterday evening Cleveland Police today confirmed the latest arrests included a 16-year-old boy as well as four men aged 18, 21, 23 and 55 – taking the total number detained up to 17. The five people arrested today are: - A 16-year-old boy on suspicion of participating in an organised crime group; - An 18-year-old man on suspicion of participating in an organised crime group; - A 21-year-old man on suspicion of participating in an organised crime group; - A 55-year-old man on suspicion of assisting an offender; - A 23-year-old man on suspicion of threats to kill. The 12 people detained yesterday included four arrested on suspicion of participating in an organised crime group and four on suspicion of possessing a firearm. Four of the suspects are also accused of possessing Class A and B drugs with intent to supply. Cleveland Police said two of the 12 previously arrested have now appeared in court. Kai Beattie, 19, of Foxrush Court, Redcar, appeared before Teesside Magistrates' Court charged with possession of a firearm with intent to endanger life, according to the force. Cameron George, 26, of Micklow Close, Redcar, was remanded in custody to appear at Teesside Crown Court on September 21 charged with drugs offences and possession of criminal property, it said. The chain of events that led to the deaths of the two officers, as well as the five occupants of the car, began in the well-to-do Middlesbrough suburb of Acklam, on a usually quiet street of post-war semis. A neighbour said: 'The huge bang woke everyone up and it was then followed by a second. 'Whatever happened was quick because, by the time my husband looked out of the window, the car was gone, but there was very bad damage to the house.' One neighbour of the Worthys said Cole's father had lived at the address for around ten years and led 'a normal life'. He is listed at Companies House as the director of a private limited company called Worthylift Ltd, which was incorporated in July this year. A source told the Mail: 'After the attack on his father's house, Cole Worthy and his mates were out in the VW Passat looking for whoever had done this. 'There have been a series of houses rammed in Middlesbrough in the last few months and it's all part of a feud between rival criminal factions. 'When they fight among themselves it is one thing, but when innocent lives like those of two police officers are lost, that's completely another.' Cleveland Police has confirmed both the Volvo and VW Passat formed part of their enquiries in the early hours of August 22. Mr Worthy's home was rammed at around 3am. By 3.36am, the VW Passat was caught on CCTV racing at between 80 and 100mph down Lime Road in Teesville, pursued by three police vehicles. At 3.39am, it turned the wrong way on to the A66 dual carriageway, at which point the pursuing police vehicles stopped. Just ten seconds later, it smashed head-on into the armed response vehicle carrying PCs Clough and Blades. A spokesman for Cleveland Police said: 'On the morning of Saturday 22 August, Cleveland Police received several reports of two vehicles being driven dangerously. 'The first vehicle, which is believed to be a Volvo XC60, was witnessed ramming properties in the Middlesbrough area. PC Matthew Blades, a father of two, was killed when his police vehicle was struck head-on by a VW Passat travelling the wrong way down the A66 near Middlesbrough PC Tom Clough also died in the crash, which occurred around ten seconds after a police pursuit of the Volkswagen had been called off Cole Worthy (right) with his friend Theo Rae, who also died in the crash on Saturday 'Both vehicles were operating with cloned number plates to avoid detection but were picked up by the force's automated number plate recognition technology. 'The Volvo XC60 involved in the initial reports has been traced and recovered in Middlesbrough as part of the criminal investigation. 'Throughout the course of the weekend, Cleveland Police, supported by officers and staff from Durham, Northumbria and North Yorkshire Police, have worked tirelessly to establish the circumstances that led to the fatal collision. 'Those enquiries have resulted in the arrests of 12 people across Teesside.' Last night, balaclava-clad youths sparked anger after setting off flares and fireworks in a noisy tribute to the five dead youths. Friends and family of Jakub Matusiak, 23, gathered at a field in South Bank, just yards from the scene of Saturday's catastrophic collision. What began as a balloon release descended into confusion of smoke and noise when attendees lit up the sky with flares and rockets. A gang of around 20 masked youths on motorbikes and quad bikes roared around the field in honour of Mr Matusiak, who himself had convictions for motoring offences. Footage showed dozens of flares being held aloft, filling the field with dense orange fog as bikes revved their engines and blared their horns. Jacob Matusiak, another occupant of the car which hit the officers' patrol vehicle, also posted videos of himself driving recklessly Makai Saddington, one of the five killed in the VW, posted videos on TikTok of him being chased by police and was awaiting trial for racially abusing and assaulting officers Michael Robert Cahill was also in the VW. He was the only one out of the five not to have previous convictions The scenes sparked anger, with onlookers accusing the crowd of showing 'disrespect of the highest order'. The chaos came in stark contrast to a silent prayer vigil held at a nearby church to honour PC Clough and PC Blades. Hundreds of people on social media criticised yesterday's scenes. Diane Pennock said: 'I get it that their families and friends will be devastated and grieving but surely in circumstances where they have killed two innocent men who were simply out doing their jobs, you grieve in private? 'To me, the public show of grief is completely excusing what they have done and that they have taken two young men's lives who had families and children they should have gone home to.' Writing on Facebook, Wendy Allington accused the mourners of showing 'disrespect of the highest level for the two murdered policemen' and said a quiet vigil would have been more appropriate. The balloon release was also criticised by environmental campaigners, who insist the plastic in balloons spreads pollution that risks harming wildlife. Plenty of the residents of the surrounding streets chose not to attend the memorial events. Looking at the police officers standing beside their vehicles, a 58-year-old grandmother said: 'What must they be thinking right now? This is nothing but a mockery of the lives of those two policemen. 'I've watched them walking down there drinking and smoking weed and razzing about on their motorbikes. There's nothing here to celebrate.' Paul Williams, acting deputy national chairman for the Police Federation of England and Wales, told BBC Breakfast: 'Every day we can identify now numerous examples of young people sensationalising dangerous driving. 'Not just dangerous driving, but baiting police officers who are out there to protect the public. 'It's a real problem. It's causing officers concern. They shouldn't have to be going out and worrying about whether they're going to return home from their shift or not. 'But on top of that, wondering if they’re going to be plastered all over social media. 'It's absolutely unacceptable and social media platforms need to take some action.'
