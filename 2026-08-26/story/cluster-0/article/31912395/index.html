Email tips@dailymail.com - See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source Years before Dolly Parton's death at 80, the country songstress had already planned out what song she wanted to be played at her funeral. Parton – whose nephew, Bryan Seaver, announced that she had died peacefully in Nashville on Tuesday – weighed in on the subject way back in 2014 during an interview with Entertainment Weekly. The singer–songwriter began by noting that the logical funeral song for her would be one of her own tunes, I Will Always Love You, which had achieved greater fame after Whitney Houston recorded it for her film The Bodyguard (1992). Parton previously had a hit on the country charts with her original 1974 version of the song, which she wrote in response to the end of her working relationship with the country singer Porter Wagoner, who died in 2007. She realized just how powerful her song could be when she attended Houston's funeral in 2012. 'I’m sure they’ll be playing I Will Always Love You when I die, just like they did with Whitney Houston,' Parton said. 'When they picked her coffin up and started in on that song, I started to cry, and I thought, "Oh my Lord." That’s when it hit me that she was really gone.' Dolly Parton, who died Tuesday in Nashville, Tennessee, had already picked out the song she wanted played at her funeral years earlier; pictured in 2022 on Dolly Parton's Mountain Magic Christmas Parton assumed that her song I Will Always Love You would be played at her funeral, especially after she realized how powerful it could be when she heard Whitney Houston's cover played at Houston's funeral in 2012; Houston is pictured in 2001 in Las Vegas, Nevada But as moving as I Will Always Love You could be, Parton imagined a different song for her funeral 12 years before her death. 'There’s a song called If We Never Meet Again,' Parton told EW. 'It’s an old country–gospel church song that talks about if we never meet again this side of heaven, I will meet you on that beautiful shore.' She was so moved by the thought of the beloved song, which was written by the Southern Gospel songwriter Albert E. Brumley, that she continued to quote its lyrics. 'Where the charming roses bloom forever and where separations come no more,' Parton added. She went on to reveal that the song figured prominently in her father Robert Parton Sr.'s funeral in 2000. 'That was my daddy’s favorite,' she went on to reveal. 'We did sing it at his funeral, and I would like it to be sung at mine.' Following the news of Parton's death, her fans got a small bit of good news when it was confirmed that her Broadway dreams would still come true. 'As was Dolly's wish, the Broadway production of Dolly: A True Original Musical will begin previews December 7 at the St. James Theatre in New York City,' the show's producers said in a statement. 'Now, this isn't a story about sparkle and shine on the outside,' Parton said in a video last month. 'It's about where I really come from, what I've lived, what I've lost, what I've loved and how I've found my way and told it in my own words and through the music that's been part of me every step of the journey.' The national treasure added: 'Now, the musical has some of my big hits plus a lot of brand new songs that I've written just for the show. This really is a dream come true...and I can't wait to share it with all of you!' DOLLY will take audiences on a journey through the life of Parton 'from her barefoot beginnings in the Smoky Mountains of East Tennessee to performing in platform heels under the bright lights of Hollywood.'
