The legendary country singer's death was confirmed Tuesday in an Instagram video posted by her nephew Bryan Seaver, who also acted as her head of security. She died Tuesday morning after being admitted Friday to Nashville's Vanderbilt University Medical Center with autoimmune and kidney problems, according to TMZ. Parton's loved ones began racing to Nashville upon hearing news of her hospitalization, but not all were able to reach her while she was alive. 'This video announcement is something Dolly asked of me years ago before I fully absorbed it as a future reality,' Seaver said. He hailed his aunt for having 'lived in the light' and said she was now 'in the arms of Jesus' and the company of her late husband Carl Dean, as well as her parents and 'countless others who have watched her and longed to meet her in the heavens.' Parton, who is often referred to as the Queen of Country, enjoyed an illustrious music career that spanned an incredible six decades and was arguably best known for her hits Jolene and 9 to 5. Her passing came after months of health woes that saw her pull out of a string of public appearances, fueling fan fears for her wellbeing. Seaver fondly recalled joining Parton on tour when he was a child in the 1980s, sharing that she 'opened doors not just for me but for her entire family to reach the potential we didn’t even know was possible.' He characterized her as 'a farmer, just like her daddy. He farmed the soil. She farmed songs and happiness. Dolly called me and said she wants to rest in a beautiful bed of plush cotton because after a life of wearing heavy rhinestones, she finally deserves to be comfortable and have some rest.' In an interview published just four days before her death, Parton had declared she still had 'so many things I want to achieve' and vowed to continue 'working as long as I can to see everything come to life,' via People. She explained that she had been grappling with 'some health issues that I just didn’t pay attention to when I was watching over Carl.' The fourth of 12 children, Parton was born in Pittman Center, Tennessee, on January 19, 1946. She and her siblings - Willadeene, David Wilburn, Coy Denver, Bobby Lee, Stella Mae, Cassie Nan, Randel Huston 'Randy,' Larry Gerald, twins Estel Floyd and Freida Estelle, and Rachel Ann - were raised in the Great Smoky Mountains where they slept on beds made of straw and lived with no running water, gas or electricity. Having developed a love of music at an early age, Parton sang frequently on the local radio and made television appearances in East Tennessee as a child before releasing her first single, Puppy Love, at the age of 13. In 2008, Parton told NPR that her 1973 song Jolene was partly inspired by a local bank teller who used to flirt with her late husband. 'She got this terrible crush on my husband,' she said at the time. 'And he just loved going to the bank because she paid him so much attention. 'It was kinda like a running joke between us — when I was saying: "Hell, you're spending a lot of time at the bank. I don't believe we've got that kind of money."' Parton's 1974 hit, I Will Always Love You, was written about the experience of cutting ties with her former business partner and mentor Porter Wagoner as she set her sights on pursuing a solo career. The song reached the top spot of the US Billboard Hot Country Songs chart: first in June 1974, then again in October 1982. Parton famously refused to let Elvis Presley record his own version after his manager, Colonel Tom Parker, demanded she sign over half of the publishing rights. 'I wouldn't let Colonel Tom Parker,' she recalled in an interview with BBC Radio 2 in 2023. 'See, Elvis loved the song. In fact, I talked to Priscilla not very long ago. She said: "You know, Elvis sang that song to me when we were walking down the courthouse steps when we got divorced." She was vindicated in her determination to retain the rights when Whitney Houston recorded the song for her 1992 film The Bodyguard, whereupon it became an thunderous international hit that carried the soundtrack album to 18 times platinum. Parton went on to write and record the song 9 to 5 in 1980 for the comedy film of the same name, in which she starred alongside Jane Fonda and Lily Tomlin. The track, which she wrote on set by clicking her nails together and forming the beat, became one of her biggest hits of the decade. It went on to win numerous awards, including the Academy Award for Best Original Song and Country Song of the Year and Female Country Vocal of the Year at the Grammy Awards.
