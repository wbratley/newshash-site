Stalker with 'sexual interest' in Billie Piper who wrote her 18-page love letter is jailed for posting AI-generated images of her online

Stalker with 'sexual interest' in Billie Piper who wrote her 18-page love letter is jailed for posting AI-generated images of her online - See more Daily Mail on Google - save us as a Preferred Source An 'obsessive' stalker with a 'sexual interest' in Billie Piper has been jailed for 12 months for breaching a restraining order by posting AI-generated images of her online. Philip Jerome, who appeared in court on his 49th birthday on Tuesday, made 11 posts of the Doctor Who star on social media platform X, including 'cartoon' AI-generated images of her. He has been targeting Ms Piper, 43, for over a decade, having previously turned up at her home leaving her feeling 'unsafe'. He was subject to a stalking prevention order at the time. The court heard he had a 'sexual interest' in the actress and had tried to communicate with her by tagging her in the posts, in a 'delusional' attempt to engage in a relationship with her after meeting her 20 years ago. He has previously posted cards through her letterbox, bombarded her with social media messages and written her an 18-page love letter. Jerome has already been jailed once for breaching a restraining order by turning up at a Comicon convention where he posed for a fan photograph twice on the same day with Ms Piper, wearing different clothing for the second occasion. The court heard that on the second occasion Ms Piper recognised Jerome and he was ejected from the event by security. Jerome, from Winchester, Hampshire, was previously warned he could be jailed for five years if he breached the order. But Prosecutor Anisa Alrubaie said he attempted to communicate with her again in December 2025 via posts on X. Philip Jerome, 49, arriving at Basingstoke Magistrates' Court, Hampshire, on Tuesday where he was jailed for 12 months for breaching a restraining order by posting AI images of Billie Piper Actress Billie Piper, pictured at a Dutch Comic Con last year, did not attend the latest hearing Actress Billie Piper pictured with ex-husband Chris Evans in London in 2004 Basingstoke Magistrates' Court heard Jerome made 11 posts on X over the course of a month where he tagged Ms Piper's account. He also saved three photos of her to his gallery. Ms Alrubaie said: 'There are several instances where the defendant has tagged Billie Piper by her account and has been attempting to communicate with her by tagging her on X.' She said that he had captioned a post with a picture of Ms Piper on X: 'I think I know which image probably made me fall for [Ms Piper], the GQ Awards, and made me want to meet her at the Growing Pains Book signing. 'She looks great without a bra on.' Ms Piper did not attend the latest hearing. The court heard her personal assistant ran her X account and tried to shield Ms Piper from the comments. But at a previous court hearing Ms Piper said in a statement: 'I do not feel safe being at home. I do not believe he would show up and hurt me, however I don't know that for sure.' Caroline Bonavia, in mitigation, said the latest posts were not of an 'unpleasant nature' and were mostly repeats of the same things. Ms Bonavia said: 'The offences are essentially finding images of Ms Piper's head, her face from public sources, the internet, one magazine cover and a show and posting them on his X account and tagging her. 'They are the same images of her head in different ways, mainly repeats. Some were essentially of her face with AI, so it looked like a cartoon.' She said that this would not normally be a problem to post on social media but it was due to the 'history' of his offending that made this a breach. Ms Bonavia said: 'You have harm, of course, for a public figure, as she has suffered in the past, so there is always going to be some level of anxiety. 'There were several posts over the course of the month, but as I say, there is nothing wrong with it, if it wasn't for the history.' Ms Bonavia said Jerome had been diagnosed with autism and had previously pleaded guilty to the breaches of the stalking prevention order. The court was told that he had a harassment conviction dating back to 2013, in relation to another victim. Jerome first appeared in court in connection with Ms Piper in 2018 when he admitted stalking and was handed a restraining order after attending Ms Piper's home address, posting cards through her door and sending letters by post to her. He then breached this order in December that year by sending messages on Facebook to her mother and sister 'expressing a wish for them to meet up' as well as for attending Ms Piper's home and making comments about her choice of partner. Police also found ring binders at his home containing newspaper clippings, details of events and other merchandise. In February 2019, he appeared at a court in Southampton and admitted harassment by breaching the restraining order after he sent Ms Piper an 18-page message when she did not attend the previous court hearing. The hearing was told he sent a lengthy Facebook message of 'adoration' to the actress via her mother and sister, after tracking them down on the social networking site. Six months later he appeared at court in Southampton again after he failed to turn up to 'important' sessions with a psychologist which the court had ordered him to attend to deal with his stalking. In 2022, he was made the subject of the stalking prevention order. He was jailed for 12 months later that year for breaching the order. Jailing him again on Tuesday, District Judge Kirsty Allman said that Jerome's behaviour was 'entrenched and obsessive'. Judge Allman said: 'You have a sexual interest in Ms Piper, making reference to her without a bra on and so on.' She said that based on the totality of his offences and the 'repeated' nature of it, she could not suspend his sentence. She said: 'Your motivation being to contact her in the hope that she would engage in a relationship with you. 'It is accepted that Ms Piper doesn't have access to her X account and she doesn't view it, but you intended her to see the posts, and this would likely have caused harm and distress, particularly in light of the background.' She added: 'You have demonstrated an entrenched pattern of stalking and harassment offences.' She said that despite having been diagnosed with schizophrenic personality disorder in 2013 and recently diagnosed with autism, this did not outweigh the repeated breaches he had committed. Judge Allman said: 'You met Ms Piper 20 years ago and became obsessed with her. It is clear from the pre-sentence report that your obsession with Ms Piper has a sexual element. 'Your persistent effort to contact her is because you believe you can engage in a relationship with her. This is delusional. 'Your behaviour is entrenched, and it is obsessive. Probation services have concluded that you could not remain in the community.' He was jailed for 12 months and ordered to pay a statutory surcharge of £187 after admitting four breaches of the order. Ms Piper shot to fame aged just 15 with her first song Because We Want To. She married DJ Chris Evans in 2001 when she was 18 and he was 35. They divorced in 2007. She has three children – two sons with her ex-husband Laurence Fox and a daughter with musician Johnny Lloyd. Though she split from the Tribes frontman in 2023, the pair have reportedly since rekindled their romance.
