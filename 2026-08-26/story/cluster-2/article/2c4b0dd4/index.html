Trump threatened the US northern neighbor with a stunning 50 percent tariff on key sectors including cars, automotive parts and steel, set to go into effect on January 1. That's on top of a 50 percent tariff on $20 billion worth of Canadian goods, including hockey equipment, alcohol and more that went into effect on Saturday. Negotiations between Canadian and US officials fell through in a spectacular fashion late last week. Both sides have blamed each other for changing terms in the final rounds of talks, and the insults lobbed between the two have turned personal. Trump said Tuesday morning he is considering renaming Lake Ontario 'Lake America,' he also ripped Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney as 'weak and ineffective.' In turn, Carney has said Canada will match the US tariffs 'dollar for dollar,' and Ontario Premier Doug Ford told reporters this week Trump can 'kiss my a**.' Meanwhile, conservatives have sounded the alarm that the president's gambit is misguided and potentially damaging to US interests. 'The last thing we need right now, as our economy is getting back on its feet, is a trade war with Canada,' Trump's first-term Vice President Mike Pence told CNN. As President Donald Trump threatens further tariffs on Canada some members in his party are concerned that the trade war could impact the midterm elections 'The last thing we need right now, as our economy is getting back on its feet, is a trade war with Canada,' Trump's first-term Vice President Mike Pence said this week Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has vowed to implement 'dollar for dollar' retaliatory tariffs on the US. Canada announced up to 50 percent tariffs on some US goods on Tuesday Former Congressman Ron Paul reacted: 'It's a tariff war and a trade war. They're bragging about how wonderful it is and how necessary it is to save our economy ... Sometimes trade wars lead to the other kind of wars.' Maine's Republican Senator Susan Collins recently wrote on social media: 'The on-again/off-again trade talks between the U.S. and Canada lead to higher costs, risk, and uncertainty for Maine businesses.' 'If the Administration proceeds with these tariffs, they will increase costs for Maine families, as most businesses will have no choice but to pass on the tariffs to their customers through higher prices.' The right-leaning Wall Street Journal Editorial Board penned a piece calling the recent tariff tit-for-tat the 'dumbest' trade war in history and warned that the decision could impact the upcoming midterm elections. 'President Trump took exception last year when we called his tariffs against Canada and Mexico the dumbest trade war in history,' the Editorial Board wrote. 'It got dumber this weekend as Mr. Trump escalated with another round of border taxes on Canadian imports, a mere 10 weeks before midterm elections.' Canada's retaliatory tariffs announced on Tuesday go into effect on September 8, much sooner than the January 1 deadline Trump placed on cars and automotive parts. The timing could mean that US customers foot the bill for Canadian tariffs before customers in the northern country pay rates on US goods. The January deadline also gives the two countries some breathing room to negotiate a trade deal. The tariffs threaten to increase costs in the US, particularly in the automotive sector, should Trump's tune not change between now and January. This could spell trouble for Republicans running in the midterms this fall, as voters have remained concerned about the state of the economy. Republican Senator Thom Tillis of North Carolina noted how Americans and Canadians near the border could feel the trade war spill into their personal lives. 'They cross the border. They work together. They have families together,' he told Semafor. 'You create that tension, and the party that owns that tension is the party that probably suffers the consequences in November,' Tillis added. Meanwhile, the White House claims the tariff war will protect American interests. 'Countless American workers, farmers, and businesses have borne the brunt of America’s lopsided trade relations, including with Canada which has demanded total access to the American market without reciprocity,' WH spokesman Kush Desai told the Daily Mail.
