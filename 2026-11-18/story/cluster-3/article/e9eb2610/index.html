Irishman facing 10-year prison sentence after alleged attack on $60m US military aircraft that left plane ‘destroyed’

Article text

AN Irish national has been charged in New York by the United States Department of Justice with breaking into and destroying a $60million US military aircraft parked on the taxiway at Shannon Airport. Daithi O’Corrain, also known as David Curran, is alleged to have targeted the C-130 Hercules plane after breaching the site’s security perimeter on the morning of April 11 2026. Shannon Airport is a major transatlantic hub for US military troops and equipment. The US Air National Guard plane is said to have been en route to another country on a US military assignment. On reaching the aircraft, O’Corrain is claimed to have gained entry by breaking the lock on the rear crew door before barricading himself inside. He is then alleged to have “proceeded to inflict severe damage to the interior of the aircraft”, including cutting wires and hydraulic lines, and destroying every gauge in the cockpit. He is also said to have had with him capsules of a highly corrosive metal, known as gallium, which can eat through aluminium in minutes. Several hours after entering the airport, O’Corrain was spotted as the flight crew returned to the aircraft to leave for their next destination and alerted airport security. They then saw him escape out of a hatch in the aircraft’s roof and it is alleged “inflict substantial additional damage” to the plane’s exterior using an axe, “pouring unknown substances” and placing objects, including ball bearings, into the fuel tanks. Gardai are said to have arrived a short time later and detained O’Corrain. Most read in The Sun According to CNN, “Corrain is not in US custody”. The US Department of Justice (DoJ) claims O’Corrain carried out the attack after months of planning and preparation. It said in a statement: “The manner in which O’Corrain carried out his attack further demonstrated a detailed knowledge of, among other things, the layout of C-130H aircraft and how best to destroy critical components of the aircraft; which wires and components to cut and which not to cut, for his own personal safety; how to secure points of entry into the Aircraft, and what tools would be needed to do so; which chemicals, including gallium, could be used to corrode the aircraft’s primary material; and advanced knowledge that a US military C-130H aircraft would be located at Shannon Airport at the time of his attack.” The DoJ added: “As a result of O’Corrain’s attack, the aircraft was effectively destroyed. “The damage inflicted rendered the aircraft inoperable, and the aircraft, worth approximately 60 million dollars, will not return to active flight status.” O’Corrain’s alleged attack followed a series of similar, though less serious, incidents carried out by others at Shannon Airport over the previous year. The 48-year-old from Ireland is charged with destruction of government property, which carries a maximum penalty of 10 years in prison. US attorney Jamie McDonald for the Southern District of New York said: “These charges send a clear and powerful message that attacks on US military assets, and thus our national security, will not be tolerated. “As alleged in the indictment, the defendant destroyed a valuable US military aircraft abroad and, in the process, posed a significant threat to others, including US service members, nearby. “This indictment holds the defendant accountable for his serious crime and serves as a warning to others, wherever they may be, considering similar criminal activity.” James C Barnacle Jr of the FBI New York field office said: “As alleged, Daithi O’Corrain carried out a deliberate, planned attack against a US military aircraft, causing tens of millions of dollars in damage and jeopardising critical military operations. “This was not an act of spontaneous vandalism. The defendant allegedly spent months researching the aircraft, acquiring tools and materials, and preparing to maximize the destruction he could inflict. “The FBI and our law enforcement partners will continue to pursue anyone who targets US military assets, regardless of where those attacks occur.”
