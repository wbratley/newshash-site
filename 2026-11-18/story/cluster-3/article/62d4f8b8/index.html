Man armed with axe accused of destroying US military plane at Shannon airport in Ireland

Article text

An axe-wielding man breached a security perimeter and destroyed an American military plane while it was parked at an Irish airport, US prosecutors said. Daithi O’Corrain, 48, is accused of cutting hydraulic lines, smashing cockpit gauges and pouring ball bearings into fuel tanks. O’Corrain is charged with destruction of US government property in connection with the incident in April at Shannon airport in the west of Ireland, according to an indictment unsealed on Tuesday in Manhattan. He is not in US custody and his arraignment on the charge has not been scheduled, the AP news agency reported. If convicted, he faces up to 10 years in prison. The $60m (£45m) US Air National Guard C-130 Hercules, which had been en route to a military assignment in another country, was rendered inoperable and will not fly again, US prosecutors said. O’Corrain, described by supporters as an independent peace activist, was arrested immediately after the attack by Ireland’s police force, An Garda Síochána. He was charged with causing criminal damage and trespassing. Manhattan US attorney Jamie McDonald said the indictment sends “a clear and powerful message that attacks on US military assets – and thus our national security – will not be tolerated.” An online fundraiser organised by people describing themselves as O’Corrain’s friends said he disarmed the C-130 in protest against the US’s war with Iran and support for Israel’s war in Gaza. O’Corrain plotted the attack on 11 April for months, US prosecutors said. He searched online for information about the construction of the C-130, a quad-engine turboprop mainly used for transporting equipment and personnel, and looked up coordinates for sneaking on to the airport’s grounds, prosecutors allege. O’Corrain subscribed to a phone app that provided live flight tracking and ordered capsules of gallium, a chemical that can corrode steel and break down aluminium, prosecutors added. He is also accused of trying to contact someone who had been involved in a prior breach at Shannon airport, prosecutors said. O’Corrain breached the airport’s perimeter at about 4.40 am and broke into the C-130 as it was parked on a taxiway by destroying a locking mechanism on the rear crew door, his indictment said. The document added he used the crew’s hammocks to cover the plane’s windows but left the cockpit windshield exposed. He then barricaded himself inside the aircraft, securing the doors with straps, brackets and screws, and put ball bearings mixed with epoxy in door tracks to prevent doors from being opened or closed, the indictment said. James Barnacle, the head of the FBI’s New York office, said: “This was not an act of spontaneous vandalism.”
