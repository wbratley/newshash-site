Irishman charged by US over '$60m military plane sabotage' at Shannon Airport

Article text

Irishman charged by US over '$60m military plane sabotage' at Shannon Airport Daithi O'Corrain, also known as David Curran, is alleged to have targeted the C-130 Hercules plane after breaching the site's security perimeter on the morning of April 11 2026 An Irish national has been charged by the United States Department of Justice with breaking into and causing extensive damage to a $60 million US military aircraft that was parked on the taxiway at Shannon Airport. Daithi O'Corrain, also known as David Curran, is alleged to have targeted the C-130 Hercules aircraft after breaching the site's security perimeter on the morning of April 11 this year. Shannon Airport serves as a major transatlantic hub for US military troops and equipment. The US Air National Guard plane is understood to have been en route to another country as part of a US military assignment. Upon reaching the aircraft, O'Corrain is alleged to have gained access by breaking the lock on the rear crew door before barricading himself inside. He is then claimed to have "proceeded to inflict severe damage to the interior of the aircraft", which included severing wires and hydraulic lines, as well as destroying every gauge in the cockpit. He is further alleged to have been carrying capsules of gallium, a highly corrosive metal that is capable of eating through aluminium within minutes. Several hours after gaining entry to the airport, O'Corrain was spotted when the flight crew returned to the aircraft ahead of their departure to their next destination, prompting them to alert airport security. They subsequently witnessed him flee through a hatch in the aircraft's roof, where he is alleged to have gone on to "inflict substantial additional damage" to the plane's exterior using an axe, "pouring unknown substances" and placing objects, including ball bearings, into the fuel tanks. Irish police are understood to have arrived shortly afterwards and apprehended O'Corrain. The US Department of Justice (DoJ) alleges O'Corrain executed the attack following months of planning and preparation. It stated: "The manner in which O'Corrain carried out his attack further demonstrated a detailed knowledge of, among other things, the layout of C-130H aircraft and how best to destroy critical components of the aircraft; which wires and components to cut and which not to cut, for his own personal safety; how to secure points of entry into the Aircraft, and what tools would be needed to do so; which chemicals, including gallium, could be used to corrode the aircraft's primary material; and advanced knowledge that a US military C-130H aircraft would be located at Shannon Airport at the time of his attack." The DoJ added: "As a result of O'Corrain's attack, the aircraft was effectively destroyed. "The damage inflicted rendered the aircraft inoperable, and the aircraft, worth approximately 60 million dollars, will not return to active flight status." O'Corrain's alleged attack came after a string of comparable, though less severe, incidents perpetrated by others at Shannon Airport during the preceding year. The 48-year-old from Ireland is charged with destruction of government property, which carries a maximum penalty of 10 years in prison. Court documents indicate that O'Corrain "is expected to be first brought to and arrested in the Southern District of New York". The US also indicated in the filing that it would look to seize assets from the accused if convicted. US attorney Jamie McDonald for the Southern District of New York said: "These charges send a clear and powerful message that attacks on US military assets, and thus our national security, will not be tolerated. "As alleged in the indictment, the defendant destroyed a valuable US military aircraft abroad and, in the process, posed a significant threat to others, including US service members, nearby. "This indictment holds the defendant accountable for his serious crime and serves as a warning to others, wherever they may be, considering similar criminal activity." James C Barnacle Jr of the FBI New York field office said: "As alleged, Daithi O'Corrain carried out a deliberate, planned attack against a US military aircraft, causing tens of millions of dollars in damage and jeopardising critical military operations. "This was not an act of spontaneous vandalism. The defendant allegedly spent months researching the aircraft, acquiring tools and materials, and preparing to maximize the destruction he could inflict. "The FBI and our law enforcement partners will continue to pursue anyone who targets US military assets, regardless of where those attacks occur."
