Nvidia doesn't want Jensen Huang reinstated as CEO as company posts huge earnings win despite scandals... and now an SEC investigation

Article text

Nvidia doesn't want Jensen Huang reinstated as CEO as company posts huge earnings win despite scandals... and now an SEC investigation Nvidia's board are not intending to reinstate ousted loverat CEO Jensen Huang, despite him and his tech allies vying for his return at the chip giant. The company conference call, hosted by Chair Harvey Jones on Tuesday night, made clear Huang is not set to return back to the helm after his affair with a suspect Chinese spy and another employee. It comes just minutes after Nvidia posted its first earnings report since the Huang scandal erupted, with both earnings and revenue handily beating analysts' expectations despite the furore at the Silicon Valley company this week. The SEC also announced an investigation into Nvidia after the close on Tuesday. The focus of the SEC probe is unclear, although reports suggest the agency is looking at whether the company’s disclosures misled investors, according to the WSJ. In the company's third quarter of fiscal 2027, earnings per share were $2.68, topping the $2.48 expected. Revenue came in at $115.8 billion versus $109.1 billion expected. The company's most important business - data center revenue - was very healthy, at $107.6 billion, while guidance for the company's fourth quarter came in strong. Shares of Nvidia have dropped 35 percent since their most recent all-time high in October - and last Friday they wrapped their sixth straight weekly drop, the longest losing streak since 2012. Alice Huang - a woman alleged to have ties to Chinese intelligence services - with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang Colette Kress has taken over as interim CEO of Nvidia after Jensen Huang resigned last week The SEC investigation adds even more pressure to the troubled tech giant amid the chipmaker’s leadership crisis. Huang admitted to cheating on his wife after the $4.5 trillion technology company’s board launched an investigation into reports regarding extramarital relationships, including with Alice Huang, a woman alleged to have ties to Chinese intelligence services, and two other employees at Nvidia. 'I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children, Huang wrote in a memo to staff on Sunday. 'There is no excuse for what I did.' Last week, Huang resigned from the company he founded in 1993 – although in a shocking reversal he is now saying he would happily return if the board wants him back. Nvidia named executive vice president and chief financial officer Colette Kress to serve as interim CEO while they search for Huang's replacement. Jensen and Kress were known to have tension in the workplace, with the ex-CEO having called her 'a deer in the headlights' to her face during a meeting with Nvidia's top financial officers in August. Kress has long been skeptical of Jensen Huang's push for his company to finance other AI startups and help the industry expand. Under Huang's leadership, Nvidia would use its extensive capital and industry dominance to sign memorandums of understanding with some of Wall Street's largest asset managers, seeking investors and private capital to finance a range of computing infrastructure, including data centers and other chip factories. The funding would involve specialized financing vehicles, with Nvidia's AI chips used as collateral. And now Nvidia board members are facing insider trading and corporate misconduct claims in a new civil lawsuit that alleges they ignored 'red flags' pointing to the ousted CEO's fling with a suspected Chinese spy. President Donald Trump has confirmed that the FBI is looking into Huang's affair, though the tech tycoon has not been charged with any crime Senior executives at the US chipmaker netted an astounding $2 billion by cashing in on 'material non-public information' that had yet to reach investors, according to a federal complaint styled as a derivative action on behalf of Nvidia and reviewed by the Daily Mail. They include Mark Stevens, a billionaire venture capitalist who co-owns the Golden State Warriors and is one of Nvidia's longest-serving directors – and who allegedly sold almost $1.5 billion worth of stock before news leaked of his colleague's romance with Alice Huang. Kress is also accused in the complaint of 'unjust enrichment' by selling $63 million in shares in the months before the alleged spy scandal wiped around $200 billion off Nvidia's value. President Donald Trump has confirmed that the FBI is looking into Huang's affair, though the tech tycoon has not been charged with any crime. Jensen Huang has agreed to testify behind closed doors before three House committees on December 1 - including the House Foreign Affairs Committee, House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party and the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence.
