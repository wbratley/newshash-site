The Debate: Should Nvidia take back Jensen Huang?

Article text

The creme de la creme of Silicon Valley are backing the return of Jensen Huang, but should Nvidia really take back the rogue CEO? We hear the case for and against in this week&#8217;s Debate YES: The Nvidia board stated they have no reason to believe any secrets were compromised Yes, but the evidence needs to clear him first. I won’t defend Jensen Huang for being unfaithful. He’s apologised, and I believe that is a private matter. Beyond his affair, at the time of writing there are a lot of unsubstantiated claims about his conduct. We don’t know if Alice (the woman involved) was working for Beijing. Though internet memes would suggest otherwise, the US Justice Department has reached no conclusion on whether she is a Chinese spy. President Trump has said national security “was not put at risk”, and the Nvidia board stated they have no reason to believe any secrets were compromised. Worth noting, in 2023 MI5 said in the UK alone 20,000 individuals were targeted by Chinese agents largely through Linkedin to steal sensitive technological and industrial secrets. The further claims about Huang having had affairs with staff members are unconfirmed and anonymously sourced. I condemn bosses who use their rank to pursue staff, but until it’s proven he did, it’s hearsay. What I find interesting is, as Tech Crunch reported, “Huang has been a staunch supporter of open source AI”. His resignation may benefit closed source AI providers arguably trying to raise the regulatory drawbridge on open models. Huang has been an incredibly influential figure in AI, co-founding Nvidia (in 1993!), betting on accelerated computing a decade before the AI boom, delivering AI chip market dominance and a valuation of $5 trillion in 2025, with the company investing in a significant percentage of the AI ecosystem. The Nvidia board should finish its independent investigation to establish the facts. Did he abuse his position? Did he mislead his board? Did he break security or export rules? A no to all should see him reinstated. Matthew Blakemore is the CEO of AI consultancy AI Caramba! NO: Allowing Huang to return amid allegations would make it impossible to build an investment case for Nvidia Parting is such sweet sorrow. But sometimes it is best. Jensen Huang became one of those business legends, growing a business and then spotting an opportunity to pivot and deliver even better returns for employees and shareholders. It will have been traumatic for all concerned at Nvidia. But letting him return to the helm will just mean the dark clouds over the company take longer to dissipate. There are still too many unanswered questions around his relationship with Alice Huang and Nvidia&#8217;s China policy. Allowing him to return would mean that the company remains fettered to him as he is dragged in front of Congressional committees and hounded in the press, making it impossible to build an investment case for the company. And what if he then has to leave anyway? This isn&#8217;t the way anyone thought the story would end, and it certainly seems like there isn&#8217;t much of a succession plan in place. But the only way forward now is for him to depart and for the company to move on. Where does Nvidia go from here? Reclaiming market confidence is the first order of business, and that will take time. It also requires a commitment to transparency as the likely congressional investigation gets going. This is unlikely to be a short-lived process, but is the only way forward. Keeping internal talent is now the main concern, to avoid losing them to any competitor, either an existing one or a potential new rival. Nvidia is a $3 trillion company, and bigger than one man. Now it has to show that. Chris Beauchamp is chief market analyst at IG Group THE VERDICT You may think being caught having an affair with a potential Chinese spy would be pretty hard to come back from, but stranger things have happened in Silicon Valley. Currently, the petition to reinstate Jensen Huang as Nvidia CEO has garnered more than 180,000 signatures. This includes the likes of Elon Musk, while Donald Trump also added his backing on Monday.  Uber co-founder Travis Kalanick, who also signed, perhaps best summed up the campaign’s viewpoint: “He has erred, yes – to err is to be human – but I do not believe anything he has done rises to the bar of separating him from his company.” Nvidia’s shareholders, who have seen roughly $2 trillion wiped from Nvidia’s valuation since its October peak, may beg to differ. Mr Blakemore is right that Huang has been critical to Nvidia’s success – that probably cannot be overstated. That over 8,700 of the campaign’s signatories come from Nvidia staff members themselves is also notable. But Mr Blakemore’s innocent-til-proven-guilty argument is hard to bear out when Huang himself has admitted to being unfaithful and said his colleagues “deserve better” from him. Those 8,700 staff backers also come from a staff count of over 42,000, leaving more than 33,000 employees unaccounted for in whether they support Huang’s return. Taking back an ex can be oh so tempting, but most of us know it’s never for the best.
