Nvidia rules out Jensen Huang return after alleged spy affair - and its share price soars

Article text

Nvidia rules out Jensen Huang return after alleged spy affair - and its share price soars Former CEO Jensen Huang said he would be "honoured" to return to the company amid calls from Trump, Elon Musk, and supporters to bring him back after the affairs - but share prices rocketed after Nvidia ruled it out Nvidia's board has rejected Jensen Huang’s offer to return as CEO after he said he would be "honoured" to return. Huang, 63, resigned after admitting extramarital affairs, including to a suspected Chinese spy. Nvidia, one of the world's largest companies that manufactures computer components essential to running and improving AI, has faced pressure from Donald Trump and other AI firm executives to welcome Huang back following the scandal. However, it is standing firm over its refusal to re-hire him. Interim chair Harvey Jones said the company's board “unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO" on Tuesday on an earnings call, hours before its share price shot up by more than 5 percent after better-than-expected results. Financial results seem to be an unexpectedly positive end of the scandal, for now, which first emerged in late October. Huang admitted to extramarital affairs, including one with a Chinese businesswoman and suspected spy for the country. It led to his resignation and a surprising split in the Trump government over the fate of the disgraced business leader. While Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called for a wholesale cleaning at Nvidia on Sunday, Trump said Huang “learned a big lesson” and that he “would certainly not mind” if he was reinstated a day later. Some Silicone Valley industry figures, including SpaceX and Tesla CEO Elon Musk, called for the reinstatement of Huang, a high-profile booster for the AI industry who oversaw Nvidia’s development as the key provider of chips driving advanced AI models. A “Stand with Jensen” petition set up last week also gathered momentum, gaining signatures from current and former Nvidia staff, investors and other supporters. Earlier on Tuesday (November 17), Huang said in a social media post that if the board decided that his return was in the best interest of shareholders, employees, the company and the country, he would be “honoured to answer these calls.” He acknowledged making “serious mistakes” in his personal life. But Nvidia seems to be focused on replacing Huang, not rehabilitating him. On an earnings announcement, interim chair Harvey Jones said: “We want to install the permanent CEO as quickly as possible, but we won’t expedite the CEO before finding exactly the right person.” Huang said he received an “outpouring” of support from employees, investors, business partners and customers for his return. In a further sign the company sees its future without Huang, it confirmed it has created a new board committee to oversee national security and global trade compliance following the revelations about the ex-CEO. Trump welcomed the move. Despite the scandal, Nvidia blew past Wall Street expectations for the third quarter, pulling in US $116 billion in revenue, double the level from a year ago. The company also posted net income of US $2.70 per share, beating estimates of US $2.48 per share. In the wake of sidelining Huang and implementing the new committee, Nvidia stock shot up 5.2 percent in after-hours trading. While Nvidia seems to be certain about its future, Huang's remains unknown. He has been summoned before congressional committees on December 1, followed by the company’s general counsel, Tim Teter, the next day. “The American people need answers on Mr. Huang and Nvidia’s role in allowing dangerous PRC actors to access our most advanced chips,” said a statement from the chairmen of three House committees with oversight of China and intelligence issues.
