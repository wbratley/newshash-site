Army officer arrested by British counter-terror police for allegedly spying for Russia is now in US custody

Article text

Army officer arrested by British counter-terror police for allegedly spying for Russia is now in US custody An Army officer who was arrested in Britain last week on suspicion of spying for Russia has been handed over to US authorities. Lieutenant Colonel Brandon Tyner, 41, was detained last Tuesday in a joint MI5, FBI and counter-terror operation on suspicion of offering to spy for the Kremlin after being 'entrusted with sensitive information'. Tyner was based at Imjin Barracks – the headquarters of Nato's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps (ARRC) – in Innsworth, near Gloucester in England. Police detained the husband and father-of-two under the National Security Act and searched a property in Gloucester last week in connection with the investigation. Now, Tyner has been taken into custody by the US Military over the suspected national security and espionage allegations. FBI Director Kash Patel said: 'This FBI has zero tolerance for anyone who betrays their oath to the United States or puts our national security at risk. 'That's been demonstrated by the 167 counterintelligence and espionage arrests we've made over the last 21 months, a 53% increase. 'An Army officer is in custody as the result of an espionage investigation and the close cooperation between the FBI's Philadelphia Field Office and Legat London and our partners at U.K. Counter Terrorism Policing, MI5, U.K. Defence Counter Intelligence, and the U.S. Army Counterintelligence Command. Lieutenant Colonel Brandon Tyner, 41, was detained last Tuesday in a joint MI5, FBI and counter-terror operation on suspicion of offering to spy for the Kremlin after being 'entrusted with sensitive information' Tyner was based at Imjin Barracks – the headquarters of Nato's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps (ARRC) – in Innsworth, near Gloucester in England (pictured) 'Together we worked to identify a potential threat to national security and take swift action.' Last week, it was revealed that the soldier, who joined the US army in 2008 and served tours in Afghanistan, Iraq and Qatar, describes himself as a 'change agent' and 'master of coin' on social media. Tyner regularly posted on LinkedIn about cryptocurrency and posted images of gold coins. The human resources officer, who was last active on the platform two years ago, also shared what appears to be a selfie, edited to show him wearing a bitcoin-inspired facemask, holding a gold coin and with lasers coming out of his eyes. In other posts, he described himself as a 'disruptor' and share books he has read, including The Chaos Imperative and The Dichotomy of Leadership. Family photographs shared on other social media platforms show Tyner posing just days before his arrest, as well as enjoying holidays, visits to the beach and hiking trips. He served in Afghanistan between 2013 and 2014, and Qatar from 2016 to 2017. He also spent a year with a special forces task force that included SBS (Special Boat Service), Navy Seals and Delta Force. It was previously confirmed by US officials that Tyner had not handed over any information to Moscow. The intention is to prosecute him under the US military justice system, the official said. A second US official also confirmed Tyner's name and said he had been previously deployed to Afghanistan, Iraq and Qatar. Both spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss sensitive military matters. Lt Col Brandon Tyner, 41, who posted regularly about crypto currency online, was arrested by counter-terror police on suspicion of spying for Russia Tyner was a senior human resources officer at the time of his arrest on Tuesday on suspicion of assisting a foreign intelligence service and obtaining or disclosing protected information. Tyner was serving in Nato's ARRC – the multinational land unit within the Nato military alliance that is designed to quickly respond to crises or short-notice operations. According to the unit's website, it is made up of forces from 20 nations but is led by the British. Police said the arrest was not related to other counterterrorism investigations, indicating no connection with a suspected terror plot linked to Iran in September at a US-run base in Britain, RAF Fairford, near Gloucester. US Brigadier General Sean Stinchon said: 'This case should serve as a stark warning to any service member suspected of violating the oath we all take to protect and defend the Constitution. 'Whenever we suspect somebody of betraying the trust of their fellow soldiers and jeopardizing our collective defence, we will identify you, and we will ensure justice is served. 'This effort is a great example of the remarkable partnership Army Counterintelligence Command has with Army prosecutors, the FBI, DOJ, and the UK.'
