At a glance: Key proposals from Milburn's Neets report

Article text

At a glance: Key proposals from Milburn's Neets report - Published A major review by former minister Alan Milburn has proposed widespread changes to help get young people who are not in education, employment or training (Neet) into work across the UK. His report - which has now been published in full, external - says the UK should aim to reduce the rate of Neet young people from 13% to about 5% by 2036. Its proposals relate mostly to England, with a recommendation that Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland adapt the principles to suit their own systems. Here is a summary of the key points from the review: Rights for young people - Young people should have a legal right to a route into education, training or work, and would be supported to do so under an "opportunity guarantee" - A new, locally-led Youth Opportunity Service in England would give young people one named advisor and a personalised participation plan - spelling out what action needs to be taken, by whom, and when - Tax, spending and other policy decisions should be put through a "youth opportunity test", to ensure they consider impact on younger generations Schools - Prevention should start early, with primary and secondary schools required to identify and support pupils at risk of becoming Neet - Schools would also be required to offer work experience - A new performance measure would assess schools based on where their pupils end up - A new writing, numeracy and oracy check for pupils in Key Stage 3 (Years 7 to 9) Colleges and apprenticeships - An extra 150,000 18- to 24-year-olds studying further education courses at colleges and other training providers each year - College funding to be based on how students perform and where they end up, rather than on how many students there are - A new digital open college, part-funded by employers and private investors, which would allow young people to study courses from colleges and universities - Aim to double the number of young people completing apprenticeships over the next decade - with more than a third of young people starting one every year - Where has the most jobs for 18-24 year-olds? An extra 170,000 job and apprenticeship opportunities to be created through employer incentives - Employers who take on any young person who has been Neet for six months should receive a £3,000 payment - Small businesses - with fewer than 50 employees - which do so should receive an extra £2,000 - There would be an extra £2,000 payment if the young person has been in care or is disabled - Lower National Insurance costs for 21- to 24-year-olds, starting in retail and hospitality - Large employers should tell job applicants when they are unsuccessful Mental health - Young people who are already Neet should have guaranteed access to fast-track services like talking therapies and counselling within three months - These should be accessible without a diagnosis - A new digital-first mental health care service should be the "starting point for young people to access help" - The government should introduce national maximum waiting times for young people's mental health care from 2028 - Fitness to work, or "fit notes", to be issued through the new Youth Opportunity Service, rather than GPs - Why Milburn's Neets reforms will only save £200m a year by 2036 - Published16 hours ago Welfare - An "entirely new benefits system" for under-25s - Young people to no longer get the health top-up of Universal Credit, which provides extra money for people whose health conditions limit their capacity to work - They would also no longer receive Personal Independence Payments (Pip), which help with the extra living costs of having a long-term health condition or disability - Instead, a new youth allowance would be set at the basic rate of Universal Credit for under-25s, with a higher rate for those living independently - A "local flexible health and disability fund" to be run by the local Youth Opportunity Service would pay for extra costs while a young person is receiving support - An additional cost allowance would replace Pip, providing "targeted financial support where disability-related extra costs are well established, significant and ongoing" -
