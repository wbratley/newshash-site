Alan Milburn's report on youth unemployment proposes welfare and support reforms

Unbiased summary

Alan Milburn published a government-commissioned report on young people not in education, employment or training (Neet), recommending a legal right to education, training or work, a new Youth Opportunity Service, and reforms to schools, colleges, apprenticeships and welfare. The plan aims to reduce the Neet rate from about 13% to 5% by 2036, with a net cost of £200 million a year by 2036-37, partly funded by benefit cuts. Prime Minister Andy Burnham endorsed the principles and appointed Milburn to lead a new Youth Opportunities Unit. The report warns of a 'lost generation' and notes that Labour policies including National Insurance rises and minimum wage increases have made it harder for employers to hire young people.
