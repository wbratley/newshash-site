'I have seen first-hand how nature has the power to heal': Princess of Wales hints at cancer battle in Earthshot Prize speech as she warns 'precious' natural world is under threat

Article text

The Princess of Wales hinted at her own health battle as she took to the stage at the glittering Earthshot Prize environmental awards ceremony in Mumbai. Standing in front of a crowd of 5,000 at her husband's event on Tuesday night, Kate warned of how the 'precious' natural world is under threat, adding: 'I have seen first-hand how nature has the power to heal.' The Prince and Princess of Wales were joined by star-studded guests on the 'green carpet' for the annual event recognising innovative ideas which aim to 'repair' the planet. Kate unexpectedly took to the stage and delivered an emotional speech as she presented the 'restore nature' award to Bhutan. She told them: 'Nature is precious. 'We are not separate from it, we are part of it, deeply connected in ways both visible and unseen. 'The health of the planet and our natural world is inseparable from our own, and when it thrives, so do we. 'I have seen firsthand how nature has the power to heal and enrich our daily lives, but so much remains under threat.' William praised the determination of the 'Earthshot generation' in his speech to launch the hotly anticipated awards night at the Jio World Convention Centre. The Waleses took inspiration from Bollywood as they walked along the green carpet to a battery of flash bulbs, wearing outfits described as a 'celebration of India'. William had his arm around his wife's waist and could be seen stroking her back. The couple stopped for about 30 seconds to pose for photographers. They disappeared for a few minutes before re-emerging to meet the chief minister of Maharashtra, Devendra Fadnavis. The Waleses then walked into the auditorium to take their seats, with Kate flashing a warm smile at the media as they sat down. The princess was wearing an anarkali by Mahima Mahajan from handwoven silk georgette with traditional banarasi gold detailing. It was coloured in a rich vermilion using vegetable-based dyes, with hand embroidery and glass bead embellishments. Kate also sported the diamond and emerald tassel necklace belonging to the late Queen Elizabeth II with matching earrings as part of her sparkling apparel. William – wearing a bespoke bandhgala jacket with hand-embroidered buttons by Manish Malhotra – told 5,000 guests at the event: 'Change is not only possible. It is already happening. And that gives us every reason for optimism. 'We see that determination in generation Earthshot. Young people from around the world who are not waiting for someone else to find the answers. The royals greet Devendra Fadnavis, head of government for the state of Maharashtra – the capital of which is Mumbai, where the awards ceremony takes place – and his wife Amruta Fadnavis Kate wore her hair twisted at the back in cascading curls Prince William and Kate take inspiration from Bollywood as they stand on the 'green carpet' William and Catherine talk with British fashion editor Anna Wintour The Prince of Wales is wearing a bespoke Bandghala with hand-embroidered buttons, by Manish Malhotra, Made in India 'They are stepping forward, bringing fresh ideas and showing what is possible when optimism is matched with action.' Although in her speech Kate warned that entire species are being lost as habitats 'disappear before our eyes', she stressed there was reason for hope. 'Across the globe, people are finding new ways to protect, restore and nurture the world around us. They remind us that a brighter future is something that we can all achieve together, the Princess added. 'Just as the natural world finds ways to adapt and flourish, so too can we, when we nurture new ideas and give them the opportunity to grow. 'The people we're celebrating tonight are a huge part of that story. Their work will inspire and create seismic change that will improve lives for generations to come. 'Here are the finalists, doing remarkable things to Protect and Restore Nature.' Take That were among the performers during the evening and the event was their first-ever show in India. The band's stars, Gary Barlow, Mark Owen and Howard Donald spoke of how honoured they were to attend. Donald said that concern for the environment ran 'in the family'. They work tirelessly, and every year Earthshot is getting bigger. People are hearing about it. I think we can feel very proud of the work they are doing around the world. Barlow said he found the experience 'so inspiring' and would take it home to his children. 'How can you not?' he added. 'Especially with the world at the moment. It's such a crazy place, full of negativity. This is what we need. It's a wonderful celebration of what's to come next. This is an encouraging way of presenting what we should be doing.' The celebratory gala featured Hollywood actor Rami Malek, Indian cricketing legend Sachin Tendulkar, Bollywood stars Alia Bhatt and Sonam Kapoor, and Bridgerton's Simone Ashley. Indian singer Shreya Ghoshal performed for the guests, with the ceremony hosted by filmmaker and presenter of The Traitors India, Karan Johar. Arriving on the green carpet, Malek told the Daily Mail: 'To be here in Mumbai and India, where we have the largest contingency of Earthshot finalists and winners of any other event… it's just an honour to be here.' He said of William: 'I hate to name drop, but we have got to know each other over the years through this and through the Baftas. I hate to say it, but he's just a regular bloke. I love that about him – he cares so much. 'His family is so involved. He is carrying on this legacy, creating an environment that is crucial and vital for future generations to exist and flourish. 'We are responsible for it. That responsibility and care and attention that he brings and the way he galvanises the world to do that is quite extraordinary.' Asked as a former James Bond villain – in 2021's No Time To Die – about Robert Pattinson becoming the new 007, Malek laughed and said: 'I knew I would be asked about that! I have known Rob for quite some time and I could not be more proud. 'The fact that people are recognising, I think, maybe they all know, how talented that man is. I have known him for years. We worked together, we have become very close friends. 'We have been texting back and forth and it's been lovely to just share this moment with him and for the world to just see that he is going to be quite extraordinary. And that's coming from a villain!' Among this year's finalists were two Indian entries: Mahila Housing Trust, which is offering roof coatings to cool low-income homes and affordable insurance to protect poorer women's livelihoods when extreme heat makes work impossible. Jason Knauf, chief executive of the Earthshot Prize, said Kate had expressed an interest in expanding the Children's Earthshot Prize, which was launched in Mumbai for the first time. He said: 'She's just been blown away and so proud of what Prince William has achieved with the award. And she's also been giving us lots of ideas on how we can make the Children's Earthshot work even bigger next year. 'So it's always been great to have her on the team, but having her here in person means a lot. They're having an amazing time. 'The princess in the very beginning, when we were still debating whether or not we should do this [back in 2019], the loudest voice saying 'yes' was the princess. 'So she's always been a huge part of this, bringing the thing around children, families, nature is right at the core of it. So we're delighted to have them.' Earthshot ambassador, Australian conservationist and zookeeper Robert Irwin said: 'William and Catherine are such an incredible powerhouse duo. Catherine particularly has inspired me and inspired the world and she's just such an incredible force of nature. 'One of those people you hope to meet one day and meeting her exceeded every expectation.' Indian businessman Adar Poonawalla, worth approximately £12.3billion, attended the awards with his son, Darius. Mr Poonawalla is CEO of the Serum Institute of India, a biotechnology and biopharmaceutical company. Also in attendance was Shiladitya Mukhopadhyaya, a Tech entrepreneur reportedly worth £65million. Ahead of the ceremony, Barlow said William has taken his father's passion for the environment to the 'next level' with the Earthshot Prize. Take That recalled performing for royalty, including Diana, Princess of Wales, and getting the Royal Family dancing to Never Forget at the King's Coronation Concert at Windsor Castle three years ago. Barlow has written many of the band's hits during their career spanning more than 30 years, and he said they have met William around 20 times during that period. He said: 'William's dad, in the 70s, used to talk about microplastics, and people thought he was crazy. He's really following on [from] his dad's passion, I think. It's wonderful when dads pass down things [and]… they take the baton, taking on to the next level.' Take That have performed at the Royal Variety Performance four times, the 1993 Concert for Hope for World Aids Day at Wembley Arena attended by Diana, and they closed the King's Coronation Concert. 'Whenever I've been around anything that he's been involved in, he's there leading everybody. He's not letting other people lead him to the floor. He's the one leading everybody.' As William stepped from the vehicle, which has a top speed of 55mph and a range of almost 190 miles on one charge, he said: 'It was quite good fun, really good fun.' The three-wheeler is manufactured by Earthshot finalist Mahindra Last Mile Mobility, which has put 400,000 electric rickshaws on the streets of India in the past five years to help tackle air pollution in an affordable way. William met the company's chief executive officer, Suman Mishra, and said, laughing: 'I didn't crash your vehicle, so you're OK. I got quite excited and would like a longer promenade – I wanted to see what the top speed was. 'It's very quiet, very easy to drive – it's brilliant.' Mumbai, like New Delhi, has an issue with air quality and its skyscrapers are shrouded in smog for much of the day. Mahindra is vying for £1million in prize money in the Clean Our Air category and will use the funds to expand the education of women drivers and the use of electric rickshaws. William wished Ms Mishra good luck for the awards ceremony and said: 'This has been a labour of love for you and I know you've nearly given up several times, but well done, you're here now for a very good reason.' The company boss said the prince 'drove so well', adding: 'Before, I was a little scared to be honest and after it was such a great honour that he chose us, our vehicle and to have the Earthshot [branding] and really represent us, that really gives us a boost and momentum.' Today, William and Kate also attended the inaugural Children's Earthshot Awards and described as 'incredible' the youngsters who had created solutions to tackle environmental challenges in their local communities. As the pupils accepted their award on stage, one told the audience: 'We're all a bit anxious as Their Highnesses are here, but let's say five words together – Let's Make the World Smile!' After the ceremony held at the Museum of Solutions – a children's attraction designed to spark creativity and problem-solving – the couple posed for a group photo with the winners. William told them: 'Really well done, guys. Your energy and optimism is second to none,' while Kate added: 'You're all incredible. Honestly – watching those films, and hearing what you've all done, it was incredibly moving.' The princess had changed from a white trouser suit for the rickshaw ride to a powder-blue Gabriela Hearst jacket and matching trousers, with a bespoke Dior bag made for her by designer Jonathan Anderson, featuring traditional Indian craftsmanship on the handle. Before leaving, the couple played a water activity game with a group of children, throwing balls into a water-jet and hoping it would carry the object through a hole at the top of a Perspex waterfall. 'This is harder than it looks,' laughed William. 'I could stay here all day,' said Kate. A Kensington Palace spokesman said: 'The princess is enormously proud of what the prince has achieved with Earthshot and is really looking forward to being alongside him for what is one of the most important moments in his year. 'She's seen it develop into something truly remarkable, and is excited to experience first-hand just how far it has come.'
