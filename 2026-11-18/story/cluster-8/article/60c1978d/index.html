Princess Kate is one in vermilion as she glitters at William's Earthshot Prize

Article text

Princess Kate is one in vermilion as she glitters at William's Earthshot Prize The Prince and Princess of Wales were a living celebration of India as they dazzled on the 'green carpet' in Mumbai on Tuesday night. Despite a host of Bollywood – and Hollywood – celebrities, all eyes were on William and Kate as they arrived for the Earthshot Prize ceremony. The Princess was wearing an anarkali – a traditional South Asian outfit – by Mahima Mahajan, made from handwoven silk with traditional gold detailing, coloured in a rich vermilion. It also celebrated India's textile heritage with hand-embroidery and glass bead embellishments. And she topped off the already glittering result with Queen Elizabeth's diamond and emerald tassel necklace and earrings. The Prince, meanwhile, wore a bespoke bandhgala – a high-collar jacket – by Manish Malhotra. The award event, where the five £1million winners of William's environmental initiative were announced, capped a busy day of engagements, beginning with a trip on an electric rickshaw driven by the Prince. Founded by William in 2020, The Earthshot Prize seeks to discover, celebrate and scale innovative solutions to the world's greatest environmental challenges. The Prince and Princess of Wales dazzled on the green carpet during the Earthshot Prize Awards Night in Mumbai on Tuesday evening The Princess wore a traditional South Asian anarkali paired with Queen Elizabeth II's diamond and emerald tassel necklace and earrings The Prince donned a bespoke bandhgala to the event, which was held at the Jio World Convention Centre The event marks the first official foreign trip taken by the Princess of Wales in four years It was hosted by Bollywood's Karan Johar, with a starry line-up of performers and presenters, including Take That, actress Simone Ashley, Oscar-winner Rami Malek and Indian stars Alia Bhatt and Sonam Kapoor. And the Princess even took centre stage in a surprise moment that had the audience on its feet. In front of 5,000 people in the auditorium, and millions more streamed worldwide, she presented the 'restore nature' award to the Kingdom of Bhutan and said: 'Nature is precious. 'We are not separate from it, we are part of it, deeply connected in ways both visible and unseen. 'The health of the planet and our natural world is inseparable from our own, and when it thrives, so do we.' In what may be seen as a subtle reference to her own health, she added: 'I have seen first-hand how nature has the power to heal and enrich our daily lives, but so much remains under threat. 'Entire species are being lost as habitats that have... sustained life... for millions of years, disappear before our eyes. Fortunately, there is reason for hope. Across the globe, people are finding new ways to protect, restore and nurture the world around us. 'The people we're celebrating tonight are a huge part of that.' Kate has never been a natural public speaker but has grown in confidence and last night's performance was arguably her best. Simone Ashley stunned in an intricate gold Manish Malhotra look topped with a golden-yellow lehenga The actress glowed in the multicoloured embellished ensemble Take That stars Gary Barlow, Howard Donald and Mark Owen walked the event's green carpet Her husband then spoke of his fears for the planet – the heatwaves and the floods – but also of his determination to leave the world a better place. He said: 'We cannot look into the eyes of the next generation and tell them that we knew what was happening, we knew what was at stake, yet we did not do everything in our power to change it.' Award-winners included PFAS-Free France in the Build a Waste-Free World category, aimed at eliminating 'forever chemicals'. Niue Ocean Wide Trust, a conservation scheme to safeguard marine life around the Pacific island, won the Revive Our Oceans category; Mahindra Last Mile Mobility, which rolled out one million electric rickshaws across India, took the Clean Our Air prize, and Danish company Orsted, which has adopted offshore wind energy, received the Fix Our Climate gong. Malek, who was presenting an award, said of the Prince: 'He cares so much. He is carrying on this legacy.' As he met the stars after the ceremony, William was heard to say: 'Big year next year, Australia!' A Wales family trip Down Under has long been rumoured, so could his accidental reveal suggest it may be on the cards? Kensington Palace declined to comment.
