The first truly weird Bond: 13 things to know about Robert Pattinson – from heavy saliva to dead clowns

Article text

It’s bad news for Callum Turner, Jack Lowden and Josh O’Connor, of course, but the announcement that Robert Pattinson will be the next 007 means that those people who have never cared much about James Bond finally have a reason to. The appointment has been greeted with much excitement by everyone who has had a soft spot for R-Patz since Twilight, the sense-defying sparkly vampire franchise that transformed him into a reluctant teen heart-throb and household name. Since then, Pattinson has established himself as an unpredictable but always watchable star, known as much for his chaotic interviews and palpable discomfort with celebrity as for his blockbuster performances. In honour of the first Weird Bond, then, here’s a look back at all his lore: from tall tales about a clown car to lockdown-era experiments with “pasta you can hold”. 1. He was a teenage pin-up Pattinson was born in Barnes, south-west London, the youngest of three children. His father, Richard, ran a vintage car-import business, while his mother, Clare, worked at a modelling agency. Young Rob developed an interest in the performing arts while attending Tower House Boys’ Prep, and played Robert in the school’s production of Lord of the Flies. He went on to attend the exclusive Harrodian school and found some success as a model, appearing in the UK teen magazine Bliss as one of its “Bliss Boy” centrefolds. According to a Q&A now widely shared online, the then 15-year-old Taurus’s favourite hobby was DJing and his favourite chat-up line was: “Do you believe in love at first sight, or shall I walk past again?” 2. … and entrepreneur Pattinson has said he was expelled from Tower House for stealing adult magazines from a local corner shop, then flogging them to his classmates. “I got so cocky that I’d take the entire rack … Every single one of my friends snitched on me, literally across the board,” he told radio host Howard Stern in 2017. 3. … and an outstanding paperboy Pattinson had Bond potential even as a teenager, according to Vipin Patel, the owner of Natsons newsgents in Barnes, who gave him his first job. Interviewed by Business Insider in 2020, Patel said “Robert the paperboy” was always handsome (if not stylish) and took his duty seriously, once volunteering to do his round despite a “very bad storm”. Though Pattinson was a late entry in the 007 race, Patel called it after he was cast as Batman: “I can just picture him. His acting is superb … He has to be James Bond next.” 4. He loves a good story Pattinson is infamous for telling tall tales on the press circuit. Among them: he used to be a women’s hand model; as a teenager, he rapped under the name Big Tub and the Tabbycats; he has “extraordinarily heavy saliva” and enjoys being spat on, erotically; he can “turn on and off” his sense of smell; and, while making the film Good Time, he “basically” gave a dog a handjob. He has said that he makes up stories out of “panic”, though he has also admitted, to the New York Times Style Magazine, to doing it as a means of entertaining himself: “The only thing people would ever ask me about was being famous. You go into, like, a fugue state.” In 2011, he made up a story on the Today Show about witnessing the death of a circus clown in childhood (“His little car exploded”). Another anecdote, about him driving away a stalker by asking her to dinner and boring her to tears by complaining about his life, was widely shared as fact before Pattinson admitted it was fiction. “I’m like, ‘What on earth? Are you possessed?’” Pattinson has said of his past interviews. At other times he speaks perfect sense, as when he declined to explain the plot of Christopher Nolan’s 2020 blockbuster, Tenet. “Even if I had seen it, I genuinely don’t know if I’d be able to.” 5. … but he’s protective of his privacy Pattinson has been with actor and musician Suki Waterhouse since 2018. According to E!, their first date was to see Mamma Mia! Here We Go Again at Electric Cinema in Notting Hill; they were photographed afterwards, kissing and dancing in the street. Their engagement was confirmed in December 2023, and their first daughter was born the following March. Her name has still not been publicly revealed. 6. He’s not really built for the lifestyle of the rich and famous Pattinson was 21 when he was cast in Twilight, the film adaptation of Stephenie Meyer’s immensely popular vampire romance. He is estimated to have made about $70m (around £50m) across the five-film franchise, but it is fair to say he never took to the celebrity lifestyle. He spent some of his Twilight paycheck on a Shelby Mustang – “like the one from Gone in 60 Seconds” – but soon regretted the indulgence. “You don’t realise that it’s not a car that you can drive every day. The number of times that I had humiliating experiences, where it would choose to just not start or break down – always in front of enormous groups of tourists or something like that …” Later, he quit smoking and took up cigars, smoking the same one over four days until someone called him out. “I was like, “Yeah, you can just keep relighting it,’” Pattinson said. “They were like, ‘That’s not how you’re supposed to do it.’” 7. He’s on a box office tear Has any actor had a bigger year than Pattinson’s 2026? He’s been inescapable at the box office, with parts in The Drama, The Odyssey and Primetime, and Dune: Part Three still to come. Pattinson told GQ in September that he had “literally been going nonstop” since the writers’ and actors’ strike of late 2023. During that enforced pause, he said, he “recalibrated” his professional goals, returning with Lynne Ramsay’s Die My Love, co-starring Jennifer Lawrence. At one point earlier this year, Pattinson was prepping four films simultaneously. “I think it’s worked out,” he said. “At the end of this, I’m definitely taking a vacation.” This was before the Bond announcement – it might have to be a minibreak. 8. … but he fought his way up from the cutting-room floor Pattinson’s first film role was a small part in 2004’s Vanity Fair, starring Reese Witherspoon, his later co-star in Water for Elephants. He discovered that his scene had been cut while watching the film at the premiere. (You can find it on DVD, in the deleted scenes.) To make amends, the film’s casting director landed Pattinson an audition for Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire, and he was cast as Hogwarts heart-throb Cedric Diggory. “I spent a lot of time living off the Harry Potter money,” Pattinson told GQ in a video interview. 9. Pattinson will always be Edward Cullen The Twilight franchise grossed more than US $3bn (£2.2bn) globally and transformed Pattinson and co-star Kristen Stewart into two of the most famous people alive, helped by their off-screen romance. Fourteen years after the last instalment, Pattinson remains indelible as the sparkly vampire Cullen, but he was by no means a shoo-in for the part. According to Twilight director Catherine Hardwicke, studio execs questioned the casting, asking her: “Do you think you can make this guy look good?” “I said, ‘Yeah, I do. Did you see his cheekbones?’” Fans of the books were also sceptical: more than 75,000 reportedly signed an online petition objecting to him as Cullen. “The initial reaction was 100%, ‘He’s completely wrong for the role,’” Pattinson said in 2019, “and I agreed with them.” 10. … much as he would like to move on Pattinson has made no secret of his contempt for Twilight. Even promoting the film, he described the story as Meyer’s private sexual fantasies, which should never have been published, and denounced Cullen’s creepy courtship as like that of “an axe murderer”. “I thought it was a strange story,” he reiterated in 2019 in a Variety conversation with Jennifer Lopez. Pattinson’s unwavering disdain for the franchise eventually earned him grudging respect among the “Twi-hards” fandom, who have a running joke: “Nobody hates Twilight more than the cast of Twilight.” 11. His youthful relationship was of interest to Trump Pattinson’s highly public four-year relationship with Stewart broke down after she was photographed with director Rupert Sanders in 2012. Her rumoured infidelity was explosive among the Twilight fandom and on social media, drawing furious commentary from the future president Trump. “Robert Pattinson should not take back Kristen Stewart … He can do much better!” he said on X. Astonishingly, Stewart was pressed to publicly apologise, though she and Pattinson reconciled before permanently parting ways in early 2013. She has subsequently spoken out about the pressure on them: “People wanted me and Rob to be together so badly that our relationship was made into a product … That was gross to me,” she told the New York Times Style Magazine. 12. He has innovated in the pasta space Pattinson is no foodie, describing his preferences (at least before he became a father) to “eat like a wild animal. Like, out of a trash can.” In the pandemic he fully let his freak flag fly, giving a freewheeling interview to GQ over Zoom about his lockdown attempts to create “a pasta which you can hold in your hand”. He claimed to have designed a prototype using a panini press and “like, nine” packets of presliced cheese, and came up with a name: Piccolini Cuscino, or “Little Pillow”. He even set up a meeting to discuss the idea with celebrity restaurateur Lele Massimini. “I told him my business plan,” Pattinson told GQ, “and his facial expression didn’t even change afterwards.” Lest we mistake this for another Pattinson yarn, Massimini verified this account as “100% true”. 13. He is not a health nut Don’t expect a ripped 007: Pattinson has an ambivalent relationship to working out, and admitted to letting his training regimen for The Batman slide during the pandemic. He told GQ that actors “working out all the time” were “part of the problem … No one was doing this in the ‘70s. Even James Dean – he wasn’t exactly ripped.” (He also said he was asked only once to remove his shirt in his Twilight years: “I think they told me to put it back on again.”) Later, after people criticised his, ah, cuddlier Batman, Pattinson said he’d been exaggerating his lack of discipline, “trying to sound cool” to GQ. “I worked out every day,” he complained – sometimes even twice. “Even after that, I still look like I didn’t work out.”
