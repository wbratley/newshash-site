Robert Pattinson’s gift for cool, sensual menace makes him an exquisite choice as James Bond | Peter Bradshaw

Article text

If it wasn’t Bond … maybe it would have been Dr No in some hi-tech reboot. But Robert Pattinson – with his movie-star chops and track record in hip A24-type character turns, and his gift for cool, inscrutable appraisal and sensual menace – is an exquisite choice as 007, playing on the darker and more sophisticated notes of Pattinson’s performing personality. And let’s hope, incidentally, that the actor now isn’t forced into a five-hour daily gym regimen to bulk up. I like the taller and more willowy Bond that Pattinson represents, the Bond from whose frame the tux hangs gracefully, the Bond with elegant abandon who’s a former British Naval officer, not a former US Navy Seal, the Bond who with a crinkly-eyed reptilian grin can style out waking up with a hangover after too many whisky-and-sodas at the baccarat table. But I like to think also that Pattinson could convert the diversionary cuff-shooting gesture of consulting his Rolex, to delivering a single lethal punch to the enemy’s unguarded solar plexus. (Although fisticuffs will be for the lower order of henchpersons; the Pattinson 007’s final confrontation with the primo villain will probably involve more hi-tech weaponry.) Pattinson could be a Bond to combine Roger Moore’s insouciance with the chill of Michael Lonsdale’s portrayal of plutocrat-villain Hugo Drax. At 40 years old, Pattinson’s Bond will be a more worldly and mature agent, and his entire career has arguably been leading to this moment – establishment schooling at Hogwarts (rather than Fettes) as the doomed Cedric Diggory in Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire all the way through to the darkly conflicted caped crusader Batman in Matt Reeves’s 2022 film, lurking athletically in the shadows – like Bond, used to working in secret. He didn’t play Batman for Christopher Nolan, but for Nolan he interestingly played the slightly seedy British intelligence insider Neil in Nolan’s 2020 sci-fi Tenet and the unscrupulous Antinous this year in The Odyssey – both not good guys, character roles, both showing Pattinson’s class as well as his presence. Yet perhaps his most intriguing pre-Bond or proto-Bond outing might have been the alienated super-rich guy cruising the New York streets in his stretch limo in David Cronenberg’s Cosmopolis. Like Bond (or a future Bond) that was someone developing a tough, invulnerable carapace. Yet for all the saturnine, even serpentine presence, Pattinson’s 007 could well be the one to find something other than the traditional cliched machismo and sexism in Bond in his relationship with female characters and indeed female colleagues – perhaps we might even see Bond’s feminine side. It’s an exciting bit of (long-delayed) casting.
