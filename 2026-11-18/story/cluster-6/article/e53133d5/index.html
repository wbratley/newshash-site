Russell Brand's new prison was slammed by inspectors for major 'threats to security'

Article text

Russell Brand's new prison was slammed by inspectors for major 'threats to security' An action plan for the prison found 101 outstanding maintenance issues involving broken CCTV cameras, as well as damaged lighting in the prison grounds, which put security at risk Russell Brand's new prison was slammed at an inspection three years ago, with multiple major security threats identified before the convicted rapist arrived. Former entertainer Brand has been moved from category B HMP Wandsworth to category C institution, HMP Littlehey, in rural Cambridgeshire. The 51-year-old was moved over the weekend after being convicted and sentenced for rape and three counts of sexual assault. According to the prison's last inspection report from 2023, most prisoners spend about nine hours a day out of their cells, which the inspector said was more than other prisons. An action plan for HMP Littlehey found 101 outstanding maintenance issues involving broken CCTV cameras, as well as damaged lighting in the prison grounds, which put security at risk. The findings were included in an action plan submitted by the prison service on January 12, 2024, in response to an inspection conducted between September 4 and 14, 2023. The inspection report was published on December 4, 2023, and is the most recent report available concerning the conditions of the prison. The action plan said the 101 outstanding issues with CCTV were being managed by the prison’s facilities management provider, with progress updates expected every two weeks. The report reads: "CCTV: Multiple legacy repairs outstanding which is being managed bythe FM provider to progress and update to be provided bi-weekly. he number of issues is 101 (at time of audit) tripartite. The APOM is working with the FM provider to review this matter." The plan adds that contractors had been hired to address the broken external lighting, with the work being carried out as part of routine maintenance. The document listed the CCTV repairs as ongoing and set September 2024 as the target date for the lighting work. But it does not confirm whether all the repairs had been completed before Brand was transferred there. The other 'priority' and 'key' concerns about the prison identified in the inspection include: - Health care services lacked the resources to meet the needs of the population - The governance and oversight of medicines management was weak -medicines were found to be out of date, room temperatures were not being recorded and arrangements for the disposal of unused medicines and controlled drug administration were unsafe - There were far too many longstanding unaddressed maintenance issues - numerous closed-circuit television cameras were, for example, not working effectively and lights in the prison grounds were also in a state of disrepair, both of which were a threat to the security of the prison. A further example was the condition of showers, which were in need of refurbishment - There were not enough activity spaces, which limited the ability of prisoners to gain the knowledge and skills they needed to help them on release - The promotion of equality and diversity was limited by a failure to use data effectively and by a lack of consultation - There was insufficient provision of English, mathematics and English for speakers of other languages to meet the needs of the population - The prison needed a reading strategy to support literacy based on a meaningful assessment of need among the prisoner population - Contact between offender managers and prisoners was mostly task-driven and did not consistently support sentence progression and risk management. Key work was also limited - The late allocation of community offender managers delayed the handover of responsibility for the prisoner to the community and had a negative impact on arrangements for their release. Escalation by the prison of issues such as confirmation of MAPPA levels was not prompt or consistent Brand spent the first days of his sentence of more than 10 years for one count of rape and three of sexual assault at HMP Wandsworth, where a former prison governor says he would have been a "drain on resources", the BBC has reported. The BBC reports Lindsay Whitehouse, a former prison governor, saying Brand would have likely been held in solitary confinement, requiring direct supervision whenever he left his cell, at HMP Wandsworth, because of his profile and the offences he has been convicted of. "He's a drain on the resources because he has to be supervised constantly," he says. "That would be quite a challenge for the prison if they had too many prisoners like that." According to the prison service, about 1,200 men who have been convicted of sexual offences are imprisoned in Littlehey - the largest prison in Europe for men convicted of sexual offences. About 10% of its roughly 1,200 inmates are foreign nationals, and 41% of them are over the age of 50. Littlehey's prisoners have access to weekly yoga classes, a football league, and origami lessons, the BBC report continues. The Ministry of Justice has been contacted for comment.
