Russell Brand’s message from prison a ‘contradiction’, says alleged rape victim

Log in A woman who claims she was raped by Russell Brand when she was 16 says his first message from behind bars, in which he protested his innocence, was a “contradiction”. Brand’s wife, Laura Gallacher, shared the message after the 51-year-old served the first week of his prison sentence. He was found guilty earlier this month of raping one woman and sexually assaulting three others during his career. Mia Latter, now 37, alleges she was mistreated by the jailed comedian when she was a schoolgirl and he was aged 30. She was referred to under the pseudonym of Alice in previous reporting by the Sunday Times, The Times, and the Channel 4 Dispatches documentary. Ms Latter, who has waived her right to anonymity, told BBC Radio 4’s Today programme: “At this point, I shouldn’t really be shocked by anything that he does because he was making videos every day after being in court all day, putting them up on his various platforms, and I couldn’t believe that was allowed; I thought that was contempt of court. “But this was really shocking to me, because guidelines state that prisoners shouldn’t try and use third parties to post on social media on their behalf, and to have this Christian message alongside absolute lies, it was such a contradiction, and it was appalling; frankly, it was upsetting to watch. “And I just felt for those women that stood trial and everything they went through. “They’ve got their verdict, and for him to say, ‘I’m innocent, I’m not guilty’ – you are. You’re a convicted rapist now. There’s no arguing with that.” Ms Latter’s allegation was reported to the Metropolitan Police. While she has since said in an interview she has decided not to continue with the criminal process, the force has said the investigation into the alleged rape remains ongoing. She is calling for the introduction of a staggered age of consent, which would make relationships between adults and teens aged 16 and 17 illegal, something which she says could have stopped her relationship with Brand “dead in its tracks”. “It would have 100 per cent made a difference because my parents tried to stop the relationship,” she told the BBC. “They tried to prevent me from seeing him. They did all the things that I see in the comments from people saying, ‘What were the parents doing? Why didn’t they lock her inside?’ They did. “‘Why didn’t they take our phone away?’ They did. I had to go to school still, and that was when he sent cars to my school. But if it had been a criminal offence for him to be having a relationship with me, none of the subsequent abuse would have ever happened because they would have been able to stop the relationship dead in its tracks. “And when my mum met Brand face to face, and she wanted to look him in the eye and plead with him, adult to adult, and he just shrugged at her and said, ‘What?’ because he knew there was nothing she could do. “She had nowhere to go legally.”
